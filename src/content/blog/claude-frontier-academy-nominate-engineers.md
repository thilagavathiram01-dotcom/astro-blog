Anthropic launched Claude Frontier Academy on October 2, 2026, with a $100 million commitment to train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers by the end of 2027. You cannot self-enroll. Organizations nominate engineers, and the first cohorts are already running in San Francisco, New York, and London.

This guide covers who the program is for, what the residency actually includes, and how a company asks to take part. It is based on Anthropic’s announcement and the program page, not third-party summaries.

## What Claude Frontier Academy is

Claude Frontier Academy is Anthropic’s training program for people who will lead Claude work inside a company. The first track is the Frontier Deployed Engineer Residency. Anthropic says it trains residents to the same standard its own engineers use, drawn from deployments inside large enterprises and the professional services firms that serve them.

Steve Corfield, Global Head of Business Development and Partnerships at Anthropic, put the aim this way: a small team with the right skills, access to Claude, and a deep read of the business can change how a company runs. The Academy is meant to build that depth inside customers and partners.

The program page lists six design rules: nomination only, designed by Anthropic practitioners, grounded in real work, small cohorts with peers from other organizations, a credential that proves capability, and a start with enterprise customers and partners before wider roles later.

It sits next to, not instead of, the Claude Partner Network. Anthropic says professionals across 46,000 firms have earned more than 175,000 Claude certifications, and nearly 4,000 people have completed Basecamp, the immersive course that uses the same curriculum Anthropic’s Applied AI teams get when they onboard. Frontier Academy is the track for people who will lead production work.

## Who is in the first cohorts

Anthropic named Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley, and Novo Nordisk among the opening organizations. The program page says the residency is in early access with a select group of customers and partners, and that Anthropic expects to expand it.

Accenture’s public comment framed the match: forward deployed engineers already work inside client workflows, and the residency tests judgment on a simulated enterprise deployment rather than technical drills alone.

## Who qualifies for nomination

Anthropic is looking for hands-on software engineers, not general managers attending a briefing. The published bar includes:

- Strong software fundamentals.
- A record of building with large language models. Prior experience building AI agents is not required.
- Experience helping other people adopt AI.
- A habit of taking on the business’s most important problems.

Each nominee arrives with a named Claude project they will lead when they return. That project is part of the entry, not an afterthought. If your team cannot name the system the engineer will own, the nomination is not ready.

## Step 1: Name the production use case

Start with the work, not the badge. Anthropic describes the goal as agentic systems that change how a business runs, from faster redesigned processes to new products and services.

Write the project in one paragraph before you contact anyone. Include the user, the system it touches, the data it may see, and the outcome you will measure. A useful test: could a security reviewer understand the scope from that paragraph alone?

If you already run Claude Code or the API in a pilot, tie the project to that stack. Our walkthrough of [Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code and the API](/blog/claude-opus-5-5-claude-code-api/) covers how teams pick a model and call pattern before they commit an engineer to a 12-week build.

## Step 2: Pick the engineer, not the title

Nomination is the only door. The program page says there is a high bar for every program, and participation is by organization, not by open application.

Choose someone who already ships and already teaches. The residency ends with that person leading a real deployment and, Anthropic says, spreading the skill to the teams around them. A strong individual contributor who has never helped a colleague adopt a tool is a weaker fit than a slightly less senior engineer who has.

## Step 3: Ask your Anthropic contact

There is no public signup form for the residency. Anthropic’s instructions are specific:

1. Customers register interest with their Anthropic account team.
2. Partners register interest with their partner manager.
3. If you are unsure which applies, ask the account team whether the organization is eligible.

Cohorts are running now in San Francisco, New York, and London. Anthropic has not published a public schedule, seat price, or open-enrollment date. Do not treat a blog recap as an application link.

## What the four-day intensive covers

The program page breaks the opening block into four days. Residents spend three days in person with Anthropic engineers and licensed instructors, building a Claude system for a simulated enterprise. The path runs from the first customer request through security review to handover.

On the fourth day they are graded on a new scenario. Engineers who pass earn the Claude Resident Engineer badge and move into the residency. Those who do not pass do not continue on that track.

The news post describes the same block as a multi-day in-person program ending in a graded practical. Use the four-day split when you plan travel: three build days, then an assessment day.

## What the 12-week residency requires

After the badge, residents spend 12 weeks leading a Claude deployment at their own organization. Support comes from Anthropic engineers and from the cohort. This is not a second classroom. The named project from nomination is the work.

Plan for the engineer to be on that project as their main job, not as evening study. They will need access to the systems, a security reviewer who can respond during the 12 weeks, and a manager who can clear blockers. Anthropic assesses them again at the end. Those who pass earn the Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer badge. Anthropic expects to award the first of those credentials in early 2027.

## How this differs from Claude Academy and Basecamp

Claude Academy at academy.claude.com is open to everyone and covers certifications. Partner Basecamp is an immersive course for partner organizations. Claude Frontier Academy is nomination-only, in person for the intensive, and tied to a live deployment.

If your company is not an Anthropic customer or partner, the practical path is the public Academy and product docs, then a commercial relationship if you want the residency later. Additional Academy programs are planned for 2027. Anthropic has not named them yet.

## Tips before you nominate

- Bring a named project. Anthropic asks for one at entry.
- Confirm the engineer can travel to San Francisco, New York, or London for the intensive. Remote-only delivery is not described.
- Book security review time before the 12 weeks start. The simulated build includes a security review, and the live project will need one too.
- Do not promise the Frontier Deployed Engineer badge in a performance plan. It depends on two assessments, and the first awards are expected in early 2027.
- Keep public certifications separate. The 175,000 Claude certifications are a different program from this residency.

## What to do this week

If you already have an Anthropic account team or partner manager, send them the project paragraph and the nominee’s background this week. Ask whether the organization is eligible for a later cohort, since the opening groups are already underway.

If you do not have that contact, use Claude Academy and your current Claude Code or API work to build the record the residency asks for: shipping with language models, and helping other people adopt the tools. That record is the part you control while seats stay nomination-only.
