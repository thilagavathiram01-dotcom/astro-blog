Google expanded Personal Intelligence in the Gemini app on October 22, 2026. It now pulls your Google Maps searches and saved places, then combines them with Calendar data to suggest spots that fit your day.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="75" />

Instead of listing every restaurant you have saved, you can ask a natural question and receive options near your next meeting. The feature stays opt-in. You choose which apps Gemini can read, and you can turn any connection off later.

![Person using a smartphone with maps and apps](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1512941937669-90a1b58e7e9c?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What the Maps Expansion Actually Does

Personal Intelligence already connected Workspace (Gmail, Calendar, Drive), Photos, YouTube, and Search services. The Search services connection included Maps, Shopping, News, and Flights. The October update deepens the Maps link so Gemini can reference places you searched or saved, not just public map data.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="75" />

Google’s example pairs it with Calendar: “I have a list of restaurants I’ve been meaning to try. What’s the best spot for after my meetings today?” Gemini checks the location of your last meeting and surfaces matching saved places nearby. You stay in control—nothing is shared until you enable the connection.

The same setting applies across the Gemini app on Android, iOS, and the web. Eligibility remains limited to personal Google accounts for users 18 and older. Workspace business, enterprise, and education accounts are excluded, as are the European Economic Area, Switzerland, the UK, and Nigeria.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="23" />

## How to Turn It On and Connect Maps

Open the Gemini app or visit gemini.google.com. Tap your profile picture (or the menu on web), then choose Personal Intelligence. If the toggle is off, turn it on. Next open Connected Apps and enable Search services or the specific Maps option if it appears separately. Also turn on Google Workspace if you want Calendar data included.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="96" />

Confirm that Keep Activity is enabled in your Gemini privacy settings. For the fullest experience, make sure your Google Maps account has saved places and that location history or relevant settings are active on the device you use most. Changes sync across devices once you are signed in with the same personal account.

If you already enabled Personal Intelligence for other apps, the Maps data becomes available after the rollout reaches your account. Google is rolling the update out gradually, so it may take a day or two to appear.

## Step-by-Step: Get a Recommendation

1. Open Gemini and confirm Personal Intelligence is active with Maps and Calendar connected.
2. Ask a question that references your day and preferences. Example: “Suggest a quiet café near my 3 p.m. meeting that matches places I saved last month.”
3. Review the response. Gemini should cite or link places drawn from your saved list or recent searches.
4. Tap a place name to open it in Google Maps for directions, hours, or reviews.
5. If the answer feels off, refine the prompt: “Only show places I already saved” or “Avoid chains.”

You can also ask follow-ups in the same chat. “Is that place open after 7?” or “Add it to my route” keeps the context without starting over. For driving scenarios, the same connected data powers Gemini on Android Auto when Personal Intelligence is enabled—see the existing guide on enabling it for the car.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="16" />

## Practical Prompt Ideas

- “What’s the best lunch spot from my saved list within 10 minutes of my last calendar event?”
- “Find a park I searched for recently that works for a short walk after work.”
- “Recommend a dinner place near tonight’s event that matches the type of restaurants I save.”
- “Show me coffee shops I looked at last week that are open early near home.”

These prompts work because Gemini now reasons across the two sources instead of requiring you to paste addresses or names. Results are more specific than a generic Maps search, yet they remain limited to data you have already stored in Google.

## Tips and Privacy Controls

Start with Calendar and Maps only. Add Photos or Gmail later if you want more context. You can disconnect any app at any time from the same Connected Apps screen. Turning Personal Intelligence off entirely returns Gemini to standard answers without personal data.

Review your Google Maps saved places periodically. Delete outdated entries so recommendations stay relevant. Gemini does not train its foundational models on your personal context, according to Google’s statements on the feature. Inputs and outputs from your chats can still be used to improve the service under the usual activity settings.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="19" />

If you use Android Auto, the same Connected Apps preferences carry over. You do not need a separate setup in the car. For pure navigation without personalization, leave the feature off—Maps, calls, and music continue to work normally.

## Conclusion

The Maps expansion makes Gemini more useful for everyday planning without forcing you to retype details. Connect the apps you trust, try a few calendar-aware prompts, and adjust the connections as needed. The result is recommendations grounded in places you already care about rather than generic lists.
