Google rolled out a redesigned in-app camera for the Gemini Android app in mid-November 2026. The update replaces the old fullscreen viewfinder with a bottom sheet that appears directly on the home screen or inside an active chat.

This change makes it faster to snap a photo and ask Gemini about it. You no longer leave the conversation flow. The redesign first appeared on iOS and reached Android in Google app version 17.76 and later.

## What Changed in the Gemini Camera

The previous camera opened from the plus menu as a basic fullscreen viewfinder. It felt separate from the rest of the Gemini experience and offered limited controls.

The new version launches as a bottom sheet. You can drag the sheet upward with a smooth animation until it fills most of the screen. The viewfinder extends nearly edge-to-edge and can sit behind the system navigation bar.

Controls moved too. A three-dot overflow button sits to the right of the shutter. Tapping it animates and reveals flash options plus a front-camera toggle. A back button remains on the left side. You can also swipe down on the viewfinder to close the camera.

Note that launching the camera from the Gemini overlay still uses the older fullscreen interface.

## How to Open and Use the New Camera

Update the Google app to version 17.76 or newer if you do not see the change. Force-stop the Google app and reopen it if the new interface is missing after the update.

Follow these steps:

1. Open the Gemini app on your Android phone.
2. Tap the plus icon next to the prompt field on the home screen or inside a chat.
3. Select the camera option. The bottom sheet appears.
4. Drag the sheet upward if you want a larger viewfinder.
5. Frame your subject and tap the shutter button.
6. Add your question or instruction about the photo and send.

The photo attaches to your prompt immediately. Gemini can then describe the image, identify objects, extract text, or answer questions about what it sees.

## Camera Controls Explained

The three-dot menu replaces the scattered controls from the old viewfinder. Tap it to open flash settings. You can choose auto, on, or off depending on your lighting. The same menu contains the switch to the front-facing camera for selfies or front-view shots.

Closing options are flexible. Use the back button on the left of the shutter or simply swipe down on the sheet. Both methods return you to the chat or home screen without extra taps.

Because the sheet sits inside the Gemini interface, you can take the photo, review the attachment, and refine your prompt in one place. This is especially useful for quick lookups such as identifying a plant, reading a receipt, or translating a sign.

## Tips for Better Results

Good lighting still matters. The bottom-sheet viewfinder does not change how the phone camera captures the image. Move to a brighter area or use the flash when needed.

Hold the phone steady for a moment after tapping the shutter. This reduces blur on text-heavy images such as documents or labels.

Ask specific questions. Instead of "What is this?", try "What plant is this and how often should I water it?" or "Extract the total from this receipt." Clear prompts produce more useful replies.

If you frequently use the camera inside chats, keep the Google app updated. Newer versions may refine the animation and control placement.

## Known Limitations

The new bottom sheet does not appear when you start the camera from the Gemini overlay. That path still opens the older fullscreen viewfinder.

Availability depends on the Google app version. Some devices receive the update later than others. Force-stopping the app is the most reliable way to surface the change after an update.

The redesign does not add new camera hardware features. Zoom, resolution, and focus behave the same as before. It only changes the presentation and access pattern inside Gemini.

## Conclusion

The Gemini Android camera bottom sheet removes a small but frequent friction point. You can capture a photo and continue the conversation without switching contexts. Update the Google app, try the plus-menu camera, and drag the sheet to the size that works best for your shot.

Most users will notice the difference immediately in daily tasks such as identifying objects or reading text on the go. The change aligns the Android experience more closely with the earlier iOS version and makes visual prompts feel native to the chat.
