Google rolled out a redesigned in-app camera for Gemini on Android. The change moves the viewfinder from a full-screen takeover to a draggable bottom sheet. It appears when you open the camera from the plus menu in the prompt box on the homepage or inside a chat.

The update reached beta and stable builds starting with version 17.76. The same interface first appeared on the Gemini iOS app. Google has not published a formal announcement, but the redesign is now widely available. Launching the camera from the system overlay still uses the older full-screen viewfinder.

This guide shows what changed, how to open the new interface, and practical ways to add photos to your prompts faster.

## What the New Camera Interface Looks Like

The bottom sheet opens inline. You can drag the handle upward. The sheet expands with a smooth animation until it fills most of the screen. The viewfinder extends nearly edge to edge and sits behind the system navigation bar.

Controls sit at the bottom of the sheet. A back button appears on the left of the shutter. You can also swipe down to dismiss the sheet. On the right sits a three-dot overflow button. Tapping it animates open a menu that contains the flash toggle and the switch to the front-facing camera.

The layout keeps the rest of the Gemini conversation visible underneath when the sheet is only partially expanded. You stay in context instead of leaving the chat entirely.

## How to Open the Redesigned Camera

Follow these steps on an Android device running Gemini version 17.76 or newer:

1. Open the Gemini app.
2. Tap the plus button inside the prompt box at the bottom of the homepage or an open conversation.
3. Select the camera option.
4. The bottom sheet appears. Drag the handle upward if you want the full viewfinder.
5. Frame your shot, tap the shutter, and the photo attaches to your prompt.

If you still see the old full-screen viewfinder, force-stop the Google app. Open Settings, find the Google app, tap Force stop, then reopen Gemini and try the plus menu again. The rollout is gradual, so some accounts may need a short wait.

The overlay path remains unchanged. Long-press the power button or use the system Gemini overlay and the older interface appears.

## Practical Tips for Photo Prompts

Use the new sheet when you want to keep the chat history in view. Partial expansion lets you check previous messages while framing a follow-up photo. Full expansion gives the largest viewfinder for detailed shots of documents, products, or scenes.

The three-dot menu places flash and camera-switch controls one tap away. Toggle flash before you raise the sheet fully if lighting is low. Switch to the front camera for self-referential prompts without leaving the interface.

Swipe down to close is faster than hunting for the back button when you change your mind. The animation feels consistent with other Android bottom sheets.

Combine the camera with recent Gemini features. After you attach a photo, you can ask Gemini to create a short GIF from it using the Omni 1.2 Flash model. See our guide on [how to use Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash](/blog/how-to-use-gemini-omni-1-2-flash/) for video and animation workflows.

## Troubleshooting the Rollout

Confirm your Gemini version is 17.76 or higher. Open the Google Play Store, search for Gemini, and check for updates. Force-stopping the Google app clears cached UI state and often surfaces the new sheet immediately.

If the bottom sheet still does not appear, the server-side rollout may not have reached your account yet. Continue using the older interface; the camera still attaches photos to prompts. The change is visual only.

Restart the device if force-stop does not help. Clear the Google app cache only as a last step, since it can reset other preferences.

## Conclusion

The bottom-sheet camera redesign on Gemini for Android reduces friction when you add photos to prompts. You stay inside the conversation, gain a larger edge-to-edge viewfinder, and reach flash and camera controls with one animated menu. Version 17.76 and later include the update on both beta and stable channels.

Try the plus menu on your next prompt. Drag the sheet, snap a photo, and continue the chat without leaving context. For GIF and video generation after you capture images, explore the Omni 1.2 Flash workflows linked above.
