Google and Adobe announced an expanded partnership at Adobe MAX on November 10, 2026. The deal brings Acrobat directly into Google Workspace, expands Adobe connectors in Gemini, and places creative tools in AI Mode in Search. Google’s models move the other way into Adobe apps.

This guide shows what is announced, what is already usable, and the exact steps you will follow once the “Edit with” entry points roll out. Availability is still forthcoming—Google said “soon”—so treat the Drive and Gmail steps as the planned workflow, not a live toggle today.

## What the partnership actually delivers

Three practical changes matter for everyday work:

- **Acrobat in Drive and Gmail**: Acrobat appears as a suggested app. File previews gain an “Edit with” option that opens the PDF in Acrobat.
- **Gemini connectors**: Acrobat and additional creative tools become available inside Gemini and Gemini Enterprise chats. You can start PDF work from the conversation.
- **AI Mode in Search**: Adobe creative tools surface inside Google Search’s AI Mode for quick edits and asset creation.

On the Adobe side, Gemini models power more AI features inside Acrobat (conversational edits, summaries, polished outputs). Google’s latest imaging and video models reach Firefly, Photoshop, Premiere, and Express. Adobe and Google DeepMind are exploring a Photoshop image model that could support near-real-time prompt-based edits.

## Step-by-step: Edit a PDF from Google Drive

Once the integration lands, the flow is short. Until then, the steps below match the announced “direct pathway.”

1. Open Google Drive in a browser or the Drive app.
2. Locate the PDF and open its preview (double-click or tap).
3. Look for the new **Edit with** entry point or an Acrobat suggestion in the preview toolbar.
4. Choose Acrobat. The file opens in Adobe Acrobat (web or desktop, depending on your account).
5. Make your edits—annotate, fill forms, compress, or use AI tools powered by Gemini models.
6. Save. The updated file returns to the same Drive location or a new version you choose.

If you do not yet see “Edit with,” the rollout has not reached your account. Check Google Workspace status or Adobe’s release notes rather than forcing a third-party connector.

The same pattern applies in Gmail: open a message that contains a PDF attachment, preview it, and select the Edit with Acrobat option. This avoids the download-open-reupload loop that breaks focus.

## Use Acrobat and creative tools inside Gemini

Adobe is expanding its existing Gemini connectors. After the update you can invoke Acrobat or creative tools from a Gemini chat without switching apps.

1. Open Gemini (gemini.google.com or the Gemini app).
2. Start a new chat or continue an existing one.
3. Attach or reference a PDF from Drive, or simply describe the document.
4. Ask Gemini to edit, summarize, or convert it. Examples that match the announced capabilities: “Open this PDF in Acrobat and highlight the payment terms,” or “Turn the attached notes into a polished one-page report.”
5. Follow any permission prompt to connect Adobe. Connections remain opt-in.
6. Review the result in the chat, then open the full Acrobat view if you need precise control.

Gemini Enterprise users gain the Adobe CX Enterprise Coworker agent. It helps teams design graphics, handle PDFs, and prepare marketing campaigns inside the same Gemini Enterprise workspace.

## AI Mode in Google Search

Adobe creative tools will appear inside AI Mode. When you ask Search for an image edit, a simple graphic, or a PDF-derived asset, the interface can hand the job to Adobe tools without leaving the results page.

1. Open Google Search and switch to AI Mode (the toggle or mode selector Google provides).
2. Describe the task: “Edit this product photo to add a soft shadow” or “Create a one-slide summary from this PDF link.”
3. When the response offers an Adobe tool or connector, accept it.
4. Complete the edit in the embedded or linked Adobe surface, then download or save back to Drive.

Exact UI wording will match Google’s final implementation. The announcement confirms the destination is AI Mode, not a separate Adobe site.

## What to expect from the DeepMind Photoshop work

Adobe and Google DeepMind are collaborating on next-generation image editing, starting with Photoshop. The stated goal is an image model that could enable near-real-time edits: you type a prompt, receive variants, and pick the closest match.

This is exploration, not a shipped feature. Do not expect it in the current Photoshop release. When it arrives it will sit alongside existing generative tools rather than replace them. Google’s other models (already usable in Firefly and related apps) continue to be available in the meantime.

## Tips for a smooth rollout

- Confirm you have an active Adobe account that supports the features you need (free Acrobat tools versus paid creative apps).
- Keep Drive and Gmail permissions current. The “Edit with” path requires the standard Google-to-Adobe handoff.
- Test on a non-critical PDF first. Conversational AI edits are useful for drafts; precise legal or design work still needs manual review.
- Watch for the “suggested app” label in Drive and Gmail. That is the clearest signal the integration is live for you.
- For enterprise teams, the CX Enterprise Coworker agent inside Gemini Enterprise is the multi-user path.

Short paragraphs and clear next actions keep the workflow moving. The partnership’s purpose is to remove the app-switch tax that currently sits between receiving a PDF and finishing it.

## Conclusion

The November 10 announcement at Adobe MAX connects two platforms millions of people already use every day. You will soon open a PDF in Drive or Gmail and land directly in Acrobat, start the same work from a Gemini chat, and reach creative tools from Search’s AI Mode. Google models strengthen Acrobat and other Adobe apps in return.

Until the entry points appear, continue with the current Drive preview and Acrobat web tools. When “Edit with” shows up, the five-step Drive flow above is the complete path. Check the official Google and Adobe posts for the exact rollout date in your region.
