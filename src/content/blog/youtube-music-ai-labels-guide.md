YouTube Music now shows clear labels when a track was created primarily with generative AI. The rollout started on October 14, 2026. Labels appear on the Now Playing screen so you can check the role of AI before or while you listen.

These labels bring the same transparency already used for AI-altered videos on the main YouTube app. They focus on music created with generative models for composition, vocals, or both. Standard tools like pitch correction stay outside the label.

![Person wearing headphones while looking at a phone](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1511671782779-c97d3d27a1d4?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What the Labels Show

When a song qualifies, an AI badge appears just below the title on the player page. Tap it to open a simple menu with more details. The menu explains how the track was made and links to further information.

YouTube identifies qualifying tracks through two sources. Music partners declare the level of generative AI use when they deliver content. YouTube also runs its own detection tools and can apply a label even without a partner declaration.

The company applies the same principles it uses for video content. The goal is to give listeners the context they asked for about AI-generated tracks.

## Fully Gen AI, Partly Gen AI, and No Gen AI

Music partners choose one of three designations when they submit metadata via DDEX or CSV templates:

- **Fully Gen AI**: All content was created using generative AI. Examples include a user typing a text prompt into an AI music generator and downloading the result, or a model generating the full composition, instrumental performance, and vocals.
- **Partly Gen AI**: Part of the content was created using generative AI. An artist might generate a bassline or string section with AI, then record live vocals and instruments over it. Brainstorming lyrics, co-writing verses, or iterating on melodies with AI tools before studio recording also fits here.
- **No Gen AI**: No content was created using generative AI. Pitch-correction software such as Auto-Tune and automated AI mastering tools fall into this category.

If a partner provides no value, YouTube treats it as unknown unless its own signals allow a designation. YouTube may also use additional signals to augment any partner declaration.

These categories come from YouTube’s Help Center guidance for music partners. Partners interpret the definitions in relation to their own content.

## How to View the Labels

1. Open the YouTube Music app on your phone or tablet.
2. Play any track.
3. Look on the Now Playing screen just below the song title and next to the artist or channel name.
4. If the track qualifies, you will see an AI badge with an info icon.
5. Tap the badge. A bottom sheet opens with the “How this was made” explanation.
6. Read the details. You may see language such as “Made with AI: This audio was altered or fully generated using AI.”
7. Tap the learn-more link if you want the full disclosure standards.

The label is rolling out gradually. Not every qualifying track will show it immediately. The same labels are planned for the main YouTube app later.

## What the Labels Do Not Do

The labels provide information only. YouTube has not added a filter or block for AI-generated music based on these labels. Listeners cannot hide AI tracks from recommendations or search results through this feature.

AI involvement alone does not make a track ineligible for monetization. Disclosed tracks can still earn if they meet YouTube’s other rules. This approach differs from some other streaming services that exclude or limit royalties on detected AI music.

YouTube continues to experiment with its own AI music tools, including features that generate soundtracks with participating artists’ voices.

## Tips for Listeners

Check the player page on new or unfamiliar tracks. The badge is the quickest way to see whether generative AI played a significant role.

Pay attention to the distinction between generative creation and standard production tools. Auto-Tune and AI mastering do not trigger the label.

If you create music yourself and want to understand disclosure rules, review the music partner guidance in the YouTube Help Center. Direct uploads through YouTube Studio use a different AI-use survey for video content.

## Conclusion

YouTube Music’s AI labels give listeners a clear, tappable signal on the player when generative AI created a track’s composition, vocals, or both. The system combines partner declarations with YouTube’s detection tools and follows the same transparency approach already used for videos.

Open any track and look for the badge. Tap it for the short explanation. The labels expand to the main YouTube app in a later phase, so the same information will appear across more music content.
