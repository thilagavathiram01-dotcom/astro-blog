Google Health posted a short teaser on October 9, 2026. The message reads “Get an Edge on your health. 10.12.26.” It shows a close-up of a metal chassis in a Canyon color that matches recent Pixel Watch finishes. That single post confirms two facts: the product is called Fitbit Edge, and the reveal date is October 12, 2026.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="100" />

The post does not list full specifications, pricing, or availability markets. Everything beyond the name, date, and partial look remains unconfirmed until Google publishes official details. This guide covers only what Google has stated and how the Edge sits next to products already on the market.

## What Google Has Officially Confirmed

The Google Health account shared the teaser on X and Instagram. The caption capitalizes “Edge” and pairs it with the date 10.12.26. The image shows part of the side of the device, confirming a metal body rather than a fully plastic band-style tracker.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="108" />

No official product page, store listing, or specification sheet existed at the time of writing. Google has not published battery life numbers, sensor lists, or supported operating systems for the Edge. Those details will arrive on or after October 12.

## How Fitbit Edge Fits Google’s Current Health Lineup

Earlier in 2026 Google rebranded the Fitbit app as the Google Health app. Existing Fitbit users receive the update automatically. The app combines data from Fitbit devices, Pixel Watch, Health Connect, and Apple Health in one place. Google Health Premium (formerly Fitbit Premium) includes the Gemini-powered Google Health Coach.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="112" />

Google also released the Fitbit Air, a thin, screenless tracker designed for 24/7 wear. The Edge is positioned in reporting as the next step above the Air and below the Pixel Watch 5. Official materials have not stated this hierarchy yet. Users who already own a Pixel Watch or Fitbit Air can continue using the same Google Health account and app after the Edge launches.

Google has stated that Fitbit user health and wellness data is not used for advertising. That commitment carries forward under the Google Health name.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="109" />

## Steps to Prepare Before the October 12 Reveal

1. Update or install the Google Health app. Open the Play Store or App Store, search for Google Health, and install or update. Existing Fitbit users should see the new icon after the automatic update.

2. Sign in with the Google account you want to use for health data. If you still have an older Fitbit account that is not linked to Google, complete the account migration in the app before adding a new device.

3. Review current devices. If you own a Pixel Watch, open the Pixel Watch app and confirm it syncs to Google Health. If you own a Fitbit Air, confirm it appears under Devices in the Google Health app.

4. Check storage and battery. Make sure your phone has enough free space for the Google Health app update and that your current tracker is charged if you plan to keep using it alongside any new device.

5. Note the date. Set a reminder for October 12, 2026. Check the Google Health app, the Google Store, and the @googlehealth account on the day of the reveal for official specifications and purchase links.

## Tips for Using Google Health With New Trackers

Keep the Google Health app updated. New devices often require the latest app version to pair and display all metrics.

Use Health Connect on Android if you also track data in other apps. The Google Health app can read compatible sources so your Edge or existing tracker data stays in one dashboard.

If you want the Google Health Coach, confirm whether your Google AI plan or Google Health Premium subscription includes it. The Coach uses Gemini for personalized suggestions and is available to Premium subscribers in supported countries.

For Pixel Watch owners, features such as Raise to Talk for Gemini continue to work independently of a fitness tracker. See our guide on [Pixel Watch Raise to Talk](/blog/pixel-watch-raise-to-talk-gemini/) for setup steps that already work today.

## What to Watch for on Launch Day

Look for an official blog post on blog.google or an update in the Google Health app. Google typically lists supported phones (Android and iOS versions), battery claims, sensor capabilities, and regional availability in the same announcement.

Pricing and color options will also appear on the Google Store once sales open. Availability can vary by country; some recent Fitbit devices launched in limited markets first.

Until those details arrive, avoid third-party listings that claim full specifications. Stick to the Google Health account, the Google Store, and official support pages.
