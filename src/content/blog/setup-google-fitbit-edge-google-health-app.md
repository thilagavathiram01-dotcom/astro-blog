Google launched the Fitbit Edge on 12 October 2026. The tracker sits between the screenless Fitbit Air and the full Pixel Watch 5. It brings a 1.34-inch Hybrid OLED display, built-in GPS, an altimeter, and up to seven days of battery life for $179.99.

If you pre-ordered or will buy one when it reaches shelves on 27 October, you need the Google Health app to pair it. The Edge works with Android 11 or higher and iOS 16.4 or higher. This guide covers the official setup steps from Google support and the launch announcement.

## What you get with the Fitbit Edge

The box includes the tracker, a small Active Band, a large Active Band, and a magnetic charging cable. The case is recycled aluminum, 11.5 mm thin, and weighs about 17.7 g. It has a physical side button for quick access and is rated IP68 and 5 ATM.

Key tracking features include more than 50 exercise modes with real-time guidance, automatic recognition of up to 100 activities, continuous heart rate, SpO2, skin temperature, and an on-wrist ECG app in supported countries. Built-in GPS is claimed to be up to 40% more accurate than the Charge 6. An altimeter tracks floors and elevation.

Battery life reaches up to seven days with Always-On Display off. Turning Always-On on drops it to about two days. A five-minute charge adds a full day of use; a full charge takes about 90 minutes. The battery is user-replaceable.

You also receive three months of Google Health Premium with purchase. Premium unlocks Google Health Coach (powered by Gemini) and Health Guardian features such as blood pressure trends and insulin resistance trends. These are wellness tools, not medical devices.

## Prepare before pairing

Update your phone and install the latest Google Health app from the Play Store or App Store. Sign in with the Google account you want to use for health data. If you still have an old Fitbit account, complete any migration steps in the app first.

Charge the Edge fully before setup. Connect the magnetic cable and power it on. Google recommends having the device on the charger during the initial pairing for the most reliable connection.

The Edge can share an account with a Fitbit Air or Pixel Watch. Data syncs through the same Google Health app. For background context on the pre-launch announcement, see our earlier post on the [Fitbit Edge reveal](/blog/fitbit-edge-launch-october-12-what-to-know/).

## Step-by-step pairing with the Google Health app

Follow these steps exactly as listed in the official Google Health Help Center article for the Edge.

1. Turn on the tracker. If possible, leave it on the charger.

2. Open the Google Health app on your phone. Tap Sign in with Google if you are not already signed in. Create a Google account if needed.

3. Go to Connections. Tap Add a device or Add more devices.

4. Select the Fitbit Edge when it appears in the list. Keep the phone close to the tracker. Accept any Bluetooth permission prompts.

5. Follow the on-screen pairing instructions. You may see a code on the tracker that you enter on the phone, or the devices may pair automatically.

6. Once paired, the Edge begins syncing activity, sleep, and health metrics to the app. Sync happens automatically when the devices are nearby and Bluetooth is on.

7. Optional: Redeem the three-month Google Health Premium trial inside the app if it is not applied automatically. Premium is required for Coach and the advanced Health Guardian trends.

After pairing, open the device settings in the Google Health app to adjust tiles, watch faces, notifications, and GPS preferences. On the tracker itself, swipe down for Quick Settings (brightness, Do Not Disturb, Water Lock) and use the side button to wake the screen or open the app list.

## Using workouts and health features

Start an exercise from the wrist. The Edge supports more than 50 modes with on-screen metrics such as pace, heart-rate zones, and splits. Real-time guidance appears during the session. After the workout, a summary shows on the tracker and in the app.

Automatic activity tracking recognizes walks, runs, rides, and many other activities even if you forget to start a session. Google says the new model is more accurate and covers up to 100 activity types. The feature also rolls out to the Fitbit Air and recent Pixel Watches.

For outdoor workouts, enable built-in GPS so you can leave the phone at home. The altimeter adds elevation data. Media controls work with any connected Bluetooth device. Notifications from your phone appear on the tracker; quick replies are available on Android.

Google Maps turn-by-turn directions are listed as arriving later in 2026. Until then, you can still receive basic location context through the app.

## Tips for battery, bands, and data

Wear the tracker snug on the top of your wrist, above the wrist bone, for the best heart-rate and SpO2 readings. The new band attachment requires a firm push until you hear a click. Old Charge bands do not fit.

To maximize battery, leave Always-On Display off and use Battery Saver mode when available. Check the official battery page linked from Google support for the latest estimates under different usage profiles.

Review data in the Google Health app rather than relying only on the small screen. The app consolidates metrics from the Edge, any other connected devices, and Health Connect sources on Android.

If the device will not pair or sync, restart both the phone and the tracker (long-press the side button for about 10 seconds). Confirm Bluetooth is enabled and that the Google Health app has all required permissions. Google’s help articles list further troubleshooting for charging, sync, and notifications.

## Conclusion

The Google Fitbit Edge gives you a larger display, built-in GPS, and richer on-wrist metrics than the Air while keeping multi-day battery life. Pairing is straightforward once the Google Health app is installed and you are signed in with a Google account. Follow the official steps, redeem the included Premium trial if you want Coach and Health Guardian, and check the app for the fullest view of your data. The tracker ships on 27 October 2026; pre-orders are open now.
