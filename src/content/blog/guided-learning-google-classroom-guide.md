Teachers spend hours answering the same questions while students still struggle to build real understanding. Google's Guided Learning changes that by coaching students step by step instead of handing them answers.

On 3 November 2026 Google started rolling out teacher-led Guided Learning globally inside Google Classroom. The feature grounds conversations in the materials you upload and gives you insights into where students get stuck.

This guide shows exactly how to set it up, assign it, and use the results in class.

## What Guided Learning Actually Does

Guided Learning lives inside Gemini and acts as a personal tutor. It asks open-ended questions, breaks problems into smaller steps, and adapts explanations based on the student's replies. It prioritizes deep understanding over quick answers.

Google tested it in a randomized controlled trial with more than 300 University of Tokyo students. Those who used Guided Learning improved their statistics scores by 8.1 percentage points in half a day, compared with 5.3 points for students using only standard web tools. That is a 1.5 times higher learning gain.

A separate four-week pilot with Ark across three UK secondary schools showed teachers gained more time for one-on-one help because the tool handled routine questions. Most students said they felt comfortable asking for help when stuck.

## How Teachers Assign Guided Learning

You need a Google Workspace for Education account and admin approval for Gemini features. Once enabled, Guided Learning appears as an assignable activity inside Classroom.

1. Open the class in classroom.google.com and go to Classwork.
2. Click Create, then select the Guided Learning option (or look under Gemini tools if the label differs slightly during rollout).
3. Upload or link the specific curriculum materials, slides, or readings you want the conversation grounded in. The tool stays focused on those sources.
4. Write a clear prompt or learning goal for the session, such as “Walk students through the causes of the French Revolution using the attached chapter. Ask guiding questions and do not give the final answer.”
5. Set any time limits or due dates, then assign to the whole class or selected students.
6. After students begin, open the insights panel to see engagement, common misconceptions, and progress summaries.

Teachers remain in control. You can review transcripts, adjust materials, and decide when students use the tool. Feedback from the UK pilot led Google to improve anchoring to uploaded curriculum and add clearer educator insights.

## How Students Experience the Session

When a student opens the assigned Guided Learning activity, Gemini starts a conversation locked to the materials you provided. It will not jump straight to the answer. Instead it poses questions, offers hints, and checks understanding before moving forward.

Students can ask follow-ups, request practice problems, or request diagrams. The experience works on web and mobile Classroom apps once the rollout reaches their account. Data stays private within the education environment and is not used to train general models.

If your school also uses Gemini Notebook for broader study tools, students can combine both. See our guide on [Gemini Notebook study tools](/blog/gemini-notebook-study-tools-how-to-guide/) for quizzes and flashcards that pair well with Guided Learning sessions.

## Practical Tips for Better Results

Keep the assigned materials focused. One chapter or one concept set works better than an entire unit. Students stay on task and the insights stay useful.

Write prompts that name the desired outcome. “Help students explain photosynthesis step by step and identify one common misconception” produces clearer coaching than a vague request.

Review the insights after the first few sessions. Look for questions that multiple students struggle with and address those in the next class.

Combine Guided Learning with other Classroom Gemini tools such as rubric conversion or quiz generation. Use the tutor mode for independent practice and the other tools for materials creation.

Remind students that the goal is understanding, not speed. The tool is designed to encourage productive struggle.

## Conclusion

Guided Learning in Google Classroom gives teachers a way to scale step-by-step coaching while keeping control of the materials and the data. Research from Tokyo and UK classrooms shows measurable gains when students work through problems instead of receiving finished answers.

Open your next Classwork page, create one focused Guided Learning assignment, and review the insights the same day. Small, repeated sessions compound faster than occasional long tutoring.

Start with one subject and one set of materials this week.
