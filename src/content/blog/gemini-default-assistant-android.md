Gemini only behaves like the phone assistant when two settings agree: the **Google app** is the default digital assistant, and **Gemini** is the Google assistant you selected. Miss either one and the power button, corner swipe, or “Hey Google” still opens something else.

This guide follows Google’s Gemini Apps Help pages. Menu names vary by Pixel, Samsung, and other skins, so use Settings search when a path does not match.

## What you need first

Install or update the **Gemini** app from Google Play. Sign in with the same Google account you use for the Google app.

Google’s get-started page states that Gemini as the mobile assistant requires the **Google app** to be the device default assist app. “Google” in Default apps covers both classic Assistant and Gemini. You still pick Gemini inside Google’s own assistant settings.

On some phones Gemini is already the primary assistant. Open the Gemini app once and accept the switch screen if it appears.

## Set the Google app as the digital assistant

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Apps**, then **Default apps**.
3. Tap **Digital assistant app**, then **Default digital assistant app** (wording can differ).
4. Choose the **Google** app.

If you cannot find the screen, search Settings for `digital assistant` or `default apps`. That search is the method Google’s community managers recommend because manufacturers rename the menu.

Leaving this on **None** or on another vendor assistant blocks Gemini from the power button and from system gestures, even if the Gemini app works when you open it by icon.

## Switch Google’s assistant from Assistant to Gemini

Google documents two official paths. Use the one your build still shows.

### From the Google app

1. Open the **Google** app.
2. Tap your profile picture, then **Settings**, then **Google Assistant**.
3. Tap **Digital assistants from Google**.
4. Select **Gemini** and follow the on-screen prompts.

### From device Settings

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Apps**, then **Assistant** (or search `Digital assistants from Google`).
3. Tap **Digital assistants from Google**, then **Gemini**.
4. Finish the confirmation screens.

Some builds also offer the switch inside the Gemini app: profile picture, then **Switch** when Google Assistant is still selected.

Google has been moving eligible phones off classic Assistant. If your device already completed that upgrade, you may only see Gemini on this screen. You can still remove Gemini as the *system* default later without deleting the app.

## Launch Gemini with the buttons you already use

After both defaults are set, Google lists these activation methods:

- Say **“Hey Google”** if Voice Match is on.
- Press and hold the **power** or side button when that gesture is mapped to the digital assistant.
- Touch and hold **Home** on three-button navigation.
- Swipe up from a **bottom corner** on gesture navigation.
- Open the **Gemini** app icon.

On many Pixels and Samsung phones the long-press power action is labeled **Digital assistant**. That action opens whichever app sits in Default apps, not a hard-coded Gemini binary.

To ask about the current screen, long-press power while the page, image, or YouTube video is open, then tap **Ask about this screen** or **Ask about this video** when the chip appears.

For browsing help that stays inside Chrome instead of the overlay, use [How to Use Gemini in Chrome on Android](/blog/gemini-in-chrome-android/).

## Map or unmap the power button

Google’s manage-or-delete article treats the power button as a separate control from the default-app picker.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Search for `power button`, `side button`, or `gestures`.
3. Open **Press & hold power button** (Pixel path: **System** → **Gestures** → **Press & hold power button**).
4. Choose **Digital assistant** if you want the long-press to open Gemini.
5. Choose **Power menu** if you want shutdown and restart instead.

Samsung and Xiaomi skins nest the same idea under side-key or gesture-shortcut pages. The official advice is the same: search Settings rather than hunting a Pixel-only path.

## Turn “Hey Google” on or off

Voice launch is independent of the default-app setting. You can keep Gemini as the assistant and still silence the hotword.

Google’s help page for managing Gemini on Android points you to **Talk to Gemini hands-free** and the **Hey Google** toggle. A common 2026 path is:

1. Open **Settings** → **Google** → **All services**.
2. Open **Search, Assistant & Voice**, then **Gemini**.
3. Tap **Talk to Gemini hands-free**.
4. Turn **Hey Google** on or off and complete Voice Match if you enable it.

You can also start from the Gemini app: profile → settings related to voice. If the hotword does nothing after you enable it, Google says to confirm Voice Match is trained and that microphone permission is granted to the Google and Gemini apps.

Turn the hotword off in shared rooms, on a work desk, or anytime a TV ad can wake the phone.

## Keep the app but stop surprise pop-ups

You do not have to uninstall Gemini to quiet it. Google lists these controls on one help page:

- Change or clear the **default digital assistant app**.
- Remap the **power / side button** away from Digital assistant.
- Turn off **Hey Google**.
- Turn off **Gemini on lock screen** if you do not want the overlay while the device is locked.

To point the system assistant at nothing:

1. Settings → **Apps** → **Default apps** → **Digital assistant app**.
2. Tap **Default digital assistant app**.
3. Select **None** or another installed assistant.

Google notes that the list still shows **Google** when either Assistant or Gemini is the Google-side choice. Clearing the system default is what stops the gesture. Deleting the Gemini app alone does not reset Default apps.

## Privacy checks before you leave setup

Gemini Intelligence features that automate apps are opt-in. Google’s May 2026 security post states that connecting Gemini to Autofill is opt-in, that app automation is limited to apps you allow, and that purchases should require confirmation.

Do this on a personal phone, not a shared family profile:

- Review connected apps inside Gemini settings.
- Leave Personal Intelligence off until you read the data list.
- Confirm lock-screen access matches how you use the device in public.

Agent-style flows across apps are covered in [How to Use Gemini Intelligence on Android](/blog/gemini-intelligence-android/). Set the default assistant first; those features assume Gemini can already take the system assist role.

## Troubleshooting

**Power button still shows the power menu.** The gesture is not mapped to Digital assistant, or a manufacturer side-key setting overrides it. Search Settings for `side button` or `power`.

**Hey Google opens nothing.** Voice Match is off, the microphone permission is denied, or the Google app is not the default digital assistant.

**Gemini app works, gestures do not.** Default apps still point at None or a third-party assistant. Fix that screen first.

**You want classic Assistant back.** Where Google still offers the choice, open Gemini → profile → **Switch to Google Assistant**. On phones that completed the Assistant-to-Gemini upgrade, that toggle may be gone. Use **None** plus a remapped power button if you only want to stop the overlay.

## Conclusion

Treat Gemini setup as three switches, not one install: default digital assistant = Google, Digital assistants from Google = Gemini, then power button and Hey Google set to the behavior you actually want.

Change those later without deleting the app. Google documented the Default apps picker, the power-button gesture, the hotword toggle, and lock-screen control for exactly that reason.
