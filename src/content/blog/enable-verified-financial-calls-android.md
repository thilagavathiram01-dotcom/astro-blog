Scammers routinely spoof official bank phone numbers to trick people into transferring money or sharing sensitive details. Android's Verified Financial Calls feature works with participating banks to verify calls in real time and end fake ones before they reach you.

This protection runs in the background on Android 11 and later devices when a supported bank app is installed and you have signed in at least once. It does not require any manual toggle in most cases.

![Person holding a smartphone showing an incoming call screen](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1512941937669-90a1b58e7e9c?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What Verified Financial Calls Does

Verified Financial Calls targets caller ID spoofing. Scammers use software to display a real bank number on your screen. The feature counters this with two methods described on Google's Android Help page.

Real-time call verification asks the bank's official app whether the institution is actually placing the call. If the app confirms the call is genuine, it rings normally. If the app cannot confirm it, Android ends the call automatically.

Inbound-only number protection covers numbers that banks never use for outgoing calls. These are typically customer service lines. If a call appears to come from one of these verified inbound-only numbers, the bank identifies it as spoofed and the system ends it immediately.

After a blocked call, you receive a Security & privacy notification confirming that a potentially fraudulent call was stopped.

## Requirements and Supported Banks

You need an Android phone running version 11 or higher. The official app of a participating financial institution must be installed, and you must have signed in to it previously.

Feature availability varies by bank and region. Initial partners announced around the Android Show in 2026 include Revolut, Itaú, and Nubank. Later reports noted Banco do Brasil among participating institutions, with more banks expected to join over time. Not every call from a supported bank is covered, and the option only appears in Settings if at least one compatible financial app is present on the device.

Google has not published a full public list of every participating bank. The reliable way to check is through the Settings menu on your phone.

## How to Check Bank Compatibility

Follow these steps to see whether Verified Financial Calls is available for your accounts:

1. Open the Settings app on your Android phone.
2. Tap Security & privacy.
3. Tap More security and privacy.
4. Look for Verified financial calls.

The page only appears if you have installed and signed into at least one supporting financial app. If the option is missing, confirm that your Android version is 11 or higher, that you are using the official bank app from the Play Store, and that you remain signed in. Availability can also depend on your country, carrier, and the specific device model.

No separate enable switch is required in most cases. The verification runs automatically once the conditions are met.

## What Happens During a Call

When an incoming call appears to come from a participating bank, Android queries the installed bank app in the background. The check happens quickly. If the app reports that no legitimate call is in progress, the system disconnects the call before you can answer.

You then see a Security & privacy notification. This confirms the block without requiring you to interact with the caller. Genuine calls from the bank proceed normally, so you experience no interruption when the verification succeeds.

The same protection applies to inbound-only numbers. Any attempt to spoof one of those numbers is ended immediately.

## Additional Tips for Call Safety

Even with Verified Financial Calls active, remain cautious. Hang up immediately if a caller pressures you to transfer money, share codes, or install software. Call your bank back using the official number listed on their website or on the back of your payment card.

Avoid sharing one-time passwords or account details over the phone. Banks do not ask for full passwords or remote access to your device during a legitimate call.

Combine this feature with other Android protections. For example, review settings for Enhanced Protection in Google Messages if available, as covered in our guide on [enabling enhanced scam protection in Google Messages](/blog/enable-enhanced-protection-google-messages-android/). Keep your bank apps and Android system updated through the Play Store and system settings.

## Limitations to Know

The feature does not protect against every type of scam. It only covers calls that claim to come from participating financial institutions and that the system can verify through the installed app. Scams via text, email, or other channels remain outside its scope.

Rollout is gradual. Some banks and regions receive support later than others. If your primary bank is not yet participating, the Settings entry will not appear. Continue to verify any unexpected financial call by hanging up and dialing the official number yourself.

The protection is inbound-focused and does not alter how you place outgoing calls to your bank.

## Conclusion

Verified Financial Calls adds a practical layer of defense against one of the most effective phone scams. On Android 11 and later, the system can check with your bank's app and end spoofed calls automatically when the conditions are met.

Check Settings under Security & privacy for the Verified financial calls entry if you use a participating bank app. Pair the feature with the habit of hanging up and calling back on official numbers. These steps reduce the chance that a spoofed bank call reaches you.
