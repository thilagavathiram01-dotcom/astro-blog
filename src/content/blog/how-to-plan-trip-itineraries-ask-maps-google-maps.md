Google Maps just made trip planning faster. Ask Maps, the Gemini-powered chat inside the app, now builds day-by-day itineraries with numbered pins, insider tips, and live details. You no longer need to juggle multiple tabs or apps for a holiday shopping day or a multi-day road trip.

This update rolled out on November 16, 2026, on Android and iOS. It expands on earlier Ask Maps capabilities and arrives just before the holidays. The feature works wherever Ask Maps is available and pairs well with other Google tools for group planning.

![Person holding a smartphone showing a navigation map on a road](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1604357209793-fca5dca89f97?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What Ask Maps Itineraries Include

Ask Maps creates a structured plan from a natural language request. You describe the trip. It returns a schedule split by day, tips for each stop, and numbered pins on the map so you see the full route at a glance.

Examples from Google include a day of holiday shopping or a three-day coastal road trip with hikes and oceanfront dining. The plan shows the route and pins for every location. You can share it with friends and family or export it to Google Docs on desktop.

When you open the itinerary later, it pulls real-time information. That covers live traffic, whether a place is open, and details about the vibe at your next stop. You can also add stops on the fly, such as a scenic viewpoint or lunch spot, then tap to start directions from the itinerary itself.

A related “Know before you go” layer appears when you pull up directions. It covers parking, EV charging, weather, potential delays, and tolls. These details help you leave better prepared without switching apps.

## How to Create an Itinerary with Ask Maps

Open the Google Maps app on your phone. Tap the Ask Maps button below the search bar. Type or speak your request in plain language.

Try a prompt like: “Plan a three-day road trip from San Francisco to San Diego with coastal hikes and oceanfront lunch spots. No more than four hours of driving per day.”

Ask Maps generates the day-by-day schedule. Review the numbered pins and tips. Adjust by asking follow-up questions, such as “Add a sunset dinner on day two” or “Find a pet-friendly hotel near the second stop.”

Once the plan looks right, share it directly or export to Google Docs on a computer. The itinerary badge appears in your chat history so you can reopen it anytime.

For everyday tasks, the same chat can search your saved lists. Ask which spots from your lists have availability for five people in the next 30 minutes. It checks live data and returns options.

## Using Your Itinerary on the Go

Before you leave, ask Ask Maps to pull up the itinerary or tap the itinerary badge in chat history. The view updates with current traffic and open status.

Tap any stop for directions. The app switches to navigation while keeping the overall plan accessible. If traffic changes or a place closes, ask for an alternative and the pins update.

You can also request route tips as part of directions. The Know before you go information appears for parking, charging, weather, and tolls so you avoid surprises.

## Additional Ask Maps Tools

Beyond itineraries, Ask Maps finds local appointments. In the US first, it can search online for beauty, pet care, auto, and home repair slots and, with permission, call businesses for you. A sample request is “Find me a 4 p.m. nail appointment near me.” It returns open times across nearby places.

List search works across your saved places. You can query for availability without opening each list manually. Ask Maps also expands to 30 more European countries, including Spain, Germany, and the UK, bringing the total above 200 countries and territories.

These features build on the original Ask Maps launch earlier in 2026, which already handled complex location questions using Maps data and Gemini. The new itinerary tools make multi-stop plans concrete and actionable.

If you use other Google planning tools, you can cross-reference ideas. For example, group trip notes from Tabletop can feed into a Maps request for routes and pins.

## Tips for Better Results

Be specific in your prompt. Include constraints like maximum driving time, preferred activities, dietary needs, or budget range. The more detail you give, the tighter the plan.

Update your saved lists before a trip. Ask Maps can pull from them for restaurants or attractions that match your taste.

Check the itinerary the morning you leave. Real-time data changes, and a quick refresh surfaces current traffic or hours.

Share early so companions can add their own requests. Export to Docs if you need offline notes or a printable version.

Watch for regional limits. Appointment calling starts in the US. Itineraries and list search work more widely.
