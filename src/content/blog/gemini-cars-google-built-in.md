Google is replacing Google Assistant in cars with Google built-in with Gemini. The change is a software update, not a new head unit. Eligible drivers who are signed into a Google Account see an upgrade prompt on the center display.

This guide covers how to accept that upgrade, how to talk to Gemini while driving, and which prompts Google has documented. Features vary by brand, model, language, and country. The first wave is English in the United States.

## What Gemini in the car actually is

Cars with Google built-in run Google software on the vehicle itself. That is different from Android Auto, which mirrors an app from your phone. Gemini here can read manufacturer owner’s manuals, talk to climate and defroster controls on supported cars, and pull live data from Google Maps.

Google says Gemini is rolling out to both new vehicles and existing ones through an over-the-air update. Cars with Google built-in are available in more than 100 models from 16 brands, according to Google’s May 2026 Android-in-cars update.

You stay in control of sending messages and completing actions that leave the vehicle. Treat voice replies the same way you treat a draft on your phone: listen to the read-back before you send.

## Check that your car can upgrade

1. Confirm the vehicle is a **car with Google built-in**, not only Android Auto. The Google apps sit on the car screen even when no phone is plugged in.
2. Sign in with the Google Account you use for Maps and YouTube Music on that car.
3. Connect the car to Wi-Fi or leave it parked with a data connection long enough for system updates.
4. Watch the center display for an **Accept upgrade** (or similar) Gemini prompt.
5. After the update, try “Hey Google,” the on-screen mic, or the steering-wheel voice button.

If no prompt appears, the car is not in the current wave. Google said the rollout starts in the U.S. in English and expands over the following months.

## How to start a conversation

Say **Hey Google**, tap the mic on the home screen, or press the steering-wheel control. Rigid one-shot commands still work. Gemini is built for follow-ups, so you can refine a request without restating the whole sentence.

Google’s documented examples:

- “I need to grab lunch, find some highly rated sit-down restaurants along the way. I’m not in a rush, oh, and I’d like to eat outside.”
- Follow up with “What’s the parking like?” or “Do they have vegetarian options?”
- “I’m driving by the stadium. Is there an event happening and should I avoid it because of traffic?”
- Report a hazard: “I see an accident in the right lane.”

Maps remains the source for places, traffic, and routing. Gemini is the layer that turns a messy request into a Maps query and lets you ask one more question.

## Messages, music, and Gemini Live

Ask Gemini to summarize new texts, then dictate a reply. Google’s sample flow:

- “Reply to Jane that I’m on my way and add my ETA.”
- Then edit without starting over: “Actually, also ask her if I should pick up dessert.”

Listen to the outgoing text before you confirm. If the contact name is ambiguous, stop and pick the person on screen.

For audio, you do not need a station number. Try “Hey Google, play a jazz radio station for me,” or a tighter YouTube Music request: “Play upbeat ’70s folk-rock for a mountain drive, but skip the slow ballads.”

**Gemini Live** is in beta in the car. Start it from the overlay button or say “Hey Google, let’s talk.” Google’s destination examples include history of Lake Tahoe and hike options such as the Rubicon Trail. Interrupt when you want a shorter answer. Long monologues are a distraction, even if the model is accurate.

For a phone-side look at Gemini’s newer live models, see our [Gemini 3.8 Live walkthrough](/blog/gemini-3-8-live/). The car surface uses the same product family, with vehicle safety limits on what it will do while you drive.

## Ask questions about *this* car

The useful difference versus Assistant is vehicle context. Google worked with automakers so Gemini can read manufacturer-provided owner’s manuals. Detail varies by brand and model.

Prompts Google published:

- “How should I prepare my car for an automatic car wash?”
- “My garage ceiling is low and the trunk is hitting it. How do I program the trunk so it doesn’t open all the way?”
- “What’s my current battery level?”
- “What’s my battery on arrival?”
- “Find a charger nearby.”
- “Are there any cafes near my destination while I charge?”
- “It’s foggy and freezing in here.” (On supported cars this can raise heat and turn on the defroster.)

If the manual is not wired for your trim, Gemini will give a generic answer. Cross-check warning lights against the printed manual or the automaker app before you ignore a dashboard icon.

## Safety and privacy habits that matter

Keep eyes on the road. Use short follow-ups. Park before you start a Gemini Live history lesson that needs a map glance.

Google has previewed camera-aware features in I/O demos (reading a sign or a nearby menu). Treat those as future or limited experiments unless your model’s release notes list them. Do not assume the car is streaming cabin or road video to the cloud because Gemini answered a climate question.

Practical rules:

- Sign in with a personal account you control; avoid a shared family login for message replies.
- Confirm every outbound text.
- Do not ask Gemini to change drive modes or safety systems unless the automaker documents that command.
- If the upgrade prompt never appears, wait. Forcing developer options on an infotainment unit is a good way to lose Maps offline tiles.

On the phone, Gemini Intelligence is a separate rollout with app automation and Chrome Auto Browse. That stack is covered in [How to Use Gemini Intelligence on Android](/blog/gemini-intelligence-android/). The car does not inherit those phone agents automatically.

## What comes next

Google says later updates will add more languages and countries, plus safer access to Gmail, Calendar, and Google Home from the car. Meeting apps such as Zoom are coming to cars with Google built-in. Video apps already planned for Android Auto (YouTube at 60 fps full HD in supported parked cars) are also headed to built-in units.

None of that replaces the first job: accept the Gemini upgrade when the car offers it, learn three prompts you will actually use (lunch stop, message reply, battery or climate), and leave everything else parked.

## Conclusion

Gemini in cars with Google built-in is an Assistant replacement with better follow-ups, Maps context, and owner’s-manual answers. Set it up by signing into the car, taking the on-screen upgrade, and using Hey Google or the wheel button. Start with navigation and media. Add manual questions and Gemini Live only after you trust the read-back.

Availability still depends on the automaker’s software train. If your display still says Assistant, you are waiting on that train, not on a hidden toggle.
