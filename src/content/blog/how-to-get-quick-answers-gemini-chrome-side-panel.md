Reading a long report or product page often means copying text into another tab just to get a summary. Google’s Gemini in Chrome puts the assistant in a side panel so you stay on the page. You click the Gemini icon, ask a question, and keep your place while the model reads the current tab.

This guide shows the exact steps from Google’s November 12, 2026 post and the official Gemini Apps Help page. It works on desktop Chrome for Mac, Windows, and Chromebook Plus when the feature has rolled out to your account.

## What you need before you start

Gemini in Chrome is still rolling out, so the button may not appear for every user yet. Official requirements are straightforward:

- You must be 13 or older (or the applicable age in your country) and located in a supported region.
- Use a Mac, Windows computer, or Chromebook Plus with the latest version of Chrome.
- Sign in to Chrome with a personal Google account. The feature does not work in Incognito mode.
- Set your device language to a supported language.

Work or school accounts need an administrator to enable access. Parents using Family Link can turn the feature off for supervised teens. Update Chrome first through the three-dot menu → Help → About Google Chrome.

If the Gemini icon is missing after an update, restart the browser and check that you are signed in. A VPN can also block the feature; disconnect it and try again.

## Open the side panel and ask your first question

1. Open Chrome and navigate to any webpage you want to understand.
2. Click the Gemini icon (or “Ask Gemini”) at the top of the browser toolbar. The first time you do this, follow the on-screen steps to opt in.
3. The side panel opens beside the page. Your current tab is shared with Gemini by default.
4. Type a clear request in the text box and press Enter.

Google’s own examples match the November 12 post: “summarize a dense report,” “compare product details,” or “explain complicated topics in plain language.” The panel stays open while you scroll the original page, so you never lose your place.

You can also switch models inside the text box if more than one is available, and click Live at the bottom of the panel to talk while you browse (Live is unavailable for users under 18).

## Practical prompts that work well

Start with short, specific instructions. These match the capabilities listed in the official help article:

- “Give me the key takeaways from this article in five bullets.”
- “Explain the pricing section in simpler language.”
- “List the main differences between the two products shown on this page.”
- “Create a short quiz from the concepts in this tutorial so I can test myself.”
- “Adapt this recipe for a vegan diet and list any substitutions.”

After you have watched or listened to media on the page, you can ask for a summary of the audio or video content you already played. The “Media understanding” setting is on by default; toggle it at chrome://settings/ai/gemini if you prefer to turn it off.

For product research the side panel is especially useful. Open a product page, ask for a feature list or review summary, then keep the original page visible while you decide. The same workflow works for technical docs, news articles, and research papers.

## Share extra tabs and control what Gemini sees

The current tab is shared automatically. You can add up to ten other open tabs:

1. In the Gemini text box click “Add tabs” (or type @ and select a tab).
2. Choose the tabs you want from any open window.
3. Shared tabs show a check mark. A glowing underline appears on a tab while Gemini is using its content.

To stop sharing a tab, click “Show shared tabs” above the text box and remove it. You can also turn off “Share current tab by default” under Chrome Settings → AI innovations → Gemini in Chrome so new chats do not automatically include the active page.

When you share a Google Workspace page (such as a Doc), Gemini may access the full document through your Workspace account rather than only the visible tab content. Connecting Workspace apps expands this further if you want calendar or mail context.

## Manage the panel and settings

Once the panel is open you can:

- Pop the chat out into a floating window or dock it back to the side panel.
- Start a new chat from the More options menu.
- Find recent chats in the same menu.
- Switch models by clicking the model name in the text box.

Settings live under Chrome Settings → AI innovations → Gemini in Chrome. There you control:

- Whether the Gemini icon appears at the top of the browser.
- Menu-bar or system-tray icons and keyboard shortcuts (Mac and Windows).
- Permissions for precise location, microphone, and “Let Gemini browse for you.”
- Media understanding toggle.

Users under 18 cannot enable the microphone or auto-browse features. Always review the response and click the good/bad response buttons if something looks off. You can also send detailed feedback through the More options menu.

For mobile users the experience differs. On Android, Gemini in Chrome appears inside the Chrome app and supports page summaries plus Auto Browse for eligible Pro and Ultra subscribers. See the earlier guide on [how to use Gemini in Chrome on Android](/blog/gemini-in-chrome-android/) for those steps.

## Tips for better results

Keep requests specific and mention the section you care about. “Summarize the warranty section” works better than “summarize this page.” When comparing products, open the relevant tabs first, share them, then ask for a side-by-side table of price, weight, and key features.

If the answer feels incomplete, ask a follow-up in the same chat. Gemini keeps the shared tabs in context. For long media, play the video or audio once before requesting a summary so the model can reference what you already watched.

Update Chrome regularly. New capabilities such as Skills (reusable prompts) and deeper Workspace connections appear over time and require the latest browser version.

## Conclusion

Gemini in Chrome’s side panel removes the extra tab and copy-paste step for everyday research. Click the toolbar icon, opt in once, and ask for a summary, comparison, or plain-language explanation while the original page stays open. The feature is still expanding by region and account type, so check the official requirements if the button is missing. Start with one page you already have open and test a simple takeaway request. The time you save compounds quickly on research-heavy days.
