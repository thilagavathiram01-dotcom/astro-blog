Guided Vision puts Gemini Live behind your camera and talks you through what is in front of the lens. Google built it with blind and low-vision communities. You can use it to hear a food label, a dim restaurant menu, or the color of a shirt without guessing.

It is part of the [September 2026 Android Drop](/blog/android-september-2026-drop-guide/). Google says the feature is coming to phones on Android 9 and later in countries where Gemini is available. Rollout is gradual. If the switch is missing, update Gemini and check again in a few days.

This guide covers official setup, shortcuts, useful prompts, and the limits Google publishes.

## What Guided Vision does

Guided Vision is not a separate app. It runs inside a Gemini Live session once you share the camera. Google’s help page lists four jobs:

- Read or translate text on labels, signs, menus, and appliance displays.
- Identify objects and describe where they sit, such as a spice jar in a cabinet.
- Describe color, shape, and pattern so you can match clothes or sort items.
- Describe a room or help you hunt for something that fell, like an earbud.

If the frame is off, Gemini gives spoken cues to reframe, pan, or center the object. That feedback loop is the difference between a one-shot photo caption and a live session.

## Before you start

Confirm these items first so you do not chase a missing toggle:

1. Install or update the Gemini app from Google Play.
2. Sign in with the Google account you use for Gemini.
3. Confirm Gemini is available in your country. Google lists supported regions in [Gemini app availability](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14579026).
4. Allow camera and microphone access when Gemini asks.
5. Use a phone on Android 9 or later. Pixel and most recent Samsung Galaxy models qualify.

Google notes the feature is rolling out slowly. A current Gemini build plus a supported region is the usual fix when Settings has no Guided Vision row.

## Turn on Guided Vision in the Gemini app

Google’s official path:

1. Open the Gemini app.
2. Tap your profile picture or initial, then open Settings.
3. Turn **Use Guided Vision in Live** on.
4. If a notification appears, you can tap **Start Live** to begin a session right away.

After the switch is on, start a Live chat and share the camera. Guided Vision stays available for later sessions until you turn it off.

To stop a session, tap **End Live session**.

## Start a session with the camera

A typical first run looks like this:

1. Open Gemini and tap **Live**.
2. Tap the camera control so Gemini can see the scene.
3. Point the rear camera at the object or text.
4. Ask a short, specific question. Examples: “Read the ingredients on this label.” “What color is this shirt?” “Where is the pepper in this cabinet?”
5. Follow the spoken framing cues if Gemini asks you to move closer or pan.

Keep the phone steady for a second after you frame the item. Live audio works better when the subject fills most of the viewfinder instead of sitting in a dark corner.

## Add an Accessibility shortcut

If you will use Guided Vision often, skip the app hunt. Google lets you bind it to an Accessibility shortcut.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Accessibility**.
3. Under **Vision assistance**, tap **Guided Vision**.
4. Turn **Guided vision shortcut** on.
5. Choose how the shortcut fires:
   - **Accessibility button**: tap the floating button.
   - **Accessibility gesture**: swipe up from the bottom with two fingers.
   - **Volume keys**: press and hold both volume keys.

Google documents the same shortcut model used by other Android accessibility tools. Details live in [Android accessibility shortcuts](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7650693).

## Start Guided Vision from TalkBack

TalkBack users can launch it from the TalkBack menu.

1. Update TalkBack from Google Play if an update is waiting.
2. With TalkBack on, tap the screen with three fingers to open the TalkBack menu.
3. Choose **Guided Vision**.

That path starts Live with the camera so you do not have to hunt through Gemini Settings while TalkBack is speaking the UI.

## Prompts that work well

Vague questions waste a session. Tight prompts match how Google describes the feature:

- “Read the nutrition facts on this box, starting with serving size.”
- “Translate this menu item into English.”
- “Is this shirt navy or black?”
- “Help me find the small white bottle on this shelf.”
- “Describe the layout of this desk from left to right.”

Ask one task at a time. If Gemini misreads a label, reframe and ask again instead of stacking follow-ups while the camera is still moving.

Good lighting still matters. Guided Vision can describe a dim menu, but a lamp or phone flashlight behind you often beats asking the model to guess faded print.

## What Guided Vision is not

Google publishes hard limits. Follow them.

Guided Vision can make mistakes. It is **not** a medical device, a mobility aid, or a substitute for a safe-travel guide. Do **not** use it for navigation or obstacle detection. You stay responsible for your health and safety.

Do not point the camera at other people’s private screens, IDs, or documents unless you have a reason and their consent. Live camera sharing sends what the lens sees to Gemini for that session.

Treat spoken color and text as a second opinion. Confirm medicine names, allergen lines, and payment amounts with a second source when the cost of an error is high.

## Troubleshooting

**No Guided Vision setting.** Update Gemini. Confirm country availability. Wait out the staged rollout Google flagged on the help page.

**Live starts without camera help.** Share the camera inside the Live session. Guided Vision needs that feed.

**Framing loops.** Pull back so more of the object is visible, then move in. Extreme close-ups of glossy packaging confuse reflections.

**TalkBack menu lacks the item.** Update TalkBack, then reopen the three-finger menu.

**Shortcut does nothing.** Recheck Settings → Accessibility → Guided Vision and confirm the shortcut type you picked (button, gesture, or volume keys).

## Tips for daily use

Store the Accessibility shortcut on the side you hold the phone. Volume-key hold is reliable when you cannot see the floating button.

Practice on a cereal box at home before you need a menu in a noisy restaurant. You will learn how close Gemini wants the camera.

End the Live session when you are done. That cuts microphone and camera use and keeps the next Gemini chat from inheriting a live camera stream you forgot about.

Pair Guided Vision with other September Drop tools only when they help the same task. Find Hub remembered items stores where you put a passport. Guided Vision describes the drawer in front of you. They solve different problems.

## Conclusion

Guided Vision is useful when your eyes need a second pass at small print or a cluttered shelf. Turn the Gemini setting on, bind a shortcut, and run one practice session on a labeled package. Keep Google’s safety note in mind: it describes scenes, it does not replace a cane, a guide, or your own judgment.

Once the shortcut feels natural, you can leave Gemini in your pocket until you actually need a label read.
