Unexpected texts that demand urgent action are common. One link can lead to stolen credentials or drained accounts. Google Lens gives you a way to check the message first on almost any phone.

Google added scam-checking to Lens in December 2025. You take a screenshot, open Lens, and circle the text. An AI Overview then flags red flags and suggests next steps when the system has high confidence. The same capability works on Android and iPhone through the Google app.

This guide covers the exact steps from official Google documentation. It is useful when Circle to Search is not available or when you want to review a message later.

## What Google Lens looks for in a message

Lens does not monitor your inbox automatically. You select the screenshot. The system compares the text against known scam patterns and public information from the web.

Common signals include unknown senders, pressure to act immediately, links that do not match official domains, and requests for money, codes, or personal details. When confidence is high, the Overview lists reasons and recommended actions such as blocking the number.

A clean result does not guarantee safety. Treat it as one input and verify high-value requests another way. If your phone supports Circle to Search, that tool offers a faster path without a screenshot. See the full steps in our [Circle to Search scam guide](/blog/circle-to-search-text-scams-android/).

## Prepare the Google app

Lens lives inside the Google app. Install or update it from the Play Store on Android or the App Store on iPhone before you start.

1. Open the Google app and sign in if prompted.
2. Tap the camera or Lens icon in the search bar.
3. Grant camera and photo permissions if the app asks.
4. Confirm the app is on the latest version so the scam-checking logic is current.

The feature appears when the system has high confidence in its assessment. It is available globally in the Google app.

## Check a suspicious message on Android

Do not open any link in the original message. Capture it first.

1. Open the message so the full text, sender, and any link are visible.
2. Take a screenshot using the power and volume-down buttons (or the system gesture on your phone).
3. Open the Google app and tap the Lens icon.
4. Select the screenshot from your recent photos or gallery.
5. Draw a circle around the message body or the suspicious link text.
6. Read the AI Overview that appears. Note any red flags and the suggested next steps.

You can also scribble across part of the text or tap specific words. Add a follow-up question in the search box if you need more detail, such as “is this delivery link real.”

## Check a suspicious message on iPhone

The process is nearly identical because Lens in the Google app works the same way.

1. Open the Messages app and display the full text.
2. Take a screenshot with the side button and volume-up button.
3. Open the Google app and tap the Lens icon.
4. Choose the new screenshot.
5. Circle the message text or the link.
6. Review the Overview for scam indicators and recommended actions.

On both platforms the Overview may recommend deleting the message, blocking the sender, or contacting the real company through its official app or website. Do not use any link from the text.

## What to do after the Overview appears

Act on a clear scam warning immediately.

- Delete the message and block the number or account.
- Do not reply, even to decline.
- If the text claims to be from a bank, carrier, or delivery service, open the official app yourself and check account activity.
- Report the message inside your messaging app or to your carrier.
- Share the pattern with family members so they know to look for the same wording.

If the Overview says the message looks legitimate, still confirm through a second channel. Call the person or company using a number you already have saved. Scammers sometimes copy official tone closely.

## Tips for clearer results

Circle the entire message rather than a single word. Extra context helps the assessment. Check the sender number and any URL separately if the first Overview is vague. Keep the Google app updated; the models improve over time. Practice the screenshot-to-Lens flow once on an old spam text so the steps feel automatic when a real one arrives. The method works for messages in Google Messages, WhatsApp, iMessage, or social media notifications as long as you can screenshot the text.

Lens does not replace automatic scam detection that runs inside Google Messages on supported Android phones. Those tools watch conversations in the background. Use both where available.

## Limits to keep in mind

The system only returns a detailed Overview when it has high confidence. Newer or highly customized scams may not trigger a strong warning. A clean result is not proof the message is safe. Always verify requests that involve money or personal data through another method. Lens needs an internet connection. It cannot help after you have already clicked a link and entered information.

## Conclusion

Google Lens turns a suspicious text into a quick AI check on any phone that runs the Google app. Screenshot the message, open Lens, circle the text, and read the Overview. The process takes under a minute and keeps you from opening a bad link.

Pair it with Circle to Search on supported Android devices for an even faster path. Keep both tools ready and show the steps to people who receive unexpected texts. The tools improve, but the habit of checking first stays useful.
