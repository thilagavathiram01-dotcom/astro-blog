Google launched Universal Cart in the United States on October 15, 2026. The tool lets you save products from different retailers into one cart while using Google Search (including AI Mode) and the Gemini app.

It tracks price drops and restocks, surfaces promo codes and card perks from Google Wallet, and supports checkout with Google Pay or a transfer to the merchant site. The merchant remains the seller of record in both cases.

This guide covers how to start using it today if you are in the US.

## What Universal Cart Does

Universal Cart acts as a single hub for shopping on Google. You can add items from Search, AI Mode, Shopping results, or the Gemini app. Those items stay in the same cart across those surfaces.

The cart draws on Google’s Shopping Graph, which contains more than 60 billion continually refreshed product listings. It monitors the items you save and sends notifications when a price drops or an item returns to stock.

Because it connects to Google Wallet, it can highlight relevant promotional codes and suggest which of your saved cards offers the best perks for a purchase. Checkout options depend on the merchant and product.

Participating merchants at launch include Nike, Sephora, Target, Ulta Beauty, Walmart, Wayfair, and many Shopify merchants. You receive confirmations and support directly from the merchant.

## How to Add Products to Your Cart

Open Google Search and switch to AI Mode, or open the Gemini app. Search or ask for the product you want. For example, describe a waterproof jacket under $150 for rainy walks.

Look for a save or add-to-cart option on the product results. Save the item instead of clicking through to the retailer site right away. Repeat the process from another Google surface if you like.

Google’s help documentation confirms that products saved from Search & Shopping, AI Mode, or Gemini Apps land in the same cart. The cart organizes items by merchant so you can review details or remove anything you no longer want.

The feature is currently limited to the United States. If you do not see the option, check that you are signed into a US Google account and that the rollout has reached your account.

## Managing the Cart and Tracking Changes

Once items are saved, open the cart from Search or the Gemini app. Products appear grouped by merchant. You can review pricing, return windows, and stock status for each one.

The cart works in the background using the Shopping Graph. Expect notifications for price drops or restocks on saved items. This replaces the need to revisit each retailer site individually.

Promo codes that apply to items in the cart can appear directly there. Wallet integration also helps you compare card benefits before you decide to buy.

Your cart and related order information are stored in Google Wallet. You can manage associated data and privacy settings through the Wallet controls.

## Checking Out

When you are ready to buy, you have two main paths.

For participating merchants, choose checkout with Google Pay. You complete the transaction on Google using payment methods saved in Google Wallet. Review the item, address, shipping, and total before confirming.

For other merchants, transfer the items to the merchant’s own cart. Google helps populate the cart on the merchant site so you finish the purchase there.

In both cases the merchant stays the seller of record. You receive order confirmations and customer support from the retailer, not from Google.

After purchase, some merchants share order status with Google. Supported orders appear in the “My Orders” tab inside the cart, where you can see updates such as shipped or delivered. Not every merchant shares this data.

## Tips for Using Universal Cart

Start with products you are actively comparing across stores. Save them as you browse so you do not lose track when you switch between Search and Gemini.

Check the cart periodically for price-drop or restock alerts, especially ahead of sales periods.

Review card suggestions from Wallet before paying. The system can surface perks you might otherwise overlook.

If an item does not offer in-cart Google Pay checkout, use the transfer option and complete the purchase on the merchant site.

The feature is US-only for now. Google has indicated future expansion to Canada and Australia, with the UK later, but no firm dates appear in the October launch post. YouTube and Gmail integration, mentioned at the May preview, are not yet live.

For more on connecting Google Wallet to Gemini experiences, see our guide on [connecting Google Wallet to Gemini](/blog/connect-google-wallet-to-gemini/).

## Limitations to Know

Gemini and AI Mode have limited ability to list cart contents or add items purely through conversation in some cases. The primary way to add items remains the save controls on product results.

Order tracking depends on the merchant sharing data. Contact the merchant directly for any purchase issues.

Checkout does not combine multiple retailers into one payment in every case; items stay grouped by merchant.

## Conclusion

Universal Cart gives US users a practical way to keep shopping organized across Google Search and the Gemini app. Save products from multiple retailers, receive alerts on price and stock changes, review perks and promo codes, and choose between Google Pay checkout or a transfer to the merchant site.

It builds on the Shopping Graph and Wallet integration while keeping the merchant as the seller of record. Start by searching in AI Mode or asking Gemini, then save the items you want to track.

Official details come from Google’s October 15, 2026 blog post and the Gemini Apps Help Center page on managing the universal cart.
