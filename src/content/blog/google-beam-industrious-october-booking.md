A flat video tile hides eye contact. Google Beam is built to put that back. On 23 September 2026, Google said teams can book HP Dimension with Google Beam at select Industrious offices in the United States starting in October.

This guide covers where you can try it, what the hardware actually does, and how a session fits with Google Meet or Zoom. It is a booking and evaluation checklist, not a claim that every office already has a unit on the floor.

## What Google Beam is

Google Beam is a 3D video system delivered as HP Dimension with Google Beam. Google Workspace describes it as a light-field display with spatial audio, built so two people can make eye contact and read body language more naturally than on a standard webcam call.

The product is aimed at 1:1 conversations. Interviews, manager check-ins, and client consultations are the cases Google and Industrious have already tested. Larger groups still use ordinary video. Beam can join a normal Meet or Zoom meeting, and Workspace notes that existing interoperability also covers standard calls on Microsoft Teams and Webex.

Hardware is installed and serviced by HP and its resellers. IT can monitor the system with the same admin tools used for Meet and Zoom. You do not download a consumer app and point a laptop camera at the room.

## Where you can book a session in October

Google’s 23 September post names four U.S. cities for the first Industrious bookings:

- Atlanta
- Chicago
- New York City
- Palo Alto

Bookings open in October through Industrious. The landing page Google links is [hello.industriousoffice.com/experience-google-beam-at-industrious](https://hello.industriousoffice.com/experience-google-beam-at-industrious). Product background lives at [beam.google](https://beam.google/).

A pilot earlier in 2026 used the same network for manager check-ins, interviews, and client consultations. The October step is the public extended network, meant for startups and smaller teams that will not buy a unit yet.

Owned units are a separate path. Google says Beam hardware is shipping to customers in six countries: the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, and Japan. Eighteen channel partners handle deploy and support. If you need a permanent room, talk to a partner. If you need one session, start with Industrious.

## Book a trial session

1. Open the Industrious Google Beam page linked from Google’s announcement.
2. Pick a city from the four listed above. Confirm the exact Industrious address on the booking form. Google named cities, not every floor.
3. Choose a time long enough for a real conversation. A 15-minute look at the display is not the same as a 45-minute interview.
4. Decide who sits at the Beam unit and who joins remotely. The 3D view is on the HP Dimension side.
5. Tell the remote person which app you will use. Meet and Zoom are the native paths Google highlights.
6. Arrive early. First-time users spend the opening minutes on framing and audio, not on the agenda.

If the form is waitlisted, that matches a limited rollout. Do not treat a blog post as a guaranteed same-day slot.

## What a useful first session looks like

Google’s own eight-week internal study is the clearest published result. Teams using Beam felt 50% more connected, found it 33% easier to make sure feedback was understood, and saw a 21% drop in follow-up meetings. Those figures come from Google teams, not from Industrious guests.

Bain & Company used the same hardware for campus recruiting so interviewers could read eye contact and posture without flying to every school. Google’s video on that pilot is the best public walkthrough of an interview use case.

Run your trial against one job:

- A hiring screen where tone matters
- A manager check-in that usually needs a second call
- A client consult where slides are secondary to the conversation

After the session, write down three things: whether eye contact felt direct, whether you needed a follow-up call, and whether the remote person could hear you without repeating. That is a better scorecard than “the picture looked sharp.”

## How Meet and Zoom fit

Workspace Help says Beam is built for 1:1 3D meetings, then drops back to a standard video layout for larger groups. You do not need a new meeting product.

Practical setup:

1. Create the meeting in Google Meet or Zoom as you already do.
2. Join from the Beam room on the HP Dimension unit.
3. Have the other person join from a normal laptop or phone.
4. Keep screen share short. Google notes that shrinking the person to the side of a slide deck undoes the point of the display.
5. For a mixed room that includes Teams or Webex guests, use the standard video path. Do not expect those apps to render the light-field view.

Admins who already manage Meet or Zoom can monitor Beam with familiar tools. That matters if you later buy a unit. It does not change the Industrious booking flow.

## When a booking is the wrong tool

Beam is not a laptop feature. It will not replace Gemini on your phone or a normal Meet link for a five-person standup. It is also not a travel ban. Google’s pitch is fewer trips for interviews and check-ins, not zero travel.

Skip the booking if:

- You need more than two active speakers in 3D
- Your city is outside Atlanta, Chicago, New York, and Palo Alto and you cannot travel
- The decision needs a quote for owned hardware. Use a channel partner or [beam.google](https://beam.google/) instead of the coworking form

Early customers named by Google include Netflix and Capital Group, alongside Bain. Those are deployment stories, not a public price list. Google has not published a consumer price in the September post.

## Tips before you go

Sit at a normal conversational distance. The display is meant for eye contact, so leaning into the bezel to “see the pixels” wastes the session.

Use one agenda item. A pilot earlier this year already covered check-ins, interviews, and consults. Copy one of those instead of inventing a demo script.

Ask Industrious staff who owns the room clock. A shared unit can run long if the previous group is still wrapping audio.

If you are comparing this with other Google research bets, the October satellite test in [Project Suncatcher](/blog/project-suncatcher-tpu-satellite-orbit/) is a different kind of hardware story. Beam is something a team can sit in front of this month.

## Conclusion

From October 2026, HP Dimension with Google Beam can be booked at select Industrious sites in Atlanta, Chicago, New York City, and Palo Alto. Owned units are shipping in six countries through 18 channel partners, with Meet and Zoom as the meeting apps Google highlights.

Book one real conversation, score eye contact and follow-up meetings, and only then ask a partner about a permanent room. The display is the product. The coworking slot is the way to test it without buying it.
