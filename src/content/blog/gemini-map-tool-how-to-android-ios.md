Finding the right places nearby often means typing vague descriptions like "near the park" or "around that cafe." The Gemini app's Map tool fixes this. Available on Android and iOS since early October 2026, it lets you draw a circle on a live map and attach that exact area to your prompt.

This guide shows how to open the tool, select an area, and get better results for restaurants, shops, and trip planning.

## What the Gemini Map Tool Does

The Map tool appears in the Gemini mobile app. Tap the plus button next to the chat box, scroll the tools row, and choose Map. A live Google Maps view opens with a circle around your current location.

You can pan the map, pinch to zoom, or search for a different place. Once the circle covers the area you care about, tap Explore this area. Gemini adds a Map Area chip to your prompt. Type your question after it.

Results stay focused on that circle most of the time. Gemini returns cards with photos, ratings, price ranges, and opening hours. A map preview at the top shows pins for every suggestion. You can export the list straight to Google Maps.

The feature is mobile-only. It does not appear on the web or desktop Gemini apps.

## Step-by-Step: Open and Use the Map Tool

Follow these steps on Android or iPhone.

1. Update the Gemini app from the Play Store or App Store.
2. Open Gemini and start a new chat.
3. Tap the plus (+) icon on the left of the input box.
4. Scroll the horizontal tool list to the end.
5. Tap Map.
6. Adjust the circle: drag the map, pinch to zoom in or out, or use the search icon for a specific address.
7. Tap Explore this area at the bottom.
8. The Map Area chip appears in the prompt box. Add your question, such as "best coffee shops for working on a laptop" or "parks with picnic areas."
9. Send the prompt.

Gemini answers with place cards and a map overview. Tap a card for review snippets and a direct link to Google Maps. Tap the map preview to see all pins, then use Export to Google Maps to save the set as a list.

If the circle covers too little or too much, start a new chat and reselect the area. Long conversations can lose the exact circle context.

## Practical Examples That Work Well

Use the tool when you know the neighborhood but not the exact street names.

- Coffee and work spots: Circle a downtown area and ask for quiet cafes with power outlets and good Wi-Fi.
- Family outings: Select a park district and request playgrounds plus nearby restrooms.
- Trip planning: Zoom out to a city neighborhood and ask for walking-distance attractions that are open late.
- Shopping: Highlight a market street and request stores selling a specific item, such as running shoes under a certain price.

In well-mapped areas the results stay inside the circle and include walking times from the center. In places with sparse data, Gemini may note limited options and suggest the closest match outside the boundary. Always double-check addresses before you go.

## Tips for Better Results

Keep prompts short and specific once the Map Area chip is attached. "Coffee shops open now with outdoor seating" works better than a long paragraph.

Zoom so the circle covers roughly the walking distance you care about. A few blocks gives tighter results than an entire city.

Start a fresh chat if follow-up answers drift. Reattach the Map Area chip to restore the location context.

Combine the tool with other Gemini features. After you get a list, ask for a comparison of the top three places or request directions that avoid heavy traffic.

Check that location services are enabled for the Gemini app so the initial circle centers correctly.

If you already use Gemini for other local questions, this tool reduces the need to switch to Google Maps first. For related Gemini tips, see our guide on [search live with Gemini 3.8 on Android](/blog/search-live-gemini-3-8-android/).

## Limitations to Know

The Map tool works only in the mobile Gemini apps. Desktop and web versions do not include it yet.

Results depend on Google Maps data quality. Sparse areas produce weaker suggestions.

The circle is not a hard wall. Gemini may include a nearby place if options inside are limited.

Long chats can drop the precise location. Re-adding the chip fixes it.

## Conclusion

The Gemini Map tool turns vague "near me" questions into precise area searches. Select the circle, attach the chip, and ask. You get cards, pins, and an easy export to Google Maps without leaving the chat.

Update the app, try it on a neighborhood you know, and compare the results to a plain text prompt. Most users notice fewer rewrites and more relevant places.
