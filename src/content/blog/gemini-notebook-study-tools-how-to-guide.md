Students and professionals often struggle to turn scattered notes into focused study sessions. Gemini Notebook solves this by grounding every output in your own sources while adding interactive tools that adapt to how you learn.

Google rolled out several study-focused upgrades in September 2026. These include interactive learning overviews, expanded quiz formats, short video overviews, and real-time voice conversations. You access the tool at notebook.google.com with any Google account. Free users get solid limits; Google AI Pro and Ultra raise them further.

This guide walks through setup, the new features, and practical ways to use them for exam prep or project research. All details come from Google’s official announcements and Help Center.

## Create Your First Study Notebook

Open notebook.google.com and sign in. Click Create new notebook. Name it after a single topic, such as “Organic Chemistry Midterm” or “Q3 Product Launch Research.” Focused notebooks produce sharper answers.

Add sources next. Supported formats include PDFs, Google Docs, Word files, spreadsheets, web URLs, YouTube links, and images of handwritten notes. Each source can hold up to 500,000 words or 200 MB. Free accounts allow up to 50 sources per notebook and 100 notebooks total.

Upload lecture slides, textbook chapters, or past quiz PDFs. You can also search Google Drive or the web from inside the tool and approve sources before they are added. Once sources are in place, the chat panel summarizes them automatically and invites questions.

## Use Interactive Learning Overviews

Under Reports in the Studio panel, generate an Interactive Learning Overview. This weaves a summary with related Studio outputs such as infographics, quizzes, and flashcards into one guided path.

Google designed these overviews to keep study sessions focused. You move through sections at your own pace, then jump into a linked quiz or flashcard set without leaving the notebook. The feature is rolling out to all users.

After generating one, review the citations. Every claim links back to a specific passage in your sources so you can verify accuracy before you rely on it.

## Generate Custom Quizzes and Flashcards

In the Studio panel, choose Flashcards or Quizzes. Select difficulty (easy, medium, or hard) and the number of items. Google is adding short-answer, multiple-select, and fill-in-the-blank formats to the existing multiple-choice style.

After you finish a quiz, return to the chat and ask about your performance. The notebook can identify weak topics and suggest which sources to revisit. You can also edit any generated question or add your own.

For spaced repetition, regenerate a smaller set of flashcards focused on the items you missed. Export the set if you want to review offline.

## Create Short Video Overviews

Studio also produces Short Video Overviews—roughly 60-second clips that combine narration with educational animations. These work well for formulas, diagrams, or multi-step processes that are hard to grasp from text alone.

Generate them in more than 80 languages. Free accounts have daily limits; paid plans raise the ceiling. Share a finished video with classmates via the notebook’s share link or download it for offline use.

Pair a Short Video Overview with an Audio Overview for commute study. Audio Overviews remain available in multiple formats (Deep Dive, Brief, Critique, Debate) and lengths.

## Talk Through Concepts with Voice Chat

On the Gemini Notebook mobile apps (Android and iOS), you can hold real-time voice conversations with a notebook. Responses stay grounded in your sources. You can interrupt, ask follow-ups, and receive step-by-step explanations.

The feature supports nearly 100 languages. It is rolling out first to Google AI Ultra subscribers and then to Pro and other users. Use it when a written explanation still feels incomplete—speak the question and hear the clarification immediately.

You can also record lectures or personal thoughts with the in-app audio recorder. Those recordings become sources you can cite later.

## Connect Notebooks Inside the Gemini App

Notebooks created on the web sync to the Gemini app. Open the side panel, select New notebook or choose an existing one, and continue the same conversation. This is useful when you want to ask a quick question from your phone without opening a browser.

For deeper Workspace integration, see our earlier guide on [connecting Gemini Notebook with the Gemini app on Android](/blog/connect-gemini-notebook-gemini-app-android/).

## Tips for Better Results

Select only the sources you need before asking a question or generating a Studio output. This reduces noise and improves precision.

Configure the chat style under settings. For exam prep, choose the Learning Guide option so answers emphasize key concepts and practice questions.

Check every citation. Gemini Notebook cites specific passages; open them to confirm the model has not over-generalized.

Use the student offer if you qualify. Eligible U.S. college students can claim one year of Google AI Pro free (redeem by 31 December 2026), which raises Notebook usage limits. Students in many other markets can claim Google AI Plus.

Refresh usage every five hours under the flexible limits system. If you hit a ceiling on Video Overviews, defer generation and enable notifications.

## Conclusion

Gemini Notebook turns your own materials into interactive study tools without requiring you to leave the source set. Create a focused notebook, generate an Interactive Learning Overview, practice with custom quizzes, and reinforce with short videos or voice chat. The combination keeps sessions short and measurable.

Start at notebook.google.com today, upload one set of notes, and produce your first quiz or video overview. Track which topics still feel weak and regenerate only those items. Consistent, source-grounded practice compounds faster than passive re-reading.
