Gemini Intelligence lets you hand multi-step tasks to Gemini on Android. Instead of tapping through apps yourself, you give a command and Gemini navigates the right apps in a secure virtual window. You stay in control the whole time.

Google announced the feature as part of Gemini Intelligence in May 2026. Early support focused on food, grocery, and rideshare apps on Pixel 10 series and Galaxy S26 devices, with broader rollout later. The system only acts on your explicit command and stops when the task finishes.

![Android phone on a desk showing apps](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1516321318423-f06f85e504b3?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What app automation actually does

Gemini can open supported apps, fill in details from context you provide, and move through steps such as building a cart or booking a ride. Examples from Google include reordering a meal, booking a spin class, or turning a grocery list in your notes into a delivery order.

You can add screen or image context. Long-press the power button over a notes list and ask Gemini to build a shopping cart. Or snap a photo of a brochure and ask for a matching tour. Progress appears in notifications so you can watch, jump in, or stop at any moment.

Automation runs in a secure virtual window. Gemini sees only the apps needed for the task, not the rest of your device. Sensitive steps such as payment still require your final confirmation.

## Requirements and availability

You need a device that supports Gemini Intelligence. Google started with the latest Pixel and Galaxy phones and is expanding to more Android devices, watches, cars, glasses, and laptops over time. Availability also depends on country, language, and app support.

The feature is opt-in. Gemini only starts work after you give a clear command. Make sure the Gemini app is updated and that you have granted the necessary permissions for the apps involved.

If your phone does not yet show the automation option, check for system and Gemini app updates. Rollouts happen in waves.

## How to start a multi-step task

1. Open the app or screen that contains the context you want to use, such as a grocery list in your notes or a photo of a brochure.
2. Long-press the power button to bring up Gemini. On some devices you may also use the Gemini app or a dedicated shortcut.
3. Speak or type a clear request. Examples: “Reorder my last grocery delivery,” “Book a ride home from the airport,” or “Find a tour like this for six people on Expedia.”
4. Confirm the request. Gemini begins working in the background virtual window.
5. Watch the notification for live progress. Tap it to view the steps or stop the task if needed.

Gemini stops automatically when the task is complete. You then confirm the final step, such as placing the order or accepting the booking.

## Monitor progress and stay in control

Notifications show what Gemini is doing. You can open the notification to see the current screen in the virtual window, intervene if something looks wrong, or cancel immediately.

Because the automation only starts on your command and ends when finished, you do not leave a standing agent running in the background. You can always review the result before any irreversible action.

If the task involves payment or personal details, Gemini pauses for your explicit confirmation. Do not approve a step you did not request.

## Tips for reliable results

Give specific requests. “Reorder the usual groceries for delivery tomorrow evening” works better than a vague “get food.” Include quantities, preferences, or time windows when they matter.

Start with supported categories. Early focus was food, grocery, and rideshare. Other verticals arrive over time. If an app is not yet supported, Gemini may not be able to complete the task.

Use screen or image context when it helps. Pointing Gemini at an existing list or photo reduces the chance of missing items.

Check the result before confirming. Automation can misread details or pick a different option than you expected. The final confirmation step is there for a reason.

If a task fails or stops early, note what happened and try a more precise command. You can also open the target app yourself and finish manually.

## Limits and privacy

Gemini Intelligence app automation is limited to the apps and regions Google has enabled. It does not give Gemini unrestricted access to every app on your phone.

Audio used for voice commands is processed for the task and is not stored for later training in the way Google describes for this feature. Screen context is used only for the active task.

You can review and revoke permissions in Android settings at any time. Turning off related Gemini connections stops new automation requests from starting.

For deeper technical control, developers can expose structured tools through AppFunctions. See our guide on [Android AppFunctions for agents](/blog/android-appfunctions-agents/) for how apps can offer named actions instead of pure screen automation.

## Conclusion

Gemini Intelligence app automation turns multi-step chores into a single command on supported Android phones. Start with a clear request on a supported app, watch the notification, and keep the final confirmation for yourself. As the feature expands beyond food and rideshare, the same pattern of command, transparent progress, and user control applies.

Update your device and Gemini app, test one simple task, and note which apps respond well on your phone. That gives you a practical sense of what is ready today versus what is still rolling out.
