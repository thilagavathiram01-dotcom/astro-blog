Google added temporary threads to AI Mode in Search on 17 November 2026. These sessions let you ask questions without saving them to your history or letting them shape future recommendations. The feature works like temporary chats in the Gemini app, but it lives inside Search.

You do not change any settings. One click starts a detached session. Close the thread and it disappears. This guide shows exactly how to start one, what stays private, and when to use it.

## What Temporary Threads Do

A temporary thread runs completely detached from your Google account. Responses do not draw on past preferences. They do not influence later personalization. Nothing lands in your AI Mode history.

Google states that temporary threads are not used to train its generative AI models. The greeting switches to “Welcome, stranger.” Once you close the thread, you cannot reopen it. You return to your normal, personalized AI Mode experience.

This gives an in-product alternative to opening google.com in an Incognito window while still letting you pick models.

## How to Start a Temporary Thread

The option is rolling out globally on desktop and mobile browsers across every language AI Mode supports. Look for the icon in the upper right corner of AI Mode.

1. Open Google Search and switch to AI Mode. On desktop, click the AI Mode button or tab. On mobile, open the Google app or browser and enter AI Mode.
2. Click or tap the temporary threads icon in the upper right. It is a pen with a dotted border, the same icon used in the Gemini app for temporary chats.
3. Confirm the greeting changed to “Welcome, stranger.” A note appears under the prompt box: temporary threads do not appear in your history and will not be used to train Google’s generative AI models.
4. Type or speak your question as usual. You can still use the model picker.
5. Close the thread when finished. The conversation vanishes. Switch back to a regular session for normal searches.

No settings changes are required. You can jump in and out as needed.

## When Temporary Threads Make Sense

Google highlights two common cases: brainstorming a surprise gift, and looking up something you do not want to see again in recommendations or history.

Other practical uses include:

- Researching sensitive health or financial topics before you decide what to share later.
- Comparing options for a private purchase without feeding your usual interests.
- Testing prompts or exploring ideas outside your normal profile.
- Checking details for a one-off task that should not shape future suggestions.

Because the session is detached, answers stay general. They do not reference your past chats or preferences.

## Tips for Better Private Searches

Use full sentences. AI Mode handles longer, multi-part questions better than short keywords. Phrase your request the same way you would ask a person.

Close the thread deliberately. The conversation ends only when you close it. Leaving the tab open keeps the session active.

If the icon is missing, the rollout is still reaching accounts. Try again later or check on another browser. The feature does not require a paid plan.

For everyday personalized help, stick with regular AI Mode or the Gemini app. Temporary threads trade personalization for privacy.

## Limits to Know

Temporary threads stay limited to the current browser session. They do not sync across devices. You cannot return to a closed thread.

Ads and source links still appear according to normal AI Mode rules. The privacy controls cover history, personalization, and training use, not every data point Google may process for safety or delivery.

The feature does not replace full Incognito mode for all browsing. It specifically covers AI Mode conversations.

## Conclusion

Temporary threads give you a quick way to ask questions in AI Mode without leaving a trail in your history or recommendations. Click the dotted pen icon, confirm the “Welcome, stranger” greeting, ask your questions, and close the thread when done.

The option rolled out globally starting 17 November 2026 on desktop and mobile browsers. No settings changes are needed. Use it whenever a search should stay private.
