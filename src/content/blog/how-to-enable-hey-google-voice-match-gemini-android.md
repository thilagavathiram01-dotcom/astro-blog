You can talk to Gemini without touching your phone once you enable Hey Google and Voice Match. Many Android users still see the option greyed out or hear nothing when they say the wake phrase. This guide walks through the exact settings from Google’s support pages so the feature works on your device.

Gemini replaces Google Assistant as the primary mobile assistant on eligible Android phones. Once it is active, the same Hey Google phrase that used to open Assistant now opens Gemini—provided Voice Match is set up. Without Voice Match the phone cannot reliably tell your voice from background noise or other people.

## What you need before you start

Confirm three things first. You must be signed in to a personal Google Account (work and school accounts have limited Gemini features). The Google app must be set as the default digital assistant app on the device. Finally, your language and region must support Hey Google; some languages allow voice chat inside the app but not the wake word.

If Gemini is not yet your assistant, switch to it first. Open the Google app, tap your profile picture, go to Settings, then Google Assistant, then Digital assistants from Google, and select Gemini. Follow the on-screen prompts. On some devices you can also reach the same choice through Settings → Apps → Default apps → Digital assistant app.

For a full walkthrough of making Gemini the default, see our earlier guide: [How to Set Gemini as Default Assistant on Android](/blog/how-to-set-gemini-as-default-assistant-on-android).

## Turn on Hey Google inside the Gemini app

Open the Gemini app on your Android phone or tablet. Tap the menu icon (three lines) on the left or tap your profile picture or initial at the top right. Choose Settings, then tap Talk to Gemini hands-free. Turn the Hey Google toggle on.

If the toggle is already on but the wake word does nothing, turn it off and back on again. Android then prompts you to set up or retrain Voice Match. Follow the on-screen instructions: you will speak a few phrases so Google can build a voice model that recognises you.

The voice model is created on Google’s servers and then stored only on the devices where you have enabled Voice Match. When the phone hears “Hey Google,” it compares the audio against that model before sending the request.

## Set up Voice Match from your Google Account

You can also manage Voice Match at the account level. Open the Gemini app, tap your profile picture or initial, then Manage your Google Account, then Data & privacy, then Voice Match. Turn on Hey Google and complete the training prompts.

Account-level Voice Match works across Gemini on Android, Wear OS watches, and compatible Nest or Home devices. Once it is active, saying “Hey Google” on an unlocked phone opens a Gemini chat. On a locked phone you can still perform some quick actions (such as sending a message) if the Gemini on lock screen setting is enabled.

Voice Match is not available for supervised Family Link accounts in the same way; parents manage those separately.

## Ways to start a conversation once it is enabled

With Hey Google active you have several options:

1. Say “Hey Google” followed by your request. The microphone stays open for a short time so you can continue speaking.
2. Long-press the power button (or the side button on Samsung devices) to open the Gemini overlay.
3. Swipe up from a bottom corner on phones that support the gesture.
4. Open the Gemini app and tap the microphone icon, then speak.

In the overlay you can also tap “Ask about this screen” when another app is open. That lets Gemini use on-screen text or a screenshot (if the Screen context settings are enabled in Gemini Settings).

## Fix common problems

If Hey Google still fails after the steps above, try these checks in order:

- Confirm the Google app (not another assistant) is the default digital assistant. Search Settings for “digital assistant app.”
- Force-stop the Gemini app and the Google app, then reopen Gemini and toggle Hey Google again.
- Retrain Voice Match. In Settings → Talk to Gemini hands-free, or via Data & privacy → Voice Match, choose the option to re-record your voice.
- Clear the Gemini app cache: Settings → Apps → Gemini → Storage & cache → Clear cache. Do not clear data unless you want to reset the app.
- Check that your language supports the wake word. Some languages listed in Google’s support documents allow typed or in-app voice but not “Hey Google.”
- Restart the phone after changing the default assistant.

On Samsung phones the long-press behaviour of the side button is controlled separately under Settings → Advanced features → Side button. Make sure it is set to Digital assistant if you want the power/side button to open Gemini.

## Tips for better hands-free results

Speak the wake phrase clearly and pause briefly before your actual request. In noisy environments Voice Match still helps, but moving closer to the phone improves accuracy. You can interrupt Gemini while it is speaking by saying “Hey Google” again or tapping the stop control in the overlay.

Keep the Google app updated from the Play Store. Server-side changes sometimes alter the exact menu names; searching Settings for “Hey Google” or “Voice Match” almost always finds the right screen.

If you prefer not to use the wake word, leave Hey Google off and simply open the app or use the power-button gesture. The rest of Gemini continues to work normally.

## Conclusion

Enabling Hey Google and Voice Match takes only a few minutes once Gemini is your default assistant. The controls live inside the Gemini app under Talk to Gemini hands-free and in your Google Account’s Data & privacy section. After the voice model is trained, you can start conversations from any screen without unlocking the phone for many tasks.

Follow the official paths listed in Google’s Android Help and Gemini Apps Help documents and the feature should activate reliably. If menus differ on your brand of phone, use the Settings search box—the underlying options remain the same.
