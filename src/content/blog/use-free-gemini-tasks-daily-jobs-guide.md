Google made Gemini Tasks free for adults in six countries on 17 November 2026. You can now switch from Chat to Task in the Gemini app and let it handle longer jobs in the background while you focus on other things.

This guide shows exactly how to start using free Gemini Tasks, what it can do, and the limits you need to know. If you already use Gemini for Android or the web, the steps take under a minute.

## What Gemini Tasks Does

Gemini Tasks (previously called Spark) runs multi-step work in the background. You describe a job. Gemini works on it, checks with you when needed, and notifies you when it finishes.

It runs on the Gemini 4 Hydrogen model for free users in the supported countries. You can run up to 15 tasks at once. Schedules run once a day in a general time window on the free tier.

Common uses include building a travel itinerary, creating a morning briefing from email and calendar, comparing products, or researching a topic across multiple sites.

## Requirements to Use Free Gemini Tasks

You need a personal Google Account (not a work or school account). You must be 18 or older. Gemini History must be turned on. The feature is available in the Gemini mobile app (Android or iOS), the Mac app, or gemini.google.com.

Free access is rolling out in the United States, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia, and India. Outside these countries it still requires a Google AI Pro or Ultra plan. It is not available in the EEA, UK, Switzerland, or Nigeria.

## How to Start a Task

Open the Gemini app or go to gemini.google.com and sign in.

On the home screen, find the toggle above or below the prompt box. Switch it from Chat to Task.

Describe the job in clear language. Include any limits, such as a budget or “do not book anything.” You can attach files from your device or Google Drive.

Submit the task. Gemini starts working in the background. You can add more instructions later in the same conversation. To stop it, use the Stop button in the text box.

For US users, you can also text Gemini or forward emails to gemini@google.com after setting up the channel in Settings > Channels.

## Example Tasks to Try

“Research a three-day weekend trip to Kyoto. Include public transport options, estimated costs for attractions, and places to eat near each stop. Do not make any reservations.”

“Create a daily morning briefing that summarises unread emails and today’s calendar events. Run it around 7 a.m.”

“Compare the prices and delivery times for a specific model of headphones across three major retailers. Show the best options.”

These examples stay within free-tier capabilities and avoid actions that require extra confirmation.

## Tips for Better Results

Be specific about the goal and any constraints. Gemini will ask clarifying questions if something is ambiguous.

Start with research or organisation tasks before trying anything that involves purchases or external sites. Purchase confirmation is on by default and Gemini will pause for your approval.

Check the task progress and add instructions as it runs. You can also set simple recurring schedules on the free tier.

Keep Gemini History enabled so Tasks can use relevant context from your account.

## Advanced Features and Limits

In the US, eligible users can have Gemini call businesses to compare options or check availability (with transcripts and approval before dialing), complete supported Google Pay purchases, and access some password-protected sites via Google Password Manager.

Business calling is not available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, or Nebraska. Texting and email channels are currently US-only.

Free users get 10 active schedules that run once a day in a broad window. Paid plans raise the limit and allow more precise timing.

Tasks run in a sandboxed environment with protections against prompt injection. Google Safe Browsing applies when the agent visits websites.

## Conclusion

Free Gemini Tasks turns the Gemini app into a background assistant for multi-step work. In supported countries you can start using it today by flipping the Chat/Task toggle and describing what you need done.

Begin with clear research or planning jobs, review the results, and expand from there. The feature is still rolling out, so if the toggle does not appear yet, check again later.
