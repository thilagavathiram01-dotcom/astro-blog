Planning a trip used to mean three tabs: Google Flights, a hotel site, and a rewards portal that never quite matched the cash price. On 27 August 2026, Google folded those pieces into **AI Mode** in Search so you can ask for a route, watch the fare, and finish a hotel booking without leaving the conversation.

This guide covers what shipped, where it works, and the exact prompts that match Google’s own examples. It is a consumer walkthrough, not a recap of every partner press release.

## What AI Mode added for travel

Google’s Search blog lists three upgrades:

- **Flight price tracking** inside the chat, using the same alerts as Google Flights
- **Points and miles rates** for selected airline and hotel programs
- **Hotel booking** through a “Continue on Google” path that can use Google Pay

Flight options come from more than **300 partner airlines and travel sites**. Price tracking is available in **more than 180 countries**. Hotel booking started in the **United States in English** and is expanding.

AI Mode is the conversational tab in Google Search, not the Gemini app. Gemini already had Flights and Hotels extensions; this article is about Search.

## Open AI Mode and describe the trip

1. Go to [google.com](https://www.google.com) on phone or desktop and tap **AI Mode**, or start a search and switch to that tab.
2. State origin, destination, dates, and any hard constraints in one message.
3. Review the flight list. Google says AI Mode shows current prices from those 300-plus sources.
4. Ask follow-ups in the same thread: nonstop only, morning departures, or a different weekend.

Useful first prompts:

- “Find nonstop flights from Atlanta to Miami departing October 9 and returning October 12.”
- “Weekend trip from Seattle to Boston in late September, under $400 round trip if possible.”
- “Family-friendly hotel in Taos near hiking trails with a heated pool, check-in Friday.”

Keep dates in the first message. Vague “sometime in fall” queries make tracking and booking harder because alerts need a route and window.

## Track a fare instead of refreshing Flights

If you are not ready to buy:

1. After AI Mode shows options, say **“Track these flight prices for me.”** Google’s announcement uses that wording.
2. Confirm the alert when Search asks.
3. Watch email for a change on that destination and date range.
4. When a drop looks good, return to the same thread or start a new AI Mode chat and rebuild the itinerary before you jump to the airline site.

Tracking does **not** purchase tickets. Google still sends you to the airline or your usual booking site to pay for flights. The value is the alert living next to the conversation instead of a separate Flights saved search you forget to check.

## Compare cash against points and miles

Ask AI Mode to price the same trip in a loyalty currency. Google’s example:

> “I want to travel from Atlanta to Miami using my AA miles. Help me find some options for nonstop flights departing Oct 9 and returning on Oct 12.”

Programs confirmed at launch:

- **Airlines:** Alaska Airlines, Hawaiian Airlines, American Airlines
- **Hotels:** Choice Hotels, Hilton, Wyndham

Google said Accor, Flying Blue, Hyatt, LATAM Airlines, and Lufthansa Group would follow in later weeks. Treat that list as rolling. If a program is missing, AI Mode will fall back to cash fares.

Do not assume award inventory is complete. Independent testers noted a small partner set at launch. Use the result as a first pass, then confirm seats in the airline app before you transfer points.

## Book a hotel without leaving Search

Hotel booking in AI Mode is the piece that goes beyond a link-out.

1. Describe city, dates, budget, and must-haves (walkable, kitchenette, late checkout).
2. AI Mode returns a visual list with guest reviews and comparison factors.
3. Pick a property and choose **Continue on Google** when it appears next to partner links.
4. Select the room, read the cancellation policy, and pay with **Google Pay** when that option is offered.

Partners named for the U.S. English rollout:

- Booking.com
- Choice Hotels International
- Expedia
- Hilton
- Hotels.com
- IHG Hotels & Resorts
- Marriott International
- Priceline
- Trip.com
- Wyndham Hotels & Resorts

If “Continue on Google” is missing, the property is still bookable through the partner site. That is normal outside the U.S. English rollout and for chains that have not wired checkout yet.

## Limits and good habits

- **Flights:** AI Mode plans and tracks. It does not complete airline checkout inside Search the way the hotel path can.
- **Hotels:** In-chat checkout is U.S. English first. Everyone else still gets research and partner links.
- **Awards:** Only listed programs return miles or points rates.
- **Responsibility:** Confirm baggage rules, name spelling, and cancel-by dates on the confirmation page. Search can assemble options; it does not replace the booking contract.
- **Privacy:** Travel chats sit in your Google Account activity. Clear them in My Activity if you used a shared computer.

A practical loop that stays inside what Google shipped: track the flight in AI Mode, book the hotel with Continue on Google when the partner supports it, then buy the ticket on the airline site the same day the alert fires.

## Conclusion

AI Mode will not replace a travel agent for complex multi-city awards. It does remove the habit of opening Flights, Hotels, and a points calculator as three separate jobs. Describe the trip once, set the price alert in the same thread, compare miles when the program is supported, and use Continue on Google for hotels in the U.S. English rollout.
