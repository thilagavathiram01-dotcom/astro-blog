Google expanded the Gemini app to eligible Android (Go edition) devices on 18 November 2026. Hundreds of millions of people who rely on lower-memory phones for internet access, education, and business can now use the personal AI assistant that was previously unavailable on these handsets.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="711" />

Until this update, Android Go phones offered only basic voice commands. Google re-architected the app so the core Gemini experience runs on hardware with less memory. The result is access to Gemini Live conversations, camera-based help, and image creation on devices that meet a clear 2 GB RAM threshold.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="744" />

This guide covers eligibility, installation, what works, what does not, and practical tips drawn only from Google’s announcement and support documentation.

## Check Eligibility First

The Gemini mobile app is available on Android (Go edition) devices with 2 GB of RAM or more. You must be signed in to a personal Google Account, or a work or school Google Account that has access to Gemini Apps. Availability matches the languages and countries where Gemini is offered.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="744" />

Family Link managers can control access for users under the applicable age. On Android Go devices, Gemini is available as part of the Google Search app. You can also download the standalone Gemini app from the Play Store. Some newer devices from partners such as Transsion ship with it preloaded.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="714" />

Confirm your phone’s RAM and Android Go edition in Settings before proceeding. Devices below 2 GB of RAM are not covered by the current support page.

## Install and Start Gemini

1. Open the Google Play Store on your Android Go device.
2. Search for “Google” or “Google Search.”
3. Tap Update if available, or confirm the button says Open.
4. Alternatively, search for and install the Gemini app directly from Play Store.

To start a conversation, touch and hold the Home button. On supported devices, press and hold the Power button. Some phones may open Gemini through the updated Google Search app without a separate download.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="744" />

The first launch may prompt you to grant microphone or camera permissions. Accept these only if you plan to use voice or image features.

## What You Can Do

Google’s support page lists the following capabilities on eligible Android Go devices:

- **Stay connected**: Ask Gemini to call or send text messages to contacts.
- **Image help**: Take a photo to translate text or receive step-by-step guidance on notes, forms, or signs. Create images with Nano Banana.
- **Gemini Live**: Hold spoken conversations to brainstorm or practice for an interview. Support covers more than 45 languages.
- **Local information**: Ask complex questions such as finding a restaurant open for lunch with specific amenities nearby.
- **Day planning**: Set alarms or create calendar events.
- **File and photo uploads**: Add context to chats by uploading documents or photos.
- **Media**: Request music by song, mood, or activity.

These features rely on cloud inference, so the phone handles the interface, microphone, camera, and networking while the models run remotely. This design is what allowed the re-architecture for lower-memory hardware.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="711" />

## Limitations to Expect

Two common activation methods are unavailable. Using “Hey Google” and “Ask about this screen” to chat with Gemini are not supported on Android (Go edition) devices. You must use the Home or Power button hold methods instead.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="744" />

Performance depends on available free RAM, storage space, and network quality. Closing background apps before long Gemini Live sessions or camera translations helps avoid interruptions from the system’s low-memory killer. Image creation and Live conversations consume data, so monitor usage on limited mobile plans.

Gemini replaces Assistant for Android (Go edition) on supported devices. Older Go phones that fall below the 2 GB threshold continue with the previous experience.

## Tips for Smoother Use

Keep the Google Search app and Gemini app updated. Free storage space before uploading large files or generating images. Speak clearly and in a supported language during Live sessions; you can interrupt responses to correct course, just as on higher-end devices.

For camera tasks, ensure good lighting and hold the phone steady when photographing text or forms. Test basic functions (alarms, calls, music) first to confirm account and permission setup. If Gemini does not appear after updating, restart the device and check Play Store again.

Users who already use Gemini Live on other devices can apply the same conversational habits here. For a deeper look at real-time voice models, see the guide on [Gemini 3.8 Live](/blog/gemini-3-8-live/).

## Conclusion

Eligible Android Go devices with at least 2 GB of RAM now run the re-architected Gemini app. Download it from the Play Store, update the Google Search app, or use the button-hold activation methods. Core features such as Gemini Live, camera translation and help, image creation, calls, alarms, and media playback are available. “Hey Google” and screen queries are not.

The change brings Google’s personal AI assistant to hundreds of millions more people who previously had only basic voice commands. Check your device’s RAM and Google Account eligibility, then follow the steps above to start using it today.
