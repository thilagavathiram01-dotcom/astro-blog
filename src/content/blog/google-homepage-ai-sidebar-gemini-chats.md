Google rolled out a collapsible sidebar on its desktop homepage on November 19, 2026. The panel lists your recent AI Mode searches so you can resume them without digging through history.

Robby Stein, vice president of product for Google Search, announced the change on X. He said people ask more questions that are not one-and-done. In testing the sidebar helped users make progress and continue exploring the web.

The sidebar appears as a thin gray bar on the left edge of google.com. Click it to expand. When closed, the classic logo and search box stay centered.

![Person working at a computer desk with dual monitors](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1498050108023-c5249f4df085?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What the sidebar includes

The expanded panel shows:

- New thread button
- Search threads tool
- Notebooks section with an Add notebook option
- Recent list of past AI Mode conversations

Click any recent thread and it reopens in AI Mode. You can add follow-up questions right away. The panel also lets you connect a Gemini Notebook for shared sources and instructions.

Google keeps the sidebar collapsible so the minimal homepage remains available. No mobile version has been announced.

## How to open and use it

1. Go to google.com on a desktop browser while signed in to your Google account.
2. Look for the thin gray bar or rail on the far left of the page. It may include a Google G icon.
3. Click the bar to expand the sidebar.
4. Choose New thread to start fresh, or pick an item from Recent to continue an existing chat.
5. Use Search threads if you need to find an older conversation.
6. To close it, click the collapse control or click outside the panel.

The rollout is gradual and global for desktop. If the bar is missing, check back later or try a different browser or account.

## Why Google added the sidebar

Stein noted that users bring multi-step questions to Search. The sidebar puts recent AI Mode threads on the same page as the search box. This reduces the steps needed to pick up a research session or planning task.

It pairs with other November updates, including temporary threads in AI Mode that do not save to history. You can use permanent threads for ongoing work and temporary ones for one-off questions.

Below the search box, some accounts also see Gemini shortcuts for creating images, analyzing files, and brainstorming. These replace the old dedicated Search button in certain views.

## Tips for better results

Enable Search history and personalized recommendations in your Google account settings. Without them the sidebar may still appear but cannot resume previous threads.

Start a thread with a clear goal, such as “compare three laptop models under $800 with battery life and ports.” Then use the sidebar to return the next day and add “include student discounts.”

Connect a Gemini Notebook if you research the same topic often. Notebooks carry sources and custom instructions across AI Mode sessions.

For tasks that need actions outside Search, switch to the Gemini app and use Tasks mode. See our guide on [how to use free Gemini Tasks](/blog/how-to-use-tasks-in-gemini-free-guide/).

## What to expect next

The sidebar is desktop-only for now. Google has not shared a timeline for mobile. Some users also see the panel on results pages after an AI Mode query.

If you prefer the old layout, leave the sidebar collapsed. It does not force AI Mode on every search. Regular results remain available through the search box.

The change continues Google’s move toward conversational search while keeping the familiar homepage one click away.
