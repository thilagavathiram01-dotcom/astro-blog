Google Workspace now lets you talk to Gmail, Docs, and Keep instead of hunting through threads or staring at a blank page. The features shipped in early September 2026 under the Live name and run on Gemini Audio models first shown at I/O.

Gmail Live answers questions from your inbox. Docs Live turns a spoken brainstorm into a structured draft. Keep Live converts a brain dump into lists and notes. This guide covers who can use each tool, how to start a session, and what to ask so the answers stay useful.

## Who gets Gmail Live, Docs Live, and Keep Live

Google published the plan split in its Workspace announcement. Gmail Live and Keep Live are available to Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra subscribers. Docs Live is limited to Pro and Ultra.

Workspace business customers are next. The company said the same voice features are coming soon for those accounts, after the consumer AI plans.

You need the current Gmail, Docs, or Keep apps (or the web versions where Live is enabled) and a working microphone. English is the first language Google highlighted in its launch examples.

## How Gmail Live searches your inbox by voice

Gmail Live is conversational search, not a second copy of Ask Gemini. Open Gmail, tap the search bar, and look for the Live control next to standard search.

Ask a question the way you would ask a colleague. Official examples include “What’s my flight's gate number?” and “What’s going on at my kid’s school this week?” Gmail Live scans mail and answers from what it finds.

Follow-up questions work in the same session. If the first answer names a school event, you can ask what to bring or who sent the last note. Keep questions grounded in mail you actually received. The tool cannot invent a gate number that never arrived in your inbox.

Use it when your hands are busy: packing, driving a parked car before you leave, or standing in a kitchen. Speak the constraint if it matters: “only emails from last week” or “the message from the airline.”

## How Docs Live drafts a document while you talk

Docs Live is built for first drafts. Open Docs, start a new file or an existing one, and choose the voice / Live path Google shows as Create with voice on mobile.

Talk through the goal, the audience, and the sections you want. Docs Live organizes the ramble into headings and body text. With your permission, it can pull details from Gmail, Drive, Chat, and the web so you do not copy-paste between tabs.

Google’s own demo asked Gemini to organize an end-of-summer block party, then add kids’ activities and a recipe. That pattern is the right one: state the event, name the sections, then refine tone out loud (“make the invite shorter” or “add a grocery list”).

Stay in the conversation until the outline matches what you would send. Then stop talking and edit the document like any other Docs file. Voice gets you to a draft. You still own the final wording.

## How Keep Live turns a brain dump into lists

Keep Live is the fastest of the three. Open Keep, start a Live session, and speak without formatting. The feature is meant for the thought you would lose if you had to type.

Say the raw list: errands, ingredients, people to email, or a mix of all three. Keep Live structures that stream into notes and checklists. You do not have to say “make a bullet list” if the intent is obvious.

A practical habit is one topic per session. Grocery items in one note. Project follow-ups in another. Mixed dumps still work, but you will spend more time splitting the result afterward.

After Keep finishes, open the note and fix names, quantities, and due dates. Voice capture is strong on intent and weaker on exact spelling of uncommon words, so a 20-second pass is worth it.

## Prompts that work better than a single keyword

Ask for a fact plus a bound. “What did the contractor say about the delivery date in August?” beats “contractor.”

Name the output. In Docs, say you want an agenda, a one-page brief, or an email you can paste. In Keep, say you want a checklist you can tick off in the store.

Grant context only when you need it. Docs Live can read Gmail, Drive, and Chat if you allow it. Turn that off for drafts that should stay generic.

Repeat the audience. “Write this for parents of preschoolers” or “write this for the finance team” changes tone more reliably than “make it professional.”

Stop and correct early. If the first paragraph is wrong, say so before the model fills three pages.

## Privacy and control

These tools read the Workspace content you already store in Gmail, Drive, Chat, and Keep. That is the point of the product. Treat Live sessions like any other Gemini feature in Workspace: do not dictate passwords, one-time codes, or data you would not put in a normal email.

You stay in control of the document or note after generation. Nothing publishes until you share it. If a session pulls the wrong thread, close it and start again with a tighter prompt.

For other Android privacy controls that sit beside Workspace, see our guide to [Android Private Space](/blog/android-private-space/). That feature is separate from Live, but it is the right place to park apps you do not want mixed into a shared profile.

## Fixes when Live is missing or silent

Update Gmail, Docs, and Keep from the Play Store or App Store. Live shipped as an app-side rollout, not a one-day server switch for every account.

Confirm the plan. Plus includes Gmail and Keep. Docs Live needs Pro or Ultra. Check [Google AI plans](https://one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans/) if the buttons never appear.

Check mic permission for that specific app. A system-wide mic grant is not always enough on Android.

Try the other surface. If mobile is missing the control, open Gmail or Docs on the web on a laptop with a headset.

If answers ignore recent mail, wait a few minutes and ask again with a date. Index lag is more common than a permanent miss.

## Conclusion

Gmail Live, Docs Live, and Keep Live are useful when typing is the bottleneck. Search mail by asking a real question. Draft in Docs by talking through structure. Capture Keep notes before the thought disappears.

Start with one app this week. Gmail Live pays off first if your inbox is crowded. Docs Live pays off if you write briefs. Keep Live pays off if your notes app is a graveyard of half-typed lines.
