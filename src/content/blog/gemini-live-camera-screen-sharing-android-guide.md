Gemini Live on Android lets you talk naturally while showing the AI what you see. Point your camera at an object or share your screen, and Gemini responds in real time with guidance. This turns the assistant into a visual helper for tasks that text alone cannot explain.

The feature works on Android phones and tablets running Android 10 or later with at least 2 GB of RAM. You need the Gemini app updated and an active internet connection. Camera and screen sharing are available to free users on supported devices.

## What You Need Before Starting

Confirm your device meets the requirements. Open Settings, then About phone, and check the Android version and RAM. Update the Gemini app from the Google Play Store. Set Gemini as your default digital assistant if you want quick access via the power button. For details on that setup, see our guide on [how to set Gemini as the default assistant on Android](/blog/gemini-default-assistant-android/).

Enable notifications for the Gemini app. Screen sharing requires this so the system can maintain the session. Go to Settings, then Apps, then Gemini, then Notifications, and turn them on.

## How to Start a Gemini Live Session

Open the Gemini app. Tap the Live icon at the bottom of the screen. It looks like a waveform or three lines with a spark. The first time, follow any on-screen prompts to grant microphone access.

You can also say "Hey Google, let's talk Live" if Gemini is your default assistant and Voice Match is enabled. Or long-press the power button to open the overlay, then tap Live.

Once Live starts, speak naturally. Gemini listens continuously until you pause or end the session. You can interrupt it at any time by talking again.

## Share Your Camera in Gemini Live

With a Live session open, tap the camera icon at the bottom. Grant camera permission if asked. The viewfinder appears, showing the rear camera by default.

Point the phone at the object or scene you want help with. Speak your question. For example, hold up a product label and ask what the ingredients mean, or show a broken appliance and describe the problem.

Tap the switch icon in the viewfinder to flip to the front camera. Tap the camera icon again to stop sharing. Camera sharing ends automatically if you lock the screen, leave the Gemini app, or put Live on hold. Restart it by tapping the camera icon again.

This works well for identifying objects, getting styling advice, or troubleshooting physical items. Gemini sees the live feed and keeps context as you move the phone.

## Share Your Screen with Gemini Live

Open the app or page you want help with first. Long-press the power button to open the Gemini overlay, or start Live from the app. Tap "Share screen with Live" or the screen-share icon.

Android asks for permission to record the screen. Confirm it. The entire screen is shared, not a single window. Navigate to the content and ask questions. Scroll, tap, or switch apps while sharing. Gemini follows what appears on screen.

To stop, return to the Gemini Live controls and tap the option to end sharing, or pull down the notification shade and tap Stop on the screen-sharing card. Sharing also stops if you lock the phone or put Live on hold. It does not resume automatically. Start it again when needed.

Use this for settings menus, form filling, email review, or understanding long documents. You can ask Gemini to summarize a page or walk you through a multi-step process while you stay on the relevant screen.

## Practical Tips for Better Results

Speak clearly and give context. Instead of "What is this?", say "This is a plant I bought last week. Why are the leaves turning yellow?" Gemini uses both the visual and your description.

For camera sharing, keep the subject in frame and hold the phone steady for a moment. Good lighting helps. For screen sharing, close sensitive apps first if you do not want them visible. The share covers the full display.

Test a simple request first. Show a familiar object or open a known settings page. Confirm Gemini sees the right content before moving to more complex tasks. End the session when finished to free resources and stop any ongoing sharing.

If the camera or screen-share buttons do not appear, update the Gemini and Google apps, then restart the phone. Check that your account is signed in and that the device meets the Android 10 and 2 GB RAM requirements. On some devices the feature rolled out gradually, so a later update may be needed.

## Privacy and Controls

Camera and screen data are used only for the current Live session. Google processes the visual input to generate responses. You control when sharing starts and stops. Turn off Live notifications if you do not want background continuation options.

You can review Gemini activity in your Google account settings. For device-level controls, manage permissions for the Gemini and Google apps under Settings, then Apps, then Permissions. Disable camera or microphone access if you want to limit the feature.

Screen sharing shows everything visible, including notifications that appear during the session. Dismiss sensitive alerts before starting if needed.

## Conclusion

Gemini Live with camera and screen sharing gives Android users a hands-free way to get visual context into conversations. Open Live, tap the camera or screen-share control, and describe what you need. The tools handle everyday tasks from identifying objects to walking through phone settings without requiring you to type long descriptions.

Start with one real item or one settings screen on your next use. Adjust permissions and notifications once, then the feature stays ready for quick help. For related setup steps, check the default assistant guide linked earlier.
