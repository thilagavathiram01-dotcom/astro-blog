Chrome on Android now includes a Gemini panel that can read the page you have open. You tap a spark icon, ask a question, and stay in the same tab. Google finished the U.S. rollout for eligible phones in August 2026.

This guide covers who can use it, how to open the panel, how summaries and connected apps work, and what auto browse will and will not do without your confirmation.

## What Gemini in Chrome does on Android

Gemini in Chrome is the browsing assistant built into Chrome, not a separate website at gemini.google.com. Google’s Chrome team describes it as a way to summarize long articles, answer questions about the current page, and reach Calendar, Keep, and Gmail without jumping apps.

On Android it opens as a sheet at the bottom of the screen. You can dock that sheet so it stays visible while you scroll. Page context is on by default; you can turn that off in Settings if you do not want the model to see the tab.

Google also added image tools in the same sheet, including Nano Banana for generating or editing images from the page you are viewing.

## Check that your phone qualifies

Google’s May 2026 Chrome post and later Android coverage list these launch conditions:

- Android 12 or higher
- At least 4 GB of RAM
- Chrome updated from Google Play
- Availability first in the United States
- English as the primary language for the first wave

Gemini in Chrome does not run in Incognito. Managed work or school accounts need an admin to allow it. If the icon is missing after an update, wait a day. Google used a staged server-side rollout after the June preview.

Update Chrome, then force-stop and reopen the app once. The spark control sits to the right of the address bar on many phones. On others it lives in the three-dot menu near the bottom of the toolbar.

## Open the Gemini sheet

1. Open Chrome and load any public webpage.
2. Tap the **Gemini** spark in the toolbar, or open the overflow menu and choose Gemini.
3. When the sheet rises, confirm the current page is attached as context (a chip or page title usually appears above the prompt box).
4. Drag the sheet down if you want a docked bar instead of a full overlay.

You can keep using the page while Gemini answers. That is the point of the in-browser panel: the tab stays put.

If you already use Gemini as the system assistant, long-pressing Power still opens the overlay on top of any app. Gemini in Chrome is different. It shares the full page, not only what is visible on screen, according to Google’s Chrome product pages.

## Summarize a page and ask follow-ups

Start with a long article, a product spec, or a policy page.

1. Open the page in Chrome.
2. Tap Gemini.
3. Use the **Summarize page** chip if it appears, or type “Summarize this page in five bullets.”
4. Ask a follow-up such as “What dates and prices are listed?” or “Explain the warranty section in plain language.”

Keep questions tied to the open document when you need accuracy. Gemini can still use Search and other Google services when you ask for comparisons, but the page itself is the first source for on-page questions.

Turn off page context in Chrome’s Gemini settings if you want a general chat that ignores the tab. Turn it back on before you ask about a form, recipe, or ticket confirmation.

## Connect Calendar, Keep, and Gmail

Google documents two related layers: tools inside the Chrome sheet, and **Personal Intelligence** from the Gemini app.

Typical in-browser tasks Google has shown:

- Add an event to Calendar from a concert or class page
- Drop recipe ingredients into Keep
- Find a related message in Gmail

To connect apps:

1. Open the **Gemini** app (or gemini.google.com on a signed-in account).
2. Open your profile, then connected apps or Personal Intelligence.
3. Allow Calendar, Keep, Gmail, and any other listed sources you actually use.
4. Return to Chrome and open the tools menu in the Gemini sheet. Enable Personal Intelligence if the toggle is off.

Only connect accounts you are willing to expose to Gemini prompts on that device. Disconnect anything you do not need.

If you travel often as a passenger and already tuned [Motion Assist on Android 17](/blog/android-17-motion-assist/), Gemini in Chrome is a better fit for reading long pages on that same ride. Summarize first, then scroll the source if a detail matters.

## Use auto browse without handing over the cart

**Auto browse** is the agentic mode. Google says it can scroll, tap, and enter text to finish a task you describe. Official examples include booking parking from a ticket email, updating a recurring pet-food order, and organizing travel details.

Auto browse at launch is for **Google AI Pro and AI Ultra** subscribers on Android in the U.S. Core summarize-and-ask features do not require those plans.

How a session usually runs:

1. State the goal in one sentence: “Find parking near this venue for Saturday 7 p.m.”
2. Watch the sheet and the page. Google shows a glow around the page and a status on the spark while the agent works.
3. Confirm when Chrome asks. Purchases, social posts, and other sensitive actions require an explicit tap from you.
4. Stop the run if the agent opens the wrong site or starts filling a form you did not request.

Google states that models are trained to detect known prompt-injection patterns and that auto browse is built to pause on sensitive steps. Treat that as a control, not a guarantee. Stay on the screen until the task ends.

Do not paste passwords into the Gemini prompt. Let Chrome’s password manager or passkeys fill login fields after you confirm the site.

## Settings worth changing on day one

Open Gemini settings from the sheet or Chrome’s overflow menu.

- **Page context** — leave on for research, off for general chat
- **Personal Intelligence** — only the apps you need
- **Toolbar shortcut** — Chrome may allow only one extra icon next to the address bar; pick Gemini if you use it more than another shortcut
- **Activity** — review Gemini Apps Activity in your Google account if you do not want long retention

Sign out of shared family phones before testing auto browse. The agent uses the signed-in Google account and any connected apps on that profile.

## Fixes when the spark never appears

**Chrome is current but there is no icon.** Confirm you are in the U.S. rollout region, on Android 12+, and not in Incognito. Reboot after the Play Store update.

**The sheet cannot see the page.** Leave Incognito. Reload a standard https page. Check that page context is enabled.

**Connected apps do nothing.** Re-authorize them in the Gemini app, then toggle Personal Intelligence in the Chrome sheet.

**Auto browse is missing.** You need an eligible AI Pro or Ultra subscription and the U.S. Android client. The free summarize path still works without that plan.

**Work profile blocks it.** Ask the admin whether Gemini in Chrome is allowed on managed accounts.

## What to use it for this week

Use the free sheet for pages you would otherwise screenshot and send to yourself: lease addenda, school PDFs opened in Chrome, long product manuals. Ask for dates, fees, and exclusions, then verify those lines on the page.

Save auto browse for repetitive web chores that already live in a browser: parking, subscription quantity changes, travel lookup. Keep a finger on the confirm button.

Gemini in Chrome will not replace a password manager, a banking app, or reading the checkout total yourself. Used that way, it cuts tab-switching without giving the model a blank check.
