Chrome on Android now has a browsing assistant that stays on the page you are already reading. Google finished the U.S. rollout of **Gemini in Chrome** on August 18, 2026. The same update opened **auto browse** for Google AI Pro and AI Ultra subscribers.

You tap a spark icon, ask a question about the tab in front of you, and stay in the browser. You do not need to jump to the Gemini app for a summary, a calendar event, or a Keep note.

This guide covers who can use it, how to open the panel, what to ask, how auto browse works, and how to keep agentic clicks under your control.

## What Google actually shipped

Google’s Chrome product post is specific. Gemini in Chrome on Android can:

- Summarize long articles
- Answer questions about the open page
- Connect to Google apps such as Calendar and Keep without leaving the tab
- Generate or customize images with Nano Banana

Auto browse is separate. It is the agentic layer for **AI Pro and AI Ultra** accounts in the United States. Google’s examples include booking parking for an event, updating a recurring online order, and organizing travel.

The May 2026 preview said the stack was built on **Gemini 3.1**. Rollout targets were **Android 12 or higher**, English (U.S.), and select devices. The August post says the assistant is now available to **all Android users in the U.S.** Auto browse remains a paid-plan feature.

Google also states the models are trained to detect known threats such as **prompt injection**. Auto browse is designed to ask for confirmation before some sensitive tasks, including purchases.

## Check that you can see the icon

Do this before you hunt through Chrome flags.

1. Update **Google Chrome** from the Play Store.
2. Confirm the phone is on **Android 12 or later**.
3. Sign in with the same Google Account you use for Gemini.
4. Set language to **English (United States)** if the spark icon is missing.
5. Open Chrome and look at the top bar for a **Gemini** spark, or open the **three-dot** menu.

9to5Google’s rollout notes match the official path: a toolbar shortcut to the right of the address bar, or the overflow menu. Tapping the icon slides up a sheet that looks close to the desktop side panel.

If the icon is still gone after an update, wait. Google rolls Chrome AI features in waves. Reboot once, then check again the next day.

## Open Gemini without leaving the page

1. Load the article, ticket page, or product listing you care about.
2. Tap the **Gemini** icon or choose it from the three-dot menu.
3. Confirm the sheet can use **this page as context**. You can turn that off in Settings if you do not want the URL or title sent with the prompt.
4. Ask a direct question: “Summarize this in five bullets,” “What is the cancellation policy,” or “Compare the two plans on this page.”
5. Drag the sheet down if you want it docked while you keep scrolling.

The panel can stay on screen while you change tabs. That is the point of putting the model in Chrome instead of a separate chat app.

Connected tools sit in the tools or Personal Intelligence menu. Enable Calendar, Keep, and other apps you actually use. Leave the rest off so the model does not pull extra account data.

## Prompts that work on a phone screen

Short, task-shaped prompts beat vague ones.

- “Summarize this article for someone who already knows the basics.”
- “Extract dates, prices, and addresses into a list.”
- “Add this event to Google Calendar with the time shown on the page.”
- “Save the ingredient list to Google Keep.”
- “Turn this section into a checklist I can screenshot.”

Nano Banana image tools are useful when a page has a product photo you want to restyle. Treat generated images as drafts. Check labels, logos, and numbers before you reuse them.

## How auto browse differs from a summary

A summary reads. Auto browse **acts**.

Google’s consumer examples are chores that normally take many taps:

- Reserve parking after you already have event details (Google cited SpotHero in the May preview)
- Change a standing grocery or pet-food order when a product size changes
- Research hotels and flights across date ranges, then start the booking flow

On desktop, Google also showed auto browse filling forms from a PDF and shopping from a reference photo. The Android post is narrower. Stick to the tasks Google named for phones until your account shows more tools.

Auto browse is for **AI Pro and AI Ultra** in the U.S. A free Gemini in Chrome session can still summarize and answer questions. It will not run the multi-step agent.

## Run an auto browse task safely

1. Open Chrome on a page that already holds the facts: a ticket email view, an order history page, or a travel tab set.
2. Open Gemini in Chrome.
3. State the goal and the limit in one sentence: “Find parking near this venue for Saturday 7 p.m. Stop before payment.”
4. Watch the plan. If the agent opens the wrong site, stop it.
5. Confirm only the step you intend. Google says auto browse asks before some sensitive actions. Do not tap through a purchase prompt on autopilot.
6. When the run ends, check the site yourself. Confirm dates, address, and price.

Keep the phone unlocked and on Wi-Fi for longer runs. Agent sessions fail when the tab is killed or the network drops mid-form.

Do not paste passwords into the chat. Chrome already has the signed-in session. If a site demands a fresh login, complete it yourself, then resume the task.

## Privacy and security settings to review

Google’s Chrome AI page says you can pause Gemini in Chrome, control what it can access, and delete history. Do that before you let an agent touch shopping or travel sites.

- Turn **page context** off when you are on a banking, health, or work-confidential URL.
- Review connected apps. Calendar and Keep are useful. Extra connectors add more surface area.
- Delete Gemini in Chrome activity after a sensitive research session.
- Treat auto browse confirmations as real authorizations, not decoration.

Prompt injection is the main new risk for agentic browsing. A page can hide instructions that try to steer the model. Google says it trains for known patterns. You still need to read the confirmation sheet before the agent submits a form.

## Troubleshooting

**No Gemini icon.** Update Chrome, use a U.S. account and English (U.S.), and wait out the staged rollout.

**The sheet ignores the page.** Enable page context. Reload the tab. Some PDF or app-web views send less text than a normal article.

**Calendar or Keep actions fail.** Open the tools menu and grant those connections again.

**Auto browse is missing.** Confirm an active AI Pro or AI Ultra subscription and a U.S. account. The free assistant does not include the agent.

**The agent stalls on a login wall or CAPTCHA.** Finish that step by hand. Then give a shorter follow-up prompt.

**It wants to buy something.** Decline, rewrite the prompt with “research only,” and complete checkout yourself.

## A simple weekly routine

Use summaries every day. Save auto browse for chores you already do the same way each week.

Monday: summarize three long posts and drop one deadline into Calendar.  
Midweek: ask Gemini to pull return windows from a store policy page.  
Weekend: if you subscribe, let auto browse draft parking or a travel outline, then you tap pay.

That split keeps the free tools useful and limits agentic browsing to tasks you can verify in under a minute.
