Google Arts & Culture turned 15 on 28 September 2026 and shipped a new mobile app the same day. The update puts conversational, visual search at the centre of the app, adds two AI experiments, and expands City Guide by 27 destinations.

You do not need a new account to try it. Update the app on Android or iPhone, open the home feed, and ask a plain-language question. Answers come back with images and stories drawn from partner archives, not a blank chat box.

The platform already works with more than 3,000 cultural institutions and artists in more than 90 countries. The new app is meant to make that archive easier to browse on a phone.

## What changed in the 15-year app update

Amit Sood, VP of Google Arts & Culture, said the goal has been to keep art and culture accessible while building experiences that educate and inspire. The 28 September post lists three product changes:

- Conversational search that pairs an explanation with visuals and curated partner stories.
- A single home feed with daily stories, highlights, and shortcuts to interactive tools.
- Two experiments, Chrono Look and Dial-an-Artist, plus a larger City Guide.

The rollout started globally on 28 September 2026. Get the app from Google Play (`com.google.android.apps.cultural`) or the Apple App Store. Web collections at artsandculture.google.com remain available if the store update has not reached your device yet.

## Ask a visual question in conversational search

The search box accepts everyday wording. Google’s own examples include Caravaggio’s use of light and shadow, Impressionist museums in Paris, images from the Webb telescope, what bento boxes are, and cats in art. Use those as patterns, then swap in your own subject.

1. Open Google Arts & Culture and update it if the store shows a pending version.
2. From the home feed, open search.
3. Type a specific question, not a single keyword. “Impressionist museums in Paris” is better than “Impressionism”.
4. Read the written answer, then open the attached images and partner stories.
5. Follow a story into the institution page if you want the full zoomable artwork or a 3D artifact.

Good prompts name a place, a technique, or an object. “Show textile patterns from West Africa used in contemporary fashion” is more useful than “textiles”. If the first answer is broad, add a century or a city and ask again.

Treat the reply as a starting point. Partner stories are curated by museums and cultural groups. The model’s summary should point you at those sources, not replace the object record.

If you already use on-screen search on a phone, the same habit applies here: point the question at something you can see. Our [Circle to Search guide](/blog/circle-to-search-android/) covers that gesture on Android. Arts & Culture search stays inside the app and returns archive material instead of a general web result.

## Use the new home feed

The homepage is no longer a stack of separate sections you have to hunt for. Google describes one feed built for short daily visits. Modular blocks surface short cultural stories, daily highlights, and direct links to experiments.

Check these blocks on a first visit:

- Daily cultural moments and trending stories.
- Exhibitions and events near you, when location is available.
- Shortcuts into Chrono Look, Dial-an-Artist, and City Guide.

Scroll once, then pick one block. The feed is designed for a few minutes, not a full catalogue browse. If you want a deep dive into one collection, open the partner page from a story rather than staying on the home feed.

## Try Chrono Look

Chrono Look is an AI experiment that lets you virtually try on historical attire from different eras. Google says the output includes sketches and realistic renderings. It lives at artsandculture.google.com/camera/chrono-look and is linked from the app home feed.

1. Open Chrono Look from the home feed or the camera experiment URL.
2. Allow camera access only if you want a try-on view. You can still browse era examples if you skip the camera.
3. Pick an era or region. Google says attire spans eras and regions, not a single costume set.
4. Compare the sketch view with the realistic rendering so you can see what was inferred.
5. Stop if the clothing label does not name a source garment. Experiments are for exploration, not museum labels.

Do not treat a rendering as a reconstruction of a specific surviving garment unless the screen cites one. Historical dress varied by class, climate, and occasion. Use Chrono Look to notice silhouette and material, then open a partner textile or fashion story for the documented piece.

## Open Dial-an-Artist

Dial-an-Artist is the second experiment. Google describes it as simulated conversations with historical figures about their work. The public page is artsandculture.google.com/dial-artist.

1. Open Dial-an-Artist from the home feed.
2. Choose a figure the experiment lists. Do not assume every artist is available.
3. Ask about a specific work, method, or commission, not a vague “tell me your life story”.
4. Cross-check any claim against the partner story or a catalogue entry before you quote it.

Simulated dialogue can sound confident and still invent detail. Google presents this as an interactive tool for exploring creative journeys, not as a primary source. If you are writing a school note or a caption, cite the museum record, not the chat.

## Browse the expanded City Guide

City Guide now includes 27 newly added destinations with local cultural landmarks and real-time cultural events. The guide sits at artsandculture.google.com/city-guide.

1. Open City Guide from the home feed.
2. Pick a newly added city rather than a capital you already know.
3. Note landmarks first, then check the events list if you are travelling soon.
4. Open each landmark’s partner page before you plan a visit. Hours and tickets still come from the institution.

“Real-time” here means the events module can refresh. It does not guarantee every listing is current on the day you arrive. Confirm on the venue site.

## Tips before you rely on an answer

- Prefer questions that name a technique, place, or object. Vague prompts return broad essays.
- Open the partner story under the answer. That is the sourced layer.
- Chrono Look and Dial-an-Artist are experiments. Label them that way if you share a screenshot.
- Camera try-on is optional. Deny camera permission if you only want archive browsing.
- City Guide events can change. Check the venue before you travel.
- The archive covers more than paintings: fashion, performance, sport, craft, food heritage, natural history, science, design, and nature.

## Conclusion

The September 2026 Arts & Culture app is a search and discovery update, not a new museum. Update the app, ask one specific visual question, then open Chrono Look or Dial-an-Artist only after you know where the partner story sits. City Guide’s 27 new destinations are the practical add-on if you are planning a visit.

Start with a question you can check, such as a named painting or a city museum list. If the visuals match the partner page, keep going. If they do not, follow the institution link and ignore the summary.
