Google Photos published five library tools on 24 September 2026. The list covers Photos inside Gemini Spark, the Wardrobe digital closet, Moods film styles, 15 new Remix templates, and a markup upgrade with a redact pen.

This guide follows the official Photos Help article for Wardrobe and the product blog for Moods, Remix, and markup. Availability still depends on country, plan, and app version. Check the footnotes on Google’s post before you hunt for a missing tab.

## What Google shipped in September 2026

**Photos in Gemini Spark** lets eligible Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the U.S. select, brighten, and share portraits with one prompt. Spark is a separate agent surface. For the Photos connection itself, start with our [Gemini Spark and Google Photos guide](/blog/gemini-spark-google-photos/).

**Wardrobe** catalogs clothing that already appears in your backed-up photos. Google’s April 2026 launch post described category filters, outfit moodboards, and a virtual try-on. The September post says Wardrobe is now available to eligible Android and iOS users in the United States, India, and Brazil.

**Moods** applies vintage-style looks that adapt to the photo, including 35mm film and 2000s digicam. Google lists Moods as available in Google Photos on Android worldwide.

**Remix** adds 15 templates such as red-carpet frames, birthday cards, and photo-booth shots. Remix on Android and iOS is limited to select countries and users 18 and older.

**Markup** on Android adds a redact pen to blur sensitive details, thickness sliders for sketches, and new fonts.

## Check Wardrobe eligibility first

Google Photos Help lists hard requirements. You need an eligible Google Account that meets the minimum age for your country. You must be in Brazil, India, or the United States.

Turn on Face Groups and mark which face is yours. Without that, Photos cannot tell which person to scan for clothing.

Unless you have a Google AI Pro or Ultra plan, Help also requires more than 1,000 photos of yourself. Android users need Android 10 or later. Eligible people get an in-app notice when the tab appears. Do not assume a missing tile is a bug on day one of a rollout.

Wardrobe use is covered by Google’s Terms of Service and the Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy.

## Build the wardrobe

1. Open the **Google Photos** app on your phone.
2. Tap **Collections** at the bottom.
3. Open **Wardrobe**, then tap **Build my wardrobe**.
4. Wait. Photos scans pictures of you from the last four years and tags garments it finds.
5. Open the notification when the scan finishes.

After the build, open **Collections → Wardrobe**. You can view items one by one, sort by recently worn, and filter by type such as tops, bottoms, and skirts. Jewelry is one of the category examples in the April product post.

To remove a bad detection:

1. Open **Collections → Wardrobe → Items**.
2. Tap the item, then **Delete**.

That does not delete the original photo. It only drops the garment card from the closet.

## Create an outfit and try it on

Help limits a saved outfit to **six items**.

1. Open **Collections → Wardrobe**.
2. Tap **Create** at the bottom.
3. Select the pieces.
4. Tap **Save**, name the outfit or tap **Skip**, then save again.

You can share an item or an outfit from that screen.

For the virtual try-on:

1. Open **Collections → Wardrobe → Outfits**.
2. Pick a saved look, or tap **Create** and choose pieces.
3. Tap **Try it on**.
4. **Save** the video, **Share** it, or **Regenerate** if the preview is wrong.

You can also tap **Try it on** while you are still assembling pieces. Help notes that saved outfits and try-on outputs count toward your Google Account storage.

Google’s product post also mentions moodboards for occasions such as work or a trip. Use those boards to group saved looks, not as a replacement for the six-item outfit limit.

## Apply Moods on Android

Moods lives in the editor, not in Collections.

1. Update Google Photos from Play Store.
2. Open a still photo.
3. Tap **Edit**.
4. Look for **Moods** (or a styles row that lists film and digicam looks).
5. Preview, then save a copy if you want the original untouched.

Google describes Moods as styles that adapt to the image rather than a single global filter. If the control is missing, you are likely on iOS or on an older Android build. The September blog lists Moods as Android-only and global.

## Use the new Remix templates

Remix is a generative template, not a color grade.

1. Open a photo of a person you are allowed to edit.
2. Open the overflow menu and tap **Remix** (or **Create → Remix**, depending on the build).
3. Pick one of the new templates: red carpet, birthday card, photo booth, or another listed style.
4. Generate, review faces and text, then save only if the result is usable.

Remix requires you to be 18 or older in a supported country. Do not run templates on photos of people who did not agree to a stylized share.

## Blur private details with markup

The September markup update is rolling out on Android worldwide.

1. Open the photo.
2. Tap **Edit**, then the markup or draw tools.
3. Use the **redact pen** on plates, badges, school names, or documents in the frame.
4. Adjust stroke thickness if you sketch arrows or circles.
5. Add text with the new fonts only after the private bits are already blurred.
6. Save a copy.

Markup is the right tool when you want to post a snapshot without publishing an address or a child’s school logo. It is not a substitute for removing the photo from shared albums.

## Tips that keep the closet honest

- Photograph outfits in even light if you want cleaner garment cards. Wardrobe only sees what already exists in the last four years of photos of you.
- Delete mis-tagged items before you build outfits. One raincoat labeled as a dress will pollute every try-on.
- Treat try-on video as a preview. Fabric drape and exact color can still miss.
- Watch storage. Help is explicit that outfits and try-on outputs use quota.
- Keep Face Groups on only if you accept people grouping. Wardrobe will not run without it.
- Use Spark when the job is “pick the sharp family portraits from Saturday.” Use Wardrobe when the job is “what jackets do I already own.”

## Conclusion

Start with eligibility, Face Groups, and **Build my wardrobe**. Filter by type, cap outfits at six pieces, and run **Try it on** only after you delete junk cards. Apply Moods in the Android editor for film looks. Use Remix templates for stylized shares if you are 18+ in a supported country. Blur private text with the new redact pen before you post.

If Spark is on your plan, send it the portrait-culling prompt after the closet is built. The two tools share a library. They do not replace each other.
