Pixel Journal is Google’s first-party diary on Pixel 8 and later phones, including Fold models. It stores entries on the device, can attach photos and maps, and can pull activity from Health Connect. Official Pixel Phone Help also documents Auto format: it turns messy typed or dictated text into a cleaner page after you finish writing.

The August 2026 Journal update (app version 2026.07.27.x) added that formatter, Health Connect sleep permission, daily routine reminders, and live map search. AI extras such as reflections and inspiration run on Pixel 9 and later, including Fold. They are **not** available on Pixel 9a or Pixel 10a, according to the same Help page.

This guide covers first-run setup, a normal entry, Auto format, sleep permission, and how to shut AI off if you only want a locked notebook.

## What you need

- A **Pixel 8 or newer** (including Fold). Install **Journal** from Play Store if it is missing.
- For Auto format, reflections, Deep dive, and Mood ID: a **Pixel 9 or newer except 9a and 10a**, a signed-in Google Account, and an age above the digital consent threshold in your country.
- AI language support listed by Google: **English, German, and Japanese**.
- Optional: Health Connect sources (Pixel Watch, Google Health / Fitbit, or another sleep tracker that writes to Health Connect).

The app is a journaling tool, not therapy. Google’s Help page states it is not a substitute for professional mental health care and that AI insights can be wrong or irrelevant.

## First launch

1. Open **Journal** from the app drawer.
2. Decide whether to sign in. A Google Account is required for optional cloud backup and for AI features. You can journal without signing in; you then lose backup and AI.
3. Pick a goal if you want direction: Mindfulness, Daily reflections, Creativity, Health & Fitness, Personal growth, Productivity, or Life story. You can skip and set this later in settings.
4. Grant only the extras you want: **Photos** for memory prompts, **Health Connect** for activity and sleep-based inspiration, notifications for reminders.
5. Choose a lock: none, or the same PIN / pattern / biometrics as the screen lock.

Backup lives under Profile picture → **Journal settings** → **Back up your journal**. Leave it off if the diary should never leave the phone.

## Write or dictate an entry

1. Open Journal. The default screen is compose.
2. Tap **Draft** / **Write** and type, or use Gboard’s microphone.
3. Add media from the attach menu: take a photo or video, pick from Photos, drop in a Health Connect activity, or attach a location / live map.
4. Optional: tap **Reflect** for writing prompts. Refresh the Inspiration sheet if the first batch is stale, then close it when you are ready to write.
5. Use **Font & Color** if you want a different typeface or theme. A Customization path also offers **Compact** or **Full** media layout for photos in the entry.
6. Tap **Save**.

Gboard can send dictation through Google’s servers before the text lands in Journal, depending on keyboard settings. If that matters, review Gboard Help and prefer typing for sensitive pages.

Entries of **five words or more** can receive a personalized Reflection when AI is on. Mood ID may attach an emoji; Google describes Mood ID as a reflection tool, not a diagnosis.

## Auto format: Simple vs Expressive

Auto format is the practical part of the August 2026 changelog. Pixel Help describes it as transforming unstructured text into a clean, readable document. In the app it sits behind an icon at the top of the compose screen after you have written or spoken a draft.

1. Finish the draft. Do not format by hand first.
2. Tap **Auto format**.
3. Choose **Simple** for short cleanup (paragraphs, light structure) or **Expressive** for a more styled page.
4. Review the result. Keep it only if it still sounds like you. Undo or revert if the model invented headings you do not want.
5. Save.

App strings reported during the rollout said each mode requires a minimum word count. If the button stays disabled, add a few more sentences and try again.

Auto format uses **Private AI Compute**: encrypted processing in an isolated environment on device and in the cloud. Google’s consumer copy for Journal says no one else, including Google staff, can read that payload, that entries are not sold for ads, and that personal journal text is not used to train generative models. You can still turn **AI Features** off in Journal settings if you do not want cloud-assisted formatting.

## Connect sleep data from Health Connect

Sleep is a separate Android permission, not the same toggle as steps or workouts.

1. Long-press the Journal icon → **App info** → **Permissions** → **Health, fitness and wellness**.
2. Turn on **Sleep**.
3. Confirm Health Connect is receiving nights from your watch or Google Health. Open Health Connect (or Google Health) and check that Journal is listed as a reader for sleep.
4. In a new Journal entry, attach Health data if the composer offers it, or wait for sleep-aware writing inspiration after a recorded night.

No sleep rows? The tracker must write Sleep sessions into Health Connect. A Pixel Watch or Fitbit-class device in the Google Health app is the usual source. Journal will not invent a sleep score by staring at the phone on your nightstand.

Supervised accounts and users under the age of digital consent cannot link Health Connect to Journal and do not get AI-generated content.

## Reminders, Insights, and maps

The August 2026 build can nudge you on a custom daily routine: roughly **7:00**, **14:00**, and **21:00**. Turn notifications on during setup or later in Journal settings if those times help and ignore them if they do not.

**Insights** groups goals, history, and stats (active hours, entry count, longest page). Use it as a calendar of moods, not a clinical chart.

**Live Map** search lets you pin where an entry happened. Grant location only when you want that stamp.

## Privacy switches worth setting once

- **AI Features** off: past pages stay; new reflections, inspiration, and Auto format stop.
- Permissions: Photos, location, and Health Connect can be revoked in system Settings without wiping the diary.
- Backup: optional. Leaving it off keeps entries on-device only.
- Lock: use the screen lock if other people pick up the phone.

If you are in crisis, Journal is not monitored in real time. In the United States, Pixel Help still points people to the 988 / Lifeline network. Elsewhere, use a local crisis line.

## Conclusion

On Pixel 8 and newer, Journal is a locked notebook that can attach photos, maps, and Health Connect. On eligible Pixel 9 and later phones (not 9a or 10a), Auto format will tidy a rambling draft after you write, and a Sleep permission lets nights from Health Connect show up as context. Start with a short entry, run Simple format once so you know what it changes, then decide whether backup and AI stay on.
