Google rolled out Guided Learning globally in Google Classroom on November 3, 2026. The same day it announced a Search pilot and new default-off controls for K-12 student accounts. The feature coaches students through problems with questions and step-by-step prompts instead of delivering finished answers.

This matches Google’s stated goal of supporting “productive struggle.” Students stay challenged without becoming stuck. Research from a University of Tokyo trial and a UK school pilot shows measurable gains when the tool is used this way.

![Students gathered around a laptop in a classroom](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1758270705172-07b53627dfcb?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What Guided Learning does

Guided Learning first appeared in the Gemini app. It asks open-ended questions, breaks tasks into smaller steps, and adapts explanations. It can also include diagrams or short practice items. The goal is active engagement rather than passive copying.

In Classroom the experience is teacher-led. Teachers assign an activity anchored in their own materials. The AI then works one-to-one with each student while staying on the assigned topic. Teachers receive real-time signals about where the class is struggling.

In Search the same idea appears for student accounts. When the system detects a homework-style request—such as a pasted physics problem or a request for help writing a book report—it surfaces guiding prompts instead of a direct answer. A pilot begins in select U.S. schools later in November 2026, with expansion planned after educator feedback.

## How to assign Guided Learning in Classroom

Teachers need Classroom and Gemini enabled by their administrator. The steps are straightforward once access is on:

1. Open classroom.google.com and go to the target class.
2. Choose Classwork, then Create, then Assignment.
3. Under Attach, select Guided Learning.
4. Name the activity and add source material (Google recommends one primary source; up to five are allowed). You can pick from class materials or upload a file.
5. Assign it. Students open the activity and work through the guided session.

Students see questions that push them to reason. If they go off-topic, Focus Mode redirects them back to the teacher’s objectives. Teachers can review interaction data to spot common sticking points before the next lesson.

A high-school math teacher in Colombia said the tool “provided a personalized approach to learning and encouraged critical thinking rather than simply providing immediate answers.” Similar feedback came from pilots in India and Thailand.

## Research behind the feature

Google published two studies on the same day. In a randomized trial with more than 300 University of Tokyo students learning statistics, the group using only standard web tools improved scores by 5.3 percentage points after a half-day session. The group that also used Guided Learning improved by 8.1 points—roughly 1.5 times the gain.

A four-week UK pilot with the Ark school network across three secondary schools and four subjects found students felt comfortable asking for help when stuck. More than half said the tool helped them solve problems on their own. Teachers gained clearer visibility into progress.

These results are early and limited in scope. Google notes that insights can contain mistakes and are not intended for formal assessment or placement decisions.

## Search pilot and school controls

The Search version starts as a limited U.S. pilot this month. Systems look for homework-help patterns and insert step-by-step coaching. Google plans to expand after collecting educator feedback.

At the same time, access rules change for K-12 student accounts. Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search will be entirely off until a school administrator explicitly turns them on. Google will share more details with schools in the coming months before the controls take full effect (expected early 2027).

Chris Phillips, Google’s VP and General Manager of Education, said Search and AI “are deeply integrated.” Schools that want no AI features at all will not have student Search access; students see a message naming their school. Google later clarified that administrators can turn services on individually, and that Search for schools will offer Guided Learning modes so students do not receive quick answers to schoolwork.

Administrators therefore decide whether students get Search with AI Overviews and chat, Search with Guided Learning, or neither. The default is off for all four products.

## Tips for teachers and administrators

Start with a single short activity tied to one clear source. This keeps the session focused and makes the insight data easier to interpret.

Review the teacher dashboard after the first few sessions. Look for questions that repeatedly stall students; those become natural points for whole-class discussion.

Combine Guided Learning with other grounded tools. Students can add free textbooks inside Gemini Notebook for cited answers, then use a Classroom Guided Learning activity that references the same material. See our guide on [adding free textbooks to Gemini Notebook](/blog/add-free-textbooks-gemini-notebook/) for the catalog steps.

Administrators should map the new toggles against existing acceptable-use policies before the 2027 defaults arrive. Test the Search pilot if your district is invited so you can give Google concrete feedback on the coaching prompts.

## What to expect next

Classroom access is live worldwide for schools that have enabled the underlying Gemini features. The Search pilot remains limited. The default-off controls for K-12 accounts roll out after Google provides additional administrator documentation.

Schools that already use Gemini in Classroom can begin assigning Guided Learning activities immediately. Others will wait for the administrator opt-in. The design choice is deliberate: the tool is meant to coach rather than complete work, and access stays under school control.
