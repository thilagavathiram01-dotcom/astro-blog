Rambler turns spoken thoughts into clean text on a Pixel 11. Google built it into Gboard and powered it with Gemini. You speak at a normal pace, including pauses and filler words, and the feature returns structured sentences with proper punctuation.

It is available only on Pixel 11 series devices right now. Google lists the Pixel 11, Pixel 11 Pro, Pixel 11 Pro XL, and Pixel 11 Pro Fold as supported. Older Pixels and other Android phones do not show the option in official Gboard builds.

## What you need before you start

Confirm three things on your Pixel 11:

- The latest Gboard version is installed and set as the default keyboard.
- Microphone permission is granted to Gboard.
- You have an internet connection for the full set of rewrite and style features. Basic cleanup works offline.

Open the Play Store, search for Gboard, and update if a newer version is available. Then go to Settings > System > Languages & input > On-screen keyboard and set Gboard as default. Grant the microphone permission when prompted, or open the app info page and enable it under Permissions.

## Enable Rambler on your Pixel 11

Open any app that accepts text, such as Messages or Gmail. Tap a text field so Gboard appears. Tap the microphone icon in the top right of the keyboard.

The first time you do this, an introduction dialog appears. Tap Start to turn Rambler on. If the dialog does not appear, open the keyboard menu, tap Settings, then Voice typing, and select Rambler.

Return to the text field and tap the microphone again. A glowing animation appears around the microphone to show that Rambler is listening. Speak naturally. You do not need to say “comma” or “period.”

When you finish, tap Done or release the microphone if you are holding it. The cleaned text appears in the field. There is no live preview while you speak. The feature processes the full input after you stop.

You can switch back to standard voice typing at any time. Open Gboard Settings > Voice typing and choose Standard voice typing.

## Edit and rewrite with voice commands

Rambler accepts follow-up voice commands to change tone or content. After the first draft appears, tap the microphone again and speak a request such as “Make this sound more professional” or “Make this shorter and more direct.”

You can also ask for specific changes: “Change Saturday to Sunday” or “Add a sushi emoji.” The feature understands natural phrasing, so you do not need fixed command phrases.

Supported languages include English, Spanish, French, German, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, Arabic, Italian, and others. You can mix languages in one sentence and Rambler will handle the switch.

Offline mode keeps basic cleanup, punctuation, and capitalization. Advanced rewrites require a network connection. If the connection drops mid-session, a Retry button appears so you can process the input offline.

## Practical tips for better results

Speak at a normal conversational pace. Long pauses are fine because Rambler removes filler words such as “um” and “uh.” Review the output before you send it, especially for formal messages, because the feature can misinterpret intent.

Use it for longer notes, emails, or meeting summaries where structure matters more than word-for-word accuracy. For short replies, standard voice typing may feel faster because it shows text as you speak.

Rambler processes audio and text temporarily on Google servers and deletes the data after delivery. It also blocks certain harmful requests and shows a safety message instead of generating the content.

Usage limits exist to keep the service available for everyone. If you hit a limit, switch to standard voice typing until it resets.

## What to do if Rambler does not appear

Update Gboard first. Confirm it is the default keyboard and that microphone permission is set to “Allow only while using the app.” Restart the phone if the option is still missing after an update.

Rambler is limited to Pixel 11 series hardware at launch. Third-party modifications exist that unlock the feature on other devices, but they involve sideloading modified APKs. Those methods carry security risks and may stop working after future Gboard updates. Stick to the official path on a supported Pixel 11.

## Conclusion

Rambler gives Pixel 11 owners a practical way to turn spoken thoughts into usable text without perfect dictation habits. Enable it once in Gboard, speak naturally, and use voice follow-ups for style or content changes. The combination of filler-word removal and natural rewrite commands makes longer writing tasks faster on the phone.

Check the official Pixel support page for the latest language list and any region restrictions before you rely on it for daily work.
