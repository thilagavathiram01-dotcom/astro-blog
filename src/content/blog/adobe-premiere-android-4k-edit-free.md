Adobe Premiere mobile is on the Google Play Store, and the core editor does not ask for a Creative Cloud login. Adobe announced the Android release on 22 September 2026, about a year after the iPhone app. You can cut clips, layer tracks, and export up to 4K without a watermark.

This guide covers the official requirements, a first project, audio cleanup, and the limits Adobe has already published. If you also remix stills into short clips, the [Google Photos video remix guide](/blog/google-photos-video-remix-gemini-omni/) covers a different path that starts from your camera roll.

## Check the phone before you install

Adobe's technical requirements page lists two hard floors. The device must run Android 13 or later, and it must have at least 5 GB of RAM. Phones and tablets are both supported. Phones are limited to portrait orientation. Tablets can use portrait or landscape.

A network connection is not required to start a local edit. Wi-Fi or cellular (4G minimum, 5G recommended) is required for creative asset libraries, generative AI, and other cloud features. The app is listed in the Play Store as `com.adobe.premiere`.

Premiere Rush, the older mobile editor, is not the app you want. Wired reported that Adobe will stop supporting Rush on 30 September 2026, and the new Premiere mobile app is the successor.

## Install Premiere and start a project

1. Open Google Play and search for Adobe Premiere. Confirm the publisher is Adobe before you install.
2. Open the app. Adobe says the free editor does not force a login or an upgrade prompt while you create and edit.
3. Start a new project and import video or stills from the device. Adobe Help documents import as the first step for a new project, and you can insert more media into an existing project later.
4. Choose an aspect ratio that matches the destination. The app is built for vertical YouTube Shorts, and Adobe's Android overview lists Shorts creation as a core feature. You can still change the frame if the clip is meant for a wider player.
5. Trim, split, rearrange, duplicate, or delete clips on the timeline. Help articles also cover flip, replace, and moving a clip from the main track to an overlay track.

The multi-track timeline is the reason this app is closer to a desktop editor than a single-strip trimmer. Adobe's launch post says you can layer video, music, voiceovers, text, and effects in one view, with frame-accurate cuts.

## Clean audio and add a voiceover

Street noise ruins a lot of phone footage. Premiere's Enhance Audio tool reduces background noise and improves speech clarity in one tap. Adobe describes it for interviews, voiceovers, and other recordings.

After the cleanup, you can raise, lower, or mute clip volume, add an audio fade, and extract audio from a video clip so the picture and sound can be edited apart. You can also record a voiceover in the app, pull music from the in-app library, or add an Adobe Stock soundtrack. Your own audio files can be imported from the phone.

Export stays free and watermark-free up to 4K, according to Adobe's 22 September post. Generative tools are the paid layer, not the timeline itself.

## Use templates, effects, and titles

Creator-made templates are the fastest way to a finished short. Drop clips into a template, then edit the template text. If you prefer a blank timeline, add effects and transitions, fit or fill the frame, change opacity, and apply look presets, vignette, or grain.

Titles, text styles, color, layout, and text animations are documented in Premiere mobile Help. Custom fonts can be imported from third-party apps. Adobe Fonts support is on the coming-soon list in the Android launch post, so do not assume the full Adobe Fonts catalog is on the phone yet.

Direct export to YouTube Shorts is part of the Android pitch. Build the vertical cut in Premiere, then publish without a second editor if the Shorts path is available in your region.

## What stays behind a login or a credit balance

Firefly tools need an Adobe account and generative credits. Adobe's launch post names Generative Fill, Image-to-Video, and Generate Sound Effects. Help also documents generate image, generate stickers, expand image, and partner models. Those are cloud features. Skip them if you only need cuts, titles, and a 4K export.

Adobe's Android announcement lists several items as coming soon: Adobe Fonts, enhanced captions, keyframes, cropping, beat detection, and continued editing on Premiere desktop. Treat that list as the current boundary. Help pages shared with the iPhone app describe some of those tools, but the Android launch post is the source that says they are not all shipping on day one.

## Practical tips before a long edit

Free space matters more than the 5 GB RAM floor once you import 4K clips. Copy source files locally before you leave Wi-Fi, then edit offline. Cloud libraries and Firefly calls can wait.

Enhance Audio first, then set levels. Cleaning noise after you have already ducked music makes the speech harder to balance.

Keep one project per destination. A Shorts cut and a 16:9 cut from the same rushes should be separate projects so a portrait reframing does not overwrite the wide timeline.

On a foldable or a tablet, use the extra screen for the timeline. Adobe's Android overview says the interface adapts to the workflow, and phones stay portrait-only.

## Conclusion

Premiere on Android is a free multi-track editor with 4K export, no watermark, and no required login for basic cuts. Check Android 13 and 5 GB of RAM, import local media, clean speech with Enhance Audio, and export. Leave Firefly and the coming-soon desktop handoff for later if you do not need them.
