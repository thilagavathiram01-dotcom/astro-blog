Google Meet now supports real-time speech translation in 63 languages. Powered by Gemini 3.5 Live Translate, the feature detects the language being spoken and translates it into each participant's preferred language with matching captions.

This removes the old limit of one language pair per meeting. Teams can mix languages freely without pre-configuring pairs. The update rolled out starting October 22, 2026, for eligible plans on web, Android, and iOS.

![Person on a video call with laptop](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1760346546839-aced24accdff?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What changed with the 63-language expansion

Previously, speech translation required selecting one language pair for the entire meeting, such as English and Spanish. Only a small set of pairs were supported.

Now, Meet automatically detects the spoken language and translates it for each listener independently. Participants can join from different regions and hear everything in their own language. Captions stay synchronized with the translated audio.

Google notes that translations lag by a few seconds to ensure completeness. No audio is stored, and models are not trained on your voice.

## Eligible plans and limits

Speech translation requires an eligible plan for the meeting host (or at least one participant in 1:1 calls):

- Google AI Pro and Ultra
- Google Workspace Business Standard and Plus
- Enterprise Standard and Plus
- Frontline Plus
- Google AI Pro for Education
- AI Expanded Access and AI Ultra Access

Usage limits are 90 minutes per meeting for most plans and up to 8 hours for AI Expanded Access, AI Ultra Access, and Google AI Ultra. The feature is unavailable in live streams, recordings, client-side encrypted meetings, dial-in calls, or when using adaptive audio.

For Workspace, the admin setting is on by default when Gemini for Meet is enabled. Admins can disable it at the organizational unit or group level.

## How to turn on speech translation

### On computer (web)

1. Join or start a Google Meet call.
2. At the bottom, hover over the captions and translation button.
3. Click **Translate speech**.
4. On first use, select the language you want to hear, then click next.
5. Choose **Translate me** if you want others to hear a translation of your speech in a voice like yours.

A badge appears showing the feature is active. Other participants receive a prompt to enable it for themselves.

### On Android or iOS

Update the Google Meet app first. Then:

1. In the meeting, tap the more options (three dots) at the bottom right.
2. Select captions and translation.
3. Turn on Translate speech.
4. Set your preferred listening language and decide whether to allow your voice to be translated.

The controls are individual. Each person enables translation for their own audio stream.

## Tips for better results

Wait until the translation indicator on a speaker's tile disappears before responding. This avoids talking over the delayed audio.

Combine it with captions for an extra reference if the audio is unclear. Rephrase if a translation seems off.

Ensure a stable network connection. Poor connectivity can reduce quality. Google lists common issues such as grammatical errors, unexpected accents, or incorrect gender on nouns—normal for real-time systems.

You can turn off translation of your own voice at any time in the speech translation settings. Other participants must also enable the feature to hear translations of you.

## Limitations and privacy

Conference room hardware supports listening only; speech from the room is not translated for remote participants. Users under 18 cannot use the feature. Some regions may have restrictions.

Google states it does not save voice data or train models on it. You control whether your speech is translated.

Real-time speech translation in Google Meet now covers 63 languages with automatic detection. Eligible users can enable it during any meeting on web or mobile with a few clicks. Start with a short test call to confirm the languages and audio quality work for your team.
