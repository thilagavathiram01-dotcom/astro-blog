Complex topics often require hours of open tabs and scattered notes. Gemini Deep Research handles the browsing, synthesis, and citation work so you can focus on decisions instead of collection.

This guide covers the exact steps from official Google documentation. It works on the web at gemini.google.com and in the Gemini app. Free users can run reports with the Thinking model. Google AI Pro and Ultra unlock higher limits and the Pro model.

## When Deep Research Helps Most

Use it when a question needs multiple sources and structured output. Market scans, competitor comparisons, literature reviews, and purchase decisions fit well. A simple fact check does not. The tool shines when you would otherwise open dozens of pages.

Google describes it as an agent that plans, searches, and synthesizes. You review the plan before it runs. This keeps the report on target.

## Start a Deep Research Report

1. Open gemini.google.com and sign in with a Google account. You must be 18 or older.
2. In the text box, click Tools, then select Deep Research.
3. Optional: Click Add files to upload documents or images. Click Sources to include Gmail, Drive, or a Gemini Notebook. Google Search is on by default. Deselect it if you want only your own material. Workspace apps appear only after you connect them.
4. Type a clear prompt. State the decision, audience, geography, time window, and desired output format.
5. Click Submit. Gemini builds a research plan.
6. Read the plan. Click Edit plan to add missing angles or remove irrelevant ones.
7. Click Start research. The process usually takes 5–10 minutes. You can leave the tab. Gemini notifies you when the report is ready.

On mobile, the flow is similar inside the Gemini app. Higher limits apply on paid plans.

## Write Better Prompts

Vague prompts produce general essays. Specific ones produce usable reports. Include:

- The exact question or decision
- Who will read the report
- Time range and region
- Preferred sources or exclusions
- Output structure (executive summary, comparison table, recommendations)

Example: “Research the market for cloud accounting tools for small businesses in the UK in 2026. Cover main providers, pricing models, common customer complaints from reviews, and gaps a new entrant could fill. Cite reputable sources and note dates.”

Edit the plan if it misses a competitor or overweights one source type. A few minutes here saves a full rewrite later.

## Review and Use the Report

Open the finished report. Use the contents outline to jump to sections. Citations link back to sources. Check key claims against the originals when accuracy matters.

Ask follow-up questions in the same chat. Gemini can answer from the research already done or search again. You can also request additions, such as cost details or a specific comparison.

Export options include Markdown, PDF, or Canvas formats. Some plans let you turn the report into an infographic, quiz, or audio overview.

## Tips for Reliable Results

- Connect Google Workspace if you want Gmail or Drive included. This is useful for internal context.
- Prefer primary sources. Ask the plan to prioritize official docs, recent studies, or company filings.
- Set limits on free tiers. Paid plans raise the number of reports you can generate.
- Combine with Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) for source-grounded follow-up. Upload the report or related files there for deeper analysis.
- Always verify critical numbers and quotes. The tool cites sources, but humans confirm them.

For developers, the Gemini API offers Deep Research agents via the Interactions API. You can run background tasks and poll for results. See the official Gemini API docs for code samples.
