Android 17 QPR2 arrived on Pixel phones in November 2026 and added practical customization tools that many users had wanted for years. You can now pick any system accent color with a slider and decide exactly which icons appear in the status bar.

This guide shows the exact steps on a supported Pixel device running the stable QPR2 update.

## Confirm the Update Is Installed

The new options only appear after Android 17 QPR2. Open Settings, tap System, then System update, and check for the November 2026 build. Supported devices start with the Pixel 6a and run through the latest Pixel 11 series.

If you are still on a beta, opt out of the Android Beta Program first so the stable OTA can install without wiping data. After the update finishes and the phone restarts, the new menus become available immediately.

![Pixel phone showing the home screen after an update](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1512941937669-90a1b58e7e9c?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## Set a Custom System Color

Long-press an empty area of the home screen and choose Wallpaper & style. Tap Colors. You will see the usual presets plus a paint-palette icon. Tap the palette icon to open the rainbow hue slider.

Drag the slider until you find the shade you want. The home screen, Quick Settings, and app icons update in the preview so you can judge the result before you apply it. Once you confirm the color, return to the Colors page and choose one of the four intensity styles: Neutral, Soft, Bright, or Bold. Soft works well for everyday use; Bold makes the accent stronger across the interface.

Compatible wallpaper collections can also switch between light and dark versions to match the system theme. The change applies system-wide without a reboot.

## Control Status Bar Icons

Android 17 QPR2 adds a dedicated Status bar page. Open Settings and go to System, then Status bar. You can also reach the same page from Settings > Notifications.

Under notification icons you have three choices:

- All: every notification icon appears.
- Priority only: only important notifications show (this is the default).
- None: notification icons are hidden completely.

Under system icons you can toggle Mute, Vibrate, and Battery percentage independently. Turning off Battery percentage removes the percentage number while the battery icon itself stays. Changes take effect right away.

These controls are more limited than what some other manufacturers offer, but they finally give Pixel owners a central place to reduce status-bar clutter.

## Tips for Best Results

Start with Soft intensity if you want the custom color to feel calm. Switch to Bold only when you need the accent to stand out. After you set the color, open a few apps and check Quick Settings to confirm the palette looks balanced in both light and dark modes.

For the status bar, try Priority only first. It keeps important alerts visible while removing most of the noise. Hide Battery percentage if you prefer a cleaner look and rely on the icon alone.

If the Status bar menu does not appear, confirm you are on the stable QPR2 build and not an older beta. Non-Pixel phones will receive these options only when their manufacturers adapt the code, which can take longer.

Pixel owners who already use other Android 17 features such as App Lock can combine the new color and status-bar settings for a more personal device. See our earlier guide on [Android 17 App Lock on Pixel](/blog/android-17-app-lock-pixel/) for related privacy controls.

## Conclusion

Android 17 QPR2 finally lets Pixel users pick any accent color and decide which status-bar icons stay visible. The steps take less than five minutes once the update is installed. Open Wallpaper & style for the color slider, then visit Settings > System > Status bar for the icon controls. The result is a cleaner, more personal phone that still follows Material You.
