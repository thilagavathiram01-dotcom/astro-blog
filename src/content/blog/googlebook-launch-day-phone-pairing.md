Googlebook reaches US shelves on October 4, 2026, and Canada, the UK, Ireland, France, Germany, and Australia on October 5. Google built the laptop on the Android technology stack with desktop foundations from ChromeOS, and the first job after unboxing is not a benchmark. It is signing in so the machine already knows your phone.

John Solomon, vice president and general manager for laptops, tablets, and Android Enterprise, described that link in Google’s September 21 launch post. When you sign in, your Android phone’s settings, saved passwords, Wi-Fi networks, and messages move with you, backed by end-to-end encryption. Google’s Australia note adds a hard requirement: phone features need a phone on Android 17 or above, and setup is required.

If you are still comparing models, start with [how to choose a Googlebook before the October 4 launch](/blog/choose-googlebook-october-4-launch/). This guide assumes the box is already open.

## What moves when you sign in

Google does not publish a click-by-click wizard in the launch post. The behavior it does state is the part that matters on day one.

Sign in with the same Google Account you use on the phone. Google says the transfer covers settings, saved passwords, Wi-Fi networks, and messages, and that the transfer is end-to-end encrypted. That is the opposite of emailing a backup file to yourself. The laptop pulls account-backed data during setup instead of asking you to retype every network.

Keep the phone nearby, unlocked, and on Wi-Fi before you start. A phone stuck on Android 16 will not meet the Android 17 floor Google lists for these companion features. Update the phone first if the system offer is waiting.

Prices start at $899. Every Googlebook in the launch includes 12 months of Google AI Pro, which Google says provides 5 TB of cloud storage and higher Gemini usage limits, plus 3 months of YouTube Premium, Adobe Photoshop, and more. OS updates and feature drops run for up to 10 years. Those bundles do not replace the phone sign-in. They sit on top of it.

## Finish setup, then check the three phone tools

After the account step, Google names three ways the laptop uses the phone. Treat them as a short acceptance test before you install extra apps.

**Continue On.** Pick up a task you started on the phone from the Googlebook taskbar. Google’s example is a BandLab project started on a commute. Open the laptop and continue from the taskbar instead of hunting for the file. If nothing appears, confirm you are on the same account and that the phone app was actually in progress, not merely installed.

**Files.** Open the Files app on the Googlebook and look for files and photos from the phone. Google positions this as the replacement for emailing attachments to yourself or digging through a downloads folder. Search by name first. If a recent photo is missing, check that the phone still has the original and that both devices finished the initial sync.

**Cast My Apps.** Stream a phone app into a desktop window and interact with it there. Google’s examples are ordering from a delivery app and grabbing a one-time code from Messages. The point is to keep the laptop session intact. You are not mirroring the whole phone home screen as a second monitor. You are opening a specific phone app in a window.

Run one real task in each tool before you declare setup done. Continue a note or a creative project. Open a photo from the phone in Files. Cast Messages and read a code without picking up the handset. If one of the three fails, fix that before you layer widgets and agents on top.

## Hardware you will see on first boot

The first wave comes from Acer, ASUS, Dell, HP, and Lenovo. Google says the machines use aluminum, magnesium alloy, or carbon fiber, with touchscreens up to 2.8K OLED, haptic glass trackpads, and backlit keyboards. Processors are Intel or Qualcomm, with dedicated NPUs Google rates at over 45 TOPS, and battery life up to 14 hours.

The Glowbar sits on the exterior. Google says it lights at startup, sweeps to show battery charge, and plays animations when you talk to Gemini. It is a status light, not a second display. If it stays dark after a full boot, check that the lid sensor registered the open and that the machine is not in a low-power state.

None of that hardware pairs the phone by itself. The NPU and the Glowbar run local Gemini features. Phone continuity still depends on the signed-in account and an Android 17 phone.

## Turn on desktop Gemini only after the phone link works

Googlebook ships three desktop intelligence tools. They are useful, and they are separate from Continue On.

Magic Pointer: wiggle the cursor to bring Gemini to whatever is on screen. Google says you can ask it to organize a messy calendar or translate text without extra windows. Gemini acts only when you ask, and you can switch it off. A fuller walkthrough is in [set up Magic Pointer on Googlebook](/blog/googlebook-setup-magic-pointer/).

Rambler sits next to the Quick Insert key. Speak notes, action items, or a stream of consciousness, including more than one language. Google says Rambler returns headers, checklists, and formatted takeaways. It is dictation with structure, not a second chat app.

Create My Widget builds a desktop widget from a plain-language description and can surface updates through the day. Describe one narrow job, such as unread work mail or today’s calendar, and reject the result if it asks for accounts you did not mean to connect.

Leave Magic Pointer off until Continue On, Files, and Cast My Apps behave. A cursor-triggered assistant on a half-synced account is a faster way to mix personal and work data than it is to finish setup.

## Apps, browser, and the Linux terminal

Googlebook runs desktop Chrome with extensions and Android apps from the Play Store, including titles optimized for a large screen, mouse, and keyboard. Google names Adobe Photoshop, Adobe Lightroom, CapCut, Netflix, and HBO Max, with offline viewing supported on those streaming apps. Games include Android titles and, with the included year of GeForce NOW, PC games you already own in that service.

Builders get Antigravity on device, plus a terminal. Google says you can run Claude Code or the Antigravity CLI there. The software stack uses the same security architecture as ChromeOS, a Google Titan hardware root of trust, and on-device malware detection. A Level 5 security-certified pKVM hypervisor isolates a Linux environment with full terminal access. That is the place for repositories and agents, not a reason to disable the phone lock.

Install Play Store apps after the phone tools work. A cast session is easier to judge when the laptop is not also downloading a game.

## Tips for the first hour

Update the phone to Android 17 or newer before you open the laptop box. Google’s companion notes list that floor.

Use the Google Account that already holds your passwords and messages. A fresh account will not import a phone it has never seen.

Do not email files to yourself as a test. Open Files on the Googlebook and search. That is the path Google is replacing the email habit with.

Cast My Apps for a one-time code, then close the window. Leaving a Messages cast up on a shared desk shows notification content you did not mean to share.

Confirm the 12-month Google AI Pro offer in your account after setup. Google includes it with every Googlebook in this launch, along with 5 TB of storage. The offer does not move your phone data. Sign-in does.

Retail timing is split. US shelves start October 4. The other launch countries start October 5. Pre-orders were already open on the Google Store, Best Buy, and other retailers.

If Continue On never lists the phone task, check account match first, then app state, then Android version. Google’s example depends on a task already started on the phone.

## Bottom line

Googlebook’s launch-day advantage is the shared Android account, not the OLED panel. Sign in, let encrypted settings, passwords, Wi-Fi, and messages come across from an Android 17 phone, then prove Continue On, Files, and Cast My Apps with one task each. Magic Pointer, Rambler, and Create My Widget can wait until that link is real.
