Gemini Live already lets you talk instead of type. **Guided Vision** is the piece that uses the camera: you point the phone at a label, a shelf, or a room, and Gemini talks you through what it sees. Google’s help docs describe audio cues for jobs such as reading small print or understanding what is in front of you. The September 2026 Android Drop coverage from Google and Android Authority frames the same idea as verbal descriptions while you share video.

This guide is a how-to. It follows Gemini Apps Help for Android, Google’s Gemini Live camera article on android.com, and the official product video for camera and screen sharing. Availability still rolls out by country and device, so treat the steps as current official flow rather than a promise that every phone has the control today.

## What Guided Vision is (and is not)

When camera sharing is on in a Live session, Gemini can:

- Describe what is in the frame
- Ask you to reframe, pan, or center an object
- Answer follow-up questions about that scene
- Keep talking while you move the camera

It is not a replacement for a magnifier designed for low vision in every country, and it is not an offline tool. Live needs a network connection. Google also says Gemini Live is not available in the Gemini web app or in Gemini inside Google Messages.

Camera sharing itself is documented for Android phones and tablets with **2 GB of RAM or more** running **Android 10 and up**, according to Google’s android.com article. Guided Vision sits on top of that camera session. If you do not see the Guided Vision wording in Help on your account, you still get the same core flow: start Live, turn on video, talk about the frame.

## What you need

From [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15274899):

- An Android phone or tablet
- The Gemini mobile app, or Gemini set as the mobile assistant
- A personal Google account, or a work/school account that has Gemini Apps access
- The latest Google app from Play Store
- Age eligibility for Gemini Apps in your country
- Microphone and camera permission for Gemini

Ask people before you point the camera at them. Google’s use policy for generative AI applies to Live the same way it applies to typed chats.

## Start a Live session

1. Open the **Gemini** app.
2. Tap **Live** at the bottom, or swipe left. You can also say “Hey Google, let’s talk Live” or “Hey Google, let’s talk.”
3. Complete first-run prompts if this is your first Live chat.
4. Start speaking. Interrupt when you need to correct the model.

Tips from the same help article:

- Tap **Hold** or **End** to mute or leave.
- Pixel Buds can start Live with “Hey Google, let’s talk.”
- Captions can be turned on in Gemini Live settings if you want on-screen text for every Live chat until you turn them off.

## Turn on the camera for Guided Vision

Google’s android.com steps (power-button path):

1. Long-press the power button to open Gemini, if that shortcut is enabled.
2. Tap the **Live** icon.
3. Tap the **Camera** (video streaming) control.
4. Point the camera at the object.
5. Talk.

From inside an already-open Live chat, Gemini Help uses **Turn on video streaming**. Grant camera access if Android asks. Switch front and rear cameras with the flip control when you need your face or a wider view of a room.

### Use Guided Vision cues

Help text for Guided Vision is short and useful: with the camera shared, you can get **audio cues** for reading a small label or understanding what is around you.

Practical prompts that match those jobs:

- “Read the ingredients on this bottle. Start with allergens.”
- “This restaurant menu is dim. Read the vegetarian mains and the prices.”
- “Pan with me across this spice rack and tell me when cumin is in frame.”
- “Guide me until the error sticker on the back of this router is centered, then read the model number.”

If the model says you are off-frame, move slowly. Fast pans waste the session. Keep one object as the subject until you get the answer, then move.

## Screen sharing when the problem is on the phone

Guided Vision is camera-first. Screen sharing is the sibling feature for “what is on this display.”

Important official constraints:

- Turn on **notifications** for Gemini Live before screen share will work.
- The **full screen** is shared, not a single app window.
- Sharing does not resume automatically after you lock the phone or pause Live. You start it again.

Two official start paths:

1. In the Live screen, tap **Turn on screen sharing** and accept the system prompt.
2. Outside the app, open Gemini and tap **Share screen with Live**.

Good uses: a settings page you cannot parse, a booking form with tiny type, or a map you want described while you keep both hands on the phone.

## Everyday examples that stay within the product

**Kitchen label.** Point at a jar. Ask for sodium per serving and whether the label lists nuts. Check the printed numbers yourself before you cook for someone with an allergy. Live can misread text.

**Low-light menu.** Ask for a spoken list of dishes in one section. Follow up with “only items under 400 calories if the menu prints that.” If calories are not on the card, the model should say so.

**Find an object.** Google’s September 2026 drop language includes locating household objects. Start with a wide shot, then ask Gemini to tell you when the item enters the frame as you sweep the room.

**Repair or craft.** Google’s official camera-sharing video uses a pulled thread on a blanket and a choice of tools. Same pattern: show the damage, then show the toolkit.

Do not point Live at passwords, banking screens, medical documents you would not upload, or other people’s faces without consent.

## Privacy and data

Google documents how it handles Gemini Live data separately from this how-to. Read [how Google works with Gemini Live data](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961#live_data) before you leave the camera on in the background.

Practical habits:

- End Live when you put the phone down. Background Live requires notifications and is easy to forget.
- You cannot *start* Live from a locked screen. Unlock first.
- If lock-screen Gemini is off, locking the phone holds the session.
- Connected Apps (Calendar, Maps, and others Google lists for Live) follow the same permissions as typed Gemini chats.

## If Guided Vision does not appear

Work through this list before assuming the feature is missing forever:

1. Update the **Google** app and the **Gemini** app.
2. Confirm you are in a country where Gemini Apps are offered.
3. Sign in with an account that can use Gemini Apps.
4. Grant camera, microphone, and (for screen share) notification access.
5. Start Live again and look for **Turn on video streaming**.

Google notes that Live updates roll out gradually. Camera sharing on Android 10+ with 2 GB RAM is the documented floor for showing Gemini what you see. Guided Vision copy in Help can lag the button on your device.

## Quick checklist

- Open Gemini → **Live** → **video streaming**
- Hold the subject steady; ask one question at a time
- Use audio cues to reframe labels and objects
- Use **screen sharing** only after Live notifications are on
- End the session when you are done
- Verify any number, allergen, or medical claim on the printed source

Guided Vision is useful when describing a scene is slower than showing it. Keep the camera honest, keep the prompts narrow, and treat spoken answers as a first pass you can check against the object in your hand.
