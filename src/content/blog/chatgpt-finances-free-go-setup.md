OpenAI started rolling Finances in ChatGPT out to Free and Go users in the United States on October 2, 2026. Until then, the feature was limited to Plus and Pro. You can now link bank and investment accounts, open a spending dashboard, and ask questions that use your own balances and transactions.

The connection runs through Plaid. Credit report data is a separate link through Experian. ChatGPT cannot move money, pay bills, place trades, or file taxes. Treat the answers as planning notes, not advice from a registered adviser.

## Who can use Finances right now

OpenAI’s help center says Finances is available in the U.S. to Free, Go, Plus, and Pro users on the web, iOS, and Android. If you are outside the United States, the Finances page may not appear yet.

Android support arrived after the original May 2026 preview, which started on the web and iOS for Pro. The October 2 update also lists weekly insight updates, credit monitoring, stock watchlists, voice questions, and manual account entry for accounts you have not linked.

You still need a signed-in ChatGPT account. Temporary chats do not read connected financial accounts and do not create or update memories.

## Open the Finances page

On the web, sign in at chatgpt.com and open Finances from the sidebar, or go straight to the Finances view if your account already shows it. On Android or iPhone, open the ChatGPT app and look for Finances in the same account menu area.

If the entry is missing, check three things before you retry:

1. Your account region is the United States.
2. The ChatGPT app is updated from the Play Store or App Store.
3. You are not inside a temporary chat.

The rollout is staged. OpenAI said Free and Go access is rolling out, so a missing tile on day one does not mean your plan is excluded.

## Connect accounts with Plaid

ChatGPT walks you through a Plaid link. You pick the institution, sign in with that bank or broker, and choose which accounts to share. After you authenticate, ChatGPT syncs and categorizes the data. That usually takes a few minutes. In rare cases it can take several hours.

Plaid maintains connections to more than 12,000 financial institutions, which is the figure OpenAI cited when the preview launched. You choose the accounts. ChatGPT can use balances, transactions, investments, and liabilities to answer questions. OpenAI says it cannot see full account numbers or change anything at the institution.

After the first sync, the Finances page can show starter prompts, widgets, and a short summary. The page also shows the last sync time and whether an account needs attention. A connection that needs attention usually means you must sign in again, finish a step at the bank, or update a password.

Some institutions share balances but not a full transaction history, holdings, loan details, APRs, or due dates. If a card looks thin, open the Accounts tab, check status, and ask ChatGPT which fields it actually received.

## Add credit data only if you want it

Bank accounts and credit reports are separate connections. You can use either, both, or neither. Credit widgets reflect your latest Experian report, not live card activity. A payment you made yesterday may not show until the lender reports it.

ChatGPT can explain factors on the report it can see. It cannot edit Experian data. Dispute errors with Experian, not inside the chat.

## Read the dashboard before you ask

The Finances dashboard is a dedicated view of connection status, last sync, starter prompts, and widgets. Depending on what you linked, you may see:

- Spend by category and spend this month
- Upcoming payments, subscriptions, and bills
- Net worth over time
- Portfolio distribution across equities, bonds, ETFs, cash, and crypto
- A market update for equity holdings
- Credit score, factors, and history once Experian updates are available

Widgets appear only when ChatGPT has enough connected data. Use the pencil icon to reorder them or hide ones you do not want. If a number disagrees with your bank app, compare the source and the date on the widget. Transfers, credit-card payments, reimbursements, and duplicate pending transactions can inflate a spending total. Ask which transactions were included, then ask ChatGPT to reclassify a wrong category and remember the correction.

## Ask questions that use your accounts

You can ask from the Finances page or from a normal chat. When the question relates to linked data, ChatGPT can use that context and may cite the source rows so you can check them.

Type `/finances` or `@finances` in the chat box to point the turn at Finances. Useful prompts from OpenAI’s help article include:

- Where did I spend the most money this month?
- What subscriptions am I paying for?
- What bills are coming up?
- How has my net worth changed recently?
- How is my portfolio allocated?
- Do I have any duplicate payments?
- Which factors are affecting my credit score?

Voice is supported, so you can talk through the same questions on a supported client instead of typing them. Stock watchlists let you follow tickers you care about alongside holdings that actually synced.

## Add accounts Plaid cannot see

Manual account entry covers assets and debts that never appear in a bank feed. Tell ChatGPT about a property, a private loan, a savings target, or a purchase you are planning. It can store that as a financial memory and reuse it in later finance chats.

Examples OpenAI gives: saving for a car next year, money still owed to family, rent split with a partner, or a minimum checking balance you want to keep. Open the three-dot menu on the Finances home page to view or delete those memories. Settings is the other place to manage financial memories and to disconnect Finances entirely.

Disconnecting Finances removes connected financial data from ChatGPT. Removing one account on the Accounts tab does not erase older chats that already quoted those numbers. Delete those conversations separately if you want them gone. Bank links and the Experian link are removed on their own paths.

## What ChatGPT will not do

OpenAI is explicit about the boundary. ChatGPT cannot move money, pay bills, change account settings, make trades, change retirement contributions, open or close accounts, or file taxes. It also does not act as your financial, legal, tax, or investment adviser.

For taxes it can organize figures, explain a concept, and flag items to review, such as possible deductions or estimated payments. It does not file a return. Check anything with a legal or tax consequence with a qualified professional.

Spending categories can be wrong. OpenAI’s own help text says AI can make mistakes. Confirm totals against the institution before you cancel a subscription or move cash.

## Control training and activity

Conversations that use Finances follow the same model-training setting as the rest of ChatGPT. Change it under Settings, then Data controls. Temporary chats skip connected accounts and do not write memories.

## A short weekly check

Once accounts are synced, keep the habit small. Open Finances, read the last sync time, then ask three questions: what subscriptions renewed, which bills land in the next two weeks, and whether this month’s category totals match the bank. Fix a bad category in chat and save the correction as a financial memory so the next weekly update does not repeat it.

Free and Go access does not change the product limits. You still approve every connection, you can remove it, and ChatGPT still cannot act on the account. The gain is context: answers can cite your transactions instead of a generic budget template.
