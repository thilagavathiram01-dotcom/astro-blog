Google rolled out new Android scam defenses on October 14, 2026. The centerpiece is an opt-in Enhanced Protection mode in Google Messages that uses Gemini models to catch more sophisticated text scams.

Scammers now use slow-burn conversations, brand-new phishing sites, and social engineering. Standard filters miss many of them. Enhanced Protection adds cloud analysis for unknown senders and smarter link checks on supported phones.

This guide shows how to enable the mode, what it actually does, and the other protections rolling out alongside it. All details come from Google’s official announcement.

## What Enhanced Protection Mode Does

Enhanced Protection is a single opt-in switch in Google Messages. When you turn it on, three defenses activate:

- Scam Detection with Private AI Compute. For chats with numbers not in your contacts, Google Messages sends conversation context to cloud Gemini models. The models evaluate intent faster than on-device checks alone. Analysis runs in Private AI Compute, where data stays encrypted and inaccessible to Google or anyone else.
- Smarter link safety checks with Gemini Nano. On supported devices, the on-device model checks unfamiliar links using real-time web signals before you open them. This catches newly registered scam sites that known-malware lists miss.
- Built-in checkpoints for unfamiliar links. Redesigned warnings appear before you tap links from unsaved numbers, giving clearer context and more time to pause.

Google says the mode rolls out in the coming weeks. If you already have Personal Intelligence or similar Gemini features enabled, check Messages settings once the update arrives.

Availability notes from the announcement: cloud analysis works on Pixel 6 and newer, Galaxy S24 series and newer, Galaxy Z Flip/Fold6 and newer, and other Android AICore devices. Gemini Nano link checks require newer hardware such as Pixel 10+, Galaxy S26 series, or Galaxy Z Flip8/Z Fold8 and select other devices.

## How to Enable Enhanced Protection

The setting is opt-in, so it will not turn itself on. Once the update reaches your device, follow these steps:

1. Open the Google Messages app.
2. Tap your profile picture or the menu icon, then go to Settings.
3. Look for Spam protection or Scam detection options.
4. Find Enhanced Protection (or a similarly named toggle) and turn it on.
5. Confirm any prompts about data use or connected features. You can turn the setting off at any time.

If the option does not appear yet, update Google Messages from the Play Store and check again in a few days. Google noted the rollout happens over the coming weeks after the October 14 announcement.

While you wait, keep standard Scam Detection enabled. It already flags more than 5 million suspected scam calls and texts each month across Phone by Google and Messages.

## Other New Android Scam Defenses

Google announced several related protections in the same post:

- Expanded chat notification scam alerts. Previously limited mostly to Pixel, these on-device warnings will reach hundreds of millions more users later this year through partners including HONOR, Infinix, Motorola, OPPO, OnePlus, realme, TECNO, vivo, and Xiaomi. A “likely scam” warning can appear in the notification shade before you open the chat app.
- Automatic screen-share privacy. Android already hides sensitive fields in native apps during screen sharing. The same protection now extends to Chrome and Android WebView on Android 15+ devices, blanking login and payment screens automatically. This rolls out this month.
- Proactive call-forwarding protection. Apps can no longer quietly enable call forwarding in the background. If someone tries to trick you into entering an MMI forwarding code, Android warns you first. This starts in early December on Pixel and other select devices.
- Verified financial calls. Android works with banks such as Banco do Brasil, Itaú Unibanco, and Revolut to verify in real time whether an incoming call claiming to be from the bank is genuine. Unverified calls are ended automatically. Google reported blocking more than 3 million spoofed calls from two Brazilian banks alone since the feature launched in June.

An independent evaluation commissioned by Google and performed by Leviathan Security Group found Android devices provide the highest level of built-in scam and fraud protection among tested phones when features are left at default.

## Practical Tips While the Rollout Continues

Enhanced Protection strengthens detection, but it does not replace basic habits.

- Pause before tapping any link from an unknown number, even if no warning appears.
- Never share one-time passcodes or banking details over text or during an unexpected call.
- Keep Google Messages and Play Services updated so new protections arrive promptly.
- Review your contacts list. Scam Detection focuses on unknown senders; saved numbers receive lighter checks.
- If you use screen sharing for support, know that Chrome and WebView now hide sensitive fields on Android 15+.
