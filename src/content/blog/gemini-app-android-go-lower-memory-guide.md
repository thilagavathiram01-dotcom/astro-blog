Google expanded the Gemini app to eligible Android (Go edition) devices on 18 November 2026. These phones serve as the main internet connection for hundreds of millions of people. Hardware limits previously restricted them to basic voice commands. Google re-architected the app so the core Gemini experience runs on hardware with less memory.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="363" />

This guide shows eligibility, access steps, available features, and current limits. It draws only from the official Google Blog announcement and the Gemini Apps Help page.

## Check eligibility first

The Gemini mobile app works on Android (Go edition) devices with 2GB of RAM or more. You must sign in with a personal Google Account or a work or school account that has Gemini Apps access. Work and school accounts require a plan or license that includes it. Family Link managers can turn off access for users under 13 (or the applicable age in their country).<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="384" />

It is available in all languages and countries where Gemini is offered. On these devices Gemini runs as part of the Google Search app, or you can download the standalone Gemini app from Google Play. Some newer devices include it preloaded. Google named partners such as Transsion for upcoming preloaded devices.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="353" />

Update the Google Search app if needed:

1. Open the Google Play app on your Android (Go edition) device.
2. Search for "Google" or "Google Search".
3. Tap Update if the button appears.
4. If it says Open, you already have the latest version.

## Start a conversation

On supported Android (Go edition) devices you start Gemini by touching and holding the Home button. On supported devices you can also press and hold the Power button. "Hey Google" and "Ask about this screen" are not supported on these devices.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="384" />

The experience replaces Assistant for Android (Go edition) and provides a conversational interface designed for lower-storage hardware.

## What you can do with Gemini

The app supports core tasks that match everyday needs on entry-level phones:

- **Stay connected:** Ask Gemini to call or send text messages to contacts.
- **Get help with images:** Take a photo to translate text or receive step-by-step help. You can also ask Gemini to create images with Nano Banana (for example, a flyer for a small business).
- **Brainstorm:** Use Gemini Live for natural spoken conversations in more than 45 languages. Practice interview answers or talk through ideas out loud.
- **Find local info:** Check drive time or ask complex questions such as finding a restaurant open for lunch with an EV charger nearby.
- **Plan your day:** Set an alarm or create a calendar event.
- **Upload files:** Add docs, photos, and other files for more context.
- **Play media:** Request music by mood or activity.

These features come from the official Help page and the 18 November announcement. Camera-based help and Nano Banana image creation are highlighted as practical tools for notes, forms, and simple creative tasks.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="363" />

## Current limits

Hands-free "Hey Google" activation does not work. "Ask about this screen" is also unavailable. The app focuses on core conversational and camera features rather than every advanced capability found on higher-memory devices. Performance depends on the specific hardware; Google states it re-architected the app for smooth operation but does not publish device-by-device benchmarks.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="384" />

## Tips for better results

Update the Google Search app before you start. Sign in with an account that already has Gemini access. Test Gemini Live first for spoken tasks such as interview practice, since typing long prompts can feel slower on lower-memory hardware. Use the camera for forms and notes when translation or explanation helps. Keep file uploads modest so the device stays responsive. Check Connected Apps settings if you want additional Google services later (availability may differ on Go devices).

If you also use Gemini on other platforms, note that conversations started on the Go device follow the same account history rules as elsewhere. For related Gemini setup on other devices, see our [Gemini on Apple CarPlay guide](/blog/gemini-apple-carplay-setup-guide/).

## Conclusion

Eligible Android (Go edition) phones with 2GB of RAM or more can now run the Gemini app after a re-architecture that targets lower memory. Access is through the Google Search app or the Play Store download. Core features include Gemini Live conversations, camera help for notes and forms, image creation, calls, texts, calendar actions, and media playback. "Hey Google" and screen-specific queries remain unavailable. Confirm your device meets the RAM and account requirements, update the app, and start with a spoken or camera-based request to test it.
