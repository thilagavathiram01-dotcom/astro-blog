Google Wallet arrived on iPhone for U.S. users on November 17, 2026. The free App Store app pulls package tracking, boarding passes, concert tickets, and receipts from Gmail. It also stores loyalty cards and speeds online checkout with Google Pay.

It does not support tap-to-pay in stores. Apple Pay still handles contactless payments at the register. The app works on iOS 17 or later and stays limited to the United States for now.

This guide walks through setup, key features, limits, and practical tips so you can organize your passes and purchases without switching devices.

## What Google Wallet Does on iPhone

The app syncs automatically with Gmail and other Google apps. Package tracking, digital boarding passes, event tickets, and receipts appear in one place across your devices. You decide what to import from Gmail.

You can scan physical loyalty cards and gym memberships with the iPhone camera. Saved payment cards work for faster online checkout across Google services and sites that accept Google Pay. The app also serves as a hub for agentic purchases made with Gemini or other Google tools, showing order confirmations, receipts, and delivery updates.

Navigation uses a floating pill with two main tabs. The home tab shows recent purchases, boarding passes, and a grid of loyalty cards. The second tab lists transactions, passes you can pin, and saved payment methods.

## Requirements and Setup Steps

You need an iPhone running iOS 17.0 or later and a Google Account. The app is free and listed as about 171 MB on the U.S. App Store. It is marked for iPhone only, though the listing also notes compatibility with certain Macs and visionOS.

1. Update your iPhone if needed. Go to Settings > General > Software Update.
2. Open the App Store and search for Google Wallet. Confirm the developer is Google LLC.
3. Download and open the app.
4. Sign in with the Google Account linked to the Gmail that receives your order and travel emails.
5. Review and choose what the app imports from Gmail. Allow orders, tickets, and passes for automatic tracking.

After setup, items already in Gmail start appearing. You can also add cards manually with the camera scanner or the plus button.

## Key Features and How to Use Them

Package and order tracking pulls from Gmail. Recent purchases appear on the home screen with status updates. Boarding passes and tickets show with QR codes ready to scan.

Loyalty cards go in via the camera. Point the phone at a physical card, and the app imports it. You can pin important passes to the homepage for quick access.

Payment cards are for online use. Add them once, and they autofill during checkout on Google sites or Google Pay merchants. The app does not process in-store NFC payments.

Agentic purchases made through Gemini appear in the same view. You get order details and delivery tracking without digging through email.

## Limits You Need to Know

Tap-to-pay is not supported on iOS at this time. Google states this clearly in the announcement and the App Store listing. Use Apple Pay for in-store contactless payments.

The app is available only in the United States. No other countries have access yet. Google has not announced expansion plans.

It works best if your orders, tickets, and receipts already land in Gmail. Cross-device sync keeps the same passes available on Android and desktop when you sign in with the same account.

Some features may have age restrictions based on your Google Account. The app is rated 4+.

## Practical Tips for Daily Use

Sign in with the account that receives the most order and travel emails. Switching accounts later can hide expected passes.

Pin frequently used boarding passes or loyalty cards to the home tab. This reduces scrolling on travel or shopping days.

Use the camera scanner for any barcode card that does not fit the loyalty or gift categories. The “Everything else” option handles miscellaneous items.

Review Gmail import settings if too many or too few items appear. You control what syncs.

For online checkout, keep at least one payment card saved. It speeds purchases on Google properties without re-entering details.

If you also use Gemini for shopping or research, the Wallet hub keeps agentic order history in one view. Pair this with other Google productivity tools for a smoother workflow.
