Google has rolled out a practical update for Google Slides that lets Gemini edit multiple slides or an entire presentation from a single prompt in the side panel. You stay in control because changes land on native, fully editable slides.

This feature started an extended rollout on October 29, 2026, and is available to Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus, Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, and Google AI Pro for Education users. At launch it works in English only.

![Person working on presentation slides on a laptop](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1759215524484-89c8d7ae28f2?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What the New Gemini Editing Can Do

With one prompt you can:

- Refresh visual styles across the deck (fonts, backgrounds, color schemes) and match another presentation or reference file.
- Update content throughout the deck, including simplifying, expanding, or shifting focus, grounded in files from Google Drive.
- Combine or expand slides, such as summarizing several slides into one or adding new ones.
- Make styling consistent, for example aligning title fonts and sizes or removing page numbers everywhere.

Gemini decides which slides need changes and applies the edits directly. After it finishes, a summary appears in the side panel. Because the edits behave like normal user changes, you can undo them or review them in version history.

Through at least December 1, 2026, Workspace customers receive promotional higher limits for multi-slide work so you can experiment. After that date, standard per-user limits apply.

## Step-by-Step: Edit Your Deck with Gemini

1. Open the Google Slides presentation you want to update on a desktop browser.
2. Click the Gemini (or Ask Gemini) button in the top-right corner to open the side panel.
3. Type a clear prompt. Examples that work well:
   - "Update this whole deck to match the style of this [reference file]."
   - "Update this presentation to include more details based on [Drive file]."
   - "Summarize slides 5–7 into a single slide."
   - "Make the titles on all slides the same font size and color."
4. Send the prompt. Gemini analyzes the deck, identifies relevant slides, and applies the changes.
5. Review the summary in the side panel. Check the slides themselves for accuracy.
6. Use undo (Ctrl/Cmd+Z) or version history if you want to revert anything. Fine-tune individual elements manually as needed.

You can reference Drive files with the @ symbol or by uploading/selecting them so Gemini grounds its updates in your actual content.

## Tips for Better Results

Be specific about the scope. Naming slide numbers or describing the exact style change reduces unnecessary edits. Attach or reference your brand guide or an existing on-brand deck when you want visual consistency.

Start with a smaller section if your deck is long. After the promotional higher limits end, standard usage caps will apply, so plan larger jobs accordingly.

Because the feature is English-only at launch, write prompts in English even if your slides contain other languages. Gemini still edits the content, but the instruction understanding is optimized for English.

Pair this with other Gemini tools in Workspace. If you previously created a deck with Gemini (see our guide on [creating slides with Gemini](/blog/gemini-canvas-create-slides/)), you can now refine the whole thing in one pass.

## Limitations to Know

The feature is rolling out gradually, so it may not appear in every account immediately. There is no admin control to disable it.

It does not replace manual design work for highly custom layouts. Complex charts, animations, or third-party objects may need a final pass by hand.

PDF preview and editing inside the Gemini app works on mobile and web, but the bulk deck editing described here lives inside Google Slides on the web.

## Conclusion

Editing an entire presentation with one prompt removes the repetitive work of updating styles and content slide by slide. You keep full editability and the ability to undo, so the risk stays low while the time savings stay high.

Open a deck, open the Gemini side panel, and try a style-matching or content-expansion prompt on your next presentation. The combination of native slides plus bulk AI editing is one of the more useful Workspace updates this month.
