Google Labs has brought Dreambeans to India. The experimental app turns data from apps you choose into a daily collection of illustrated stories. It is available now for users 18 and older on Android and iOS.

Unlike endless feeds, Dreambeans keeps the list finite. Stories cover places to explore, events, hobbies, or reminders drawn from your connected Google services. You control which apps it can use. This approach aims to surface useful information without requiring constant scrolling or manual prompts.

The expansion to India marks the first market outside the United States. Support for 14 languages makes the experience accessible across the country. You can generate stories in English or major regional languages and switch later if needed.

## What Dreambeans does

Dreambeans uses Google’s Personal Intelligence to build stories overnight from the apps you enable. It then presents them as a short morning collection. The system looks for patterns in the data you permit and turns them into illustrated narratives.

Supported sources include Calendar, Gemini, Gmail, Photos, Search, and YouTube. Connecting Photos can incorporate images of you and people in your library into the illustrations. Gemini adds another signal for personalization based on your conversations and requests.

The app works best with more connections, but you must enable at least one. Choices in Dreambeans do not change settings in Gemini Apps or AI Mode. Stories stay private to your account. Google states that opt-ins in one product do not affect the other.

India gets support for 14 languages: English plus Hindi, Bengali, Telugu, Marathi, Tamil, Urdu, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia, Punjabi, Assamese, and Nepali. Select one language for the stories at setup. You can change this later from the profile settings.

Each story combines a personalized topic with information from the connected apps. Examples include tips related to an upcoming calendar event, recommendations based on recent searches, or ideas inspired by photos in your library. The illustrations are generated specifically for the collection rather than pulled from stock images.

## Download and sign in

1. Open the Google Play Store on Android or the App Store on iOS.
2. Search for Dreambeans by Google Labs.
3. Install the official app and confirm the developer is Google Labs.
4. Open it and sign in with a personal Google Account. Workspace accounts are not supported for this experiment.
5. Confirm you are 18 or older when prompted.

The first set of stories can take up to a day to generate after setup. You can provide feedback on stories to improve future ones. During this initial period, the app processes the connected data and builds the personalization model for your account.

Make sure your device meets basic requirements. Android users need a recent version of the Play Store. iOS users should be on a supported system version listed in the App Store. Keep the app updated for the latest language packs and features.

## Choose connected apps

After signing in:

1. Tap to connect apps. Select any combination of Gemini, Gmail, Calendar, Google Photos, YouTube, or Search.
2. Grant the required permissions for each service. Review the permission screen carefully before approving.
3. Pick your preferred language for the stories from the available list.
4. Finish setup and wait for the first collection to appear.

You can change connections later. Open the profile icon, review connected apps, and toggle them on or off. Deleting data from a source also removes it from Dreambeans personalization. This gives you ongoing control over what information the app can access.

If you already connect apps to Gemini, the process is similar. See the guide on [connecting apps to Gemini](/blog/connect-apps-to-gemini/) for related permission steps and privacy considerations. Both systems rely on explicit opt-in, so enabling one does not automatically enable the other.

Connecting Calendar helps the app surface stories around upcoming events or travel. Photos adds visual context and can include people from your library. Search and YouTube help identify interests and hobbies. Gmail can supply practical details such as confirmations or reminders. Gemini contributes conversational context when enabled.

## Use the daily stories

Open the app each morning. Browse the finite list of illustrated stories. Tap one to read more, bookmark it, or share it with others. The collection is designed to be small so you can finish it without extended scrolling.

Provide feedback with likes or dislikes. Use the tuning options to request more of certain topics or exclude others. This updates future collections. You can also leave more detailed feedback through the chat-style interface inside individual stories.

Some stories may include animated elements powered by Veo. The focus remains on inspiration rather than infinite scrolling. Bookmark stories you want to revisit later. Sharing creates a link that others can open, though the full personalization stays tied to your account.

Check the app regularly during the first week. Early feedback has a larger effect on the topics that appear. Over time, the system learns which categories you engage with and which ones you skip.

## Privacy controls

You decide which apps connect. Review or delete data from the profile icon. Select “Delete my data” to clear history, or manage specific feedback history. The app provides clear options to review what has been used and remove it.

Dreambeans does not affect Gemini or AI Mode settings. Stories are visible only to you. When you remove data from a connected service, it leaves the Dreambeans system as well. This separation keeps the experiment isolated from other Google AI products.

Start with one or two apps you trust, such as Calendar and Photos. Expand later if the stories prove useful. You can disconnect any service at any time without deleting your entire account. Review the connected list monthly to confirm only active services remain linked.

Google’s help documentation notes that you control the data flow. Permissions granted during setup can be revoked from the profile section. Deleting feedback history removes specific signals you previously provided. These tools let you fine-tune the experience without starting over.

## Tips for better results

- Connect Photos and Calendar for personal context around events and people.
- Give clear feedback on the first few days so the app learns your preferences.
- Check language settings if stories appear in the wrong language.
- Update the app if the Play Store or App Store shows a new version.
- Disconnect unused apps to limit data access.
- Use the bookmark feature for stories you want to act on later.
- Share interesting stories with family or friends to see how the illustrations appear to others.
- Revisit the connected apps list after a week and add one more service if the current set feels limited.

Dreambeans remains an experiment from Google Labs. Features and availability can change. Monitor the official Google Labs page for updates on language support or new connected services.

## Common questions

**Does Dreambeans work offline?** The app needs an internet connection to generate and refresh stories. Once generated, you can browse previously loaded collections for a short time.

**Can I use it with multiple Google Accounts?** Sign out and sign in with a different personal account. Each account maintains its own connections and story history.

**What happens if I disconnect all apps?** The app requires at least one connection to generate new stories. You can keep previous collections while you decide which services to re-enable.

**Is the data used to train models?** Google positions the system as a personalization layer for your private feed. Review the current terms on the Labs site for the latest details.

## Conclusion

Setting up Dreambeans in India takes a few minutes: download the app, sign in with a personal account, pick at least one Google service, and choose a language. The result is a short daily set of personalized illustrated stories instead of another endless feed.

Use the privacy controls to keep only the connections you want. Pair it with existing Gemini workflows if you already connect apps there. Provide feedback early so the collections become more relevant over the first week.

The experiment offers a different way to interact with information already stored in your Google services. Start with minimal connections, review the first stories, and expand only if the format matches how you prefer to receive suggestions.
