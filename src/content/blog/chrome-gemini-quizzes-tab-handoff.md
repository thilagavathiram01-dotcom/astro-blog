You can now test yourself on the page you already have open in Chrome, then send that same tab to your phone with the scroll position intact. Google announced the study-focused updates on 24 September 2026.

The official Chrome post lists five habits: ask Gemini about more kinds of media, generate quizzes from the current tab, organize work with tab groups and split view, hand off tabs between devices, and tidy pages with reading mode.

This guide walks through the new pieces first, then the older tools that still matter. If you already use [Gemini in Chrome on Android](/blog/gemini-in-chrome-android/), treat this as the desktop companion.

## What Google shipped on 24 September 2026

Charmaine Dsilva, Senior Director of Product Management for Chrome, published the list on the Google blog. Three items are new or expanded. Two are reminders of features that already shipped earlier in 2026.

**New or expanded**

- Gemini in Chrome on desktop can work with podcasts and videos hosted outside YouTube, after you play the file through.
- Gemini in Chrome can generate interactive quizzes from the content of the current tab.
- Send to Your Devices now keeps scroll position and partially filled form fields.

**Already available, called out again**

- Vertical or horizontal tabs, tab groups, and split view.
- Immersive reading mode and read aloud, including AI playback on Android.

Quizzes start in English in the United States and India on desktop. Google says mobile and more regions follow in the coming months.

## How to generate a quiz from the current tab

Open the page you want to study. That can be a Google Doc of notes, a research paper, or a YouTube lecture. Then open Gemini in Chrome on desktop.

1. Sign in to Chrome with the Google account that has Gemini in Chrome enabled.
2. Open Gemini from the Chrome toolbar or side panel.
3. Ask it to quiz you on the current tab. Name the topic if the page is long.
4. Answer the questions in the panel. Use the results to mark weak spots in your notes.

Google’s help article for Gemini quizzes (`gic_quizzes`) is the official reference for controls such as hints and extra questions. The Chrome blog’s examples include physics notes, a paper on the Byzantine Empire, and a machine-learning video.

Do not treat a generated quiz as an official exam. It reflects the tab you opened, not a syllabus. If the page is thin or paywalled, the questions will be thin too.

## How to ask Gemini about podcasts and non-YouTube video

Gemini in Chrome already answered questions about documents and YouTube lectures. The September update extends that on desktop to podcasts and videos on other sites.

Play the audio or video through first. Google’s wording is explicit: Gemini can identify key takeaways, find specific information, or clarify confusing moments **once you have played the file through**.

Useful prompts after playback:

- “List the three claims the speaker made about Roman trade.”
- “Where in this lecture does the host define opportunity cost?”
- “Explain the part I found confusing in simpler language.”

If playback never finishes, do not expect a full summary. That limit is in the official post, not a rumor.

## How to send a tab and keep your place

Chrome already let you send a tab from a laptop to a phone, or the other way. The gap was progress inside the page. You landed at the top and scrolled again.

That handoff now stores the exact state of the tab. Google includes scroll position and partially filled forms. The example in the blog is a 50-page paper on behavioral economics that you continue on a commute.

**On desktop**

1. Open the three-dot menu.
2. Choose Cast, Save, and Share.
3. Select Send to Your Devices and pick the signed-in phone or laptop.
4. Optional: add Send to Your Devices to the toolbar from Customize Chrome on the New Tab page.

**On Android**

Open Chrome’s share menu and pick the device from the carousel. Both devices must use the same Google account.

Forms that contain passwords or payment fields still follow Chrome’s normal autofill and security rules. Do not assume every field survives the hop. Confirm sensitive values before you submit.

## Keep research readable with tabs and split view

Quizzes help after you read. Organization helps while you read.

Right-click a Chrome window and choose Show Tabs Vertically if you want full titles instead of favicon soup. Group tabs by course or project. Split view keeps a source on one side and a draft on the other. Paired tabs stay linked, so you can leave and return to the same pair.

These controls shipped earlier in 2026. Google restated them in the study post because students still drown in open tabs.

## Reading mode and read aloud

If ads and sidebars break focus, right-click the page and open immersive reading mode. You can change font, letter spacing, and contrast.

Read aloud narrates the page when your eyes need a rest. On Android, read aloud’s AI playback can generate a podcast-like conversation from the page you are viewing. That is an Android-only note in the official article.

## Tips that keep the tools honest

- Update Chrome on desktop before you hunt for quizzes. The rollout is staged.
- Stay in English in the U.S. or India if you want quizzes this week.
- Play long media once before you ask for takeaways.
- Send the tab only after you reach a stopping point you care about. Handoff stores state at send time.
- Pair this workflow with Gemini in Chrome on your phone when you leave the desk. The Android side panel is covered in our earlier [Gemini in Chrome on Android](/blog/gemini-in-chrome-android/) guide.

## What this is not

This is not a new “Study Mode” product name. Google framed the work as five study habits inside Chrome.

It is not a replacement for NotebookLM or Gemini app study guides. Those products have their own quiz and flashcard flows. Use Chrome when the source already lives in a tab.

It is not available on every phone on day one. Quizzes expand to mobile later.

## Conclusion

Open the source. Quiz the tab. Send the page to your phone with the scroll bar where you left it. That is the September 2026 study loop in Chrome, according to Google’s own product post.

Start with one long article today. Generate five questions. Hand the tab to your phone and finish the last section on the train. Then decide whether tab groups or split view deserve a place in the same routine.
