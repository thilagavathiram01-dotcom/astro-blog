Google rolled out Play Store version 53.8 on October 26, 2026. The update adds relevant videos with citations and timestamps to Ask Play responses. It also brings Playbacks, a Sidekick utility that lets you save the last 30 seconds of gameplay after the moment happens.

These changes help you evaluate apps faster and share game highlights without planning a recording in advance. Rollout is gradual, so the features may not appear on every phone right away. Check for Play Store updates in your system services page.

## What Ask Play video answers change

Ask Play is Google’s conversational tool inside the Play Store. It answers questions about apps and games using store listings, reviews, and related content. With version 53.8, responses can now include relevant videos that include citations and timestamps.

You can jump to the exact part of a video that answers your question instead of watching the full clip. This works in search results and on app pages where Ask Play appears. Google has not published the full list of video sources it uses.

## How to use Ask Play video answers

1. Open the Google Play Store app on your Android phone.
2. Search for an app or game, or open an app’s details page.
3. Look for the Ask Play section or the Ask Play button in search.
4. Type a question such as “How do I use offline mode?” or “Does this game support controllers?”
5. Read the response. If a video appears, tap the timestamp or citation to open the relevant part of the video.
6. Ask a follow-up question if the interface allows it.

Verify important details on the app’s official listing, privacy policy, or developer site before you install or pay. AI responses can be incomplete.

Features may be limited by language, country, or account. If you do not see videos, update the Play Store and try again later.

## What Playbacks does in Sidekick

Playbacks is a new gaming utility inside Play Games Sidekick. Sidekick is the in-game overlay available on many games installed from Google Play. It already offers screenshots, screen recording, Do Not Disturb, YouTube streaming, achievements, and Play Points tools.

Playbacks keeps a buffer of the last 30 seconds of gameplay video frames on your device. The capture is limited to the current game surface. When you tap Capture Playback, the buffer is saved as an MP4 to local storage. If you do not tap the button, the buffered frames are deleted.

Google states that Playback does not record audio. Videos contain only the gameplay visuals. Playback is on by default on compatible devices. Saved clips go to the Play Games Media folder on your phone.

## How to capture gameplay with Playbacks

1. Install a supported game from the Google Play Store and sign into your Play Games profile if prompted.
2. Launch the game. A small floating Sidekick handle appears on the edge of the screen (or a notification if you switched the entry point).
3. Play normally. The system keeps the most recent 30 seconds in a local buffer.
4. When a moment you want to keep happens, open Sidekick by dragging the handle toward the center of the screen.
5. Tap Capture Playback (or the equivalent control under Playbacks).
6. The clip is saved locally. Find it later in the Play Games Media folder on your device.

To turn Playbacks off:

1. Open Sidekick.
2. Tap Settings.
3. Under Playbacks, toggle the switch off.

You can also hide the Sidekick handle entirely from Play Store settings under Google Play Games.

## Tips for better results

- Update Google Play services and the Play Store regularly. Go to Settings > Google > All services > System services to check for updates.
- For Ask Play, ask specific questions. Vague queries produce broader results.
- Playbacks works only on the game surface. It does not capture other apps or the home screen.
- Because there is no audio, add your own commentary or music if you plan to share the clip later.
- Share Play Shorts via the new link feature if you want others on Android, non-Android devices, or the web to view related short videos.
- Check official Google support pages for Sidekick if controls differ on your device.

## Conclusion

Ask Play video answers and Playbacks give Android users practical new tools for app discovery and gaming. Videos with timestamps let you evaluate apps more quickly. The 30-second retroactive capture removes the need to start a recording before a good moment happens. Both features depend on the Play Store 53.8 update and device compatibility, so results can vary while the rollout continues.

Try them on a few apps and games this week and note which questions or clips work best for you.
