Scammers now use slow conversations and brand-new phishing sites that slip past basic filters. Google announced Enhanced Protection Mode for Google Messages on 14 October 2026 as part of Cybersecurity Awareness Month updates.

This opt-in setting adds cloud-based Gemini analysis through Private AI Compute, on-device Gemini Nano link checks on supported phones, and clearer warnings for links from unknown numbers. It builds on existing on-device Scam Detection without sending your full message history to Google by default.

## What Enhanced Protection Mode adds

Google Messages already flags many suspicious texts on-device. Enhanced Protection Mode layers three extra defenses when you turn it on.

First, Scam Detection can use Google’s stronger cloud Gemini models via Private AI Compute. The analysis looks at conversation context with unrecognized senders to spot intent earlier. Your data stays encrypted in a dedicated secure space that Google cannot access for other purposes. You can still optionally report a message as spam, which does share content for further review.

Second, on-device Gemini Nano checks unfamiliar links using real-time web signals. This catches brand-new domains that have not yet appeared in known-malicious lists. The feature works on Pixel 10 and later, Galaxy S26 series, Galaxy Z Flip 8 and Z Fold 8, and select other devices.

Third, redesigned warnings appear before you tap links from numbers not saved in your contacts. These give extra context and a moment to pause. The checkpoint was previously limited to certain countries or devices running Android Advanced Protection.

The full mode rolls out in the coming weeks. Device support for the Private AI Compute portion starts with Pixel 6 and newer, Galaxy S24 series and newer, Galaxy Z Flip/Fold 6 and newer, and other Android AICore devices.

## How to enable Enhanced Protection Mode

Once the update reaches your device, the toggle appears in Google Messages settings. Follow these steps:

1. Update Google Messages from the Play Store and restart the app.
2. Open Google Messages and tap your profile picture or the menu icon.
3. Select Settings, then look for Spam protection or the new Enhanced protection entry.
4. Turn on Enhanced protection mode. Confirm any privacy notices about Private AI Compute.
5. Ensure standard Spam protection is also enabled if the options appear separately.

The setting is opt-in. Turning it off returns you to the existing on-device Scam Detection only. Rollout timing varies by region, account, and device manufacturer. Check for app updates daily for the next few weeks.

If the option is missing, confirm your phone meets the minimum requirements listed above and that you are signed in with a Google account. Some carrier-customized Messages apps may lag behind the Play Store version.

## What happens when a scam is detected

With the mode active, Google Messages can surface warnings during a conversation with an unknown sender. The alert typically lets you report and block the number or dismiss it if the message is legitimate.

Link checks run before you open an unfamiliar URL. On supported devices, Gemini Nano evaluates real-time signals rather than relying only on a static blocklist. Redesigned warning screens add clearer language and a short delay so accidental taps are less likely.

These protections complement rather than replace other Android tools. For stronger baseline security across Chrome, Phone, and more, see the guide to [setting up Android Advanced Protection](/blog/android-advanced-protection-setup/).

## Additional Android scam safeguards rolling out

Google paired the Messages update with other changes. Scam Detection warnings in chat notifications expand beyond Pixel to devices from HONOR, Infinix, Motorola, OnePlus, OPPO, realme, TECNO, vivo, Xiaomi, and others. These on-device alerts can appear in the notification shade for popular third-party chat apps.

Screen sharing now automatically blanks sensitive fields such as login forms and payment details in Chrome and Android WebView on Android 15 and later. Call-forwarding protection, arriving in early December on Pixel and select devices, blocks apps from quietly enabling forwarding and warns you if you enter an MMI code on the dial pad.

Google reports that existing Scam Detection tools already flag more than 5 million suspected scam calls and texts each month. Independent testing commissioned by Google found Android devices offered stronger built-in scam defenses than the compared iOS devices in several default categories.

## Tips for staying safer while you wait for the rollout

- Keep Google Messages updated and enable standard Spam protection today.
- Never reply to unexpected texts asking for codes, payments, or personal details.
- Verify bank or delivery issues by opening the official app or website yourself instead of tapping a link.
- Add frequent contacts so unknown-number warnings trigger less often for real conversations.
- Review app permissions and avoid installing APKs from messages or unknown sources.

If a message still looks suspicious after the new warnings, report it. Optional spam reports help improve detection for everyone, but they are not required for the Private AI Compute analysis.
