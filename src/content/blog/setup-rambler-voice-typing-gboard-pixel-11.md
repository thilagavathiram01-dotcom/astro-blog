Rambler turns messy spoken thoughts into clean, punctuated text right inside Gboard on Pixel 11 devices. It strips filler words, fixes grammar, and lets you revise by voice. Google rolled it out as part of Gemini Intelligence features for the Pixel 11 series.

This guide walks through official setup steps from Google Support, what to expect when dictating, and how to get the best results. It works in any app where Gboard is active.

## What you need before starting

Rambler requires a Pixel 11 series phone. Update Gboard from the Play Store and set it as your default keyboard. Grant microphone permission to Gboard when prompted. Full features need an internet connection; limited offline mode is available.

If you do not see Rambler options, confirm your device model and Gboard version first. For broader Gemini Intelligence features including widgets and app automation, see our guide on [using Gemini Intelligence on Android](/blog/how-to-use-gemini-intelligence-on-android/).

## Enable Rambler in Gboard

Open any app with a text field, such as Gmail or Messages. Tap the field so Gboard appears. Tap the microphone icon in the top-right corner of the keyboard.

If this is your first time, an introduction dialog appears. Tap Start. Rambler becomes the active voice-typing mode.

If no dialog shows, open the Gboard menu (three dots or settings icon). Go to Settings, then Voice typing. Select Rambler from the list. Return to your text field and tap the microphone again.

Grant the microphone permission if Android asks. Choose "Allow only while using the app" for best results. You can change this later in app permissions.

## How to dictate with Rambler

Tap the microphone and speak naturally. A waveform appears in the suggestion strip while you talk. When finished, tap the checkmark to process your speech. Rambler cleans the output and inserts it into the text field.

It removes "um," "uh," and similar fillers. It adds punctuation and formats sentences. Speak as you normally would; you do not need to pause for every period.

To edit or add more, tap the microphone again. You can also use voice commands to rewrite sections after the text appears. A setting lets Rambler use terms from your Personal Dictionary to improve accuracy on names and custom words.

Rambler processes after you finish speaking rather than showing live text as you talk. This differs from older Standard voice typing. Switch back to Standard anytime in Voice typing settings if you prefer real-time preview.

## Tips for better results

Speak at a normal pace in a reasonably quiet environment. The feature handles multilingual speech in supported languages. Review the output before sending, especially for names or technical terms.

Enable the Personal Dictionary option in Gboard settings so Rambler learns your corrections. Recent beta updates add better contact-name recognition and memory of corrected words.

If Rambler does not appear, force-stop Gboard and reopen it, or clear the cache. Ensure your Google account is signed in and Play Services are up to date. On non-Pixel devices, unofficial mods exist, but they are unsupported and carry risks.

## Limitations and troubleshooting

Availability is limited to Pixel 11 series at launch. Broader Android rollout may come later. Offline mode has reduced accuracy and fewer rewrite options.

If the microphone button does nothing, check permissions under Settings > Apps > Gboard > Permissions. Restart the device if the feature fails to activate after setup. For persistent issues, check Google Support for the latest Gboard version requirements.

Rambler is one piece of Gemini Intelligence. Pair it with Create My Widget for custom home-screen dashboards or app automation for multi-step tasks across apps.

## Conclusion

Rambler makes voice typing practical for longer messages and notes on Pixel 11. Setup takes under a minute once Gboard is updated. Speak normally, review the cleaned text, and use voice edits for quick revisions.

Try it in Messages or Gmail today. Combined with other Gemini Intelligence tools, it reduces the friction of everyday phone use.
