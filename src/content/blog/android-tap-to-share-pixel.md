Google added **Tap to share** so two nearby Android phones can exchange contact cards, photos, videos, links, and other files without hunting through a device list. It sits on top of Quick Share. You overlap the tops of two unlocked phones, wait for the glow, and confirm what should move.

This guide follows Google’s August 2026 product post and the Android Help article “Tap to share content with others.” Availability is still device- and version-gated. If the glow never appears, check the requirements before you assume the radios failed.

## What you need before you tap

Google’s Help page states Tap to share is available for **Pixel 6 and newer phones running Android 17 and higher**. The company also said the feature would reach Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra, Fold8, and Flip8, then more Android devices later in 2026.

Both phones must be unlocked, with screens facing you. NFC must be on. Work Profile apps cannot use Tap to share. Managed accounts can ship with the toggle off.

Tap to share is **on by default** for personal devices. You can turn it off later under Settings → Google → Quick Share.

If you also use the rest of this year’s Pixel extras, pair this habit with the [September 2026 Android Drop guide](/blog/android-september-2026-drop-guide/) after you confirm Android 17 is installed.

## Swap contact cards from the home screen

Google’s blog describes contact exchange as the fastest path: stay on the home screen and bring two compatible phones close together. Android Help is more specific about how the hardware should sit.

1. Unlock both phones.
2. Overlap the **tops** of the devices with both screens facing up and visible.
3. Hold them together until the screens glow.
4. Choose which fields from your contact card to send. You can send your card, receive only the other person’s card, or do both.
5. Save the received card in Contacts if you want it stored.
6. Pull the phones apart to cancel before you confirm.

Update your own card first if the name, number, or email is stale. Google points you to the Contacts app for that edit. You control the fields that leave the phone. The other person still has to accept and save.

Use this at events instead of typing a number into Notes. Do not treat it as a silent dump of every field on your card. Review the checklist each time you meet someone new.

## Send photos, videos, links, and files

For anything other than a contact card, start in the app that holds the file, then tap.

**From the Share menu**

1. Open the photo, video, file, or link.
2. Tap **Share**.
3. Overlap the tops of both unlocked phones, screens up.
4. Hold until they glow.
5. Wait for the other device to accept.
6. Separate the phones to cancel.

**From the Quick Share page**

1. Select the items on the Quick Share page.
2. Overlap the phones the same way and wait for the glow.
3. Confirm on the receiving phone.

Google’s August post matches this: open the system Share sheet, then tap the devices together. The receiver still has to accept. That confirmation is the safety net if someone bumps a phone in a crowded room.

Received files land in the **Quick Share** folder. Open the default Files app, then **Downloads → Quick Share**.

You can still use classic Quick Share if the other phone is not tap-capable. Open Share, tap Quick Share, and pick the device from the list. Tap to share only removes the hunt when both phones support it.

## Turn it off or limit who can see you

Tap to share is a convenience switch, not a requirement. To disable it:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Google**, then **Quick Share**.
3. Turn off **Tap to share**.

That path is the official one in Android Help. It does not uninstall Quick Share. You can still send files from the Share sheet by picking a named device.

Visibility for ordinary Quick Share still lives in the same settings group. If strangers keep appearing in a list you did not expect, tighten “who can share with you” to contacts, or use the short “everyone for 10 minutes” window only when you are standing next to the person.

Work phones and school accounts may hide the toggle. Google says Tap to share is off by default on managed accounts. Do not fight that policy on a device you do not own.

## Fix a tap that never glows

Google’s troubleshooting list is short and worth running in order:

1. Overlap **more** of the two screens, not just the camera bump.
2. Remove thick cases.
3. Try tapping the phones **back to back** if top-to-top fails.
4. Confirm **NFC** is on: Settings search for “NFC,” then enable it.
5. Check where the NFC antenna sits on each model. Pixel 11 moved the antenna toward the top of the phone. Older Pixels keep it in a different spot. Align the documented antenna areas, not the logos.

Both screens must stay on and unlocked. A lock screen or Always On Display is not enough. If one phone is on Android 16, Help says Tap to share will not start even if Quick Share still works.

Bluetooth and location still matter for the Quick Share session that follows the NFC handshake. If the glow appears and the transfer stalls, turn those radios on and retry without walking away.

Do not use this as a substitute for a cable when you are moving an entire photo library. Tap to share is for the file in front of you, not a backup job.

## What Tap to share is not

It is not AirDrop on every Android phone. Google is rolling hardware support in waves. Pixel 6 and newer on Android 17 is the documented floor. Fold8 and Flip8 were named next. Everything else is “by the end of the year” in the August post.

It is not a silent publish to the other person’s cloud. Files land locally in Quick Share downloads unless that person later uploads them.

It is not available inside a Work Profile app. Keep work documents on the work share path your admin already set.

Cross-platform Quick Share to iPhone AirDrop is a separate Pixel 10-era feature that still needs the Quick Share extension and an AirDrop “Everyone for 10 minutes” window on the Apple side. Tap-to-share contact exchange in Help is written for Android-to-Android.

## A two-minute practice run

1. Confirm Android 17 and NFC on both test phones.
2. Stand on the home screen and exchange contact cards once. Save only the fields you need.
3. Open one photo, tap Share, overlap the tops, and accept on the second phone.
4. Open Files → Downloads → Quick Share and confirm the photo arrived.
5. Decide whether to leave Tap to share on. If you carry the phone on transit with the screen unlocked often, turning the toggle off is reasonable.

That loop tells you whether your case, antenna, and OS version actually work. Reading the announcement is not a substitute for one successful glow.

## Conclusion

Tap to share is Quick Share with an NFC start instead of a device picker. Overlap the tops, wait for the glow, pick the contact fields or accept the file, and pull the phones apart if you change your mind. Keep Android 17, NFC, and an unlocked screen in the loop. Use Help’s case and antenna tips when nothing happens.

When Google widens the device list, the same motion should work. Until then, treat Pixel 6 and newer on Android 17 as the reliable pair, and fall back to the Quick Share list for everything else.
