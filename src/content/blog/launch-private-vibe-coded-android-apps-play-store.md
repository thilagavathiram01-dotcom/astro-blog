Google Play now lets you launch personal Android apps you create in Google AI Studio without a developer account. These vibe-coded apps stay private in a personal library on your devices.

This change, announced on October 13, 2026, removes the need for a paid developer account or sideloading via USB for personal use. You only need a standard Google account. Access is rolling out in select markets including the United States for users aged 18 and over.

If you have already explored building Android apps in AI Studio, this update makes running them on your phone simpler. See our guide on [building Android apps in Google AI Studio](/blog/build-android-apps-google-ai-studio/) for the earlier workflow.

## What Changed in Google Play

Previously, installing an app from AI Studio required connecting your phone via USB and using Android Debug Bridge, or publishing to an internal test track with a developer account. Now, personal apps launch directly through the Play Store.

Your creations remain private, secure, and separate from the public catalog. They do not appear in search results or recommendations. Google Play still requires these apps to follow existing policies.

In the coming months, you will be able to share personal apps with a close circle of friends and family. If you later want a public release, you can graduate to a professional developer account. At that point, the app goes through the same Trust & Safety reviews and quality standards as other Play Store apps.

## Step-by-Step: Build Your App in AI Studio

Start by signing in to Google AI Studio at aistudio.google.com with your Google account.

1. Open the Build tab.
2. Select the option to build an Android app.
3. Describe the app you want in plain language. For example: "Create a minimalist daily habit tracker with local storage and a simple progress chart."
4. Click Build. The system generates Kotlin code using Jetpack Compose and shows a browser-based emulator.

You can iterate by adding prompts for changes, such as adjusting colors or adding a new screen. The embedded emulator lets you test interactions without installing software locally.

![Laptop screen showing code editor](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1555066931-4365d14bab8c?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## Launch to Your Personal Library

Once the app is ready, use the launch option tied to Google Play. With the new feature, this sends the app to your personal library instead of requiring a developer console or USB connection.

On your Android device, open the Play Store. Look for the "Your personal library" section. Your vibe-coded apps appear there for install and restore across your devices signed into the same Google account.

No one-time $25 developer fee applies for this personal path. The process keeps apps isolated from the public store.

## Tips for Better Results

Write specific prompts that cover the main screens, data storage, and any device features like camera or location. Mention the design style you prefer.

Test thoroughly in the emulator before launching. Fix issues by describing them in follow-up prompts rather than editing code yourself unless you export the project.

Keep apps focused on personal utilities such as trackers, calculators, or simple tools. Complex server-dependent features are limited in the current Android build experience in AI Studio.

When sharing becomes available, start with a small group and confirm the apps meet policy requirements.

## What Comes Next

This personal library path lowers the barrier for trying custom Android apps. It also provides a clear route to public publishing if an idea grows.

Monitor the rollout in your region and update the Play Store app on your devices. Google continues to expand AI tools for both personal and professional app creation.
