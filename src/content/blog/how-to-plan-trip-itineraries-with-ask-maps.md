Google Maps just made trip planning conversational. Ask Maps now builds full day-by-day itineraries with numbered pins, real-time updates, and the ability to share or export your plan.

This feature rolled out on November 16, 2026, on Android and iOS. It expands Ask Maps, which already let you ask natural language questions about places. Now you can describe a multi-day road trip or a shopping day and get a structured schedule grounded in Maps data.

If you have used Gemini in Maps before, the experience feels familiar. You open Ask Maps, type or speak your request, and refine it in the chat.

## Open Ask Maps and start your itinerary

Update Google Maps to the latest version from the Play Store or App Store. Then open the app.

Tap the Ask Maps button below the search bar on mobile. On desktop, look under the menu. A chat interface appears.

Describe your trip clearly. Include the destination, number of days, and preferences. For example: “Plan a 3-day trip from San Francisco to San Diego this holiday weekend with stops for coastal hikes and oceanfront lunch spots. No more than 4 hours driving per day.”

Ask Maps returns a day-by-day schedule. It includes insider tips, numbered pins on the map for each stop, and a visual route. The response draws from Maps reviews, hours, and real-time information.

You can refine the plan immediately. Say “Add a scenic viewpoint on Day 2” or “Replace the second dinner with a seafood restaurant that has outdoor seating.” The itinerary updates.

## Share, export, and use the itinerary on the go

Once the plan looks good, share it with friends or family directly from the chat. On desktop, export it to Google Docs for further editing or printing.

Before you leave, reopen the itinerary by asking Ask Maps or tapping the itinerary badge in your chat history. You see live traffic, whether each place is open, and even notes on the vibe at the next stop.

Tap any stop for directions. You can add stops mid-trip the same way: “Add a coffee shop with parking along the current route.”

Pull up directions and look for “Know before you go” tips. These cover parking, car charging, weather, potential delays, and tolls. The feature launches first in the U.S., with more regions coming soon.

Travel itineraries themselves are available everywhere Ask Maps is offered.

## Find appointments and search your lists

Ask Maps now handles agentic tasks for local services. Ask “Find me a 4 p.m. nail appointment near me.” It shows open slots across nearby businesses so you can book.

The same works for pet care, auto service, or home repair appointments. Ask Maps can call the businesses for you in supported cases.

If you maintain saved lists in Maps, query them conversationally. Try “Which spots saved in my lists have availability for five people in the next 30 minutes?” Ask Maps checks live availability across your lists and surfaces options.

This saves time when guests visit or when you need a last-minute table from places you already like.

## Tips for better results

Be specific with constraints. Mention driving time limits, cuisine preferences, outdoor seating, or budget. Ask Maps handles multi-part requests better when details are clear.

Verify critical details. Hours, prices, and availability can change. Open the place card and confirm before you commit.

Use follow-ups. The conversation keeps context, so you can adjust one day without restarting the whole plan.

Availability varies by region. Travel itineraries and list search work broadly. Appointment finding and “Know before you go” tips start in the U.S.

Ask Maps is expanding to 30 additional European countries, including Spain, Germany, and the UK, bringing the total to more than 200 countries and territories.

## Conclusion

Ask Maps turns vague trip ideas into actionable, shareable itineraries with live data. You describe the trip once, refine in chat, and navigate with real-time context. Combined with appointment finding and list search, it reduces the back-and-forth that usually fills holiday planning.
