ChatGPT can now build interactive flashcards inside the chat. You ask for a deck on a topic, or you upload notes and ask ChatGPT to turn them into cards. The cards appear in the reply. You flip them, mark what you know, and find the deck later in your library.

OpenAI added the feature in the ChatGPT release notes dated September 22, 2026. It is available on all ChatGPT plans on mobile and the web. You do not need a paid plan to try it.

This guide follows OpenAI’s Help Center article on flashcards. It also notes where study mode still helps when you want a tutor instead of a deck.

## What ChatGPT flashcards actually do

A flashcard in ChatGPT is not a pasted Q-and-A list. It is an interactive card in the conversation.

You read the front, try to recall the answer, then tap the card to flip it. Select the check mark if you know the answer. Select the X if you want to practice it again later. You can shuffle the deck so the order changes.

Decks save to your library automatically. You can open a saved deck from the library, or ask ChatGPT to bring the flashcards back in a later chat.

OpenAI is clear that ChatGPT can make mistakes. Check cards against your notes or study guide before an exam. If a card is wrong or vague, ask ChatGPT to edit it in the same conversation.

## Create a deck from a topic

1. Sign in at ChatGPT on the web or open the iOS or Android app.
2. Start a new conversation.
3. Ask for flashcards on one subject, with a level and a goal.

OpenAI’s own examples include beginner French phrases for ordering food and asking for directions. A tighter prompt works better than “make cards about biology.”

Try prompts like these:

- Make flashcards for beginner French phrases I can use to order food and ask for directions.
- Create 15 flashcards on enzyme kinetics for a first-year college exam. One fact per card.
- Make flashcards on Pixel theft-protection settings I should review before travel.

Name the audience. “First-year college,” “A2 Spanish,” or “Android power user” keeps definitions at the right depth.

Ask for one idea per card. Compound cards that mix three facts on the back are harder to mark as known or unknown.

## Create a deck from notes you already have

Upload the source, then ask ChatGPT to turn that file into flashcards. OpenAI’s help text uses the example “turn this study guide into flashcards.”

Good uploads include class notes, a syllabus excerpt, lecture slides, a worksheet, or a photo of a problem set. If the file is long, point ChatGPT at a chapter, a page range, or a heading.

Example prompts:

- Turn the uploaded study guide into flashcards. Cover only the section on mitochondria.
- Use these lecture notes. Make 20 cards. Short questions. Short answers. Skip anecdotes.
- Convert this vocabulary list into flashcards. Front: target language. Back: English plus one example sentence.

If the upload is blurry or ChatGPT skips a page, paste the key paragraphs or upload a clearer scan. Study mode’s help article gives the same advice for images and PDFs.

Do not upload answer keys from a live exam or content you are not allowed to share. Treat the chat like any other copy of your notes.

## Review, shuffle, and mark cards

OpenAI’s review flow is short:

1. Read the front of a card and try to recall the answer before you flip it.
2. Tap the card to see the back.
3. Select the check mark if you know it, or the X if you need it again later.
4. Shuffle when you start repeating the same order from memory.

The library can show cards you know and cards you do not know. Use that split when you have ten minutes, not a full study block. Drill the unknown pile first.

If you only skim the back without recalling the front, you are rereading, not testing yourself. Pause before the flip.

## Edit a deck without starting over

You can change cards in the same conversation. OpenAI’s examples:

- Add cards about mitochondria.
- Rewrite this definition in simpler language.

Other useful edits:

- Split this card into two cards with one fact each.
- Replace the answer with the formula only, no extra sentence.
- Drop cards that repeat the same definition.
- Add three application cards that ask me to choose the right formula.

Review the updated set before you close the chat. Confirm the new cards still match your notes.

If a card is factually wrong, say so and paste the correct line from your source. ChatGPT will rewrite that card. You still own the check against the textbook.

## Find the deck later

Flashcards save to your library. To practice again:

- Open the saved deck in your library, or
- Ask ChatGPT to bring your flashcards back.

Give the request a name if you keep several decks. “Open my beginner French restaurant deck” beats “find my cards.”

Library access depends on being signed in on the same account. A temporary chat will not give you a lasting library item the way a regular chat does. If you care about keeping the deck, use a normal conversation.

If you share a device, check which account is signed in before you upload notes.

## When to use study mode instead

Flashcards test recall. Study mode walks you through a problem instead of handing you a finished answer. OpenAI launched study mode as a guided learning experience and still documents it separately from the September 2026 flashcard control.

Use study mode when you need:

- A concept explained from the start
- One practice problem at a time
- Feedback on why an answer is wrong
- A path through homework, not a deck

On the web, signed-in users can type `@study` in the composer, or select **+** and search for study. You can also open chatgpt.com/studymode. Study mode works with materials you upload, including notes, slides, and photos of problems.

A solid loop is: study mode to learn a section, flashcards to lock the terms, then a quiz prompt to check mixed recall.

OpenAI’s education newsletter on September 24, 2026 also points students to interactive quizzes in ChatGPT. Ask for a quiz on the same notes after you finish the deck.

## Tips that keep decks useful

**One fact per card.** “What does ATP synthase do, where does it sit, and what gradient does it use?” is three cards, not one.

**Write the level in the first prompt.** “Explain like a lab manual” and “explain like a middle-school review” produce different backs.

**Prefer your notes over a blank topic.** Cards from a study guide you already read stay closer to the exam wording.

**Mark honestly.** If you only recognized the answer after the flip, tap the X.

**Edit before you trust the deck.** OpenAI says to check cards against original notes, especially before an exam.

**Keep sensitive files out of casual chats.** Course notes are usually fine. Passport scans and medical records are not study material. If you need a map of privacy controls first, use the [ChatGPT Privacy Center setup guide](/blog/chatgpt-privacy-center-setup/).

**Name the deck in the prompt.** “Save these as Enzyme Kinetics midterm” makes the library search easier later.

## A 15-minute first session

1. Open ChatGPT on web or mobile and start a regular chat.
2. Upload one short study guide or paste one section of notes.
3. Ask: “Turn this into 12 flashcards. One fact per card. Short answers.”
4. Flip every card once. Mark known and unknown.
5. Ask ChatGPT to rewrite any card that feels too broad.
6. Shuffle and run the unknown pile again.
7. Confirm the deck appears in your library.
8. Tomorrow, ask ChatGPT to bring that deck back instead of generating a new one.

That last step matters. A new generation can drift from the source. The saved deck is the set you already corrected.

## Conclusion

ChatGPT flashcards are a built-in study object, not a prompt trick. You create them from a topic or from uploaded notes, flip them in the reply, mark what you know, and reopen the deck from your library.

The feature ships on every ChatGPT plan on web and mobile as of the September 22, 2026 release notes. Use it for retrieval practice. Use study mode when you still need a walkthrough. Check every card against the material you will be tested on.
