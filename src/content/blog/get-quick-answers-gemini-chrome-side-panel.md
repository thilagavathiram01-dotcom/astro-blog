Google added a simple way to ask Gemini about any open page in Chrome. The side panel keeps the site in view while Gemini summarizes reports, compares product details, or explains hard topics in plain language.

This works on desktop Chrome (Windows, Mac, Chromebook Plus). You stay on the same tab. No new window opens. The current page content is shared by default so answers stay relevant.

## What you need before you start

Sign in to Chrome with a Google account. Gemini in Chrome is not available in Incognito mode. You must be 13 or older (or the applicable age in your country) and in a supported region. Use the latest Chrome version and set your device to a supported language.

The feature rolls out gradually. If the Gemini icon is missing, update Chrome, restart the browser, and check again. Work or school accounts need an administrator to enable it.

## Open the Gemini side panel

1. Open Chrome on your computer and go to any webpage.
2. Click the Gemini icon (or “Ask Gemini”) in the top toolbar.
3. The first time, follow the on-screen steps to opt in.
4. The side panel opens on the right. Type your question and press Enter.

Your current tab is shared automatically. Gemini uses that content for its reply. A glowing underline appears on the tab while it is shared.

You can also open the panel from the Mac menu bar or Windows system tray after opting in. Keyboard shortcuts become available once the feature is active.

## Ask useful questions while you browse

Google’s own examples show three practical uses:

- Summarize a dense report into key points.
- Compare product details (price, weight, materials, reviews).
- Explain a complicated topic in simpler language.

Try prompts like “List the key features of this product,” “Summarize the customer reviews,” or “Explain this section for a beginner.” Stay on the page while the answer appears beside it.

You can switch models from the text box if more than one is available. For spoken questions, click Live at the bottom of the panel (not available for users under 18).

## Share extra tabs for comparisons

Gemini can use up to 10 open tabs across windows. This helps when you want a side-by-side comparison.

1. Open the side panel.
2. Click Add tabs (or type @ and select a tab).
3. Choose the tabs you need. Shared tabs show a check mark.
4. Ask a question that needs information from more than one page, such as “Compare these three laptops by price, battery, and weight.”

To stop sharing a tab, click Show shared tabs and remove it. Starting a new chat shares the current tab by default. You can turn this off in Chrome settings under AI innovations → Gemini in Chrome → Share current tab by default.

## Manage the panel and settings

- Pop the chat out into a floating window or dock it back to the side panel.
- Start a new chat from the More options menu.
- Find recent chats the same way.
- Hide or show the Gemini icon: Settings → AI innovations → Gemini in Chrome.
- Control microphone, precise location, and auto-browse permissions in the same place.

Auto browse (multi-step web tasks) is a separate, gradual feature that may require a Google AI Pro or Ultra plan in supported regions.

## Tips and common fixes

Update Chrome and restart if the icon does not appear. Confirm you are signed in and not in Incognito. Check that your language and region are supported. Turn off any VPN that might affect location detection.

For work or school accounts, ask your administrator to enable Gemini in Chrome. Parents can manage access for supervised accounts through Family Link.

If answers seem off, verify the information against the original page. Gemini uses the content you share; it does not invent page details on its own.

You can also use Circle to Search on Android for image-based lookups. See our guide on [Circle to Search on Android](/blog/circle-to-search-android/).
