You already open YouTube to watch phone unboxings, skincare hauls, and tool tests. The hard part is turning that pile of reviews into a decision. At Made on YouTube 2026, YouTube expanded **Ask YouTube** so you can ask follow-up questions, get organized comparison tables, and keep shopping from the watch page.

Johanna Voolich, VP of Product Management for viewer products, wrote that viewers already come to YouTube for reviews of phones, skincare, and back-to-school hauls. The new Ask YouTube shopping tools sit on top of those creator videos. You ask a question. You get structured recommendations. You click a video, then keep asking about the product on the watch page.

This guide covers what YouTube announced on 23 September 2026, who can use it today, and how to run a clean product search without treating the answers as a purchase order.

## What Ask YouTube shopping actually does

Ask YouTube is a conversational layer on search and the watch page. You type or speak a question. The system returns videos and, for product searches, **organized recommendations** such as a comparison table with attributes and categories based on your preferences.

YouTube’s official viewer post lists three behaviors that matter for shopping:

- You can ask questions and follow-ups in an interactive session.
- You can use voice on TV while a video plays.
- After you open a review, you can keep asking about that product on the watch page.

Gigazine’s recap of the same event notes that Ask YouTube, first released in May 2026, can also use **image input** and answer questions from what is on screen even without a transcript. That is useful when a reviewer holds a label to the camera and you want the ingredients or model number without pausing to type.

Ask YouTube is not a storefront checkout. It is a way to find creator videos and extract claims so you can compare them. Tagged products and creator shopping tools still live in the video and in YouTube Shopping.

## Who can use it right now

Availability is uneven. Treat these as the official constraints, not a promise that the button is on every account.

- **Ask YouTube** is available to signed-in users in the **United States**, according to coverage of the Made on YouTube 2026 announcements.
- YouTube says you can use it on the go or with **voice on TV**.
- **Custom Feeds**, a related homepage tool announced the same day, will roll out to U.S. users on web, mobile, and TVs later this fall. That is a separate feature. Do not wait for Custom Feeds if you only want Ask YouTube.

If you do not see Ask YouTube, confirm you are signed in, update the YouTube app, and try a desktop session at youtube.com. Features announced at Made on YouTube often ship in waves through late 2026 and early 2027.

## Start a product search from Home or Search

1. Open YouTube on phone, desktop, or TV and **sign in**.
2. Go to Search, or open the Ask YouTube control if your build shows it on Home.
3. Ask a **specific product question**, not a brand slogan. Example: “Compare mid-range Android phones with a 3x optical zoom under $700.”
4. Review the **organized recommendations**. YouTube says product searches may return a comparison table with attributes and categories based on your preferences.
5. Open the video that matches the use case you care about (battery test, camera sample, durability drop test).
6. Stay on the **watch page** and ask follow-ups: “Does this reviewer mention wireless charging?” or “Which unit did they use for the low-light test?”

Write constraints into the first prompt. Include budget, region, screen size, or “no subscription required.” Vague prompts return generic haul videos.

## Use Ask YouTube while a review is playing

The watch-page flow is the part that saves time. You do not have to scrub a 22-minute video for one spec.

1. Start a review from the recommendation set.
2. Open Ask YouTube on that page.
3. Ask about **what is on screen** if image input is enabled on your account: a label, a box spec sheet, or a comparison graphic.
4. Ask for a **summary of claims** the creator made about battery, camera, or returns.
5. Ask where the creator linked a product tag or shopping shelf, if the video uses YouTube Shopping.
6. Open a second review from the same table and ask the same three questions so you can compare answers, not vibes.

On TV, use voice so you are not hunting for a remote keyboard. YouTube explicitly called out voice follow-ups for living-room sessions.

## Pair Ask YouTube with Custom Feeds

Custom Feeds are homepage tabs built from a prompt. YouTube’s examples include “witty podcasts for my daily 30-minute commute,” “evening wind down with long documentaries,” and “help me discover new DIY creators.” Saved feeds update with new recommendations.

If you research products often, make a feed that is **review-shaped**, not brand-shaped. Prompt ideas that stay useful:

- “Long-form tech reviews with camera samples, no unboxing-only clips.”
- “Home tool tests with measured cuts, not gift-guide montages.”
- “Skincare ingredient explainers, English, 10 minutes or longer.”

Use Ask YouTube when you have a SKU in mind. Use a Custom Feed when you want a steady stream of reviewers you already trust. We covered the feed setup in [How to create Custom Feeds on YouTube](/blog/youtube-custom-feeds-made-on-2026/).

## Prompts that produce usable tables

Ask YouTube follows your wording. Tight prompts get tighter tables.

**Good:** “Compare three wireless earbuds under $150 with ANC, USB-C case, and multipoint. Prefer reviews that show a leakage test.”

**Weak:** “Best earbuds 2026.”

**Good:** “I need a compact vacuum for hardwood and pet hair. Show reviews that measure pickup on a 10-foot run.”

**Weak:** “Robot vacuum haul.”

**Good:** “Which Pixel 11 review covers Call for Me and the camera in mixed indoor light?”

**Weak:** “New Pixel video.”

After the table appears, ask one follow-up per attribute: battery method, sample rate, warranty region, or whether the unit was a press loan. Do not stack five questions in one sentence if the first answer is already wrong.

## Limits you should plan around

- **Region.** U.S. signed-in accounts are the documented audience for Ask YouTube in current coverage. Do not assume a VPN fixes a missing control.
- **Not a receipt.** A comparison table is a map of videos, not a certified spec sheet. Confirm numbers on the manufacturer page.
- **Creator bias.** Sponsored hauls still rank as reviews. Ask whether the video discloses a paid partnership before you treat a rating as independent.
- **Shopping tags.** Ask YouTube can point you at tagged products. Checkout still follows YouTube Shopping and merchant rules.
- **Rollout lag.** Custom Feeds, Shorts series on TV, and several Studio tools ship on different calendars. YouTube’s FAQ says Custom Feeds arrive for U.S. users this fall.

## Tips for a faster research session

Keep a notes app or a Doc open. Copy the three attributes you care about before you start Ask YouTube. Reuse the same three questions on every video so the answers line up.

Watch at least two creators who disagree. If both tables highlight the same weakness, that weakness is more useful than a single five-star thumbnail.

On mobile, start the search, open one video, ask follow-ups, then jump back to the table. Do not build a queue of twelve tabs. The point of the watch-page chat is to stay inside one review until you have the claim you need.

If image input is on, point the camera or pause on a spec card instead of typing model numbers. That is the May 2026 capability YouTube carried into this year’s shopping expansion.

## Conclusion

Ask YouTube is now a product-research loop: ask, scan a comparison table, open a trusted review, and keep asking on the watch page. Use it when you already know the category. Use Custom Feeds when you want that category waiting on Home the next time you sit down.

Start with one purchase you were going to research this week. Write a prompt with a budget and two must-have specs. Open two videos from the table and ask the same follow-ups. That is the whole workflow YouTube described at Made on YouTube 2026.
