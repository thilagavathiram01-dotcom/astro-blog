Pixel 10 and Pixel 11 owners can now activate Extreme Charging Mode for a short-term boost during wired charging sessions. The feature, first introduced with the Pixel 11 series, arrived on Pixel 10 models (except the Pixel 10a) with the November 2026 Pixel Drop and Android 17 QPR2 update.

It does not raise the phone’s maximum wattage. Instead, it temporarily pauses background activities so the device can focus more of its available power on charging. You still receive calls, texts, and notifications. The mode only works with a compatible wired charger and only when the battery is at 50% or lower.

This guide shows exactly how to turn it on, what charger you need, and what to expect from real-world use.

![Phone connected to a charging cable](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1741144837506-9986b01d8e27?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What Extreme Charging Mode Actually Does

Google describes Extreme Charging Mode as a software optimization. While it is active, the system pauses background activities such as app updates. This frees up a small amount of system resources and can help the phone sustain higher charging power for a longer portion of the session.

The hardware limits stay the same. A base Pixel 10 or Pixel 11 still tops out around 30W wired. The Pixel 10 Pro XL and Pixel 11 Pro XL support up to 45W. The mode does not unlock higher peak wattage; it simply helps the phone stay closer to its normal peak for more of the charge cycle.

Reviews and early tests show the time savings are modest—often a few minutes on a full charge and even less on a quick top-up from low battery. The phone may also run slightly warmer while the mode is on. Google notes that the device automatically manages temperature and keeps the battery within a normal range.

The feature is available on Pixel 10, Pixel 10 Pro, Pixel 10 Pro XL, Pixel 10 Pro Fold, and the full Pixel 11 series. It is not available on the Pixel 10a. It only works with wired charging, not wireless.

## Requirements Before You Start

You need three things:

- A supported Pixel phone updated to the November 2026 Pixel Drop / Android 17 QPR2 or later.
- Battery level at 50% or lower.
- A compatible wired charger. Google recommends a 30W or higher power adapter that supports the PPS protocol for best results. Official Google chargers (such as the 45W or dual-port options) work well. A quality third-party USB-C PPS charger rated for the phone’s maximum wattage also works.

If the option is missing, confirm the software update has fully installed and that your battery is below 50%.

## How to Turn On Extreme Charging Mode

You can activate the mode from three places. After the first use, a Quick Settings tile becomes available for faster access.

### From the lock screen (automatic prompt)

1. Connect your phone to a compatible wired charger.
2. Make sure the battery is at 50% or lower.
3. On the lock screen, tap the **Use Extreme Charging** button that appears.

### From Settings

1. Open the Settings app.
2. Tap **Battery and charging**.
3. Tap **Extreme Charging Mode**.
4. Tap **Turn on now**.

### From Quick Settings

1. Swipe down from the top of the screen.
2. Tap the **Extreme Charging** tile (it appears after you have activated the mode once).
3. Tap **Turn on**.

The mode stays active until the battery reaches a high charge level or you unplug the charger. You must turn it on again for each new charging session.

## What to Expect in Real Use

Tests from independent reviews show the biggest gains on higher-wattage models such as the Pixel 11 Pro XL. On lower-wattage models the difference is smaller. In one set of measurements the Pro XL reached roughly 75% in about 28 minutes with the mode on versus a slower pace without it. Gains on the standard Pixel 10 and Pixel 11 were more modest.

You will still receive incoming calls, messages, and notifications while the mode is active. App updates and other non-essential background work are paused temporarily.

Because the phone prioritizes charging, it can feel warmer than usual. This is expected. If the phone gets uncomfortably hot, unplug it and let it cool. Google states the system manages temperature automatically.

For everyday top-ups the time saved may not feel dramatic. The feature is most useful when you need the maximum possible speed on a low battery and have a strong PPS charger ready.

## Tips for Best Results

- Use a 30W or higher PPS USB-C charger matched to your phone’s maximum rated speed.
- Activate the mode only when the battery is under 50%. The option is unavailable above that level.
- After the first activation, add the Quick Settings tile so you can toggle it faster on future sessions.
- If the lock-screen prompt or Settings option does not appear, check that the November 2026 update is fully installed and restart the phone.
- The mode does not work with wireless charging or Battery Share.

## Conclusion

Extreme Charging Mode gives Pixel 10 and Pixel 11 users a simple way to prioritize charging when the battery is low. It pauses background work, keeps notifications active, and works only with a compatible wired charger. The time savings are real but modest, and the phone may run a bit warmer.

If you frequently need a quick boost from a low battery and already own a good PPS charger, the feature is worth using. Turn it on from the lock screen, Settings, or Quick Settings the next time your battery drops below 50%, and see how much difference it makes on your specific model.
