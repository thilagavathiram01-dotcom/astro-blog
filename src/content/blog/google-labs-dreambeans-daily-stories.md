Dreambeans is a Google Labs experiment that builds a short, personal stack of stories each day instead of another infinite feed. With your permission it reads the Google apps you choose, then turns upcoming events, hobbies, and photos into a finite collection you can tap, save, or share.

Google Labs introduced the app on 3 June 2026 for eligible U.S. Google AI Ultra subscribers. On 10 September 2026 it opened the same experiment to all qualifying U.S. accounts age 18 and older on Android and iOS, including consumer accounts and Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra. This guide follows those official posts, the Play Store listing, and Google Labs Help.

## What Dreambeans is (and is not)

Dreambeans is not a news wire and not Discover with a new coat of paint. Official copy from Google Labs describes it as an experimental mobile app that uses **Personal Intelligence** and image models such as **Nano Banana 2** to “proactively dream up personalized daily stories.”

Each day’s pack is meant to stay short:

- Personalized topics such as places to explore, shows to watch, or a reminder of an upcoming event
- Distilled context from the Google apps you connect
- A unique illustration on each story
- A way to tap in, bookmark, or share instead of scrolling forever

Google’s own examples are useful. A shipment email about dog treats can become a training-tip story. A calendar visit from a friend plus a pet in your Photos library can become a list of dog-friendly restaurants near you.

## Who can use it today

Use the 10 September 2026 announcement as the current eligibility list. Older Help pages still mention only Ultra and a waitlist; those pages have not fully caught up.

You need all of the following:

- Age 18 or older
- Location in the United States
- A supported Android or iOS phone
- A personal Google Account (consumer, Google AI Plus, Google AI Pro, or Google AI Ultra)

Google Labs Help still lists English as the officially supported language. Android devices should be on **Android 10 or later** to install from Play.

If the app signs you in but shows no access, treat that as a rollout delay, not a broken install. The Labs site at [labs.google/dreambeans](https://labs.google/dreambeans) remains the product home page.

## Step-by-step: install and sign in on Android

1. Open the Play Store and search for **Dreambeans**.
2. Confirm the publisher is **Google Labs** and the package is `com.google.android.apps.labs.lotus`.
3. Tap **Install**, then open the app.
4. Sign in with the personal Google Account you actually use for Calendar, Gmail, and Photos.
5. Read the Personal Intelligence explanation before you connect anything.

On iPhone, install **Dreambeans** from the App Store (listing id `6748778630`) and use the same account.

## Connect apps on purpose

Dreambeans requires **at least one** connected app. Google says it works best with more sources, but you control the list and can change it later.

As of the September 2026 post you can connect:

- **Calendar** — trips, birthdays, visits
- **Gmail** — bookings, receipts, shipping notes
- **Photos** — people and places that appear in illustrations
- **Search** — recent interests
- **YouTube** — active hobbies
- **Gemini** — added in the September expansion so stories can pick up nuance from chats you already had

Official privacy note: the apps you enable **inside Dreambeans do not change** Personal Intelligence settings in Gemini Apps or AI Mode. Treat Dreambeans as its own consent surface.

A practical first setup:

1. Connect Calendar and one content source (YouTube or Search).
2. Wait for the first morning collection before you add Gmail or Photos.
3. Add Photos only if you want faces and places in the illustrations.
4. Add Gemini only if you already use that account for personal planning chats.

## How to use the daily collection

Open the app in the morning. You should see a finite set of “beans,” not a never-ending stream.

For each story you can:

- **Read** the generated piece
- **Dive deeper** when Dreambeans pulls extra web context (Google’s example: nearest dog parks or a class near you)
- **Bookmark** items into your library
- **Share** a story you want someone else to see
- **Leave feedback** so later packs get closer to what you actually open

Do not treat a Dreambeans story as a sourced news article. It is a generated brief grounded in *your* connected apps plus public web context. If a date, price, or reservation matters, confirm it in Calendar or Gmail.

## Privacy checklist before you leave it running

Personal Intelligence is the same family of features used in Gemini apps and AI Mode in Search. In Dreambeans it only sees the apps you attach here.

Before you connect Gmail or Photos, decide:

- Is this a personal account, not a work account with other people’s mail?
- Are you comfortable with photos of family appearing as illustrations?
- Will you review connected apps once a month?

Revoke an app the same way you enabled it. If you stop using Dreambeans, disconnect every source inside the experiment rather than only deleting the icon.

Google’s product pages point at the standard [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) and [Terms](https://policies.google.com/terms). Read those plus the in-app Personal Intelligence screen; do not assume a Labs experiment keeps data the same way a shipping Google app does.

## What this is useful for

Dreambeans is a good fit if you already live in Google apps and want a **short morning brief** instead of another social feed.

It is a poor fit if you need:

- Availability outside the United States
- A work or school account
- A fully sourced news product
- An experiment that is guaranteed to stay in the Labs catalog

Google Labs products can stay, merge, or close. Use the library bookmark feature for anything you would miss if the app disappeared.
