Google made Gemini Tasks free for millions of users on November 17, 2026. You can now hand off multi-step jobs like booking appointments, comparing products, or sorting emails without a paid subscription in six countries.

This guide shows exactly how to start a task, what works where, and the safety controls that keep Gemini from acting without your approval. It runs on the Gemini 4 Hydrogen model and connects to apps you already use.

![Person holding a smartphone in a modern office](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1746608943132-065d1d4b3c5d?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## Who can use free Gemini Tasks

You need a personal Google Account, to be 18 or older, and to have Gemini Activity History turned on. Work and school accounts are not supported yet.

Free access is rolling out in the United States, India, Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Australia. In other countries where Gemini works, a Google AI Pro or Ultra plan is still required. The feature is not available in the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, or Nigeria.

Tasks appear in the Gemini mobile app on Android and iOS, the Gemini app on Mac, and at gemini.google.com. The rollout is gradual, so the Chat/Task toggle may not appear on your account immediately.

## How to start your first task

Open the Gemini app or go to gemini.google.com and sign in.

Look for the toggle next to the prompt box. On mobile it sits above the box; on the web it is below. Switch from Chat to Task.

Describe the outcome you want in plain language. Include any limits, budget, preferred time, or things Gemini should avoid. Examples:

- Book an oil change at the nearest Honda dealer for early Friday morning next month.
- Compare three USB-C chargers under $30 with good reviews and help me check out.
- Turn these school emails into calendar events and invite my spouse.

Tap or click submit. Gemini shows its plan, then works in the background. You can close the app. It checks back when it needs approval or finishes.

You can run up to 15 tasks at once. Use the Stop button if you want to halt one.

## What Gemini Tasks can actually do

Normal chat answers a question and stops. A task keeps working across apps and the web until the job is done or it hits a step that needs you.

It can access Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Keep, Maps, Flights, Hotels, YouTube, Photos, and more. It also uses Google Shopping Graph for product comparisons and connected third-party apps such as Instacart or OpenTable.

In the United States it can:

- Call businesses (not available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, and Nebraska). Gemini identifies itself as an AI and businesses can opt out.
- Complete purchases with Google Pay after you approve. You set a spending cap and still earn card rewards. Receipts go to Google Wallet.
- Accept tasks by text (US phone number on an eligible carrier) or by forwarding email to gemini@google.com.

Outside the US those advanced channels are not yet available, but research, scheduling, and app actions still work.

Gemini runs each task in a dedicated, hardware-isolated virtual machine on Google Cloud that is never shared with other users. Prompt-injection filters and Safe Browsing limit risky links.

## Set up text and email channels (US only)

To text Gemini:

1. Open the Gemini app.
2. Tap Settings.
3. Tap Channels.
4. Tap Set up under Text with Gemini.
5. Verify your phone number.

Then send a text describing the job. Gemini replies with a link to the task thread.

To use email, forward messages to gemini@google.com. Gemini turns dates into calendar events and can ask follow-up questions.

## Safety controls and tips

Gemini is designed to pause before critical actions such as clicking Buy or confirming a booking. You can set “Always allow” for routine steps you trust.

Review connected apps in settings and disconnect any you no longer want used. Delete history anytime.

Write clear instructions. “Find the cheapest round-trip flights to Tokyo in March under $900 and put the top three in a Sheet” works better than “flights to Tokyo?”

Add constraints: budget, dates, preferred stores, or “do not purchase without asking.”

If a task misses the mark, give it a thumbs-down so Google can improve the system.

You can schedule recurring tasks. On free plans the timing is once per day in a rough window; paid plans allow more precise hourly schedules.

## Practical examples that save time

A user whose minivan needed service told Gemini to call two nearby dealerships and book the earliest Friday morning slot that fit the calendar. Gemini handled the hold music, found a time, and added the appointment in under five minutes.

Another asked it to analyze past utility bills. Gemini spotted the heat pump running during peak pricing and suggested a simple schedule change that cut the bill.

For groceries, a text with a photo of an empty box added items to an Instacart order. The order arrived while the user was still out.

These examples come from Google’s official announcement. Results depend on the specific apps and permissions you grant.

## Limits and what to expect

Free users share compute resources with everyone else on the free tier. Complex or long-running tasks may take longer or hit temporary limits. Paid plans increase capacity and unlock tighter scheduling.

Some websites still treat AI agents as bots and may block them. Gemini often gets most of the way through and then hands the final click back to you.

Tasks are experimental. They can fail or produce incomplete results. Always review before approving payments or important bookings.

If you do not see the Task toggle, wait for the rollout or confirm you meet the age, account, and history requirements.

For developers who want to add their own apps, Google offers the Connected Apps Platform.
