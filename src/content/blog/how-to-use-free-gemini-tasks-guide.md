Google made Tasks in the Gemini app free on 17 November 2026 for adults in the United States, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia and India. Previously called Spark and limited to paid plans, Tasks now lets you hand off multi-step jobs so Gemini works in the background while you continue your day.

This guide shows how to turn on Task mode, write clear requests, use US-only channels, and keep control of spending and privacy. All details come from Google’s official announcement.

## What Tasks does differently from Chat

Chat answers questions and stops. Tasks receives a goal, plans the steps, works across connected Google apps and the web, then reports back. It can compare products, fill forms, book appointments, analyse bills and, in the US, place calls or complete purchases after your approval.

Tasks runs on the Gemini 4 Hydrogen model for free users. Up to 15 tasks can run at once. Free accounts can set 10 recurring schedules that run once a day in a general window (overnight, daytime or evening). Higher plans unlock more precise timing and additional monitors.

## Who can use free Tasks

You need a personal Google Account (work and school accounts are not supported for free Tasks), age 18 or older, and Gemini Apps Activity turned on. Free access is available only in the six countries listed above. Elsewhere, Google AI Pro or Ultra is required where Gemini is offered, except in the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland and Nigeria where Tasks is not available.

Platforms are the Gemini mobile app (Android and iOS), the Gemini Mac app, and gemini.google.com.

## How to start a task

Follow these steps:

1. Update the Gemini app or open gemini.google.com and sign in.
2. Confirm Gemini Apps Activity is enabled in settings.
3. Locate the Chat / Task toggle. On mobile it sits above the prompt box; on the web it sits below.
4. Switch the toggle to Task.
5. Describe the job in plain language. Include the goal, any budget, deadline, preferred store, and what Gemini should avoid.
6. Submit. Answer follow-up questions and approve critical steps when Gemini pauses.

Example request: “Find the three cheapest USB-C chargers under 30 dollars with at least 65 watts and good reviews, then hold the best option for me.”

You can stop any task with the Stop button in the interface.

## US-only features: text, email and calls

Starting in the United States, two extra channels are available:

- **Text with Gemini**: Open Settings, tap Channels, then Set up under Text with Gemini and verify your phone number. You need a US number on an eligible carrier. Texts work through Google Messages and iMessage over RCS.
- **Email**: Forward a message to gemini@google.com. Gemini replies only to you and never uses Reply-All.

Calling businesses is also US-only and is not available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana and Nebraska. Gemini discloses that it is an AI at the start of the call. Businesses can opt out.

Shopping uses Google Pay with digital tokens on supported cards from Mastercard and Visa (American Express coming). You can set a hard spending cap. Receipts land in Google Wallet. You still earn card points and protections.

## Practical examples that save time

Google’s own examples illustrate the range:

- Book a vehicle service: tell Gemini the model and preferred time window. It checks nearby dealers, calls them, finds a free slot on your calendar and adds the appointment.
- Analyse a high utility bill: grant access to the portal via Password Manager and past emails. Gemini identifies usage patterns such as peak-hour heating.
- Build a grocery order: text or type items, add a photo of an empty box, then hand off to Instacart for checkout.
- Process school emails: forward them to gemini@google.com so dates appear on your calendar and conflicts are flagged.

Gemini connects with more than 60 popular apps through the Connected Apps Platform. It uses Personal Intelligence when you have opted in, so it already knows relevant details from Gmail, Calendar and other Google services without you retyping them.

For heavier reasoning inside a task you can pair it with higher models if your plan supports them. See our guide on [using Gemini 4 Neon for complex work](/blog/how-to-use-gemini-4-neon-guide/).

## Safety and control settings

Tasks runs inside a hardware-isolated virtual machine dedicated to your account and sandboxed from other users. Prompt-injection filters and Google Safe Browsing limit risky links.

Gemini is designed to pause and ask for confirmation before purchases, form submissions or other critical actions. For routine steps you can mark “Always allow.” You can disconnect apps, adjust task settings, or delete history at any time.

Provide thumbs-down feedback when a task misses the mark so the system improves. High-stakes decisions still need your review.

## Tips for better results

- Write the job, not a question. “Book an oil change next Friday morning at the nearest Honda dealer that takes appointments” works better than “oil change?”.
- Set a spending cap before shopping tasks.
- Start with low-risk requests (research, calendar entries) before enabling payments or calls.
- Check the Scheduled section to manage recurring tasks.
- Keep Activity on so Tasks can use context; new privacy controls let you retain history while opting out of model training.

## Limitations to know

Free usage has compute-based limits that Google does not publish in detail. Calling and shopping features are geographically restricted. Some websites still block or challenge automated agents, so Gemini may hand the final click back to you. Work and school accounts cannot use free Tasks.

## Conclusion

Free Gemini Tasks turns the Gemini app into a background assistant that handles multi-step errands in six countries. Flip the toggle from Chat to Task, describe the job with clear constraints, and stay in control of approvals. Try one practical request this week—such as comparing a product or adding calendar dates from an email—and measure the time you save.
