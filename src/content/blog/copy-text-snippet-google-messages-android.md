A delivery address, a one-time code, or a single sentence used to force a full-message copy in Google Messages. The redesigned touch-and-hold menu, which Google said had officially launched by the end of September 2026, puts partial copy on the message itself. You long-press once for actions, long-press again on the text, then drag handles over only the words you need.

The same menu works on phones, tablets, and foldables. Google notes full support for light and dark themes, and the layout is optimized for landscape so the actions stay reachable on a wide screen. This guide covers the stable rollout, the official copy steps, and what to do if the new menu has not appeared yet.

## What the new menu actually changes

Older Google Messages builds parked actions in a top toolbar. Reply, forward, and copy sat far from the bubble you had just pressed, which was awkward on a tall phone and worse in landscape on a foldable.

The redesign uses a floating action menu next to the message. Google's help page says touch and hold works on every message type, not only RCS chats. That includes text, images, videos, links, files, locations, and contact cards. A row of emoji reactions still appears at the top of the bubble. The rest of the conversation blurs so the menu stays in focus.

Common actions in the menu include Reply, Forward, Copy, Star, Delete, Select more, and Info. Edit appears only for a message you sent, and only within 15 minutes. Photos can offer Save. Copy on the menu duplicates the whole message. Partial copy is a second gesture on the text.

9to5Google reported the stable rollout on version 20260910_03_RC02, with some devices still waiting on a server-side switch. If your app is current and the floating menu is missing, force-stop Google Messages from App info and open a chat again.

## Copy only the words you need

Google's support steps and the launch notes line up. Use this sequence on a phone or a wide screen.

1. Open Google Messages and the conversation that contains the text.
2. Touch and hold the message bubble until the emoji row and action menu appear.
3. Touch and hold again directly on the text inside that bubble.
4. Drag the selection handles until only the address, code, link, or sentence is highlighted.
5. In the dialog that appears, choose Copy, Translate, or Select all.

Copy places that selection on the Android clipboard. Open another chat, Gmail, or Keep, touch and hold the input field, and tap Paste. Select all expands the highlight to the full bubble if you started too narrow. Translate runs on the highlighted span, which is useful when a group chat mixes languages and you only need one line.

Google specifically calls out phone numbers and one-time passwords as reasons to copy a part of a message rather than the whole bubble. That avoids pasting extra instructions into a banking app or a form field.

If you already covered [partial copy in the first rollout notes](/blog/google-messages-partial-copy-long-press/), the gesture is the same. The practical change on a tablet is where the dialog sits: it stays near the selection instead of jumping to a top bar you cannot reach with one hand in landscape.

## Use it on tablets and foldables

Landscape used to hide the old toolbar behind the status area or push it to the far edge of the inner display. Google says the new menu is optimized for landscape so actions stay accessible on wider screens.

On a foldable, open the chat on the inner display, rotate to landscape, and long-press a long bubble. The emoji row stays attached to the bubble. The action menu should remain beside it rather than pinning to the top of the window. If you are in split screen with Maps or Chrome, the menu still targets the message you pressed. Shrink the Messages pane if the handles feel cramped, then expand it again after you copy.

Dark theme and light theme both use the same gestures. The blur behind the menu is there in both. You do not need a separate setting for partial copy.

Chat themes are separate from this menu. If you want a custom background on an RCS thread, the steps in the [Google Messages chat themes guide](/blog/google-messages-chat-themes/) still apply. Themes do not change how selection handles work.

## Other actions in the same menu

Partial copy is the headline, but the menu is the path for the rest of message management.

**Reply** quotes that bubble in your next send, which keeps a group thread readable. **Forward** sends the message to another chat. **Star** marks it so you can find it later from conversation details. **Info** shows sender and send time.

**Delete** can offer Delete for everyone or Delete for me, depending on the message and whether the other person uses a compatible RCS setup. **Select more** lets you tap additional bubbles and then delete or forward them together. That is the replacement for hunting a top-bar select control.

**Edit** is limited. Google says it shows only within 15 minutes of a message you sent. After that window, the item disappears from the menu. You cannot edit a message someone else sent.

Reactions stay on the emoji row above the bubble. The menu does not replace reactions. Tap an emoji, or the create option if you use Photomoji, without opening the text-selection dialog.

## Update the app if the menu is missing

The feature is server-gated, so two phones on the same Play Store version can differ for a day or two.

1. Open the Play Store and tap your profile photo.
2. Open Manage apps and device, then Updates available.
3. Update Google Messages if a build is listed. Reporters saw the menu on stable version 20260910_03_RC02.
4. If the update is already installed, force-stop the app and reopen the thread.

Touch and hold does not require RCS. Google states the feature works on standard texts as well as RCS chats. RCS still matters for other chat tools, including themes and some delete-for-everyone cases. Turn it on under your profile photo, Messages settings, RCS chats, if you want those extras.

Samsung phones that still use a separate Samsung Messages app will not show this Google menu. Set Google Messages as the default SMS app if you want these controls.

## Tips that save a second paste

Copy the code only. A bank SMS often wraps the digits in a sentence. Highlight the digits, tap Copy, and paste into the field. Extra words can fail autofill or a form validator.

Use Translate before you forward. Highlight the sentence, tap Translate, read the result, then copy if you need to send it on. That avoids forwarding a full thread.

On a wide screen, select from the middle of a paragraph outward. The handles are easier to grab than the first character when the bubble stretches across a tablet.

If the second long-press only opens reactions, lift and press the text body again, not the emoji row. The first press opens the menu. The second press on the words starts the selection.

## What to remember

Google Messages now treats partial copy as a normal touch-and-hold action, not a hidden overflow item. Open the menu, press the text again, drag the handles, then pick Copy, Translate, or Select all. The same path works in portrait, landscape, and on a foldable inner display, as long as the redesigned menu has reached your account.

Update to a current stable build, force-stop once if the floating menu is absent, and keep RCS on only if you also want themes or richer chat features. For the full bubble, the Copy item on the menu is still one tap.
