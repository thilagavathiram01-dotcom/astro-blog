OpenAI rolled out Live Dictation in ChatGPT on November 18, 2026. The update turns your speech into text as you speak. You no longer wait for transcription after you stop talking. The 10-minute recording limit is gone. The feature is available on all ChatGPT plans and on iOS, Android, web, and the desktop app.

This change helps when you draft long prompts, take notes, or outline ideas. Words appear in the message box while you talk. You can spot errors mid-sentence and correct them before you send.



![Person holding a smartphone showing a speech bubble interface](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1712002641287-f9c8b7161c8f?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)



## What Live Dictation Does

Live Dictation uses the microphone icon in the ChatGPT message bar. It records audio and returns a transcription as editable text. You review the text, fix any mistakes, and send it as a normal message. This differs from Voice mode. Voice mode starts a spoken back-and-forth conversation. Dictation produces text you control before it goes to the model.

OpenAI says the update makes dictation faster and more reliable. You can tap or click the waveform to show or hide the live text. The feature works while the rollout reaches accounts. If the live text does not appear yet, the standard dictation still functions.

## How to Start Live Dictation

Follow these steps on any supported platform. The exact button placement can vary slightly by app version.

1. Open the ChatGPT app or go to chatgpt.com in a browser.
2. Start a new chat or open an existing one.
3. Locate the microphone icon next to the message input field.
4. Tap or click the microphone icon. Grant microphone permission if your device or browser asks.
5. Speak clearly at a normal pace. Words appear in the input field as you talk.
6. Tap or click the waveform if you want to hide or show the live text.
7. Stop speaking when you finish. Review the transcription.
8. Edit any errors, then send the message.

On mobile, hold the phone close enough for clear audio. On desktop, use a headset or a quiet room for better accuracy. Background noise can still affect results.

## Platform Differences

The core experience is the same across devices. A few practical details differ.

- **Mobile apps (iOS and Android):** The microphone sits in the composer bar. After you add text or an attachment, the dictation button stays available. Update the app from the App Store or Play Store if the live text does not appear.
- **Web:** Open chatgpt.com in Chrome, Safari, Edge, or Firefox. Allow microphone access for the site in browser settings.
- **Desktop app:** Download the official ChatGPT app for macOS or Windows from chatgpt.com/download. The same microphone control appears in the input area.

If dictation does not start, check that your account has the update. Rollouts take time. Restart the app or refresh the browser and try again.

## Tips for Better Results

Speak at a steady pace. Pause briefly between sentences so the model can catch up. Use clear pronunciation for names, technical terms, or numbers. If the transcription misses a word, edit it before you send. You can continue speaking after a short pause; the session does not cut off at a fixed time.

For long drafts, dictate in sections. Review each section, then add the next. This keeps the text accurate. Pair Live Dictation with other ChatGPT features such as file uploads when you need to reference documents while speaking.

Check your data controls if you prefer not to share audio for model improvement. Go to Settings, open Data controls, and adjust the option for audio recordings. Business and Enterprise users follow workspace policies.

## When to Use Dictation vs Voice Mode

Use Live Dictation when you want editable text for a prompt, outline, or message. Use Voice mode when you prefer a spoken conversation with ChatGPT responding out loud. You can switch between them in the same chat. For more on voice features, see our guide to [ChatGPT Voice with GPT-5.6 Astra](/blog/chatgpt-voice-gpt-5-6-astra/).
