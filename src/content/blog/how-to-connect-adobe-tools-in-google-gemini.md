Google and Adobe expanded their partnership in November 2026. Adobe’s creative tools now connect directly to Google Gemini. You can enhance photos, design marketing assets, and refine graphics without leaving the chat.

This guide shows how to connect the tools and use them for real tasks. You need a Google account for Gemini and an Adobe ID. A free Adobe account works for many features. Some advanced tools require a paid Adobe plan.

## Why Use Adobe Inside Gemini

Switching between apps slows creative work. The Adobe connector keeps Photoshop, Lightroom, Express, and Firefly capabilities inside Gemini. Describe the result you want. Adobe selects the right tools and steps.

You can upload product photos and ask for consistent lighting and color. You can generate social media variants from one design. You can retouch multiple images at once. The chat keeps context so follow-up edits stay simple.

## Requirements Before You Start

Confirm you have these items ready:

- A Google account signed into Gemini at gemini.google.com or the Gemini mobile app.
- An Adobe ID. Create a free account if you do not have one.
- A stable internet connection.
- Supported browser: Chrome 143+ or Safari 26+ on macOS; Chrome 143+ or Edge 143+ on Windows.

The connector works on web and mobile once connected. Some enterprise Gemini plans may need admin approval for connected apps.

## Step-by-Step: Connect Adobe to Gemini

Follow these steps on a computer first. The connection then works on mobile.

1. Open Google Gemini in your browser and sign in.
2. Click the gear icon (Settings) in the bottom-left corner.
3. Select Personal Intelligence from the menu.
4. Click Connected Apps.
5. Find Adobe in the list and click Connect.
6. Sign in with your Adobe ID or create a free account when prompted.
7. Approve the permissions Gemini requests.

The Adobe toggle should now show as connected. You can disconnect it later from the same menu if needed.

## Start Creating with @Adobe

Open a new chat in Gemini. Type @Adobe in the prompt bar and select the Adobe option that appears. You can also ask “What can Adobe help me with?” to see available actions.

Attach images or documents if the task needs them. Then describe the outcome in plain language. Gemini and Adobe handle the rest.

### Example Workflows

**Consistent product photos:** Upload several product shots. Prompt: “@Adobe give these photos consistent lighting, color, and cropping for an online store.” Review the results and request tweaks.

**Social media assets:** Start with one design. Prompt: “@Adobe turn this into three social-ready sizes while keeping the brand colors and text.” Download the variants.

**Batch retouching:** Upload a set of portraits. Prompt: “@Adobe adjust color and lighting so the set looks uniform.” Adobe applies similar edits across the batch.

**Promotional flyer:** Prompt: “@Adobe design a fall sale flyer using these brand colors and the attached logo.” Specify size and text if you want more control.

More tools continue to roll out. Check the Adobe help page for the current list of supported features.

## What Adobe Tools Are Available

The connector currently brings capabilities from:

- Photoshop for advanced photo edits and retouching.
- Lightroom for color and lighting adjustments.
- Adobe Express for quick designs and social assets.
- Firefly for generative image work.

It does not include every feature from the full desktop apps. Paid Adobe plans unlock additional options. Free accounts still cover many everyday tasks.

Google’s frontier models are also moving into Adobe apps. Image and video models appear in Firefly, Photoshop, Premiere, and Express. Acrobat gains Gemini-powered document tools.

## Tips for Better Results

Be specific in your prompts. Mention lighting style, color palette, crop ratio, or output format when it matters. Attach reference images when possible.

Review outputs before downloading. You can continue the conversation to refine details. “Make the background warmer” or “Crop tighter around the product” usually works.

Watch usage limits. Free Gemini plans have message caps. Adobe features may also have their own limits depending on your plan.

Test with small batches first. Confirm the look you want, then scale to larger sets.

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

**Adobe does not appear in Connected Apps.** Refresh the page, sign out and back in, or try a different browser. Confirm you are on a personal Google account, not a restricted work account.

**Connection fails.** Clear browser cache or disable extensions that block pop-ups. Try the mobile Gemini app after connecting on web.

**Limited features.** Some tools require a paid Adobe plan. Check your Adobe account status.

**Slow results.** Complex edits or large batches take longer. Stay in the chat; results appear when ready.

If problems continue, check Adobe’s help article for the Gemini connector or Google’s Gemini support.

## What’s Next from the Partnership

Adobe and Google continue expanding the integration. Acrobat is coming to Google Drive and Gmail with an “Edit with” option. Creative tools are heading to AI Mode in Google Search. Businesses will get Adobe’s CX Enterprise Coworker agent inside Gemini Enterprise.

Adobe and Google DeepMind are exploring new editing experiences, starting with near-real-time prompt edits in Photoshop. These changes aim to keep creative work inside the apps people already use.

## Conclusion

Connecting Adobe to Gemini removes the friction of app switching for everyday creative tasks. Set it up once, then describe what you need. The tools handle the technical steps while you stay in control of the vision.

Try a simple product photo edit or social asset first. Once the workflow feels natural, expand to larger projects. For more on Google’s latest AI models, check the official Gemini documentation and Adobe’s connector guide.
