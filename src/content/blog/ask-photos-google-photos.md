Keyword search in Google Photos is good at “beach,” “dog,” or a person’s name. **Ask Photos** is the Gemini-powered layer that accepts a full question: what you ate on a trip, which photos would make a lock-screen, or what the handwritten recipe on the fridge actually says.

Google still labels Ask Photos as experimental. It is opt-in, region-limited, and it can be wrong. This guide follows Google Photos Help and Google’s own product posts so you can turn it on, use it, and turn it off without guessing at menus.

## What Ask Photos is (and is not)

Ask Photos is a feature inside the Google Photos app. Gemini models read the query, look through photos and videos that are already backed up to your account, and return a results page. Simple one-word searches often stop there. Complex questions keep running in the background so the app can highlight a smaller set of matches or a short answer.

It is not the same as:

- Classic Photos search (people, places, things, text in images)
- The **Ask** button on a single open photo (chat, identify, “Help me edit”)
- Connecting the Gemini app with `@Google Photos`

Google’s Help pages treat those as related but separate paths. Use classic search when you know the keyword. Use Ask Photos when the request is a sentence. Use the on-photo Ask button when you already have the image open.

Eligibility, from the Gemini features in Photos privacy hub (updated 18 February 2026):

- You must be **18 or older**
- You must be in a launched country or region
- **Location estimates** should be enabled in Photos
- Functionality still varies by language and device

Ask Photos is **not available on the Photos website**. If the toggle is missing, you are not in the current wave. Use classic search instead of hunting for a hidden flag.

## Turn Ask Photos on

Opting in renames the Search tab to **Ask**. Google documents two official routes.

### From the Search tab

1. Open the **Google Photos** app on Android (or iOS).
2. Tap **Search** at the bottom.
3. If you see a welcome or **Try Ask Photos** banner, tap **Try now** (wording can be **Try asking** / **Turn on**).
4. Finish onboarding. Do not skip the face-group step if the app shows it.

If you tap **Switch to classic search** during that welcome flow, Ask Photos stays off and Search remains the default.

### From Settings

1. Open Photos and tap your **profile photo**.
2. Open **Photos settings → Preferences → Gemini features in Photos**.
3. Turn on **Search with Ask Photos**.

If neither path appears, you are not eligible yet. That is expected.

### Confirm your face group

Onboarding may ask you to mark which face cluster is you and to label other people. Google says Ask Photos treats onboarding as incomplete if you skip the “me” face group. Names you add are saved with People & pets in Collections. Better labels usually mean better answers to questions like “photos of my sister at the lake.”

## Search the library in plain language

1. Tap the **Ask** tab.
2. Type the question, or tap the **microphone**. Google states that voice audio is **not stored**; only that the query was voice or text is saved.
3. Wait for the first grid. For “beach” that grid is often enough.
4. For a longer question, stay on the page while Gemini narrows the set.
5. Use the **classic / Ask Photos** toggle at the top of results when you want keyword search back without turning the feature off.

Prompts that match what Google has published:

- “What did I eat on my trip to Barcelona?”
- “Photos that’d make great phone backgrounds”
- “Find my photos that feel like spring”
- “Find more photos like this”
- “What’s my license plate?”

Name a person, a place, a year, or a feeling. Follow-up questions work: after a broad spring set, ask which of those were taken in one park, then which flowers are in the frame.

Treat answers as a shortlist. Open the original photo before you trust a plate number, a date, or a recipe.

## Use the Ask button on one photo

The in-photo **Ask** control is documented for eligible Android and iOS users in the U.S. It only works on images that are **backed up**.

1. Open a backed-up photo.
2. Tap **Ask**.
3. Read the short description Photos may already show, or tap a chip such as **Tell me more**, **related photos**, or **Help me edit**.
4. Type a follow-up: identify a dish, transcribe handwriting, describe an item for a listing, or request a specific edit.

Google’s February 2026 tips post is useful here: Ask can name a trail you forgot, write a product description, or turn a photographed recipe into a grocery list you can copy out of the chat.

Edits create a **new copy**. The original stays in the library. Review the new file before you share it.

## Remember List

If you tell Ask Photos a fact it should reuse (“this is my apartment building,” “Maya is my niece”), the chat may show **Added to Remember List**. You can undo that line in the moment.

Later:

- Open **Photos settings → Preferences → Gemini features in Photos** and open the Remember List, or
- Use the three-dot menu on an Ask Photos text response

Delete any fact you do not want reused. Do not store addresses, ID numbers, or medical details there.

## Ask from the Gemini app

You can search the same library from Gemini without leaving the assistant:

1. Open the Gemini app or [gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com) on the same account as Photos.
2. Include `@Google Photos` or “my photos” in the prompt.
3. Connect Photos if Gemini offers the link. If Ask Photos is off, Gemini may ask you to opt in.

Google’s Help article notes that the Photos app connection in Gemini is English-only for now and is not available inside Gemini in Google Messages. Uploading a single image to a Gemini prompt is a different path and follows Gemini’s privacy notice, not the Photos-only notice.

## Privacy controls worth using

From Google’s privacy hub:

- Personal Photos data is **not used for ads**
- Google says it does **not** train generative models *outside* Google Photos on your personal Photos data
- Humans do not review Ask Photos *responses* unless you send feedback, or in rare abuse cases
- Queries may be reviewed to improve Photos after Google disconnects them from your account and strips sensitive content. You can **opt out of query donation** in Gemini features settings
- Feedback can include the conversation, an optional screenshot, and metadata lists of photos, albums, and face groups that were studied—not the pixels unless you attach a screenshot

Turn Ask Photos off anytime:

1. Profile → **Photos settings → Preferences → Gemini features in Photos**
2. Disable **Search with Ask Photos**

Classic Search returns. Your library is unchanged.

## A first session that actually tests it

1. Opt in and finish the face-group step.
2. Run one keyword (“dog”) and flip the classic / Ask toggle so you see both result styles.
3. Ask one question you can verify (“what did I eat the first night in [city]”).
4. Open one photo, tap Ask, and try **Help me edit** on a copy.
5. Open the Remember List and delete anything you did not mean to save.
6. If Ask Photos is slower or worse than keyword search for your library, turn it off. That is a supported choice, not a broken install.

## Limits to plan around

- Experimental: wrong people, wrong places, and missed text-in-image hits are possible
- Not on web
- Ask button and some edit flows are still limited by country
- Only backed-up items participate
- Connecting Photos to Gemini or sharing a file into another Google product uses *that* product’s policy
- Do not use Ask Photos as the source of truth for plates, IDs, medical labels, or anything you would not want in a feedback packet

## Conclusion

Ask Photos is useful when the question is a sentence and the photo is already backed up. Keep classic search one toggle away for single keywords. Label face groups if you want name-based answers, keep the Remember List short, and shut the feature off if Gemini search is slower than the old grid.

Start with one verifiable question and one edit on a copy. That is enough to decide whether the experimental layer belongs on your phone.
