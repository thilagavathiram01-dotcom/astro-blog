Google Play Store version 53.9 adds a reminder when Play Protect stays off for an extended period. The change arrived with the November 2026 Google System updates alongside Play services v26.43.

Play Protect is Android’s built-in scanner for harmful apps. It checks apps from the Play Store and from other sources. Google recommends keeping it on. The new reminder helps users who turned it off and forgot.

This guide shows how to check the status, respond to the reminder, and keep scanning active. It also covers related settings that improve detection.

## Why the Play Protect Reminder Matters

Play Protect runs safety checks before you download apps from the Play Store. It also scans installed apps periodically. If it finds a potentially harmful app, it can warn you, disable the app, or remove it.

Many people turn scanning off temporarily to install an APK from outside the Play Store. Some forget to turn it back on. The reminder in Play Store v53.9 surfaces after the feature stays off for a while. Google has not published the exact delay or how often the message appears.

The reminder applies to phones and PCs that use the Play Store. It is part of the same November update that brought the Android Family Hub and automatic Quick Share photo backups to Windows.

Keeping Play Protect on reduces exposure to malware that arrives through sideloading or compromised apps. Official guidance states that Play Protect is on by default and should stay enabled.

## How to Check Whether Play Protect Is On

Open the Google Play Store app. Tap your profile icon in the top-right corner. Select Play Protect.

The main screen shows the last scan time and whether harmful apps were found. Tap the settings gear in the top-right of the Play Protect screen. Confirm that “Scan apps with Play Protect” is switched on.

If the toggle is off, the reminder will eventually appear in the Play Store. You can turn the feature on immediately without waiting for the prompt.

On some devices, Settings > Security also shows an overall app security status. The Play Store path is the direct control for the scanner.

## Steps to Enable or Re-Enable Play Protect

Follow these steps if scanning is off or if you see the reminder:

1. Open the Google Play Store.
2. Tap your profile icon at the top right.
3. Tap Play Protect.
4. Tap the settings gear icon.
5. Switch “Scan apps with Play Protect” to on.
6. Optionally switch “Improve harmful app detection” to on. This lets Play Protect send unknown apps to Google for analysis.
7. Return to the main Play Protect screen and tap Scan to run a check now.

Google’s support page confirms that Play Protect is on by default and recommends keeping it enabled. The same page describes the improve detection toggle for users who install apps from outside the Play Store.

After enabling, the reminder should stop appearing. If you turn scanning off again for a legitimate reason, remember to switch it back on afterward.

## What the Reminder Looks Like and How to Respond

Google’s release notes state that you will get a reminder if Play Protect stays off for a while. Exact wording and placement are not detailed in the public notes. Expect a message inside the Play Store that points you toward the Play Protect settings.

When the reminder appears, open it or go directly to Play Protect as described above. Turn scanning on. You do not need to uninstall anything unless Play Protect later reports a harmful app.

If you intentionally left scanning off, the reminder still appears. You can dismiss it for the moment, but the safer choice is to re-enable scanning and only pause it briefly when needed.

Play Protect also cannot be turned off during phone calls on many devices. This is an earlier protection against social-engineering scams. The November reminder adds another layer for users who disable the feature outside of calls.

## Related November 2026 System Updates

The same Play services v26.43 and Play Store v53.9 release includes other changes. You can now access the Android Family Hub as a dedicated space for family management tools in Settings. Quick Share can automatically back up photos and videos from Android to Windows. Google Password Manager lets you choose which credentials to keep when importing conflicting passwords and passkeys.

Game detail pages in the Play Store also gained at-a-glance highlights for play style, review summaries, and community activity.

These updates roll out gradually through Google Play services and the Play Store. They do not require a full Android firmware update. Check Settings > Google > All services > System services to see the installed version of Play services.

## Tips for Keeping Play Protect Useful

Leave “Scan apps with Play Protect” on most of the time. Turn it off only when you must install a trusted APK and switch it back on immediately afterward.

Enable “Improve harmful app detection” if you sideload apps. This helps Google analyze unknown packages and improves protection for other users.

Review Play Protect notifications promptly. If it flags an app, investigate before dismissing the warning. You can uninstall the app from the notification or from the Play Protect screen.

Check your device’s Play Protect certification status in the Play Store: profile icon > Settings > About. A certified device receives the full set of Play protections.

Combine Play Protect with the November security patch level of 2026-11-05 or later. The scanner and the system patches address different layers of risk.

## Conclusion

The Play Protect reminder in Google Play Store v53.9 is a small but practical addition. It surfaces when scanning has been left off, making it easier to restore protection without hunting through settings.

Open the Play Store, go to Play Protect, and confirm the scan toggle is on. Run a manual scan after you enable it. The same November update also brings Family Hub, Quick Share backups, and password import controls.

For official details on how Play Protect works, see Google’s support page. Keep scanning enabled and install the latest system updates for the strongest baseline protection on Android.
