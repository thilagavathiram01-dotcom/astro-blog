Google rolled out free access to Tasks in the Gemini app on November 17, 2026. Powered by the Gemini 4 Hydrogen model, it lets hundreds of millions of people in the U.S., Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India hand off multi-step work instead of chatting back and forth.

You flip a toggle, describe the job, and Gemini works in the background. It can call businesses, analyze bills from email, compare products, or schedule appointments. It pauses for your confirmation on sensitive actions.

## What Changed on November 17

Tasks started as Gemini Spark for select testers and later became available to Google AI Ultra and Pro subscribers in some markets. The November 17 update opens the Chat-to-Task toggle on the free tier for users in six countries, running on Hydrogen by default.

Advanced actions such as phone calls via Google Duplex, purchases through the Agent Payments Protocol with Google Pay, email forwarding to gemini@google.com, and texting via Channels are rolling out starting in the U.S. Other countries receive the core Task mode first.

You must be 18 or older and use a personal Google Account. Work and school accounts are not supported yet. History must be on. The feature is not available in the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, or Nigeria.

## How to Start a Task

Open the Gemini app on Android or iOS, or go to gemini.google.com. On the home screen you will see the Chat and Task toggle. Switch it to Task.

Type a clear description of what you need done. You can mention a time if it is recurring. Gemini shows a plan and starts working. You can leave the app; it notifies you when finished or if it needs input.

For U.S. users who want to text or email instead of opening the app:

1. Open Gemini and tap Settings.
2. Tap Channels.
3. Tap Set up under Text with Gemini.
4. Verify your phone number. You need a U.S. number on an eligible carrier.

You can also forward emails to gemini@google.com once Channels are set up. Gemini reads the email and can act on it, such as adding an event to Calendar after you confirm.

## Safety and Confirmation Flows

Google designed Tasks to check with you before irreversible steps. It asks for confirmation or prior approval before clicking Buy. It asks clarifying questions if your instruction is ambiguous.

Each task runs in a hardware-isolated virtual machine on Google Cloud’s Agent Compute Engine. Your data stays inside Google’s ecosystem. Connected Apps such as Gmail and Calendar are used only with the permissions you already granted.

You can set a hard spending cap for purchases. You can also mark certain routine actions as “Always allow” after you review them once. Transcripts of calls are available for review.

## Practical Examples

Josh Woodward, Vice President of Labs for the Gemini app, described using Tasks to call local car dealerships and schedule a service appointment for a minivan. Gemini contacted the shops, compared availability, and added the appointment to Calendar after approval.

Another example involved a surprise $389 electric bill. Gemini reviewed past statements in email, identified that a heat pump was running during peak pricing, found a discount code, and noted an additional $10 in savings.

You can ask it to:

- Prepare a morning briefing from unread emails and today’s Calendar events.
- Compare prices for a specific phone charger and add the best option under a spending cap.
- Research a three-day weekend itinerary with transit and restaurant options, then add selected items to Calendar.
- Forward a party invitation email and have Gemini RSVP after you confirm the date.

Start with low-stakes tasks such as research or Calendar updates. Expand to calls or purchases only after you have tested the confirmation flow.

## Tips for Better Results

Be specific in your first message. Include any constraints such as budget, preferred time, or location. Gemini can ask follow-up questions, but clear starting instructions reduce back-and-forth.

Connect the apps you want it to use before you start the task. Review permissions in Settings under Connected Apps.

If a result is wrong, use the thumbs-down feedback. This helps Google improve the system.

Monitor the first few tasks closely. Once you trust the confirmation checkpoints, you can let longer jobs run while you do other work.

## Conclusion

Flipping the toggle from Chat to Task and describing the job is now enough for hundreds of millions of people to hand routine multi-step work to Gemini 4 Hydrogen. Start with calendar and email tasks today, set spending and confirmation preferences, and expand to calls or purchases only where they are available. The feature is designed to check with you before it spends money or books something irreversible—use that checkpoint.
