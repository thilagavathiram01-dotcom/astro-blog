Finding the right app used to mean scrolling endless lists or guessing the perfect keyword. Ask Play changes that. It turns Google Play search into a conversation powered by Gemini.

You ask in plain language. The store answers with recommendations, summaries, and now side-by-side comparisons. It draws from store listings and user reviews so you see real differences instead of marketing claims.

This guide shows exactly how to open Ask Play, ask useful questions, compare apps, and avoid common pitfalls.

## What Ask Play does

Google introduced Ask Play as a Gemini-powered overlay for deeper discovery. It already answered most app-specific questions inside listings. In October 2026 the company expanded it so the same tool works from the main search bar and can generate comparisons.

Key capabilities:

- Natural language recommendations instead of keyword matching
- Follow-up questions that keep context
- Ask Play Highlights that summarize complex searches above regular results
- Side-by-side app comparisons that highlight differences and user sentiment
- Answers grounded in the developer’s store listing and user reviews

The feature is rolling out through Play Store updates. Update the Play Store app from the Play Store itself if you do not see the new options yet.

## How to open Ask Play

Start from the Play Store home screen or any search results page.

1. Open the Google Play Store app on your Android phone or tablet.
2. Tap the search bar at the top.
3. Type a natural question instead of a short keyword. Example: “best offline note-taking app for students.”
4. Look for an Ask Play button or chat-style prompt near the search bar or results.
5. Tap it to enter the conversation view.

On some devices the entry point is an “Ask Play” chip or a sparkle icon. If the button is missing, update the Play Store and restart the app.

You can also open an individual app listing and use the “Ask Play about this app” card that appears on supported pages. That version focuses on questions about one title.

## Ask useful questions

Vague prompts produce vague answers. Specific ones surface better matches.

Good examples:

- “Which budgeting apps work offline and support multiple currencies?”
- “Show me running apps that include interval training and watch connectivity.”
- “I need a photo editor that can remove backgrounds without a subscription.”

After the first answer, continue the conversation. Ask “Which of these has the highest rating for offline use?” or “Does the top one support dark mode?”

The system keeps context, so you do not need to restate the full requirement each time.

## Compare apps side by side

The October 2026 update added direct comparisons. Once Ask Play suggests two or more apps, you can request a comparison.

Try these prompts:

- “Compare these apps for safety features, watch connectivity, and route tracking.”
- “Which one has better offline support and lower subscription cost?”
- “Summarize user sentiment on battery impact for both.”

Google states that comparisons pull from the developer’s store listing and user reviews. Expect columns or cards that cover monetization, connectivity, key features, and common review themes.

Read the summary, then open the actual listings if you need full screenshots or recent reviews. AI summaries can miss recent updates.

## Use Ask Play Highlights

On many searches you now see an AI-generated highlight block above the regular results. It names a few apps and gives a one-sentence reason for each.

The highlight changes with phrasing. “Apps for learning chess tactics” may surface different titles than “best chess trainer for beginners.” Treat it as a starting point, not a ranked list.

If the highlight looks wrong, refine the question or scroll to the organic results. You can also ask Ask Play to explain why it chose a particular app.

## Practical tips

Update the Play Store app first. New features arrive through server-side and app updates.

Keep questions focused on one job. “Best app for tracking macros and meal prep” works better than a long list of unrelated features.

Verify critical claims. Check the official listing for pricing, permissions, and recent update notes before you install.

Combine with other tools. If you already use Gemini for research, you can later ask the Gemini app about an app you found in Play. Related: our [Gemini Intelligence on Android guide](/blog/gemini-intelligence-android-how-to/).

Watch for regional and language limits. Some advanced pieces started in English and the United States before expanding.

## Limitations to know

Ask Play is helpful but not perfect. Early tests showed occasional inaccurate answers about specific features. Always open the listing to confirm pricing, offline support, or hardware requirements.

Not every app page shows the “Ask Play about this app” card yet. The feature still rolls out gradually.

Comparisons depend on the quality of the store listing and reviews. Sparse listings produce thinner summaries.

## Conclusion

Ask Play turns the Play Store into a conversational discovery tool. Ask a clear question, review the recommendations and comparisons, then verify the details on the actual listing.

Update your Play Store app today and try one focused search. You will spend less time scrolling and more time installing the app that actually matches what you need.
