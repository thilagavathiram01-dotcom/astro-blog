UK drivers now have a simple way to compare petrol and diesel prices without switching apps. Google Maps and Waze display near real-time fuel costs at forecourts, pulled from the government’s Fuel Finder scheme.

This feature launched on 8 October 2026. It arrives as average diesel prices briefly topped £2 a litre. The Department for Energy Security and Net Zero estimates car-owning households could save around £40 a year by shopping around.

## What the Fuel Price Feature Shows

Open Google Maps and search for “petrol” or “fuel”. Nearby stations appear with their current prices displayed on the map and in the list. Tap a station to see the full breakdown of available fuels.

The same data appears in Waze. Google only shows a price when it has high confidence the figure is current. Stations must report changes to Fuel Finder within 30 minutes. Coverage reaches about 99 percent of fuel sold on UK roads.

## How Fuel Finder Powers the Prices

Fuel Finder is the UK government platform that collects live pump prices. Forecourts must submit updates promptly or face enforcement action. Apps including the AA, RAC Fuel Watch, Petrol Prices, and now Google Maps and Waze draw from this feed.

Sarah-Jayne Williams, Director of Global Product Partnerships for Geo at Google, noted that the integration helps drivers make intentional choices. Prices reflect official submissions rather than user reports.

## Step-by-Step: Check Prices on Google Maps

1. Open the Google Maps app on Android or iOS, or visit maps.google.com on a computer.
2. Make sure location services are enabled for accurate nearby results.
3. Tap the search bar and type “petrol”, “diesel”, or “fuel”.
4. Review the pins and list. Prices appear next to stations that have current data.
5. Tap any station for the complete list of fuel types and last update time.
6. Select Directions if you want navigation to that forecourt.

The process is identical in Waze. Search for the same terms and compare options before you leave home or while you plan a route.

## Tips to Get the Best Price

Compare several stations within a short distance. A few pence per litre adds up on a full tank. Check the update time shown on the station page; older figures may lag behind a recent price change.

Use the feature along a planned route as well as near your current location. On longer trips this can reveal cheaper options a short detour away. Combine it with Google Maps traffic data so the total time cost stays low.

## Limitations to Know

Not every station will show a price every time. Google withholds figures when it cannot confirm they are up to date. Motorway services and some rural sites may update less frequently.

Always confirm the price at the pump before you fill. The displayed figure is near real-time, not guaranteed live at the exact moment you arrive. Prices can still change between the last submission and your visit.

## Conclusion

Checking live fuel prices on Google Maps takes under a minute and requires no extra app. Search for petrol or fuel, compare the figures, and choose the best option for your journey. With Fuel Finder covering nearly all UK sales, the data is reliable enough for everyday use.

Start with your next fill-up. Open Maps, search, and see how much you can save.
