Google added in-chat file generation and editing to the Gemini app in late October 2026. On Android you can create and refine PDFs without leaving the conversation. Other formats stay on the web for now.

This guide shows the exact flow on mobile, what works today, and how to get clean results. It follows Google’s official announcement and help pages.

## What the feature covers on Android

PDF preview and editing work on both the Gemini mobile app and the web. You can generate a PDF, expand it, type changes, highlight sections, and ask Gemini to rewrite parts.

Google Docs, Sheets, slide decks, Markdown, TXT, and code files can be generated and edited only in the web version at gemini.google.com. On your phone, stick to PDFs for full in-chat editing.

The feature is available to all users in every language where the Gemini app runs. No paid plan is required.

If you have not set Gemini as your default assistant yet, finish that first so voice and power-button shortcuts reach the right place. See our guide on [setting Gemini as the default assistant](/blog/gemini-default-assistant-android/).

## Generate a PDF from a prompt

1. Open the Gemini app on your Android phone.
2. Start a new chat or continue an existing one.
3. Type a clear request. Examples: “Create a one-page PDF study guide from these lecture notes” or “Make a PDF shopping list with categories and quantities.”
4. Attach photos of handwritten notes if you want Gemini to read them.
5. Send the prompt.

Gemini returns a preview card under the reply. Tap the card or the expand control to open the full-screen editor.

The preview loads the file so you can scroll and check layout before you edit.

## Edit the PDF inside the chat

Once the preview is open:

- Tap text to edit it yourself. The keyboard appears and changes save as you go.
- Highlight a sentence or paragraph, then ask Gemini to rewrite it, shorten it, or change the tone.
- Ask follow-up questions about a highlighted section. Gemini answers in the chat and can update the file.
- Use the undo button if a change goes wrong.

You can also type a new prompt in the chat such as “Add a bullet list of key takeaways at the end” and Gemini updates the open file.

Export options appear after editing. Download the PDF to your device or share it. On the web you can also send the file to Google Drive.

## Turn handwritten notes into a study guide

Google’s own example is useful for students:

1. Photograph your handwritten lecture notes or diagrams.
2. Attach the images in the Gemini chat.
3. Prompt: “Turn these notes into a clean PDF study guide with headings, bullet points, and a short summary.”
4. Review the preview. Highlight any section that looks wrong and ask Gemini to fix the wording or add a missing diagram description.
5. Export the finished PDF.

The same flow works for meeting notes, recipes, or trip plans. Keep the original photos if you need to verify details later.

## Tips for better results

Be specific in the first prompt. Name the file type (“PDF”), the length, and the structure you want. Vague requests produce generic layouts.

Upload clear photos. Blurry handwriting reduces accuracy. Gemini handles printed text better than messy notes.

Edit in small chunks. Highlight one section at a time instead of asking for a full rewrite. This keeps the rest of the document stable.

Check facts before you export. Gemini can invent numbers or dates. Verify anything important against your original notes.

On mobile, stick to PDFs. If you need a Google Doc or Sheet, open the same chat on a computer and continue there. The conversation history syncs.

## Limits and workarounds

Mobile editing is PDF-only right now. Other formats require the web. If you start on the phone and need a spreadsheet, copy the key points into a new prompt on gemini.google.com.

Large files can take a few seconds to preview. Wait for the card to finish loading before you expand it.

You cannot yet open an existing PDF from your phone’s Files app and edit it inside Gemini. Generation starts from a prompt or uploaded images.

Privacy note: uploaded notes and the generated file stay in your Gemini activity unless you delete the chat. Review your activity settings if the content is sensitive.

## Conclusion

On Android, Gemini now lets you move from a prompt or a photo of notes to a finished PDF without switching apps. Generate the file, expand the preview, edit by hand or by highlighting, then export.

For Docs, Sheets, or slides, use the web version. The same chat works across devices. Start with clear prompts and small edits, and you will get usable documents faster than copying text into another app.
