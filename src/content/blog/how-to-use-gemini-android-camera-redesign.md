Google has rolled out a redesigned in-app camera for the Gemini app on Android. The update replaces the old fullscreen viewfinder with a bottom sheet that opens inline on the homepage or inside a conversation. You can drag the sheet up with a smooth animation so it fills most of the screen and extends behind the system navigation bar.

The redesign first appeared in the Gemini app on iOS. It has now reached Android users on both the beta and stable channels starting with Google app version 17.76. If the new interface does not appear right away, force-stop the Google app and reopen Gemini.

The old fullscreen camera still appears when you launch the camera from the Gemini system overlay. The new bottom-sheet design applies only when you open the camera from the plus menu next to the prompt box.

## Open the new camera interface

Follow these steps to reach the updated camera:

1. Open the Gemini app on your Android phone.
2. Tap the plus button in the prompt box at the bottom of the screen.
3. Select the camera option from the menu.
4. The viewfinder appears as a bottom sheet over the current screen. Part of the Gemini page remains visible above it.
5. Drag the sheet upward. It expands with an animation until the viewfinder takes most of the display.

The controls sit at the bottom of the sheet. A back button appears on the left of the shutter button. A three-dot menu sits on the right. Tapping the three-dot button opens an animated menu with flash controls and the option to switch to the front-facing camera. You can also swipe down on the sheet to close the camera.

## Adjust settings and capture

Once the sheet is open, frame your shot in the viewfinder. Tap the three-dot menu to toggle the flash or switch cameras. The menu animates open so the options stay close to the shutter button. After you take the photo, it attaches to your prompt so you can add text and send the request to Gemini.

The edge-to-edge layout keeps the viewfinder large while the bottom sheet stays anchored to the conversation. This makes it easier to capture something, attach it, and continue chatting without leaving the app flow. The design reduces the number of full-screen transitions compared with the previous interface.

If you prefer the older layout for some tasks, launch the camera from the Gemini overlay instead of the plus menu. That path still uses the fullscreen viewfinder.

## Tips for the new camera

- Update the Google app to version 17.76 or later before you look for the change.
- Force-stop the Google app if the bottom sheet does not appear after the update.
- Drag the sheet only partway if you want to keep more of the chat visible while you frame the shot.
- Use the swipe-down gesture when your thumb is already near the bottom of the screen. The back button remains available as a fallback.
- Combine a photo from the new camera with a text prompt to describe what you want Gemini to analyze or generate.

The bottom-sheet approach keeps you in context. You can see the conversation above the viewfinder, capture the image, and stay inside the same chat. This matches the direction of other recent Gemini interface updates that reduce full-screen switches.

## Related media tools in Gemini

Around the same time as the camera redesign, Gemini added GIF creation powered by the Omni 1.2 Flash model. You can generate short looping GIFs of about three seconds from a text prompt, images, or a video. Select GIF from the format menu or ask Gemini to create a GIF in your prompt. For the full steps, see our guide on [creating custom GIFs with Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash](/blog/create-gifs-gemini-omni-1-2-flash/).

The camera redesign and GIF tools both expand the ways you can add visual content inside Gemini without leaving the app. The camera handles real-time capture while the GIF feature turns descriptions or references into short animations.

## Conclusion

The redesigned Gemini camera on Android opens as a bottom sheet from the plus menu, expands with a drag gesture, and keeps the viewfinder nearly edge-to-edge. Flash and front-camera controls live in the three-dot menu, and you can close the sheet by swiping down or using the back button. The change is live in Google app version 17.76 and later on both beta and stable builds. Force-stop the app if you do not see it yet. The older fullscreen camera remains available from the system overlay.

These updates make it faster to attach a photo to a Gemini prompt while staying inside the conversation. Pair the new camera with other recent Gemini media features for more flexible visual workflows on Android.
