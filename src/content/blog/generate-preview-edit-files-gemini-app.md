Google rolled out a major update to the Gemini app on October 29, 2026. You can now generate, preview, and edit documents, PDFs, presentations, spreadsheets, and code right inside your chat. No more copying text into another app just to fix a typo or adjust a chart.

This works for all Gemini users in every language where the app is available. Personal Google accounts and Workspace plans both get the feature. PDF preview and editing works on mobile and web. Other formats stay web-only for now.

## What the New File Tools Cover

Gemini creates the file from your prompt and shows a live preview in the conversation. You expand it to full screen, type changes yourself, highlight a section to ask a question, or tell Gemini to refine the content. When finished, open the result in Google Slides or Sheets, download it, or upload to Drive.

Supported actions include:

- Slide decks: outline or full slides, adjust tone and structure, then open in Google Slides or export as PPTX/PDF.
- Spreadsheets: trackers, tables, and data models that open directly in Google Sheets.
- Documents and PDFs: draft Google Docs, LaTeX, Markdown, TXT, or PDF files, edit inline, and save to Drive.

On Android and iPhone the mobile apps currently limit in-chat editing to PDFs. Everything else requires the web version at gemini.google.com.

## Step-by-Step: Create and Edit a File

Open the Gemini app or go to gemini.google.com. Start a new chat and describe what you need. Clear prompts produce better results.

1. Write a specific request. Example: "Create a one-page PDF study guide from these lecture notes. Include key formulas and a simple diagram." Upload photos of handwritten notes if you have them.
2. Gemini generates the file and shows the preview inside the chat.
3. Tap or click the preview to expand it to full screen.
4. Type direct edits in the full-screen view, or highlight any text and ask Gemini to rewrite that section or turn a table into a graph.
5. Ask follow-up questions about the content without leaving the preview.
6. When the file looks right, download it, open it in the matching Google Workspace app, or save a copy to Drive.

The same flow works for slide decks and spreadsheets on the web. On mobile you get the full experience only with PDFs.

## Tips for Better Results

Upload source material first when you have it. Handwritten notes, existing spreadsheets, or rough outlines give Gemini concrete data to work from instead of inventing everything.

Be precise about the output format. Say "PDF study guide" or "Google Sheets tracker with columns for date, task, and status" so Gemini chooses the right file type.

Highlight small sections instead of asking for a complete rewrite. This keeps your edits focused and reduces the chance of Gemini changing parts you already like.

Check the platform limit before you start. If you need to edit a Google Doc or slide deck on the go, switch to the web version on your phone's browser.

## How This Fits with Other Gemini Features

The new editing tools sit on top of the file generation capability Google added earlier in 2026. They also complement Gemini's existing Workspace integrations. If you already use Gemini inside Google Docs or Sheets, the in-chat versions give you a faster path for one-off files.

## Limitations to Know

PDF editing is the only option that works fully on the Android and iOS apps today. Docs, Sheets, Slides, Markdown, TXT, and code files require the web. Google has not published a timeline for expanding mobile support.

Generated files still need a human review. Gemini can invent numbers or miss context from uploaded images, so verify any data, formulas, or citations before you share the file.

Large or complex requests may hit context limits. Break big projects into smaller steps (outline first, then full content) for more reliable results.

## Conclusion

The October 29 update removes the last manual step between a Gemini prompt and a finished file. Generate the document, preview it, make quick edits, and export—all without switching apps. Start with a simple PDF on your phone or a full slide deck on the web to see how the new flow works for your own tasks.
