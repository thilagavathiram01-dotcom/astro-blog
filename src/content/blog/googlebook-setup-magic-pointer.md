Google opened [pre-orders for Googlebook](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/googlebook/pre-order-googlebook/) on 21 September 2026. The devices are partner laptops from Acer, ASUS, Dell, HP, and Lenovo. They run Googlebook OS: Android under the hood, desktop habits from ChromeOS, and Gemini Intelligence on the cursor, keyboard, and taskbar.

This guide is a first-week playbook based on Google's launch posts. Hardware ships starting 4 October in the United States and 5 October in Canada, the United Kingdom, Ireland, France, Germany, and Australia. Prices start at $899.

## What you are buying

Googlebook is not a Chromebook rebadge and not a Windows clone. Official specs for the first wave:

- Premium chassis in aluminum, magnesium alloy, or carbon fiber
- Touchscreens up to 2.8K OLED
- Haptic glass trackpads and backlit keyboards
- Intel or Qualcomm processors plus an NPU rated over 45 TOPS
- Up to 14 hours of battery life
- Glowbar on the lid that shows charge and reacts when you talk to Gemini
- Updates and feature drops for up to 10 years

Every unit includes 12 months of Google AI Pro (5TB of storage and Gemini Advanced tools) plus three months of YouTube Premium, Adobe Photoshop, and related creative apps. A year of GeForce NOW is also bundled.

If you already use Gemini Intelligence on a Pixel or Galaxy phone, the same family of tools—task automation, Gemini Live, Rambler, Create My Widget—lands on the laptop. See our [Gemini Intelligence on Android guide](/blog/gemini-intelligence-android/) for the phone side of that stack.

## Pre-order and pick a model

1. Open the official storefront at [googlebook.google](https://googlebook.google/).
2. Compare the Acer, ASUS, Dell, HP, and Lenovo flagships. Google lists them as the first wave; exact RAM, storage, and screen sizes vary by partner.
3. Confirm the included software bundle (Google AI Pro, YouTube Premium, Photoshop) before you pay.
4. Pre-order on the Google Store, Best Buy, or another listed retailer.
5. Plan for shelf dates: 4 October in the U.S., 5 October in the other launch countries.

Buy the machine you will carry. The $899 floor is the entry Acer-class model. Dell XPS-class units start higher. Do not assume every partner SKU has the same OLED panel or the same processor family.

## Pair the laptop with your Android phone

Googlebook is built to treat your phone as the same account, not a Bluetooth accessory.

Google's setup path:

1. Sign in with the Google account you already use on Android.
2. Accept the transfer of settings, saved passwords, Wi-Fi networks, and messages. Google says that move is backed by end-to-end encryption.
3. Keep the phone nearby the first afternoon so Continue On, Files, and Cast My Apps can finish linking.

Three tools then keep work moving between screens.

**Continue On.** Start a task on the phone—Google's example is a BandLab project on a commute—and resume it from the Googlebook taskbar.

**Files.** Open the laptop Files app to view, search, and open photos and documents that live on the phone. Skip emailing yourself attachments.

**Cast My Apps.** Stream a phone-only app into a desktop window. Use it for a delivery checkout or a one-time code in Messages without putting the phone in your other hand.

If a file or app does not appear, confirm both devices are on the same account and that the phone is unlocked and online. Treat this as account sync, not a full desktop clone of every OEM skin feature.

## Use Magic Pointer on real work, not demos

Magic Pointer is Gemini attached to the cursor. Wiggle the pointer. Gemini reads text, images, and nearby context and waits for a request. Google's published examples:

- Highlight a training plan on a web page and ask Gemini to map the runs onto Google Calendar.
- Hover a suspicious email and ask Gemini to check the text and images for spam signals.
- Select a set of images and ask Gemini to combine them into one visual. No save-upload-prompt loop.

Rules that matter in daily use:

- Gemini acts only after you summon it. Google built the wiggle as an explicit on-switch.
- Turn Magic Pointer off when you share the screen or work through confidential layouts.
- Verify calendar events, money figures, and security claims in the original app. Magic Pointer is a shortcut, not a notary.

The same Gemini Live and task-automation features you know from Chrome and Android are available on Googlebook out of the box. Proactive suggestions can point to a next step based on what is on screen. You can close the lid while Gemini Spark keeps a long request running in the background.

## Dictate with Rambler instead of raw speech-to-text

Rambler sits next to the Quick Insert key. It is not a stenographer. Google's pitch is that it drops filler, fixes stumbles, and structures the result.

A useful first pass:

1. Open the document or notes surface you will paste into.
2. Start Rambler and talk through the meeting in the order things happened.
3. Let it turn the dump into headers, checklists, and short takeaways. Google says it can add relevant emoji.
4. Switch languages mid-sentence if that is how you actually think. Rambler is natively multilingual.
5. Edit names and deadlines by hand before you share.

Use Rambler for notes, action lists, and first drafts. Do not use it as the final text of a contract or a medical record.

## Pin one custom widget, then stop

Create My Widget builds a desktop widget from a plain-language description. Google's examples: a live score tile for one team, or a countdown to a family trip.

Write the prompt as a job, not a vibe. “Show next three Calendar events and a link to the team Doc” beats “make my desktop smarter.” Generate one widget, live with it for a week, then add a second. A crowded desktop hides the point of the feature.

Developers get more surface area. Every Googlebook includes Antigravity, Google's AI agent development platform, plus a full terminal. Google also documents a Level 5 security-certified pKVM hypervisor that hosts an isolated Linux environment for repos, CLI tools, Claude Code, or Antigravity CLI.

Security architecture matches ChromeOS: Titan hardware root of trust, defense in depth, and on-device malware detection.

## Apps that actually run

Googlebook ships desktop Chrome with extensions plus the Play Store. Many Android apps are tuned for large screens, mouse, and keyboard.

Google calls out native creative tools (Adobe Photoshop, Adobe Lightroom, CapCut) and streaming apps with offline viewing, including Netflix and HBO Max. Games come from the Play catalog plus the bundled GeForce NOW year.

Expect some phone apps to look like stretched mobile layouts on day one. Prefer the web or a desktop-class Android build when you need dense tables or multi-window editing.

## A 30-minute first-boot checklist

1. Sign in and finish the encrypted transfer of passwords, Wi-Fi, and messages.
2. Open Files and confirm photos from the phone appear.
3. Trigger Continue On with one app you used on the commute.
4. Cast Messages once so a two-factor code is reachable from the laptop.
5. Wiggle Magic Pointer on a calendar-heavy web page and create one event you then open in Calendar to verify.
6. Run Rambler on a five-minute voice dump and edit the checklist.
7. Create one widget. Leave the rest of the desktop alone.
8. Decide whether you need the Linux environment. If you do not write code this month, leave the hypervisor alone.

## Limits to plan around

- Shelf dates are October, not the pre-order day. You are reading a setup guide before most units exist in homes.
- Partner hardware is not identical. Check weight, ports, and display before you assume the $899 SKU matches a $1,199 XPS-class unit.
- Magic Pointer and Spark still need you to confirm actions that change calendars, send mail, or spend money.
- Cast My Apps depends on the phone being reachable. It is not a replacement for installing a desktop-class version of the same app.
- Ten years of updates apply to Googlebook OS as Google describes it. Accessory support and partner drivers still follow each vendor.

## Conclusion

Treat Googlebook as an Android desk, not a Windows substitute and not a cheap Chromebook. Pair the phone first. Use Continue On and Files until that loop feels boring. Then add Magic Pointer and Rambler to the work you already do in the browser.

Pre-order only if you want that phone-to-desk path and the Gemini tools on a partner chassis. If you do not live in Android, the bundle will not convert you.
