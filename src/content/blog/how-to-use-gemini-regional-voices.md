Google rolled out 66 new regional voices for the Gemini app on November 16, 2026. These options cover U.S. English, U.K. English, Indian English, Hindi, Brazilian Portuguese, Indonesian, and Korean. The goal is a voice that matches local speech patterns instead of a generic accent.

The change applies across the app. Your choice works in Gemini Live conversations and when you tap Listen on a text response. Google previewed the feature at I/O earlier this year and began the global rollout on Android and iOS.

## What the new voices include

Each voice focuses on natural cadence, pitch, and inflection rather than exaggerated slang. Google tuned them for everyday clarity so conversations feel familiar without forcing local idioms into every reply.

U.K. English receives the widest selection, with more than 10 options. These include Geordie (Newcastle area), Scouse (Liverpool), Brummie (Birmingham), Scottish, Welsh, Northern Irish, Modern Received Pronunciation, and Multicultural London English. Other languages receive tailored regional variants as well. Indian English and Hindi options help users in India hear speech patterns closer to everyday conversation, while Brazilian Portuguese, Indonesian, and Korean variants do the same in those regions.

U.S. English also gains additional regional choices so users can move away from a single neutral American tone. The full set of 66 voices spans these seven language groups, with the exact count per language varying by demand and available data.

The voices appear in the Gemini app on both Android and iOS. They may take time to reach every account as the rollout continues. Updating the Gemini app from the Play Store or App Store can speed up access on some devices.

## How to change Gemini’s voice

You can switch voices from the app settings or during a Live session. The process takes only a few taps on either platform and does not require a separate download.

### From app settings

1. Open the Gemini app on your Android phone or iPhone.
2. Tap your profile picture or initial in the top corner.
3. Select Settings, then Gemini’s voice.
4. Swipe left or right to preview each option. Each preview plays a short sample sentence.
5. Tap Select when you find the one you want.

The selected voice stays active until you change it again. It carries over to Live mode and the Listen button on chat replies. The same setting also applies when Gemini reads longer answers aloud or responds in other Google apps that use the Gemini voice.

### During Gemini Live

1. Start a Gemini Live conversation by tapping the Live button or using the power button shortcut on supported Android devices.
2. Tap the three-dot menu in the Live interface.
3. Choose a voice from the list and confirm.

This method lets you test options without leaving the conversation. You can switch mid-session if needed and immediately hear the difference on the next reply.

Note that the menu does not label voices by city or region. Gemini also will not name the accent if you ask it directly. Preview a few and listen for the rhythm that fits. Some users find it helpful to ask the same question in each voice, such as “What’s the weather like today?” or a local food recommendation, to compare how natural each one sounds.

## Tips for choosing and testing

Preview several options before settling on one. Ask Gemini about a local topic such as food, sports, or a neighborhood to hear how the cadence comes through. Because the focus is on rhythm rather than slang, the replies stay clear even when the accent is strong.

The setting is account-wide, so the same voice follows you on every device signed into the same Google account. If the new options do not appear yet, check for app updates and try again later in the day. Restarting the app or signing out and back in can also refresh the voice list on some accounts.

For hands-free use, the regional voice works with Gemini Live on Apple CarPlay as well. See our guide on [setting up Gemini Live on Apple CarPlay](/blog/how-to-use-gemini-live-on-apple-carplay/) for the full steps. The voice you pick in the mobile app carries over to the car display, which makes longer drives more comfortable when the assistant sounds familiar.

Language learners can use the new voices to practice listening. Switch to a regional option in the target language and ask Gemini to slow down or repeat phrases. The natural cadence provides a better model than a single neutral voice.

## Why the update matters

A voice that matches local speech patterns makes longer conversations feel more natural. Students practicing a language, drivers using Live for navigation help, or anyone who prefers a familiar accent can benefit. The earlier set of roughly ten voices worked for many people, but users in specific regions often noted that the default sounded too generic or occasionally mixed accents mid-sentence.

The November 16 rollout addresses that feedback by expanding choice without adding complexity. You still pick one voice and it applies everywhere. No extra settings or subscriptions are required. The feature is available to all Gemini app users as the update reaches their accounts.

The rollout is global for Gemini app users. Mobile support was the focus of the November 16 announcement, with the feature available on both Android and iOS. Google has not published a full list of every accent name, so the practical approach remains previewing options inside the app.

If you already use Gemini for daily briefings or quick questions, try a regional option and compare it to the previous default. Most people find a closer match within a few previews. You can always switch back if the new voices do not suit you.

## Troubleshooting common issues

If the new voices do not appear:

- Confirm the Gemini app is updated to the latest version.
- Check that you are signed into the correct Google account.
- Wait a few hours and reopen the app; staggered rollouts are common.
- Try the three-dot menu inside Live even if the settings page still shows the older list.

If a voice sounds different than expected, remember that the design prioritizes cadence over strong regional slang. The result should still be clear and easy to follow.
