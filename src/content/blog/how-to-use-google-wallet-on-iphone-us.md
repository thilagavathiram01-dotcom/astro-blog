Google Wallet arrived on iPhone for US users on November 17, 2026. The free App Store app pulls tickets, boarding passes, loyalty cards, and order details from Gmail into one place. It also stores payment cards for faster online checkout with Google Pay and lets you monitor purchases made through Gemini Tasks.

Tap-to-pay is not available. Apple platform limits block contactless payments in stores, so Apple Wallet remains the option for in-person taps. The iOS app focuses on organization, tracking, and online use.

This guide covers setup, daily use, and what the app can and cannot do right now. You need an iPhone running iOS 17 or later and a Google Account.

## Download and set up Google Wallet

Open the App Store on your iPhone. Search for Google Wallet and install the free app from Google. Sign in with the same Google Account you use for Gmail and other services.

Once signed in, the app begins syncing items from Gmail. Package tracking numbers, digital boarding passes, concert tickets, and receipts appear automatically. No manual entry is required for items already in your Google ecosystem.

You can also scan physical cards. Open the app, choose the option to add a pass or card, and use the camera to capture loyalty cards or gym memberships. An “Everything else” scanner handles other barcodes and QR codes.

## Organize passes and track orders

The home screen shows recent purchases awaiting delivery first, followed by boarding passes and a grid of loyalty cards. Pin important items so they stay visible. Unpin anything you no longer need on the main view.

A second tab lists recent transactions linked to your Google Account. The app summarizes purchase confirmation emails and displays order details, tracking status, and receipts. Recent deliveries stay on the homepage until they arrive.

For Gemini agentic purchases, the app includes a section to view active tasks, completed orders, and receipts. Set spending limits inside Gemini Tasks; the Wallet app then shows the results. Receipts land in Google Wallet after you approve any purchase.

## Manage payment cards for online use

Add payment cards stored in your Google Account. These cards work for faster checkout on Google services and any site that supports Google Pay. The app does not process contactless payments at physical terminals.

You control which cards appear and can remove them anytime in settings. Google uses tokenized cards for security during online transactions. You still earn card rewards, points, or protections on supported purchases.

## What is missing on iOS

Tap-to-pay is unavailable. Google states this clearly in the launch post and App Store listing. Use Apple Wallet or the physical card for store payments.

Digital IDs and certain transit tap features available on Android are not listed for the initial iOS release. The app is currently limited to the United States. Google has not announced expansion timelines for other countries.

## Tips for daily use

Keep Gmail connected so new tickets and tracking numbers appear without extra steps. Review the Agents section if you use Gemini Tasks for shopping. Confirm spending caps in Gemini before any agent-driven purchase.

If you carry both Android and iPhone devices, the same Google Account syncs passes across them. Update the app through the App Store for the latest interface improvements.

## Conclusion

Google Wallet on iPhone gives US users a single place for Gmail-linked tickets, boarding passes, loyalty cards, and order tracking. It supports online payment cards and Gemini purchase monitoring without replacing Apple Wallet for in-store taps.

Download the free app, sign in, and let it pull your existing passes. Check the official Google blog for the latest feature notes as the rollout continues.
