Google Wallet’s stacked card list is rolling out more widely on Android in October 2026. The vertical layout replaces the old horizontal carousel, but cards from the same bank can look nearly identical. A few settings fix the confusion so you pick the right card on the first try.

The change arrives as a server-side update. Open Wallet and look for one full card with a thin sliver of the next above it. Tap or swipe down to expand the full stack. Payment cards move into this view; loyalty cards, tickets, and passes stay on the main screen.

## Give Each Card a Clear Nickname

Nicknames solve the biggest problem. When two cards share the same design, the stack shows almost no difference at a glance.

1. Open Google Wallet.
2. Expand the card stack by tapping or swiping down.
3. Tap Manage payment methods at the bottom.
4. Select the card you want to rename.
5. Tap the three-dot menu in the top right.
6. Choose Edit nickname and enter a short, unique name such as “Everyday Visa” or “Rewards Mastercard.”
7. Save.

The nickname appears when you swipe the card slightly in the stack or open its details. Use the last four digits or the card’s purpose in the name so you never guess at the terminal.

## Reorder Cards for One-Handed Use

Place your most-used cards where your thumb naturally rests.

1. Expand the stack and open Manage payment methods.
2. Scroll to the bottom and tap Reorder cards.
3. Drag the cards into the order you prefer.
4. Put frequent cards toward the bottom of the list. In the expanded stack those sit closer to your thumb.

The home-screen preview shows the top card of the stack. After reordering, check which card appears first so the preview matches your usual choice.

## Set a Default for Tap to Pay

A default card removes an extra decision when you only need to unlock and hold the phone to the reader.

1. From Manage payment methods, select the card.
2. Open the three-dot menu.
3. Choose Make default for tap to pay.

Confirm the choice by expanding the stack once. The default card should appear at the top of the preview. You can still pick a different card before paying; the default only speeds up the common case.

## Open Card Details When You Need Them

Transaction history and full card info now sit behind a gear icon.

1. Expand the stack and tap the card.
2. Tap the gear icon in the top-right corner.
3. Review recent transactions or the full card number if required.

This extra step is part of the redesign. Keep bank apps installed for statements or disputes; Wallet shows a limited view.

## Pay With the Organized Stack

1. Unlock the phone and confirm NFC is on.
2. Open Wallet or use the lock-screen Wallet shortcut if you added one.
3. Expand the stack if the preview is not the card you want.
4. Tap the correct card.
5. Hold the back of the phone to the contactless reader until the confirmation appears.
6. Authenticate with fingerprint, face, or PIN if prompted.

If the wrong wallet app opens on a Samsung phone, set Google Wallet as the default contactless payment app under Settings > Connected devices > Connection preferences.

## Tips for Similar Cards

- Add the last four digits to every nickname. Color and bank name alone are not enough when designs match.
- Test the order with a small purchase. Note which card appears first on the home preview after you reorder.
- If a card still looks identical after nicknaming, open the gear icon once before paying until the habit forms.
- Update the app from the Play Store if the stack has not appeared. The layout is server-side, so a refresh can help, but there is no public toggle.

For adding a new card or state ID, see the steps in [Google Wallet digital state ID guide](/blog/google-wallet-digital-state-id/). Connecting Wallet to Gemini for spending insights is covered separately in [Connect Google Wallet to Gemini](/blog/connect-google-wallet-to-gemini/).

## What Stays the Same

Tap to pay still requires an unlocked phone, NFC, and a supported terminal. The merchant processes the charge on the card network you selected. Wallet does not change issuer limits, rewards, or verification rules.

The stacked list is a picker change. Once nicknames and order are set, selection takes one tap instead of several sideways swipes.
