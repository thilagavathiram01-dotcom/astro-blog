Google Intelligent Eyewear brings Gemini help into everyday frames. Guided Vision adds conversational audio descriptions for people who are blind, have low vision, or simply want extra visual support while reading small print or locating items.

This feature works with compatible Android phones at launch. It turns the glasses camera into an on-demand guide without storing visuals in your Gemini history.

## What Guided Vision Does

Guided Vision uses the built-in camera to describe what you see. Ask Gemini about a menu, a parking sign, or a shelf of products and receive spoken guidance in your ear. The system detects languages automatically for translation tasks across more than 70 languages when combined with Google Translate.

Camera visuals are not saved in Gemini History. They are not used for model training or ad targeting. Data stays encrypted on the glasses and in transit to your phone or cloud processing.

## Supported Devices and Requirements

Intelligent Eyewear pairs with Pixel 7 series and newer phones running the Android 17 September 2026 update or higher. Samsung Galaxy S, S FE, Z Fold, Z Flip, and Fold Ultra series on One UI 9.0 or higher also work. iPhone 13 Pro and newer are supported, but Guided Vision requires a compatible Android phone at launch.

You need a Google account, and the wearer must be 18 or older. Pre-orders opened in the United States and South Korea through Warby Parker and Gentle Monster. Full availability begins December 10.

Check the official Google blog for the latest supported phone list before ordering.

## How to Activate Guided Vision

1. Pair the glasses with your supported Android phone using the Glasses app. Confirm Bluetooth is on and the glasses appear in the connection list.
2. Open the Gemini app or say "Hey Gemini" after setup. Grant camera permission when prompted for visual assistance features.
3. Say "Hey Gemini, describe what I am looking at" or tap the frames to start a visual query. Speak naturally about the object or scene in front of you.
4. Review the spoken response. Ask follow-up questions such as "What color is the next item?" or "Read the fine print on this label."
5. End the session by saying "Stop" or tapping the frames again. The camera feed stops immediately.

The process takes under a minute once the glasses are charged and paired. Battery life reaches about nine hours of typical use, with the charging case holding additional full charges.

## Practical Tips for Daily Use

Hold the object steady and in good lighting for clearer descriptions. For navigation, combine Guided Vision with Google Maps turn-by-turn audio directions already available on the glasses.

Use short, specific prompts. "Is there a step down ahead?" or "Find the blue folder on the desk" works better than broad questions. The system handles multi-turn conversation so you can refine the request without restarting.

Privacy light on the frames indicates when the camera is active. Keep it visible to people nearby.
