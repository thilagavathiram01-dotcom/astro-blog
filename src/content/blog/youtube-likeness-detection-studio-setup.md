AI-altered clips can put a creator’s face in a video they never recorded. YouTube’s likeness detection tool is built for that problem. It searches newly uploaded videos for the face of a creator who has opted in, then lets that creator review matches and decide what to do.

The feature is still experimental, and it is not available in every country. Setup happens in YouTube Studio on a computer. This guide follows the steps in [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16440338) so you can enroll, review matches, and turn the tool off if you change your mind.

## What likeness detection actually does

Likeness detection looks for videos where an enrolled creator’s face appears altered or generated by AI. YouTube says the scan works in a similar way to Content ID, except it searches for a consented face reference rather than a copyrighted file.

The system runs a one-time search of newly uploaded videos. It is not a general facial-recognition product for every person on YouTube. YouTube says it can identify likeness only for creators who enrolled and submitted a face reference. Faces that do not match an enrolled creator are discarded.

Right now the live match is facial. YouTube Help says the team is working to extend likeness detection to audio in 2026. If you find a clip that imitates your voice before that lands, you can still file a privacy complaint outside the tool.

During this experimental phase, the review list can also include real footage of your face, such as a short clip from your own videos uploaded by another channel. YouTube says that kind of content cannot be removed under its privacy policies. Unauthorized reuse of your original work may still be a copyright issue, but you need to consider fair use, public domain, and other exceptions before you file.

## Who can set it up

YouTube lists three requirements:

- You must be over 18.
- You must be a Channel Owner or a Manager to complete setup.
- You must finish identity verification with a government-issued ID and a short selfie video.

Channel Owners, Managers, Editors, and Editors (limited) can later review matches and report privacy issues for an owner or manager who already enrolled. If several people appear on camera, each person who wants coverage has to enroll separately.

Shared Google accounts cause a common failure. YouTube says the account can get tied to the first person who completed Payments or AdSense verification, which then mismatches the next person’s ID. Add each teammate’s own Google account as a Manager, have them sign in with that account, and make sure the name on the Google payment profile matches the ID they submit.

## Turn on likeness detection in Studio

Open [YouTube Studio](https://studio.youtube.com) on a computer. The mobile app is not the setup path YouTube documents.

1. In the left menu, open **Content detection**, then **Likeness**, then **Start now**.
2. Agree to YouTube using biometric technology to search for your likeness. The consent covers a face reference so YouTube can search for facial matches. The same screen notes that audio likeness would use audio from your YouTube content, and that YouTube may later ask for a voice reference.
3. Complete Google identity verification. Submit a government-issued ID and a brief video of your face. Use a clear photo of the official ID. YouTube uses the ID and selfie to block fraudulent setup, and it uses the selfie plus images of your face from your YouTube content as the detection reference.
4. Wait for the confirmation email. YouTube says verification can take up to five days after you submit the ID and selfie video.

You may also see an optional consent to let YouTube use face and voice templates to improve likeness detection models. That consent is separate from enrollment. YouTube says the setup data is not used to train Google’s generative AI models unless you expressly agree. You can revoke the model-improvement consent later from **Manage likeness detection**.

## Review matches and choose an action

After verification, open Studio on a computer or in a mobile browser. Go to **Content detection**, then **Likeness**, then **For review**.

An empty list is normal. YouTube says matches are not instant, and channels that rarely show up in synthetic clips may see few results. A missing match does not block a manual privacy complaint.

For each result:

1. Filter by total views or by channel subscriber count if the list is long.
2. Select **Review** next to the video.
3. Pick one action.

**Submit a likeness removal request** if you believe the video breaks YouTube’s privacy policy. That path continues through the privacy complaint form. YouTube emails you about the outcome. Parody and satire are among the factors reviewers weigh, so a request is not an automatic takedown.

**Submit a copyright removal request** only if your original copyrighted content was used without permission. Check fair use and other exceptions first.

**Move to archive** if you want the video off the review list but still live. You can reopen it from the **Archived** tab and start a privacy complaint later.

If you file a likeness removal request and then change your mind, use the retraction link in the confirmation email. That link opens a short form.

## Tips before you rely on the list

Keep a manual report path handy. If an AI-altered video of your face never appears in the tool, report it through YouTube’s privacy complaint process. The same applies to voice clones while audio matching is still in progress.

Do not treat every match as a removal case. Real footage of you, reaction clips, and news use can land in the list during this experiment. Archiving those items keeps the review queue usable.

Send feedback from Studio if a clear synthetic match is missed. YouTube asks enrolled creators to report those misses while the model is still being tuned.

Pair this check with the rest of your Studio routine. Custom feeds and Ask Studio tools covered in our [YouTube custom feeds guide](/blog/youtube-custom-feeds-2026/) help viewers find your real uploads. Likeness detection is the other side of that work: spotting uploads that look like you but are not yours.

## Turn likeness detection off

You can opt out at any time.

1. Open YouTube Studio on a computer or mobile browser.
2. Open **Content detection**, then **Likeness**.
3. Select **Manage likeness detection**.
4. Select **Stop finding matches**, then **Stop**.

YouTube says it can take up to 24 hours to stop finding matches. After you opt out, you lose the list of detected videos. YouTube also says it deletes the likeness information used for the feature. Government ID stored in your Payments profile is separate, and you can access or delete that profile data on your own.

## What to do next

If you are over 18 and you own or manage the channel, enroll from a computer, use your own Google account, and wait for the confirmation email before you expect matches. Review the For review tab on a set day each week, archive real footage, and reserve privacy complaints for altered or synthetic clips.

Voice matching is not the live product yet. Until YouTube ships that extension, file voice complaints through the privacy form and keep the face enrollment current if your on-camera look changes a lot.
