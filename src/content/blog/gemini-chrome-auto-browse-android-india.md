Google brought Gemini in Chrome to all Android users in India on 7 October 2026. For AI Pro and AI Ultra subscribers, it also activated Auto Browse. This agentic tool lets Gemini handle multi-step web tasks on your phone, such as filling forms or ordering groceries, while you stay in control.

Auto Browse works in a dedicated tab. Gemini shows a plan first. You approve it, then watch progress or take over at any time. Daily limits apply: 20 tasks for Pro and 200 for Ultra.

## What you need

Confirm these before you start:

- An Android phone with the latest Chrome app.
- A personal Google Account signed in to Chrome. Work or school accounts do not support Auto Browse.
- Google AI Pro or AI Ultra subscription. Free accounts get page summaries and Nano Banana image tools but not Auto Browse.
- Device language set to English. Auto Browse is limited to English for now, with more languages planned.
- Chrome Safe Browsing set to Standard or Enhanced.
- Age 18 or older.

Gemini in Chrome itself is available more broadly for summaries, questions about pages, and connections to apps like Keep and Calendar. Auto Browse is the paid agentic layer.

If Skills in the Gemini app help you reuse instructions, the same idea of reusable control applies here. See the guide on [stacking Gemini Skills](/blog/stack-gemini-skills-one-chat/) for related productivity patterns.

## Open Gemini in Chrome and start a task

1. Open the Chrome app on your Android device.
2. Tap the Ask Gemini icon (sparkle) at the top or bottom of the browser.
3. Describe one clear multi-step task in plain language. Example: “Book parking near the conference center for tomorrow at 9 AM and fill my contact details.”
4. Review the plan Gemini displays. It lists the sites it intends to visit and any personal details it may share.
5. Tap Start Task only after the plan looks correct.
6. Gemini opens a separate Auto Browse tab marked with an icon. Progress appears in the side panel or Gemini view.

You can switch away from the Auto Browse tab and keep using other apps. Gemini notifies you when it needs input or finishes.

## Handle confirmations and takeovers

Gemini pauses before sensitive actions. These include submitting forms with personal data, preparing payments, or posting content.

- Review the exact fields or amount before you confirm.
- Tap Take Over if the path is wrong. You regain full control of the tab.
- Grant sign-in permission only if you want Gemini to use Google Password Manager. It never sees your actual passwords.

Do not start tasks in Incognito mode. Auto Browse does not run there.

## Practical examples that stay safe

Start with low-risk tasks while you learn the flow:

- Compare three hotels for a date range under a fixed budget and shortlist those with free cancellation.
- Add grocery items from a list to a cart on a familiar store site, then stop before checkout.
- Fill a long public form with information you supply in the prompt, then review before submit.

Avoid tasks that move money or change account settings until you have tested the confirmation steps several times.

## Tips for reliable results

Name the target site when you know it. “Order from BigBasket” works better than “order groceries.”

State hard limits: budget, dates, quantities, or “stop before payment.”

Keep the first few tasks short. Long multi-site chains raise the chance of an off-path step.

Check the daily quota. Pro accounts hit the 20-task cap quickly if you experiment.

Update Chrome and the Gemini app. Availability expands in waves; restart the app if the Ask Gemini icon is missing after you meet the requirements.

Google trains models to detect threats such as prompt injection. Still, treat any unexpected navigation or data request as a signal to take over.

## Conclusion

Gemini Auto Browse in Chrome on Android turns multi-step web chores into supervised tasks for AI Pro and Ultra users in India. Open Ask Gemini, describe the job, review the plan, and confirm only the sensitive steps. Start with comparisons and form fills, watch the dedicated tab, and keep the takeover button ready. The feature remains experimental and English-only for now, with broader language support planned.
