Pixel owners on the 10 and 11 series can now describe a home-screen widget in plain language and let Gemini build it. Create My Widget turns a short prompt into a glanceable card that can pull from Gmail, Calendar, or Search.

This guide walks through eligibility, setup, creation, editing, and practical limits based on Google’s November 2026 announcements.

## What Create My Widget Does

Create My Widget is part of Gemini Intelligence. You type a description or pick a suggestion. Gemini generates a widget you can preview, edit, and pin to the home screen. No coding is required.

Google lists examples such as a trip dashboard, a monthly spending tracker, or a race-day countdown. The widget can surface information from connected Google apps when you allow it.

The feature began rolling out with the November 2026 Pixel Drop on supported devices.

## Who Can Use It

Eligibility is limited. You need:

- A Pixel 10, Pixel 10 Pro, Pixel 10 Pro XL, Pixel 10 Pro Fold, or a Pixel 11 series phone. The Pixel 10a is excluded.
- English language.
- Age 18 or older.
- Location in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, or India.

Motorola’s signature 27 also received early support. Google said the feature would expand over the following weeks after the 10 November 2026 announcement.

If the Create button does not appear, join the Create My Widget beta in the Play Store, update the app, then check Widgets again.

## Step-by-Step: Create a Widget

1. Long-press an empty area on your home screen.
2. Tap **Widgets**.
3. Tap **Create** in the top-right corner.
4. Choose a suggestion or tap **Describe your widget**.
5. Type what you want. Be specific: “Show my next three Calendar events and today’s weather.”
6. Tap **Send**. Gemini builds a preview. This can take a minute.
7. Review the preview. Tap **Edit** if you need changes, then send a follow-up prompt.
8. Grant permissions if the widget needs Gmail, Calendar, or device data.
9. Tap **Add** to place it on the home screen.

Changes save automatically. You can long-press an existing widget later, choose Edit, and refine it with a new prompt.

## Practical Tips

Start simple. A countdown to a fixed date or a basic weather tile usually works on the first try. Complex requests that need live third-party data often fall back to a button that opens a Gemini chat.

You can have multiple widgets. Google has not published a hard limit in the official posts, but early testers reported practical ceilings around five.

Review privacy settings. You control which apps the widget can read. Disconnect access anytime in the widget or Gemini settings.

For broader Gemini Intelligence tools on the same devices, see our guide to [using Gemini Intelligence features on Android](/blog/use-gemini-intelligence-features-android-guide/).

## Limits and What to Expect

Create My Widget works best for glanceable information that Gemini can source from Google services or general knowledge. It is not a full mini-app builder. Some generated widgets rely on preset layouts rather than fully custom designs.

UK users may have fewer options for pulling personal data from Gmail or Calendar. Always double-check the information the widget displays, especially for time-sensitive items.

The feature continues to roll out. If it is missing, confirm your device model, language, age, and country settings, then try the beta enrollment path.

## Conclusion

Create My Widget gives Pixel 10 and 11 owners a fast way to put useful information on the home screen without installing third-party apps or writing code. Describe what you need, preview the result, and pin it.

Start with one clear prompt today. You can always edit or replace the widget later as Google expands the feature.
