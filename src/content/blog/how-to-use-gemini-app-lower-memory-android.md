Google expanded the Gemini app to lower-memory Android devices on 18 November 2026. Eligible Android (Go edition) phones with 2 GB of RAM or more can now run the core Gemini experience.

This change reaches hundreds of millions of people who rely on entry-level phones for daily internet, education, and business use. Previously these devices were limited to basic voice commands. The re-architected app brings conversational AI without requiring high-end hardware.

![Person holding a smartphone showing app icons](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1556656793-08538906a9f8?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## Check Eligibility First

Confirm your device meets the requirements before installing. Official support documentation lists these conditions:

- Android (Go edition) with 2 GB of RAM or more.
- Signed in to a personal Google Account, or a work or school account that has Gemini Apps access.
- Availability in languages and countries where Gemini is offered.

On these devices Gemini runs as part of the Google Search app and replaces Assistant for Android (Go edition). Parents can manage access for younger users through Family Link.

If your phone has less than 2 GB of RAM, the feature is not supported. Newer partner devices from makers such as Transsion may ship with Gemini pre-installed.

## How to Open Gemini

Installation is straightforward. Download the Gemini app from Google Play if it is not already present. On supported phones you can also start it without searching the store.

1. Open the Google Play Store and search for Gemini. Install or update the app.
2. Touch and hold the Home button to launch Gemini.
3. On supported devices, press and hold the power button instead.

Note that 'Hey Google' wake word and 'Ask about this screen' are not available on Android (Go edition) devices. You must use the hold gestures or open the app directly.

Once open, the interface behaves like the standard Gemini experience, optimised for lower memory.

## What You Can Do

The core features match the broader Gemini app while staying efficient on limited hardware. Official examples include:

- Voice conversations with Gemini Live in more than 45 languages. Practice an interview aloud or talk through an idea instead of typing long prompts.
- Camera input. Point the phone at notes, forms, or signs. Gemini helps you learn from the content or translate it.
- Image creation with Nano Banana. Generate a custom flyer for a weekend bakery sale or similar everyday graphics.
- Everyday tasks such as calling or texting contacts, setting alarms, creating calendar events, or playing music by mood.

You can also upload photos or documents to add context to a conversation. These capabilities replace the earlier basic voice-only experience on Go devices.

## Practical Tips for Daily Use

Start with short, clear requests. On lower-memory hardware the app prioritises the core experience, so complex multi-step tasks may take longer or use more data.

Use Gemini Live when you prefer speaking. The support page notes it works well for free-flowing brainstorming. Review responses before acting on calls or messages, since you remain in control of any actions.

For camera help, hold the phone steady and ensure good lighting. The same applies when creating images: describe the outcome clearly, such as style, text, and purpose.

If you also use Gemini on a higher-end phone or desktop, the lower-memory version keeps the same account and history where supported. For more advanced task automation on supported devices, see our guide on [using free Gemini Tasks for daily assistance](/blog/how-to-use-free-gemini-tasks-for-daily-assistance/).

Check for app updates in Play Store regularly. Google continues to optimise the experience for these devices.

## Limits to Know

The official help page is clear on what is missing:

- No hands-free 'Hey Google' activation.
- No 'Ask about this screen' feature.
- Performance depends on the specific device and network conditions.
- Work and school accounts must already have Gemini Apps access enabled by the administrator.

Storage remains a consideration. The app is designed to be efficient even on devices with lower storage, but keep free space available for updates and cached content.

## Why This Matters

Entry-level Android phones are the primary computing device for many people in markets across India, Africa, Southeast Asia, and Latin America. Bringing full conversational Gemini, Live voice, camera understanding, and image creation to phones with 2 GB of RAM expands access without forcing hardware upgrades.

Google stated that the team re-architected the app specifically so the core experience runs smoothly on less memory. Upcoming devices from partners will include it pre-loaded, further widening reach.

## Conclusion

Eligible Android (Go edition) phones with at least 2 GB of RAM can now run the Gemini app. Download it from Play Store, launch by holding Home or power, and use Live voice, camera help, or image creation for everyday tasks. The feature replaces the previous basic voice commands and follows the same account and language availability as the main Gemini service.

Verify your device meets the RAM and account requirements first. Start with a simple spoken request or a photo of a note to test the experience on your hardware. Official documentation from Google provides the exact eligibility and start methods.
