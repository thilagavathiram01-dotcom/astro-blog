A school Google account can now open the same Gemini student hub that personal accounts have used since August. Google’s Workspace update on October 1, 2026 says the hub is available to Google Workspace for Education users of all ages, on both Rapid Release and Scheduled Release domains.

The hub is a starting page, not a separate app. From it you can start a study notebook, create flashcards, and take a practice quiz. Extra learning tools land in the same place as Google adds them.

## Who can open it

Google lists two availability lines that matter for schools.

The October 1 Workspace post says the hub is available now to Google Workspace for Education users of all ages. The same post says it is available to all Google Workspace customers and Workspace Individual subscribers outside the European Economic Area, and to personal Google accounts globally.

On a school-issued account, the Gemini app and Gemini Notebook still have to be turned on for your organizational unit. Google’s September 22 note on study notebooks says an admin controls that pair of settings. If either service is off for your group, the Study and learn tile will not appear.

The free college plan is a different offer. Google’s August 19 post says eligible U.S. college students can claim one year of Google AI Pro, listed at $19.99 a month after the trial, and eligible students outside the U.S. can claim one year of Google AI Plus. Redeem by December 31, 2026, with a valid payment method. That plan claim is not the same screen as the hub, and Google says the related YouTube Premium bundle is not available on school-issued accounts.

## Open the hub

1. Sign in to the Gemini app with the account you want to study on. Use a personal account for the college plan, or a school account if your admin has Gemini and Gemini Notebook enabled.
2. On the web, go to [gemini.google.com/students](https://gemini.google.com/students).
3. On mobile, open the Gemini app and look for the student hub entry. Google says the hub is on mobile and web for signed-in consumer accounts, and the October 1 update extends that page to Education users.
4. Confirm you see shortcuts for a study notebook, flashcards, and a practice quiz. If the page is missing, check with your admin before you assume the rollout skipped your domain.

Google’s August footnote said the hub was rolling out to school-issued accounts in the following weeks, with usage limits. The October 1 post is the confirmation that Education accounts are in that rollout.

## Start a study notebook from the hub

The hub is the front door. The notebook is where the lessons live. Google’s Workspace steps, published September 22, match the path inside the Gemini app:

1. From the left sidebar, select **New notebook**.
2. Choose the **Study and learn** tile.
3. Tell Gemini what you are studying.
4. Upload class materials or other sources, such as notes, a syllabus, or readings.

Google says those sources ground the lessons, so the notebook stays closer to the curriculum you uploaded than to a general chat.

After the notebook exists, request a progress-check quiz. Google says that quiz pinpoints strengths and weaknesses and sets a baseline. Then follow the short lessons. Each lesson includes practice quizzes. You can ask a question in the lesson without leaving it.

The progress view splits topics into **Strengths** and **Focus areas** and recommends the next lesson. Google’s June study-notebook post says the dashboard breaks a goal into learning objectives, then groups those objectives into topics.

For a longer walkthrough of the notebook itself, see [how to set up Gemini study notebooks](/blog/gemini-study-notebooks-setup/).

## Use flashcards and a practice quiz

Google’s student-hub posts name three actions on the page: start a study notebook, create flashcards, and take a practice quiz. Use them in that order on a new topic.

Start with the notebook so Gemini has sources. Then create flashcards from those materials instead of from a blank prompt. A quiz after a short lesson tells you whether the flashcards matched the reading.

Google’s June post also points at Gemini Notebook, formerly NotebookLM, for flashcards, Video Overviews, and other study formats built from the same uploaded files. If the hub quiz feels short, open the notebook sources there and generate a second set. Keep the class file as the source of truth and read any answer that is not tied to a page you uploaded.

## What admins should check

Teachers cannot turn the hub on for a domain. Google says study notebooks are available only when both Gemini Notebook and the Gemini app are set to On for the user’s group or organizational unit.

Admins can review those switches in the Admin console before a class assigns the hub. The October 1 post does not add a separate student-hub control. If Gemini and Gemini Notebook are already on, Education users should see the hub without another opt-in.

Availability outside the EEA applies to Workspace customers and Workspace Individual subscribers. Personal Google accounts are listed as global. If a student is in the EEA on a Workspace account, do not promise the hub until it appears on that account.

## A first session that stays inside the docs

Pick one unit, not the whole course. Upload the notes for that unit, name the goal in one sentence, and run the progress-check quiz before you open a lesson. Google says the quiz sets the baseline. Skipping it means the Focus areas list has less to work from.

Read the lesson, answer the built-in quiz, and only then make flashcards for the items marked as focus areas. If a card states a fact you cannot find in the upload, delete it. Google’s own study-notebook description treats uploaded class materials as the ground for the lesson.

College students who want higher Gemini limits can claim the separate plan at the offer page Google links from the August post, [one.google.com/ai-student](https://one.google.com/ai-student). That claim needs student verification, a payment method, and a cancel date if you do not want the monthly charge after the free year. School-issued accounts are not the account type Google lists for the YouTube Premium bundle.

The hub does not replace a teacher or a textbook. It groups the study tools Google already ships—notebooks, flashcards, and quizzes—on one page, including on Education accounts as of the October 1 update.
