Googlebook laptops land on U.S. shelves on October 4, 2026, three days after this guide. Pre-orders are already open on the Google Store, Best Buy, and other select retailers. Canada, the U.K., Ireland, France, Germany, and Australia get devices on October 5.

Google is not selling one own-brand laptop. The first wave comes from Acer, ASUS, Dell, HP, and Lenovo. Every machine shares the same software story: an Android stack with desktop foundations from ChromeOS, Gemini on the desktop, and phone continuity for Android users. The hardware split is what decides the order.

## What every Googlebook includes

Google says the lineup starts at $899. Shared hardware includes premium chassis materials such as aluminum, magnesium alloy, and carbon fiber, touchscreens up to 2.8K OLED, haptic glass trackpads, and backlit keyboards. Processors come from Intel and Qualcomm, each paired with a dedicated NPU capable of over 45 TOPS. Google lists up to 14 hours of battery life.

The Glowbar sits on the exterior. It lights at startup, sweeps to show charge, and plays animations when you talk to Gemini.

Software is the same across the five models. Sign-in can bring Android phone settings, saved passwords, Wi-Fi networks, and messages, backed by end-to-end encryption. Continue On resumes a supported phone task on the laptop. The Files app can open phone files and photos. Cast My Apps streams phone apps into a desktop window.

Gemini features ship on every unit: Magic Pointer (wiggle the cursor to ask Gemini about what is on screen), Rambler (voice notes turned into structured writing), and Create My Widget. You also get desktop Chrome with extensions, Play Store apps, Photoshop, Lightroom, CapCut, and a year of GeForce NOW. Developers get Antigravity plus an isolated Linux terminal under a Level 5 security-certified pKVM hypervisor, with a Titan hardware root of trust.

Every purchase includes 12 months of Google AI Pro (5TB of cloud storage and higher Gemini limits), plus 3 months of YouTube Premium, Adobe Photoshop, and more. Googlebook OS gets feature drops and updates for up to 10 years.

## Step 1: Pick the job, then the screen

Start with how you will use the laptop on day one, not with the badge on the lid.

Writing, browsing, and Gemini tasks do not need the $1,299 configs. The Acer Googlebook 14 is the entry point at $899, with a 14-inch 2.8K OLED and a hinge that folds. That is the practical pick if you want a tablet-like posture for video or markup and you can live with 16GB of RAM.

If you want a smaller, lighter-feeling chassis and more base storage, the Dell XPS Googlebook starts at $999 with 512GB. The tradeoff is the panel: 13.4-inch 2.5K IPS, not OLED. Text will look sharp. Blacks and video contrast will not match the OLED models.

The Lenovo Googlebook 15 is the large-screen option at $1,099. A 15.3-inch 2.8K OLED helps with side-by-side docs and Android apps that still waste space on a 13-inch panel. It stays at 16GB, so heavy local agents may push memory harder than on the 32GB configs.

HP and Asus both start at $1,299. HP pairs Snapdragon X Elite with a 14-inch 2.8K OLED and optional 32GB. Asus pairs Intel Core Ultra 5/7 with a 14-inch 3K OLED and optional 32GB. Choose these if you want headroom for Antigravity sessions, browser tabs, and local Linux tools at the same time.

## Step 2: Match the chip to your apps

Both platforms target on-device AI. Google’s blog cites NPUs over 45 TOPS. Ars reports 45 TOPS on the Snapdragon X Elite and Intel Core Ultra Series 3 parts used at launch.

Intel Core Ultra Series 3 (Panther Lake) is on Acer, Lenovo, and Asus. Snapdragon X Elite is on Dell and HP. Google has not published a public app-compatibility matrix that splits the two. Native Android apps and desktop Chrome should run on both. If a specific Linux binary or creative plugin matters, check that vendor’s ARM versus x86 notes before you lock a Snapdragon order.

Battery claims are shared, not per model: up to 14 hours from Google, with Ars also citing up to 14 hours of video playback and 16 hours of web browsing from Google’s briefing. Treat those as lab figures until independent tests land after October 4.

## Step 3: Price the bundle, not just the box

The sticker is not the full first-year cost. Google includes 12 months of Google AI Pro on every Googlebook, including the $899 Acer. That bundle is 5TB of cloud storage and higher Gemini usage limits. You also get 3 months of YouTube Premium and Adobe Photoshop, plus a year of GeForce NOW.

If you already pay for Google AI Pro, the included year still has value, but it is less of a discount. If you do not, the Acer at $899 plus that year is the clearest deal in the set.

Do not assume 32GB or 512GB on the cheap configs. Acer and Lenovo start at 16GB and 256GB in the Ars table. Dell starts at 512GB. HP and Asus list 16–32GB. Open the exact SKU and read RAM and storage before checkout.

## Step 4: Plan the phone handoff

Googlebook is built for Android phones. During setup, settings, passwords, Wi-Fi, and messages can move with you. After that, three features do the daily work:

1. Continue On picks up a supported app task from the phone on the laptop taskbar.
2. Files searches phone photos and documents without emailing them to yourself.
3. Cast My Apps opens a phone app in a window for a one-time code or a delivery order.

Continue On needs developer support, so not every app will resume. Cast My Apps is the fallback when the laptop build of an app is missing. Pairing steps are covered in our [Googlebook pre-order and phone setup guide](/blog/googlebook-preorder-android-setup/).

iPhone owners still get Chrome, Play Store apps, and Gemini. They do not get the phone continuity Google is selling as the main reason to switch.

## Step 5: Check the developer path if you code

Googlebook includes Antigravity for building, testing, and deploying apps on device, plus a full terminal for Claude Code or the Antigravity CLI. The Linux environment sits behind a Level 5 certified pKVM hypervisor, separate from the main desktop.

If coding is the job, favor a 32GB config (HP or Asus at the $1,299 tier, or a Dell XPS step-up if the retailer lists 32GB). 16GB is enough for docs, Chrome, and light Gemini use. It is tight once an agent, an emulator, and a browser are open together.

Sideloading follows Android’s developer verification rules. Plan on Play Store installs for daily apps.

## A short buying order

Use this order if you want a decision today:

1. Need the lowest price or a convertible: Acer Googlebook 14 at $899.
2. Want more storage in a smaller XPS body and can skip OLED: Dell XPS Googlebook at $999.
3. Want the largest OLED for documents: Lenovo Googlebook 15 at $1,099.
4. Want Snapdragon, OLED, and up to 32GB: HP Googlebook 14 at $1,299.
5. Want Intel, a 3K OLED, and up to 32GB: Asus Googlebook 14 at $1,299.

Then confirm the SKU’s RAM, storage, and return window. Independent battery and thermals reviews are unlikely before U.S. retail day.

## Tips before you pre-order

- Compare the same config, not the hero price. A 32GB step-up can close the gap between Dell, HP, and Asus.
- OLED matters if you edit photos or watch video. The Dell’s IPS panel is the outlier.
- The included AI Pro year and GeForce NOW year apply even to the $899 model.
- Updates run for up to 10 years. That is part of the value versus a short-support Windows laptop at a similar price.
- Magic Pointer only acts when you ask, and you can turn it off. You do not have to use Gemini to get phone continuity.

## Conclusion

October 4 is a hardware choice, not a software choice. The OS, Gemini tools, phone handoff, AI Pro year, and 10-year update promise are shared. Spend the extra money only if you need a larger OLED, 32GB of RAM, 512GB of storage, or a non-convertible metal build.

For most Android users ordering before reviews land, the Acer Googlebook 14 is the sane default. Move up the table only when a spec on the product page removes a limit you already hit on your current laptop.
