Google started delivering the first Health Guardian trend reports in early October 2026. On Pixel Watch 3, 4, and 5, blood pressure trends, insulin resistance trends, and sleep breathing quality are free in the Google Health app. You still have to turn them on, wear the watch long enough, and read the results as wellness signals, not a diagnosis.

This guide walks through the official setup path, the wear-time rules, and what the monthly report actually means.

## What Health Guardian tracks

Health Guardian is a set of background features in the Google Health app. Compatible watches use optical and motion sensors to look for shifts over days and weeks, not a single cuff reading.

Google lists three trend tools that Pixel Watch 3 and later can set up now:

- Blood pressure trends estimate whether your cardiovascular pattern looks persistently high. In Japan and Mexico the same feature is labeled Vascular Stress or Vascular Stress Trends.
- Insulin resistance trends look at multi-week metabolic signals without a blood draw. In Japan and Mexico this is labeled metabolic stress trends.
- Sleep breathing quality shows how much of a night was spent in optimal breathing, then rolls those nights into a monthly view.

Pixel Watch 4 and 5 in select European countries also include Breathing Emergency Detection. That feature can prompt a call to emergency services after a severe drop in blood oxygen. It is CE marked in the EU and has not been cleared or evaluated by the FDA.

None of these tools diagnose hypertension, diabetes, or sleep apnea. Google says they are not for medication changes, are not for people who are pregnant, and are not a substitute for a clinician.

## Check your watch and app first

Blood pressure trends need the Google Health app at version 5.09 or higher and a Pixel Watch 3 or newer. Google Help currently says Fitbit and third-party watches are not supported for that specific trend. Fitbit Air owners are scheduled to get insulin resistance and blood pressure trends later in 2026 with a Google Health Premium subscription.

Availability still depends on country. If the Health Guardian menu is missing, update the Google Health app and the Pixel Watch app from the Play Store, then restart the phone.

If you are setting up a new watch, pair it in the Pixel Watch app, then open Google Health and sign in with the same Google Account. The watch syncs on its own once that link is in place. For other Pixel health tools that landed this year, see the [Pixel 11 sign-to-text setup](/blog/pixel-11-sign-to-text-asl/).

## Turn on blood pressure trends

Google documents this path in the Google Health Help Center.

1. Open the Google Health app on the phone paired with your watch.
2. Tap your profile picture or initial at the top right.
3. Tap Google Health settings, then Health Guardian.
4. Under Blood pressure trends, tap Turn on, then confirm Turn on.
5. Tap the back arrow to return to Health Guardian settings.

Update height and weight in your Google Health profile before you expect a report. The app uses those values when it calculates cardiovascular trends.

To turn the feature off later, open the same Health Guardian screen, tap Settings under Blood pressure trends, then Turn off feature and confirm.

## Wear time that actually counts

A monthly blood pressure insight needs enough clean sensor data. Google Help asks for at least 5 continuous days in a month, with at least 8 hours a day of high signal quality. Sleep wear is recommended. The launch notes also say blood pressure insights need at least 5 consecutive days in a week.

Insulin resistance insights need a minimum of 7 days in a month. Both reports are scheduled for the first of every month once those thresholds are met. The first wave of reports was set for early October 2026.

Loose fit, tattoos over the sensor, and charging through the night are the usual reasons a month lands on “Not enough data.” Wear the watch snug, one finger above the wrist bone, and sync before you open the report.

## Read the blood pressure result

The app does not show a live mmHg number. It shows a pattern label:

- High means the watch saw a persistent pattern of the heart working harder. Google recommends 7 days of cuff readings and a conversation with a physician. Chest pain or shortness of breath is an emergency, not an app alert.
- Not high means the month did not show an unexpected elevation. That label does not rule out high blood pressure.
- Not enough data means wear time or signal quality missed the minimum.

The same screen points to habits with the strongest evidence behind them: aerobic activity, sodium and potassium balance (including the DASH eating pattern), and consistent sleep plus short stress breaks such as box breathing.

## Sleep breathing quality and insulin resistance

Sleep breathing quality is a morning and monthly view, not an apnea test. Each morning you can see how much of the night was spent in optimal breathing. The monthly assessment is meant to flag longer patterns so you can change the sleep setup or ask a clinician about daytime fatigue.

Insulin resistance trends sit in the same Health Guardian area. They track multi-week shifts from watch sensors. They are not a diabetes screener and should not be used to adjust treatment.

Open Google Health, go to your profile, then Google Health settings and Health Guardian. Turn on each trend that appears for your watch and region. If a toggle is absent, the feature has not reached that account yet.

## Breathing Emergency Detection limits

Breathing Emergency Detection is separate from the monthly breathing-quality score. It is for Pixel Watch 4 and 5 in certain European markets. A severe, persistent drop in blood oxygen can trigger a prompt to call emergency services.

It is not an FDA-cleared medical device. It does not replace a pulse oximeter used under clinical direction, and it will not run if the watch is off your wrist or out of battery.

## What Premium adds

The core trends are included with Pixel Watch 3, 4, and 5. Google Health Premium adds extras on top of the sensor data: a personalized plan across activity, nutrition, sleep, and stress; Ask Coach for questions about your metrics; monthly insights that tie habits to cardiovascular trends; and a 12-month doctor-visit summary. Premium is sold separately and is not required to turn the Pixel Watch trends on.

## Practical tips before the next report

Keep the watch on for full days, including sleep, for at least one solid week before the first of the month. Charge during a shower or a desk block instead of overnight.

Confirm the Google Health app is 5.09 or newer. Sync from the watch face if the phone has not pulled data in a day.

Treat a High label as a prompt to use a cuff, not as a prescription change. Share cuff logs with a clinician if the pattern repeats.

## Bottom line

Health Guardian on Pixel Watch 3, 4, and 5 is a free background check for blood pressure patterns, insulin resistance trends, and sleep breathing quality. Turn it on under Google Health settings, wear the watch for the documented minimum, and read the first-of-month report as a wellness signal. Confirm anything concerning with a cuff and a clinician.
