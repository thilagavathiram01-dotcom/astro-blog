Gemini Daily Brief is a morning list, not another chat thread. It scans connected Gmail, Google Calendar, and your Gemini chats, then ranks what actually needs attention today.

Google introduced the feature at I/O 2026 for Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra subscribers in the United States. In early September 2026, Google said Daily Brief is available to more Gemini app users **for free in the U.S.** Official Help still lists eligibility gates: you must be 18 or over, in the US, on a **personal** Google Account, with Personal Intelligence and Memory turned on. Availability is a rollout, so the sidebar item can be missing even when the announcement is out.

This guide follows Google’s Help Center and product pages. It does not invent extra sections, countries, or Workspace-account support.

## What Daily Brief is — and is not

Daily Brief is an **opt-in agent** that builds a skimmable digest. Google’s I/O post describes it as gathering urgent Gmail updates, upcoming Calendar events, and follow-ups into one briefing, then organizing items around your goals and suggesting next steps.

It is **not**:

- A full replacement for Gmail search
- Gemini Spark (Spark is a separate 24/7 task agent and still requires a Google AI Pro or Ultra plan, per Google’s Help and product footnotes)
- Available on work, school, or supervised accounts
- Available outside the US or in languages other than English (Help: “For now… only available in English”)

Official product surfaces: Gemini mobile app, [gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com), and Gemini Live.

## Requirements before the sidebar appears

Google Help lists all of these:

1. **Age and location:** 18+ and in the United States.
2. **Account type:** Sign in with a **personal** Google Account. Work, school, and supervised accounts are out.
3. **Personal Intelligence + Workspace:** Connect Google Workspace to Gemini Apps so Gmail and Calendar can feed the brief.
4. **Memory:** Turn Memory on so past Gemini chats can inform longer-term items.

If any of those four are off, do not hunt for a hidden flag. Finish setup first.

### Enable Personal Intelligence and Memory

Exact labels move slightly between web and mobile, but the path on the computer is:

1. Open [gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com) and sign in with the personal account you actually use for mail and calendar.
2. Open **Settings & help**.
3. Open **Personal Intelligence**.
4. Connect **Google Workspace** (Gmail and Calendar are the sources Daily Brief documents).
5. Turn **Memory** on. Google documents Memory as using past Gemini chats for personalization.

On a phone, use the Gemini app account / settings menu and look for the same two names: Personal Intelligence and Memory.

Keep Activity / Gemini Apps Activity on if you later chat about a brief item. Help notes that a chat started from a Daily Brief item is saved to Gemini Apps Activity.

## How to open Daily Brief

### On the web

1. Go to [gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com).
2. In the sidebar, click **Daily brief**.
3. You can also open the product entry point at [gemini.google.com/daily-brief](https://gemini.google.com/daily-brief).

### In the Gemini mobile app

Open Gemini, then open the side navigation. Daily Brief sits with the other Gemini modes when you are eligible. Google also sends a morning notification for some accounts; you can turn that off under account **Notifications**.

### In Gemini Live

On 26 August 2026 Google documented a spoken path: start Gemini Live and say **“What’s my daily brief?”** Gemini Live then reads a digest drawn from Gmail and Calendar. That is the same briefing, spoken, not a second product.

## How to read the two sections

Help currently documents **two** main sections:

**Top of mind**  
Timely, actionable items. Sources are primarily Gmail, Calendar, and Gemini chats. Treat this as “do or decide today.”

**Looking ahead**  
Longer-term goals plus suggested next steps. Help says these suggestions are generated from Personal Intelligence.

Some press coverage of the free US rollout also described an **FYI** block for dated items that are not yet urgent. Google’s Help page you should follow still names the two sections above. If your account shows a third block, use it the same way: dated, lower priority than Top of mind.

### Work an item without leaving the brief

Under each item, open **More**, then:

- **Chat** — ask a follow-up (“Draft a reply that confirms Thursday 3 p.m.”). That chat is stored in Gemini Apps Activity.
- **Mark complete** or dismiss — tells Gemini the item is handled.
- **Mark incomplete** — if you completed the wrong card.
- Source line — Help says the popup lists where the item came from. Check it before you act on a deadline.

Give **Helpful** / **Not helpful** on items that are noise. Daily Brief is supposed to adapt; thumbs data is how you stop seeing last year’s newsletter as “top of mind.”

## A practical morning loop

Use this once, then adjust.

1. Open Daily Brief before you open the raw Gmail list.
2. Scan **Top of mind** only. Ignore Looking ahead until the urgent row is empty.
3. For each item, open the source. Confirm the date and the sender.
4. Either mark complete, start a Chat to draft the reply, or dismiss junk.
5. Say “What’s my daily brief?” in Gemini Live only when your hands are busy (commute, kitchen). Treat the spoken version as a reminder, then tap the source on a screen before you send mail.
6. Once a week, turn off a source in Personal Intelligence if a connected app is polluting the list.

Good first-week jobs:

- Students: class times and application mail, not every campus newsletter.
- Job search: recruiter threads and interview calendar holds.
- Parents: school mail with dates, not every PTA blast.

Do not ask Daily Brief to interpret medical, legal, or financial documents as advice. Use it to surface the email; open the original.

## Privacy and control

Google is explicit that Daily Brief depends on connected apps and Memory. That is a trade: a better list in exchange for Gemini reading those sources.

Practical limits:

- Use a personal account you control. Do not enable this on a shared family login.
- Disconnect Workspace from Personal Intelligence if you do not want inbox contents in Gemini.
- Turn Daily Brief off without deleting Gemini: **Settings & help → Personal Intelligence → Daily brief** off.
- Review Gemini Apps Activity after you use **Chat** from an item.

Spark is a different switch. If you only wanted a morning list, you do not need to enable Spark, remote browser control, or paid agent tasks.

## Troubleshooting

**No Daily Brief in the sidebar**  
Confirm US + 18+ + personal account + Personal Intelligence (Workspace) + Memory. If all five are set, you are waiting on the gradual rollout Google documents.

**Empty or generic brief**  
There is nothing for Gemini to rank. Add real Calendar events and use Gmail on that same account for a few days. Memory with zero past chats will not invent goals.

**Wrong items ranked first**  
Mark complete, dismiss, and use Helpful / Not helpful. Check the source popup so you know which app to disconnect.

**Work mail never appears**  
By design. Help says work and school accounts are not supported.

**You expected Spark-style automation**  
Daily Brief lists and suggests. Spark (Pro/Ultra) runs multi-step tasks in the background. Keep them separate so you do not grant browser-control permissions for a morning digest.

## Conclusion

Daily Brief is useful when it replaces the first ten minutes of inbox triage. Turn on Personal Intelligence and Memory, open the sidebar (or ask Live), work only Top of mind, and grade the cards. The September 2026 US free expansion matters only if those settings are on and you stay on a personal English-language account.

If the control is missing, wait for eligibility rather than flipping unrelated Gemini Labs flags. When you need an agent that books, files, or drives a remote browser, that is Spark — a different product with a different subscription line.
