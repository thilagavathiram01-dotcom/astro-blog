Promo codes, headline alerts, and social likes can bury a bank code or a family message. Pixel Notification Organizer is Google's on-device answer: it sorts matching alerts into categories, bundles them, and silences the ones you mark as lower priority.

Google first flagged the feature in the [November 2025 Pixel Drop](https://blog.google/products/pixel/november-2025-pixel-drop/), saying it would arrive the following month to reduce notification overwhelm. The official setup steps now live on the [Pixel Phone Help page for AI notification tools](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/16691280). This guide follows that page, plus the device and category details reported by Android Authority after the rollout.

If a switch is missing, check language, region, and phone model before you assume the feature failed.

## Which Pixels get Notification Organizer

Google's help page says Notification Organizer is available on **Pixel 9 and later, excluding A-series** phones. That leaves out the Pixel 9a. Older lines, including the Pixel 8 series and the first Pixel Fold, are not on the supported list reported by [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/how-to-use-pixel-notification-organizer-3624003/).

Supported models from that report:

- Pixel 9, Pixel 9 Pro, Pixel 9 Pro XL, Pixel 9 Pro Fold
- Pixel 10, Pixel 10 Pro, Pixel 10 Pro XL, Pixel 10 Pro Fold

The same report says the feature started shipping with Android 16 QPR2 stable (build BP4A.251205.006) or a newer stable release. It is Pixel-only at launch. Samsung, OnePlus, and other Android phones do not get this Pixel setting.

You also need both of these, per Google:

- Primary system language set to **English**
- Region set to Australia, Canada, Germany, Japan, the United Kingdom, or the United States

Change those under **Settings > System > Languages & region** (wording can vary slightly by Android version). If you switch region and the menu still does not appear, reboot, stay on Wi-Fi, and give the phone time to download the model. Android Authority noted the setting sometimes showed up only after a reboot and idle time with a healthy battery.

## Turn the organizer on

Google says the feature is **on by default** once it is available. Confirm it yourself:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Notifications**.
3. Tap **Notification organizer**.
4. Leave **Use notification organizer** (or the main toggle) on.

If the row is missing, confirm English as the primary language, a supported region, and a non-A-series Pixel 9 or later. Then restart the phone. Google's troubleshooting note also says to update apps in the Play Store under **Manage apps & device > Update all**, and to restart after you change settings.

You may see a prompt card in the notification shade when the feature first lands. You can accept it there or open the same screen from Settings.

If the toggle is on and nothing is bundled, turn it off, turn it back on, and wait for a few new alerts. Android Authority found that cycle restarted sorting when the feature had arrived but was idle.

## Pick the four categories

Google lists four buckets. **Promotions** and **News** are on by default. **Social** and **Suggested** stay off until you enable them.

Descriptions used in Google's help copy and in Android Authority's walkthrough:

- **Promotions:** sales, new features, and marketing offers
- **News:** top stories, editorials, and headlines
- **Social:** likes, posts, and other social updates
- **Suggested:** newsletters and recommended media

Open **Settings > Notifications > Notification organizer** and tick only the categories you want silenced and bundled. Leave Social off if you still want likes and mentions to ping you. Leave Suggested off if newsletter alerts are how you track a project.

Android Authority's testing showed an **Always expand bundles** option. When it is on, a category opens into individual alerts instead of a collapsed count. Use that if you want a quieter shade but still want every line visible without an extra tap.

Organized alerts land in the **Silent** section of the shade. They should not sound or vibrate. Tap a bundle to expand it, then open the app if you need the full item. One swipe can clear a whole category when the bundle is collapsed.

The sorter looks at notification **content**, not only the app name. Android Authority's test app sent three kinds of alerts: conversational messages stayed as normal interruptions, while promotion and suggested items were bundled. A shopping app can still buzz you for an order update and stay quiet for a coupon.

Google warns that the organizer uses AI on your device and **may sometimes make mistakes**. Treat a misfiled alert as expected, not as a broken phone.

## Exclude apps that must always alert

Do not rely on categories alone for banking, two-factor codes, medical apps, or a child's school chat.

Google documents two ways to opt an app out:

**From a notification**

1. Long-press the notification.
2. Turn off Notification organizer for that app.

**From Settings**

1. Open **Settings > Notifications > Notification organizer**.
2. Open **Don't include these apps**.
3. Tap **Manage apps** and add the apps that should always appear as normal alerts.

Excluded apps skip bundling even if the text looks like a promotion. Review that list after you install a new finance or health app.

If sorting never starts, Google also points to two system switches:

- **Settings > Notifications > Enhanced notifications** — turn Enhanced notifications on
- **Settings > Notifications > Privacy > Notification read, reply & control** — allow **Android System Intelligence**

Those services are what let Pixel read notification text on device so it can classify it. If Android System Intelligence is blocked, the organizer has nothing to sort.

## How this differs from Notification Summaries

The same Pixel help page covers a second tool: **Notification Summaries**. Do not mix them up.

Summaries recap longer unread chats in the shade, marked with a sparkle icon. Google says they work on Pixel 9 and later, excluding A-series, in English and Japanese. They are **off by default**. Short texts, emoji-only messages, mixed languages, and an active screen can stop a summary from generating. Google also says the screen needs to be off for a moment.

Organizer does not rewrite the message. It classifies and silences. Summaries try to shorten a thread. You can use both, or only the organizer.

To turn summaries on: **Settings > Notifications > Notification Summaries**. Use **Don't include these apps** there too, so private chats stay unsummarized.

If summaries never appear, Google suggests leaving Battery Saver off, staying on Wi-Fi, charging overnight so the AI download can finish, and restarting after setting changes.

## A short setup checklist

1. Confirm a Pixel 9 or later that is not an A-series phone, on a current stable update.
2. Set the primary language to English and the region to AU, CA, DE, JP, UK, or US.
3. Reboot, then open **Settings > Notifications > Notification organizer**.
4. Keep Promotions and News on. Add Social or Suggested only if those alerts annoy you.
5. Exclude banks, authenticators, and family chats under **Don't include these apps**.
6. Confirm Enhanced notifications and Android System Intelligence are allowed.
7. Send yourself a promo-style test from an app you do not mind silencing, and check the Silent section.

## Practical limits

Notification Organizer will not fix a phone that grants every app full notification access and then expects perfect judgment. The model can misfile a useful deal as noise, or leave a marketing alert in the alerting section. English-only support means non-English notification text may not sort even if the system language is English.

It also will not replace per-app channel controls. If a game has a separate channel for events and another for messages, turn the noisy channel off in the app's notification settings. Use the organizer for the leftover low-priority pile.

Because classification runs on the device, Google's help flow does not ask you to upload notification text to turn the feature on. Keep Android System Intelligence allowed if you want sorting. Revoke it if you would rather handle every alert yourself.

## Conclusion

Start with the default pair: Promotions and News. Exclude the apps that must interrupt you. Add Social or Suggested only after a day of normal use, so you can see what landed in Silent.

That is the whole product. It does not read your mind, and Google says it can be wrong. Used with an exclusion list, it keeps coupon blasts and headline stacks out of the way of the alerts you still need to hear.
