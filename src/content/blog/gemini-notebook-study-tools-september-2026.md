Google added a fresh study stack to [Gemini Notebook](https://notebook.google/) on September 15, 2026. The product formerly called NotebookLM now supports real-time voice chat on mobile, an in-app lecture recorder, interactive learning overviews, extra quiz types, and Short Video Overviews you can share.

This guide walks through a practical exam-week workflow. Every feature below comes from Google’s official Gemini Notebook and Workspace posts. Rollout is staged by plan and language, so treat missing buttons as a waitlist item, not a broken app.

## What changed in mid-September

Trond Wuellner, Director of Product Management for Gemini Notebook, listed four study upgrades:

- Real-time spoken conversations with a notebook on the Android and iOS apps, in nearly 100 languages.
- A mobile audio recorder that captures lectures or voice notes and stores them next to your sources.
- Interactive learning overviews under Reports, which combine summaries with studio outputs such as infographics, quizzes, and flashcards.
- Short Video Overviews of about 60 seconds, available in more than 80 languages.

Google also said new quiz formats — short answer, multiple select, and fill in the blank — are arriving for all users in the following weeks. After you finish a quiz or flashcard set, you can ask the notebook chat how you did and where to review next.

Voice chat started with Google AI Ultra subscribers age 18 and older, then Pro and other plans. Audio notes with English as the selected output language work on web and mobile, including users under 18. Interactive overviews and new quiz types are a broader rollout.

## Step 1: Build one notebook per course

Open [notebook.google.com](https://notebook.google/) or the Gemini Notebook mobile app ([Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.labs.language.tailwind) / [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gemini-notebook/id6737527615)). Create a notebook named after the course, not after “exam week.”

Upload only materials you trust:

- Lecture slides and the syllabus
- PDFs of assigned chapters
- Photos of handwritten notes or worksheets (already supported)
- Links or docs you would cite in a paper

Gemini Notebook answers from those sources. That grounding is the point. Do not dump random web pages into the same notebook as your professor’s slides if you need clean citations.

On Workspace accounts, notebooks also appear in the Gemini app side panel. Google’s September 17 Workspace update says you can add up to 10 sources in that Gemini-app notebook path. The standalone Gemini Notebook product still supports larger source sets on paid plans. Keep one course per notebook so citations stay readable.

If you already use Pixel or Android study shortcuts from the [September 2026 Android Drop guide](/blog/android-september-2026-drop-guide/), keep Gemini Notebook as the long-form notes layer and Find Hub or Keep for the small reminders.

## Step 2: Record the lecture instead of typing every line

Starting the week after the September 15 announcement, the mobile app includes an audio recorder. Google’s description is direct: capture lectures and record thoughts on the go. Those recordings sit beside your sources. You can chat with them, cite them, and keep editing the notes.

A workable classroom flow:

1. Open the course notebook before class.
2. Start the recorder when the lecture begins.
3. After class, add the slide deck if you have it.
4. Ask chat: “List the three claims the lecturer added that are not in the slides, with citations.”

Use this only where recording is allowed. Many campuses require consent. The recorder is a capture tool, not permission.

English output language is required for this notes path, per Google’s footnote. Switch the notebook output language in settings if the recorder control does not appear.

## Step 3: Talk through a hard section out loud

Voice chat is the feature students asked for after Audio Overviews. On the mobile apps you can speak, interrupt, and ask follow-ups in nearly 100 languages. Every spoken reply is still tied to your sources.

Try a Socratic pass rather than “explain chapter 4.” Google for Education already teaches that pattern for faculty. A prompt that stays grounded:

> Act as a Socratic tutor using only the sources in this notebook. Ask me one question at a time about [topic]. If I am wrong, point to the source passage and ask a follow-up. Do not lecture unless I ask.

Interrupt when the model drifts. Because the session is live, you can say “stop, show the definition from the syllabus instead.”

Availability note: Google limited the first wave to AI Ultra users 18+. Pro and remaining plans follow. If you only see typed chat, stay on the written Socratic loop until voice lands on your account.

## Step 4: Build an interactive learning overview

Under **Reports**, create an interactive learning overview. Google says these overviews weave summaries together with Studio outputs such as infographics, quizzes, and flashcards. Workspace’s follow-up post also lists mind maps in that mix.

Do this after you have at least one lecture recording plus the official slides. Thin source sets produce thin quizzes.

Suggested order:

1. Generate the overview so you can see the shape of the unit.
2. Open the quiz and flashcard Studio items it references.
3. Edit weak questions. Google explicitly says you can add or rewrite quiz and flashcard items.
4. Complete the set, then ask chat: “Based on the questions I missed, which source pages should I reread?”

New formats coming to everyone: short answer, multiple select, and fill in the blank. Until those appear, multiple-choice plus flashcards still work for active recall.

## Step 5: Make a 60-second video for the formula you keep missing

Short Video Overviews run about a minute. They mix a spoken narrative with educational animations aimed at formulas, diagrams, and science concepts. Google lists support in more than 80 languages and allows sharing with classmates.

Use one video per stubborn idea, not one video for the whole midterm. A single derivation or circuit diagram holds up in 60 seconds. A full unit does not.

Share the link only if the notebook sources are materials you are allowed to redistribute. Lecture recordings and paywalled PDFs usually are not.

## Student plan limits worth checking

Google is running a student offer through December 31, 2026:

- Eligible U.S. college students can claim one year of Google AI Pro (listed at $19.99/month). That plan includes 4x higher Gemini Notebook usage limits versus non-AI subscribers.
- Eligible college students in 140+ other markets can claim one year of Google AI Plus, with 2x higher Notebook limits. The Plus offer excludes the U.S., Bolivia, Albania, Canada, Macau, Hong Kong, and Tunisia.

Both offers require a valid payment method at sign-up. Unless you cancel, billing starts after the year at the standard monthly price. Claim the deal at Google’s [AI student offer page](https://one.google.com/ai-student?plans=1%2C2).

Higher limits matter once you generate quizzes, video overviews, and voice sessions in the same week. Free-tier daily caps on Studio outputs run out fast during finals.

## A compact weekly routine

Monday: create or open the course notebook. Add the week’s slides.

During class: record if permitted. After class, ask for claims that were not on the slides.

Midweek: run one interactive overview. Edit five flashcards that look generic. Take the quiz once, then quiz the chat about misses.

Weekend: generate one Short Video Overview for the concept you still cannot explain out loud. Play it once, then close the laptop and write the same explanation from memory.

Keep Gemini app study notebooks in sync when you want a diagnostic quiz and bite-sized lessons. Google’s June 2026 education post says those study notebooks share sources with Gemini Notebook, so you do not rebuild the library.

## Limits you should plan around

- Voice chat and some Studio features ship by subscription tier and age.
- Audio-note support currently requires English as the output language.
- Interactive overviews and extra quiz types were announced as a multi-week rollout, not a same-day switch.
- The model can misread a slide. Click every citation before you memorize an answer.
- Recording other people without permission can violate school policy even when the app allows it.

Gemini Notebook is a source-grounded study desk. It is not a substitute for the exam rules your instructor published.

## Conclusion

The September 2026 study tools are useful when you treat them as a loop: capture the lecture, talk through the hard page, test yourself, then make a one-minute video of the leftover gap. Start with a single course notebook this week. Add voice only after the written chat already cites the right paragraph.

If the new controls are missing, update the mobile app and check whether your account is on Ultra, Pro, Plus, or the student trial. Google is still turning the features on in waves.
