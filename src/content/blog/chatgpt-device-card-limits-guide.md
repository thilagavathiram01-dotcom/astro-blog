OpenAI added card and device limits to ChatGPT as privacy and security measures. If too many paid subscriptions link to one payment card or one device, new purchases can be blocked. You may also see **Device limit reached** when signing in, which means that paid account cannot be used on the current device.

These rules target ChatGPT Go, Plus, Pro 100, Pro 200, and Pro 500. Free accounts and Business, Enterprise, and Edu workspace plans do not count toward the limits. Signing out of one account does not automatically free a slot for another paid account.

## Which plans and situations are affected

Device limits apply only to the personal paid plans listed above. Workspace plans stay outside the count. The device restriction is separate from how many accounts you can stay signed into at once. Account switching on the web and mobile lets you keep two accounts active in the switcher, but that does not override the device limit.

A blocked checkout message reads: “Too many subscriptions associated with this card or device. We can’t complete this purchase.” This is not a bank decline. The purchase simply does not go through because of the card or device association.

You can still buy eligible credits or use available usage resets on an account that is already permitted. Credits and resets do not clear a card or device restriction.

## What to do when a purchase is blocked

1. Confirm the exact message on the checkout screen. If it mentions too many subscriptions on the card or device, the block is from ChatGPT, not your bank.
2. Stay on an existing permitted account. Open Settings > Usage in ChatGPT (or Usage & Billing in the Codex app) to check credit purchase options or remaining resets.
3. If you share a card with family or colleagues, each person should use their own device where possible. Contact Support if you believe the restriction is wrong and include only your own account details.

OpenAI notes that a shared household or public device can hit the limit even when each person uses a separate account. Provide Support with the warning text (screenshot with personal info removed), your email and sign-in method, the plan you tried to buy, the app or site, the time (including time zone), and whether you were charged.

## Steps if you see Device limit reached

Confirm the warning shows the account you expected. Then choose one of these paths:

1. Select **Log into another account** and sign in with a different permitted account you own.
2. Cancel and request a refund for the affected subscription(s).
   - For purchases billed through ChatGPT or Google Play, choose the cancellation and refund option on the warning. OpenAI cancels the affected subscriptions and starts the refund. Paid access ends immediately when cancellation succeeds.
   - For Apple purchases, cancel the subscription and request the refund directly through Apple.

Refunds take time to appear on your payment method. If further information is needed, Support opens a case and emails you. Seeing the warning does not mean a refund has already been processed.

If the purchase completed and you were blocked immediately, or if you used the subscription before being signed out, tell Support. Include the account, any subscription ID shown, purchase details, and when access stopped.

## Using credits and resets on an existing account

When you need more usage on a plan you can already access, check Settings > Usage. Credit availability depends on your account, plan, region, and the feature. Credits pay for eligible usage after included limits are reached. They do not unlock models your plan cannot access, combine subscriptions, or transfer usage between accounts.

If credits are not available, look for other options such as waiting for the next reset or using an eligible reset already on the account. Options vary by plan.

These steps do not remove a device or card restriction. For more on managing multiple accounts, see our guide to [ChatGPT plugins and multiple accounts](/blog/chatgpt-plugins-multiple-accounts/).

## Common questions

**Will canceling let me buy another subscription?**  
Self-service cancellation normally ends at the close of the current billing period. Using the cancellation-and-refund option on the device warning for ChatGPT or Google Play ends paid access immediately. Neither route guarantees that a new purchase or paid-account sign-in becomes available. Subscriptions bought before the restrictions can continue under an existing-subscription exception. Contact Support before making changes if you are unsure.

**Is this the same as a usage limit?**  
No. A usage limit controls how much you can use on a subscription you already have. A card or device limit can block a new purchase. “Device limit reached” means you cannot use that paid account on this device.

**Does the warning mean someone accessed my account?**  
A blocked purchase or device-limit warning alone does not indicate unauthorized access. If you also see unfamiliar activity, contact Support and describe what you observed.

## Tips to avoid the limits

Keep paid personal plans on separate devices when possible. Use Free accounts or workspace plans for additional logins that do not count toward the device limit. Check your current usage and credit options before starting a new checkout. If you share a payment card, coordinate with the other people who use it so you do not hit the association limit unexpectedly.

For privacy-related controls, review the settings in our [ChatGPT privacy center setup guide](/blog/chatgpt-privacy-center-setup/).

## Conclusion

Card and device limits are active privacy and security protections in ChatGPT. They stop new paid subscriptions from stacking on the same card or device and can sign you out of a paid account that exceeds the device allowance. Free and workspace plans are unaffected. When you hit either restriction, use another permitted account you own, request a refund through the appropriate platform, or stay on an existing account and buy credits or wait for a reset. Contact Support with clear details if you believe the restriction is incorrect.
