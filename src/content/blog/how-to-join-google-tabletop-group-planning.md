**How to Join Google Tabletop and Build Shared Planning Tools**

Group chats get messy fast when you plan a trip, potluck, or rec league. Google Labs just released Tabletop to fix that.

Tabletop is a new experiment that gives groups a shared space. You drop in links, files, and ideas. Then you prompt @tabletop (or pick a starter template) to generate interactive tools like trip itineraries, dinner polls, expense splitters, or tournament brackets. Everyone invited can use and edit the space at the same time, the same way people work in a Google Doc.

Access is limited right now. Tabletop is rolling out in the United States for people 18 and older through a waitlist. Sign up at labs.google/tabletop.

![Team collaborating around a whiteboard during a meeting](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1758873268998-2f77c2d38862?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What Tabletop Does

Tabletop turns rough group ideas into ready-to-use mini tools. Official examples include:

- Interactive road-trip maps and brunch finders
- Dinner-decider wheels and expense splitters
- Live tournament brackets for pickleball or other leagues
- Drag-and-drop seating charts
- Spin wheels that decide who walks the dog

You can start from a template or describe what you need. After the tool appears, anyone in the space can refine it. The shared chat stays in the same place, so updates do not scatter across separate apps.

Google has not named the underlying model. The product focuses on real-time collaboration rather than long solo reasoning.

## How to Join the Waitlist

1. Open labs.google/tabletop in a browser.
2. Confirm you are 18 or older and in the United States.
3. Enter your email and submit the form.
4. Wait for access. Google is admitting people in waves.

No public timeline exists for other countries. Until you get in, you can watch the official demo to see the interface.

## Create Your First Space

Once you have access:

1. Start a new space inside Tabletop.
2. Add any links, files, or notes the group already has.
3. Type a prompt that starts with @tabletop, for example: “@tabletop Make an interactive bracket for our pickleball league with random matchups.”
4. Or select a starter template such as a poll or itinerary.
5. Review the generated tool and adjust it with follow-up prompts if needed.
6. Copy the invite link and paste it into your existing group chat.

Members who open the link can view and edit the tools live. Changes appear for everyone without separate exports.

## Practical Tips

- Collect the key links and files before you prompt. Context helps the first version stay useful.
- Keep prompts short and specific. “Build a dinner poll that tracks dietary restrictions and preferred cuisine” works better than a vague request.
- Use follow-ups to refine. You can ask Tabletop to change rules, add fields, or restyle a tool after the first version appears.
- Share the invite early so the group can test and improve the tool together.
- Treat Tabletop as an experiment. Features and availability can change while it stays in Labs.

## Limitations

Tabletop is U.S.-only and 18+ for now. It requires a waitlist. Google has not published pricing details or a general release date. External guest rules and data retention details are not fully documented in the public announcement. Check the product page for the latest terms once you join.

## Conclusion

Tabletop offers a practical way to turn group chat chaos into shared, interactive tools. If you are in the United States and 18 or older, join the waitlist at labs.google/tabletop and try a starter template or a short @tabletop prompt on your next event.
