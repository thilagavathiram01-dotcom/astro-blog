Google Wallet arrived on iPhone in the United States on November 17, 2026. The free App Store app pulls tickets, boarding passes, loyalty cards, and order details into one place that syncs with your Gmail and other Google apps. It also speeds up online checkout with saved payment methods. Tap-to-pay in stores is not available.

This guide walks through setup, adding cards, tracking packages, and practical tips. You need an iPhone running iOS 17.0 or later and a Google account. The download is about 171 MB.

## What Google Wallet on iOS Actually Does

The app organizes everyday digital items that already exist in your Google account. Package tracking numbers from Gmail appear with delivery status. Boarding passes and concert tickets show up automatically when they arrive in email. Loyalty cards and gym memberships can be scanned with the camera.

Payment cards stored in your Google account become available for faster online checkout across Google services and any site that accepts Google Pay. Receipts and order confirmations land in the same list. Google positions the app as a secure hub for agentic purchases made through Gemini and other Google tools.

It does not replace Apple Wallet for contactless payments at physical stores. Google’s official announcement states that tap-to-pay is not supported on iOS at this time.

## Step-by-Step Setup

Open the App Store on your iPhone. Search for “Google Wallet.” Confirm the developer is Google LLC and the app is free. Install it. The listing requires iOS 17.0 or later.

After installation, open the app and sign in with the same Google account you use for Gmail. Grant the requested permissions so it can read relevant emails and sync data. The app will begin importing existing tickets, boarding passes, and order information tied to that account.

Once signed in, the home screen shows recent purchases, boarding passes, and a grid of loyalty cards. A floating navigation pill lets you switch between the main view and transaction history. You can pin important passes so they stay at the top.

If you use both an Android phone and an iPhone, the same Google account keeps the data consistent across devices. Changes made on one appear on the other after a short sync.

## Adding Cards and Passes

To add a physical loyalty card or membership, tap the plus button and choose the camera option. Hold the card steady under the camera. The app reads the barcode or QR code and saves it. You can also add gift cards the same way.

Payment methods already saved to your Google account appear automatically. You can review or manage them inside the app. These cards are used for online checkouts; they do not enable store tap-to-pay on iPhone.

For passes that arrive by email, most appear without extra steps once the app has access to Gmail. If a specific boarding pass or ticket is missing, open the original email and confirm it is associated with the signed-in Google account.

## Tracking Orders and Using Online Payments

Order emails in Gmail feed directly into Google Wallet. You see expected delivery dates, tracking numbers, and status updates in one list. This is especially useful if you shop across multiple retailers and previously hunted through your inbox.

When you check out online at a site that supports Google Pay, the saved cards from Wallet appear as options. You can complete the purchase faster without re-entering card details. Receipts return to the app for later reference.

Google notes that the app will also serve as the place to review purchases made by AI agents across its services. Order confirmations and delivery updates for those transactions appear alongside ordinary orders.

## Tips and Current Limitations

Keep the app updated through the App Store so new import rules and UI improvements arrive promptly. If a pass fails to appear, force-close the app, reopen it, and wait a minute for the next sync.

You can adjust which Google services feed data into Wallet through your account settings. Review privacy controls if you share the device or want to limit which emails are scanned.

The main limitation remains tap-to-pay. For in-store contactless payments you will still use Apple Wallet or a physical card. Google has not announced a timeline for adding that feature on iOS.

The app is currently limited to the United States. Users outside the US will not see it in the App Store.
