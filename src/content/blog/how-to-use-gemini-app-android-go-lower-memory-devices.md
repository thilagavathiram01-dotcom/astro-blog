Google expanded the Gemini app to eligible Android (Go edition) phones on 18 November 2026. These devices often have only 2GB of RAM, yet they now run the core Gemini experience after Google re-architected the app.

Hundreds of millions of people use lower-memory Android phones as their main link to the internet, school, and work. Until now those phones offered only basic voice commands. The update brings Gemini Live, camera help, image creation, and everyday tasks to them.

This guide shows how to check eligibility, install the app, start conversations, and work around the limits. All details come from the official Google blog post and Gemini Apps Help page.

## Check if your phone qualifies

Your device must run Android (Go edition) and have 2GB of RAM or more. Sign in with a personal Google Account, or a work or school account that has Gemini access. The feature works in every language and country where Gemini is already available.

Parents who manage accounts through Family Link can turn Gemini off for users under 13 (or the age required in their country).

The rollout is gradual. Some eligible phones may not show the app yet.

## Install or update Gemini

On Android Go phones, Gemini lives inside the Google Search app. Update that app first:

1. Open the Google Play Store.
2. Search for “Google” or “Google Search”.
3. Tap Update if the button appears. If it says Open, you already have the latest version.

You can also download the standalone Gemini app from the Play Store. Some newer devices include it preloaded. Google is working with partners such as Transsion (parent of Tecno, Infinix, and itel) so future phones arrive with Gemini already installed.

## Start a conversation

Two simple methods work on supported phones:

- Touch and hold the Home button.
- Press and hold the Power button.

“Hey Google” voice activation and “Ask about this screen” are not supported on Android Go devices. Use the button methods instead.

Once Gemini opens, speak or type your request. The app replaces the older Assistant Go experience with a more conversational interface.

## What you can do

The re-architected app keeps the core features people use most often:

- **Stay connected.** Ask Gemini to call or send a text message to a contact.
- **Camera help.** Point the camera at notes, forms, or signs. Gemini can translate text or walk you through the content.
- **Gemini Live.** Hold a natural spoken conversation. Practice for an interview or brainstorm ideas in more than 45 languages.
- **Image creation.** Generate pictures with Nano Banana. Google’s example is a flyer for a weekend bakery sale.
- **Local information.** Check drive time or ask for a restaurant that is open on Tuesday with an EV charger nearby.
- **Daily planning.** Set an alarm or create a calendar event.
- **Files and media.** Upload a document or photo for context. Ask Gemini to play music by mood or activity.

These tasks run through Google’s cloud, so the phone itself does not need to store the full model.

## Practical tips for lower-memory phones

Close other apps before you start a long Gemini Live session. The 2GB limit leaves little room for background processes.

Keep the Google Search app and Play Store updated. New improvements often arrive through those packages first.

If the Gemini app does not appear after updating, wait a day or two. Google is rolling the feature out gradually.

Test camera features in good light. Translation and form help work best when the text is clear and steady.

For multi-step work such as comparing prices or booking, try the free Tasks feature if it is available in your country. See our guide on [handing off daily errands with Gemini Tasks](/blog/how-to-use-gemini-tasks-free-android/).

## Limits to remember

“Hey Google” and screen questions remain unavailable. Button shortcuts are the reliable way to launch Gemini.

Work and school accounts need an active Gemini plan or license. Personal accounts work without extra steps.

Performance can vary by exact phone model. Google has not published a full list of supported devices beyond the 2GB RAM and Android Go requirements.

## Conclusion

The Gemini app now reaches hundreds of millions more people on entry-level Android Go phones. Update the Google Search app or download Gemini from the Play Store, then launch it with a long-press on Home or Power. Core tools—Live conversations, camera translation, image creation, calls, and daily planning—are ready to use.

Check the official help page if a feature is missing on your device. The gradual rollout means more phones will gain access over the coming weeks.
