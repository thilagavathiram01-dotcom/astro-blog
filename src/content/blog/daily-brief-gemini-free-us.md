Gemini Daily Brief used to sit behind a paid Google AI plan. In early September 2026, Google opened it to more personal accounts in the United States. You still need to flip a few settings. After that, the brief lands in the Gemini app, on the web, and in Gemini Live.

This guide follows Google’s official help article. It covers who qualifies, how to connect the right apps, where to open the brief, and how to mark items done without treating the list as gospel.

## What Daily Brief actually is

Daily Brief is a generated list, not a second inbox. Gemini pulls timely items from Connected Apps such as Gmail and Google Calendar, plus your Gemini chats. It then groups them so you can scan instead of hopping between tabs.

Google’s help page lists two main sections:

- **Top of mind:** time-sensitive items from mail, calendar, and recent chats.
- **Looking ahead:** longer-term goals with suggested next steps, based on Personal Intelligence.

9to5Google also reported an **FYI** group for dated items that are not urgent. Treat extra labels as product polish. The official source of truth remains Top of mind and Looking ahead.

Google positions the brief for students, parents, job seekers, and small-business owners. The same machinery runs for all of them. Only the source mail and calendar events change.

## Who can use the free US rollout

Google still gates the feature. Official requirements:

- You are **18 or older** and in the **United States**.
- You sign in with a **personal** Google Account. Work, school, and supervised accounts are excluded.
- You are eligible for **Personal Intelligence**.
- You **connect Google Workspace** to Gemini Apps.
- You **turn on Memory**.
- You use **English**.
- You open Gemini on **mobile**, **gemini.google.com**, or **Gemini Live**. Daily Brief is not a Google Messages feature.

Google states that it is releasing Daily Brief gradually. Missing the menu item usually means your account is not in the current wave, not that you installed the wrong APK.

A paid Google AI Plus, Pro, or Ultra plan is no longer required for this US expansion. That change was confirmed by Google’s Gemini account and by reporting from The Verge and 9to5Google around 8–9 September 2026. Do not assume every other Gemini agent is free. Spark and some media tools still sit on paid tiers.

## Step 1: Sign in on the right account

Open the Gemini app on Android or iOS, or go to [gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com). Confirm the avatar is your personal account. If you also use a Workspace login on the same phone, switch before you touch Personal Intelligence.

Keep Activity must stay available for Connected Apps on the web and on iOS. Official Connected Apps help notes that when Keep Activity is off, most third-party and Workspace connectors drop off those surfaces. Android still keeps a short list of device apps, which is not enough for Daily Brief.

## Step 2: Connect Workspace and turn on Memory

Daily Brief will not invent a useful list from a blank profile.

1. In Gemini, open **Settings** (on the web: **Settings & help**).
2. Open **Personal Intelligence** or **Connected Apps**, depending on the client.
3. Connect **Google Workspace** so Gemini can read Gmail and Calendar you already use.
4. Turn **Memory** on so past chats can inform Looking ahead.
5. Leave Daily brief enabled if the toggle is visible. On the web, Google documents the path **Settings & help → Personal Intelligence → Daily brief**.

Third-party connectors from the September 2026 wave (Airtable, Linear, Peloton, and others) can enrich Personal Intelligence later. They are not listed as required sources for Top of mind. Start with Workspace. Add extras only if you want those tools in other Gemini chats. Setup for that wave is in [How to Use Gemini Connected Apps After the Sept 2026 Wave](/blog/gemini-connected-apps-september-2026/).

## Step 3: Open the brief

On a computer:

1. Go to gemini.google.com.
2. In the sidebar, click **Daily brief**.

On a phone:

1. Open the Gemini app.
2. Tap **Menu**, then **Daily brief**.

In Gemini Live, start a session and say “What’s my day look like?” or “Brief me.” Google also documents “What’s my daily brief?” as a Live prompt.

The Gemini account said Google can send a morning notification. Turn that off under account **Notifications** if you already check the sidebar at breakfast.

Windows users who installed the native client still open the same Google Account. The desktop overlay is a launcher, not a second brief. Install notes live in [How to Use the Gemini App for Windows](/blog/gemini-app-windows/).

## Step 4: Act on items without losing the thread

Google documents four actions:

- **Chat** about an item. That chat is saved to Gemini Apps Activity.
- **Mark complete** after you handle it.
- **Dismiss** items that should not reappear.
- **Mark incomplete** if you closed something too early.
- **Check the source** so you can open the original mail or event.
- **Give feedback** when an item is helpful or not.

Use the source chip. Daily Brief can surface an older thread when a newer reply exists. Official Workspace help for Gemini apps says responses can be outdated. Open the message before you RSVP or pay anyone.

Do not treat Looking ahead as a project plan. Those lines are model suggestions from Personal Intelligence, not commitments you typed.

## Limits you should expect

- Rollout is **staged**. Eligibility can lag a week or more after the announcement.
- Language is **English only** for now.
- Work and school accounts are **out**.
- Offline phones will not refresh a new brief. Gemini needs a network to assemble sources.
- Remembered household objects in Find Hub are a different product. Do not look for passports inside Daily Brief.
- Gemini can miss a deadline or invent a soft next step. Confirm dates on Calendar.

If the menu never appears after you connect Workspace and Memory, wait for the wave. Reinstalling the app will not force a server flag.

## Tips that keep the list short

Turn Memory on only if you want past chats in Looking ahead. If you use Gemini for throwaway drafts, Memory will keep recycling those topics.

Connect the Calendar you actually honor. A secondary calendar full of optional webinars will crowd Top of mind.

Mark complete on the same day. Feedback is how Google says the list learns what belongs at the top.

Read the source before you forward a brief item to a coworker. The summary is yours. The underlying mail may include details you did not intend to share.

Use Live when your hands are busy. Use the sidebar when you need to tap Mark complete and jump into Gmail.

## Conclusion

Daily Brief is useful when Workspace is connected, Memory is on, and you treat the page as a skim list with links back to real mail and events. The September 2026 US change removes the paid-plan gate for personal accounts. It does not remove age, region, language, or gradual-rollout limits.

Set the two switches, open the sidebar tomorrow morning, and spend thirty seconds on sources instead of rereading your entire inbox.
