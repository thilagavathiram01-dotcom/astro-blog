YouTube Music already holds a catalog of more than 300 million tracks. Finding the next artist or podcast still takes work. At Made On YouTube on 23 September 2026, YouTube announced two Music-app tools meant to shorten that search: **Ask Music** and **Podcast Lineup**.

Ask Music is a conversational layer inside the YouTube Music app. You describe a queue, a mood, or an artist question in plain language. Podcast Lineup is a weekly spoken briefing of recommended shows for Music and Premium subscribers. Both are rolling out worldwide after the event. This guide covers what YouTube published, how to try each tool, and what is still coming soon.

If you already built homepage Custom Feeds on YouTube, treat this as the audio counterpart. Our [Custom Feeds walkthrough](/blog/youtube-custom-feeds-made-on-2026/) covers the video homepage path.

## What YouTube actually shipped

T. Jay Fowler, senior director of product management for YouTube Music, wrote that Music users discovered an artist new to them more than 450 million times in July 2026. Ask Music and Podcast Lineup sit on that discovery stack.

Official scope:

- **Ask Music** lives in the YouTube Music app. It covers podcasts plus official studio recordings, remixes, live performances, covers, and DJ sets.
- You can customize a queue or ask for artist background, not only “play this song.”
- **Podcast Lineup** is a personalized AI-audio guide. Once a week it reads recommended shows, explains why they fit you, then jumps into suggested episodes. YouTube calls it eyes-optional and hands-free.
- Both updates are coming to **YouTube Music and Premium subscribers worldwide**.

Ask Music is not brand new. In May 2026 YouTube said Premium subscribers in select countries already used it to generate personalized radio and playlists, then added podcast requests. The September update expands queue control and artist lore across the catalog YouTube now quotes at over 300 million songs.

## Before you open Ask Music

1. Update **YouTube Music** from the Play Store or App Store.
2. Sign in with the Google account that holds your Music or Premium subscription.
3. Confirm the app is YouTube Music, not only the main YouTube video app. Ask Music is documented for the Music app.
4. If the Ask control is missing, you are still waiting on the worldwide wave. YouTube’s own post says the updates are “coming soon,” not that every account received them on 23 September.

Premium is not required to use YouTube Music, but Podcast Lineup is described as a Music **and Premium** subscriber feature. Treat Premium as the safer path for the weekly spoken guide.

## How to run Ask Music

YouTube did not publish a pixel-perfect button path in the September post. It did publish the jobs the tool is built for. Use this sequence in the Music app once Ask Music appears:

1. Open YouTube Music and look for **Ask Music** on Home or in the search/Ask field.
2. State the outcome, not a single title. Examples that match official language:
   - “Build a queue of live performances and covers in this artist’s lane.”
   - “Keep this mix going but lean more toward late-night remixes.”
   - “What is the story behind this track?”
   - “Find podcasts like this show for a 30-minute commute.”
3. Review the queue before you walk away. Ask Music can add official recordings, remixes, and DJ sets in one pass. Remove anything that does not belong.
4. Follow up in the same thread: “Drop the covers” or “Add more live sets from the same year.”

May 2026 already let some Premium users ask for podcast recommendations by genre, mood, or a show they like. Keep that habit. One constraint per prompt works better than a paragraph of rules.

Do not treat Ask Music as a rights dashboard. It recommends and queues licensed catalog items. It does not grant you a license to reuse a track in your own video.

## How to use Podcast Lineup

Podcast Lineup is a weekly audio briefing, not a second inbox of show notes.

When it appears in YouTube Music:

1. Open the Music app on a day you usually commute or cook.
2. Find **Podcast Lineup** (YouTube also writes it as “your podcast lineup”).
3. Play the spoken preview. It names recommended shows and why they were picked, then can jump into suggested episodes.
4. Save or subscribe only to the episodes you want. The briefing is a guide, not an auto-download of every recommendation.

YouTube stresses that the guide is hands-free. Use it with headphones and background play if you already pay for Premium. Premium users already had listener-friendly controls such as on-the-go mode, Jump ahead, and faster playback from the May 2026 podcast update. Lineup sits on top of that listening surface.

If you never listen to podcasts in Music, skip Lineup. It will not improve a queue you never open.

## Prompts that match the official jobs

Copy these and swap the names:

- “Queue instrumental remixes and live sets for two hours of desk work.”
- “Add official studio versions only. Remove DJ edits.”
- “Explain this artist’s recent releases in two minutes, then play the latest official single.”
- “Podcasts about city planning for a 25-minute walk. Nothing longer than 40 minutes.”
- “More shows like this one, but weekly instead of daily.”

Custom homepage feeds on YouTube video use the same style of prompt. YouTube’s viewer post gives examples such as “Witty podcasts for my daily 30-minute commute.” Ask Music is the place to run that request when you want audio now, not a homepage tile.

## What this is not

- It is **not** Live auto-dubbing. Real-time live translation is a separate Live pilot scheduled for early 2027, starting with English and Spanish in YouTube’s own FAQ and Live post.
- It is **not** Gemini Shorts editing. Conversational edit tools in Shorts and YouTube Create are a creator product. See [Gemini conversational editing for Shorts](/blog/youtube-gemini-conversational-editing/) if that is the job.
- It is **not** a guarantee the control is on your phone today. Fowler’s post says worldwide rollout is coming, not complete.

Availability still varies by country, app version, and subscription. If Ask Music only builds radio and cannot edit a queue, you are on the older May 2026 surface.

## A 10-minute check

1. Update YouTube Music.
2. Search Home for Ask Music. Run one queue prompt and one artist-lore prompt.
3. Play a single Podcast Lineup briefing if the card is there. Subscribe to at most one new show.
4. Delete recommendations you will not play. A bloated queue trains the next briefing in the wrong direction.
5. Keep Custom Feeds on the video homepage for long documentaries and DIY channels. Keep Ask Music for audio.

YouTube’s job with these two tools is discovery, not production. Use them to fill a commute. Keep editing, dubbing, and brand pitches in Studio.
