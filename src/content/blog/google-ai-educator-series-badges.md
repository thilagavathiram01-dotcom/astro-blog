Google wants every U.S. K-12 teacher and higher-education faculty member to have free, practical AI training. On 21 September 2026 it added a $4 million Google.org grant to Digital Promise so more districts and community colleges can run that training at scale.

You do not wait for the grant to land at your school. The [Google AI Educator Series](https://edu.google.com/learning-center/google-ai-educator-series/) is already live on the Google for Education Learning Center. Sessions last 15 minutes for K-12 and 30 to 45 minutes for higher ed. Finish a quiz and you get a digital badge.

This walkthrough covers how to sign in, pick a track, complete a module, and claim that badge. Facts come from Google’s September 2026 blog post, the Learning Center program page, and the earlier teacher AI literacy announcement.

## What the series is

Google built the series with ISTE (now ISTE+ASCD) using a teachers-teaching-teachers model. Content focuses on transferable skills, instructional judgment, and classroom or campus tasks you can run in Gemini and related Google tools.

Modules sit in three buckets:

- **Foundational understanding** — what generative AI can and cannot do, and how to keep students in control of the work.
- **Pedagogical applications** — grade-band or campus use cases such as custom materials, scaffolding, and study supports.
- **Administrative applications** — lesson planning, syllabus refresh, and other staff workflows.

Sessions are non-sequential. Skip anything you already teach. Google describes them as snackable and stackable: take one over coffee, or string several into a faculty meeting.

The September 2026 Digital Promise grant does not replace this catalog. It funds state agencies, districts, and community-college systems to deliver tailored cohorts, plus a public higher-ed adoption guide over the next two years.

## Create your Learning Center account

1. Open the [Google AI Educator Series](https://edu.google.com/learning-center/google-ai-educator-series/) page.
2. Sign in with the Google account you use for school or campus work.
3. Use **Sign up** / **Sign up for updates** if you want the monthly content drop notice.
4. Optional: complete the pre-survey at [goo.gle/gespresurvey](https://goo.gle/gespresurvey) so later recommendations match your role.
5. Bookmark the badge hub at [educertifications.google/google-ai-educator-series](https://educertifications.google/google-ai-educator-series).

Use one account for training and badges. Mixing a personal Gmail with a district Workspace login is the fastest way to lose a completed quiz.

District admins who want a facilitated day should also grab the facilitator materials from the Learning Center (Google publishes a facilitator guide short link at goo.gle/GES-FG).

## Pick K-12 or higher ed, then one session

On the program page, choose **K-12** or **Higher Ed**. K-12 modules run about 10 to 15 minutes and are often grade-band specific. Higher-ed sessions run about 30 to 45 minutes and cover topics such as cognitive load, inclusive design, agentic workflows, and syllabus updates.

Then:

1. Open **View all sessions** for your track.
2. Pick one use case you will teach this week. Google’s own examples include Gemini lesson materials and Notebook study supports.
3. Click **Learn more**, then **Review course content** / **Get started**.
4. Watch or read the short module. Do the practice activity if one is listed.
5. Return to the session card and choose **Earn your badge**.

The badge path is a short quiz. You can attempt the quiz after the materials; some educators report passing without the video, but the point of the series is the classroom habit, not the score.

If your students already use source-grounded notebooks, pair the pedagogical modules with the student-facing walkthrough in [Gemini Notebook study tools](/blog/gemini-notebook-study-tools/). That keeps staff training and student workflows on the same product names.

## Claim and store the badge

After a passing quiz, the Learning Center attaches the badge to the signed-in email. Open **See badge** on that session card to confirm.

Export or screenshot the badge page if your district needs a PDF for license hours. Google presents these as recognized digital badges for a resume, portfolio, or school site. They are not a state teaching license by themselves.

Google has separately described paths that stack some AI educator training toward continuing education or college credit through partner institutions. Confirm those offers on the current Learning Center page before you promise hours to a principal. Terms change by state and partner.

## What Digital Promise adds this fall

The 21 September 2026 Google.org announcement funds three Digital Promise workstreams:

- **Scale with states and districts.** Tailored training and teacher networks, not a one-off webinar.
- **Community college faculty.** Help instructors fold AI into daily teaching and job-prep programs.
- **Open research.** A free public guide, over two years, on what helps college faculty adopt AI well.

If you lead a school system, contact Digital Promise and your state education agency rather than waiting for a new button in Gemini. Classroom teachers should still start on the Learning Center today. The grant expands delivery. It does not lock the self-serve modules.

Google’s earlier 2026 commitment still stands: free comprehensive AI training aimed at all 6 million K-12 teachers and higher-education faculty in the United States.

## Practical study plan for one week

**Day 1.** Create the account and finish one Foundational module plus its quiz.

**Day 2.** Complete one pedagogical session that matches tomorrow’s class. Save one prompt or notebook template you will actually assign.

**Day 3.** Run that template with a real assignment. Note where students need a citation rule or an allowed-tool policy.

**Day 4.** Take one administrative session (planning, syllabus, or comms).

**Day 5.** Share the badge link with your instructional coach and pick next month’s session when Google drops new content.

Do not binge ten badges in an evening. The series is built for transfer into the next lesson, not a streak counter.

## Tips that keep the hours useful

- Stay on one Google account from sign-in through badge claim.
- Treat Gemini output as a draft. Google’s own framing keeps the educator in charge of instructional judgment.
- If you facilitate a staff meeting, use the official facilitator guide instead of restating the videos.
- New sessions have been rolling on a monthly cadence. Check the Learning Center on the first week of the month.
- India and other markets have localized pages. Use the Learning Center URL for your country so you do not enroll in the wrong language track.

## Conclusion

The Google AI Educator Series is the self-serve path: short ISTE-aligned modules, a quiz, a badge. The Digital Promise grant is the systems path: districts, community colleges, and a public higher-ed guide funded at $4 million in September 2026.

Open the Learning Center, finish one 15-minute K-12 session or one 45-minute higher-ed session this week, and store the badge on the same account you use for Classroom. Then bring one classroom-ready Gemini or notebook workflow to Monday’s lesson instead of collecting unused certificates.
