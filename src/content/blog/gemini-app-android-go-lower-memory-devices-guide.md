Google expanded the Gemini app to eligible Android (Go edition) devices on November 18, 2026. Hundreds of millions of people use lower-memory phones as their main connection to the internet, education, and business. Until this update, those devices only supported basic voice commands. Google re-architected the app so the core Gemini experience runs smoothly on hardware with less memory.

This guide shows how to check eligibility, install the app, start conversations, and use key features on Android Go phones with 2GB of RAM or more. It replaces the previous Assistant for Android Go experience.

## Check device requirements

The Gemini mobile app works on Android (Go edition) devices that have 2GB of RAM or more. You must sign in with a personal Google Account or a work or school account that has access to Gemini Apps. Availability matches the languages and countries where Gemini is offered.

On these devices, Gemini is available as part of the Google Search app. Some newer partner devices, including certain Transsion models, may include it preloaded. Family Link managers can restrict access for users under the applicable age in their country.

## Update Google Search or install the app

Open the Google Play Store on your Android Go device. Search for "Google" or "Google Search." If an Update button appears, tap it. If the button says Open, you already have the latest version. You can also search for the Gemini app and install it directly from the Play Store.

After the update, Gemini is ready. The re-architected design keeps the core experience efficient even when storage and RAM are limited.

## Start a conversation

Touch and hold the Home button to open Gemini. On supported devices, press and hold the Power button instead. These gestures launch the conversational interface.

Note that "Hey Google" wake-word activation and "Ask about this screen" are not supported on Android Go devices. Use the button hold methods above.

## What you can do with Gemini

The app supports a range of practical tasks:

- Ask Gemini to call or send text messages to contacts.
- Take a photo of notes, forms, or signs for translation or step-by-step help.
- Create images with the built-in tools (Nano Banana).
- Use Gemini Live for natural spoken conversations, including interview practice or brainstorming. It supports more than 45 languages.
- Check drive times or ask complex local questions, such as finding a restaurant open for lunch with an EV charger nearby.
- Set alarms or create calendar events.
- Upload documents, photos, or other files to add context to a chat.
- Play music by song, mood, or activity.

These features run through the optimized client. The underlying models process requests in the cloud, so the phone mainly handles input, rendering, and networking.

## Practical tips for lower-memory devices

Keep storage free. Clear cache in the Google Search app or Gemini app if responses slow down. Close unused background apps before long Gemini Live sessions. Image creation and file uploads use more temporary space, so finish those tasks when storage is available.

## Common limitations and workarounds

Hey Google and screen-context queries do not work. Hold the Home or Power button to start chats instead. On very low-storage devices, prefer short text queries over large file uploads. Gemini Live works for spoken practice, but background noise or weak connections can interrupt longer sessions.

If the app does not appear after updating Google Search, restart the phone and check Play Store again. Confirm your account has Gemini access in regions where the service is live.

## Conclusion

Eligible Android Go devices with 2GB of RAM or more can now run the Gemini app after a Google Search update or direct install. Core features such as calls, calendar events, camera help, image creation, and Gemini Live are available without the previous hardware barrier. Start with the Home or Power button hold, keep storage free, and use the tools for daily tasks or practice.

Official details are on the Google blog announcement and the Gemini Apps Help page for lower-memory devices.
