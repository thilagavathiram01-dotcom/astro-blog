Finding the right Android app often means scrolling long lists and reading scattered reviews. Ask Play now generates side-by-side comparisons directly in Google Play results.

Google announced the upgrade on October 13, 2026. Ask Play already answered natural-language questions about apps. It can now evaluate suggested apps and highlight key differences plus user sentiment drawn from store listings and reviews.

This guide shows how to use the comparison feature, what information it surfaces, and how to verify the results yourself. Availability is rolling out gradually.

## What Ask Play comparisons include

Ask Play sits inside the Google Play Store. You type a question or request in plain language. The system returns app recommendations. A new compare option appears with those results.

The comparison draws on official Play Store data. It can note differences in monetization (free, one-time purchase, subscription), connectivity needs (online only versus offline support), and summarized user sentiment from reviews. Google has not published a full list of comparison categories, so results vary by query.

## Prerequisites

Update the Google Play Store app on your Android device. The feature appears after the October 13 rollout reaches your account. It is available in select markets first; the United States is one confirmed region.

You do not need a special account type. A standard Google account signed into Play is enough.

## Step 1: Open Ask Play and describe what you need

Open the Google Play Store. Tap the search bar or look for the Ask Play entry point in the search interface.

Type a clear request. Examples that work well:

- "Best free photo editor that works offline"
- "Note-taking apps with handwriting support and no subscription"
- "Fitness trackers that sync with Google Fit and have free tiers"

Submit the query. Ask Play returns a set of recommended apps with short explanations.

## Step 2: Trigger the comparison

Look for a Compare or similar option on the results screen. Tap it when it appears.

Ask Play generates a side-by-side view. It pulls details from each app’s store listing and review summaries. Common points include pricing model, whether the app works without an internet connection, and a short sense of user feedback (positive themes or recurring complaints).

The comparison is AI-generated. Treat it as a starting summary, not a final verdict.

## Step 3: Review and verify the results

Open each app’s full Play Store page. Check the official description, the Data safety section, recent reviews, and the In-app purchases list.

Confirm any claims the comparison made. If Ask Play says an app works offline, look for that detail in the description or reviews. If it flags a subscription model, verify the exact pricing on the listing.

Read a mix of recent high-rated and low-rated reviews. AI summaries can miss edge cases or newer changes.

## Tips for better comparisons

Be specific in your first prompt. Include constraints such as “free,” “offline,” “no ads,” or “works with Wear OS.” Vague questions produce broader, less useful comparisons.

Ask follow-up questions inside Ask Play if the interface allows it. You can narrow results after the first set of suggestions.

Compare three or fewer apps at once when possible. Larger sets can make the summary harder to scan.

Cross-check privacy and permissions on the Data safety page before installing. Comparison summaries do not replace that step.

If the Compare option is missing, update Play Store, restart the app, or try again later. Rollouts are staggered.

## Limitations to know

The feature is rolling out. It may not appear on every device or in every country on day one.

Comparisons rely on existing Play Store content. Apps with thin descriptions or few reviews produce thinner comparisons.

AI can mis-summarize. Always open the individual listings for final decisions, especially for apps that handle sensitive data.

Play in Gemini, which brings Play recommendations into Gemini chats, is a separate upcoming feature and is not required for Ask Play comparisons.

## Conclusion

Ask Play’s comparison tool turns a natural-language search into a structured look at app differences. Start with a clear request, generate the comparison, then verify the details on each app’s official page.

The feature reduces the time spent switching between listings. It does not remove the need to check permissions, recent reviews, and your own requirements before you install.

Update Google Play, try a specific query, and use the comparison as a quick filter before you dig deeper.
