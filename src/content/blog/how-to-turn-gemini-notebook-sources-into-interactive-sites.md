Google’s Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) now turns your uploaded sources into interactive websites with a single chat prompt. Type “make me a site” plus a short description, and the tool builds visual explainers, dashboards, or project trackers grounded only in the materials you added.

The feature rolled out globally on the web starting 16 November 2026 for users aged 18 and older, including Google Workspace subscribers. Mobile app support is coming soon. You need edit access to the notebook to create or delete sites. Usage limits apply.

This is useful when notes, PDFs, or research feel dense. An interactive site lets you click through sections, filter data, or quiz yourself without leaving the grounded sources.

## What you can build

Google lists three practical types:

- Visual explainers for study, such as an interactive periodic table or a historical timeline.
- Interactive dashboards to explore data, for example quarterly sales performance with filters.
- Project management trackers that outline tasks and deadlines.

Once generated, open the site in a new tab to interact with it, or share it. On shared sites, each person sees only their own inputs. Your selections (checked boxes, notes, filters) save and reopen for you.

The site draws exclusively from the sources you selected in the notebook. Citations and source links remain available so you can verify claims.

## Step-by-step: create an interactive site

1. Go to [notebook.google.com](https://notebook.google.com) and sign in. Create a new notebook or open one you can edit.
2. Add sources in the left panel. Upload PDFs, paste links, add Google Drive files, or use the free textbooks catalog. Check only the sources you want the site to use.
3. In the Chat panel, type a prompt that starts with “make me a site” (or “make a site”) followed by a clear description. Specify sections, interactive elements, audience, and style.
4. Wait while Gemini Notebook generates the site. You can keep working in the notebook. Generation usually takes a few minutes depending on source volume.
5. Find the finished site in the Studio panel on the right. Select it, then choose “Open in a new tab” for full-screen interaction. Use the More menu to share or download.

Example prompt:  
“Make me a site to help me understand the periodic table. Include an interactive table, plain-language sections for each group, and a five-question quiz at the end.”

For a dashboard:  
“Make me a site with an interactive quarterly sales dashboard based only on the uploaded reports. Add filters by quarter and region.”

## Tips for stronger results

Name the sections you want and what each should contain. Ask for specific interactive features such as a slider, filters, flashcards, or a quiz with immediate feedback.

Describe the intended audience (students preparing for an exam, team reviewing sales data) and preferred visual style.

After generation, check key claims against the cited sources. Dashboard numbers should match the original sheet or PDF. You can request revisions in chat; new versions are created while originals stay preserved.

Combine this with other Gemini Notebook tools. If you added free textbooks, pair them with this feature for richer study sites. See our earlier guide on [how to add free textbooks to Gemini Notebook](/blog/add-free-textbooks-gemini-notebook/).

## Limits to know

The feature is web-only at launch (mobile coming soon). Users under 18 cannot access it. You must have edit permission on the notebook.

Sites stay inside Gemini Notebook’s sharing model. You can open them in a new tab and share the link, but they are not automatically published as independent public websites. Download options may be available via the Studio menu for further use.

Generation uses your sources only. If information is missing from the selected materials, the site will not invent it.

Usage limits apply; heavy source sets or frequent generation may hit them.

## Conclusion

Turning sources into interactive sites is one of the faster ways to make dense research or notes usable. Open a notebook, add the materials you trust, type “make me a site” with a clear description, and open the result from Studio. Verify important details against the original sources before you rely on them for study or work.

The capability is available now on the web at notebook.google.com for eligible accounts.
