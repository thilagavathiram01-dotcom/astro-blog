Google’s Pixel Tag went on sale November 11, 2026, as part of the November Pixel Drop. The slim tracker attaches to keys, bags, or luggage and works with the Find Hub network of more than one billion Android devices. It pairs with any phone running Android 9 or later, not only Pixel phones.

This guide walks through official setup steps, locating items nearby or on a map, sharing the tag, and practical tips. For the full list of features that arrived in the same update, see our [November 2026 Pixel Drop overview](/blog/november-2026-pixel-drop-gemini-intelligence-pixel-tag/).

## What the Pixel Tag offers

Pixel Tag is IP67-rated, uses a replaceable CR2032 coin-cell battery rated for over a year, and includes Bluetooth 6.0 with Channel Sounding plus Ultra-Wideband on supported phones. Price at launch was $29.99 for one or $99.99 for a four-pack in available markets. A physical button on the tag can ring your phone when pressed twice, and NFC lets you tap the tag against a phone to open its details.

The Find Hub network helps locate the tag even when it is far away. When you are close and your phone supports UWB, Find Hub shows directional guidance and a distance meter. Carbon-fiber or aluminum luggage and RFID-blocking wallets can block the signal, so place the tag in an exterior fabric pocket when traveling.

## Pair the tag with your phone

Official setup uses Fast Pair. Follow these steps from Google’s Pixel Phone Help:

1. Turn on Bluetooth and ensure the phone has an internet connection. Keep the phone unlocked on the home screen.
2. Place the Pixel Tag right next to the phone.
3. Completely remove the plastic tab from the battery contact point on the tag. This powers it on.
4. A pop-up appears. Tap **Connect**.
5. Read the Acceptable Use Policy and tap **Continue**.
6. Read the Terms and Conditions and tap **I accept**.
7. When asked about location permissions, tap **When using this app**.
8. The tag is added to Find Hub. You can choose a category such as Keys, Backpack, or Other. The default name is your name plus the category (for example, “Elisa’s keys”).

If the pop-up does not appear, wait five minutes (or 24 hours after a second dismissal). You can also toggle Bluetooth off and on or restart the phone to clear the wait. Alternatively, open Settings → Connected devices, or open Find Hub and tap Add Device, then follow the prompts.

## Locate the tag

Open the Find Hub app and select the Pixel Tag.

- **Nearby**: When the tag is within Bluetooth range, tap **Find nearby**. Compatible phones show on-screen navigation, an arrow (on UWB devices), and a distance reading. Play sound makes the tag chime so you can hear it in cushions or bags.
- **On a map**: When the tag is farther away, Find Hub shows its last known location reported by the network. Location data is end-to-end encrypted; only you and people you share the tag with can see it.

You can also enable left-behind alerts so the phone notifies you if you walk away from the tagged item.

## Share the tag with others

You can share one tag with up to 10 people so family members can also locate it. From Google’s help:

1. Open Find Hub and select the tag.
2. Tap **Share ownership** (or Share device) and send the invitation via any messaging app.
3. The other person opens the link on an Android device, installs Find Hub if needed, and taps Accept.
4. On your phone, open the tag’s Settings, select the person, and tap Confirm.

It can take several minutes before the shared person sees the location. Invitations expire after 24 hours. You can stop sharing at any time.

## Additional controls and maintenance

- Ring your phone: Double-press the button on the tag when it is nearby; the phone sounds. Press once to stop, or unlock the phone.
- Adjust volume, rename, or change category inside Find Hub settings for the tag.
- Battery replacement: Twist the circular battery door open, swap the CR2032, and close it. The app notifies you when the battery is low.
- Reset: Prefer the Find Hub method (select the tag → Settings → Remove from Find Hub). A hardware-button reset is also available if needed. After reset the tag returns to factory defaults and can be paired again.

## Tips and limitations

Precision finding with directional arrows requires a phone that supports Ultra-Wideband (typically Pro models of recent Pixels and certain Samsung Galaxy devices). Base models may still show distance via Channel Sounding but not an arrow. The tag works with Android 9 and later in all regions and languages according to Google’s announcement footnotes.

Test the setup once after pairing: walk to another room and confirm the location updates or that Find nearby works. Attach the tag securely so it does not detach during travel. For luggage, keep it in an outer pocket to avoid signal blocking.

Pixel Tag gives Android users a first-party option that integrates directly into Find Hub alongside phones, watches, and other devices. Setup takes only a few minutes once the battery tab is removed and the Fast Pair prompt appears.
