YouTube Music began rolling out clear labels on 14 October 2026 for tracks where generative AI played a significant role. The goal is transparency so listeners know when a song’s composition or vocals came mainly from a model rather than traditional recording.

These labels appear on the player page in the YouTube Music app and website. They will expand to the main YouTube app later. The system uses partner disclosures plus YouTube’s own detection tools, including signals similar to those used for video labels.

![Person wearing headphones while listening to music](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1487180144351-b8472da7d491?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What the label looks like and how to find it

Open the YouTube Music app or site and start playing any track. On the player page, look for a small label near the track controls. When AI played a significant role, the label appears directly there.

Tap or click the label. A simple menu opens with more details about how the track was made. The exact wording can vary, but it signals that generative AI altered or fully generated key parts of the song.

Not every track shows the label yet. The rollout is gradual across regions and accounts. Update the app and check a few AI-known tracks or new releases if nothing appears at first.

## What counts as Fully or Partly Gen AI

YouTube works with music partners on three declaration levels: Fully Gen AI, Partly Gen AI, and No Gen AI.

Fully Gen AI covers tracks where a generative model created the composition and vocals. An example is typing a prompt into an AI music tool, refining it, and downloading the result.

Partly Gen AI includes cases such as an AI-generated bassline or string section with the artist’s own live vocals and instruments layered on top. It also covers AI help with lyrics or melodies before recording, or a human performance paired with an AI-generated music video.

Ordinary pitch correction such as Auto-Tune and automated mastering do not count as generative AI under these rules. They stay in the No Gen AI category.

Partners can declare these values when delivering content through DDEX (ERN 4.3.2 or later), CSV templates, or YouTube Studio. If no declaration is provided, YouTube may still apply a label based on its own signals.

## How YouTube identifies the tracks

YouTube teams with music partners for voluntary disclosures and runs its own detection. The approach mirrors the labeling principles already used for AI content on the main YouTube platform.

Detection can draw on metadata, audio analysis, and watermarks. Google’s SynthID, for instance, has been used on certain AI-generated audio. The public SynthID Detector at synthid.com can check images, video, and audio for partner watermarks, though the YouTube Music labels operate separately for the streaming catalog.

If a partner leaves the field blank, YouTube treats it as unknown and may still designate the track as fully or partly Gen AI when other signals support that conclusion. Creators and distributors should declare accurately to avoid mismatched labels.

## Step-by-step for listeners

1. Open YouTube Music and play a track you suspect or want to check.
2. Look on the player page for the AI label near the controls.
3. Tap the label to read the additional details in the menu.
4. Repeat across a few tracks. Labels may appear only on some titles while the feature expands.
5. If you upload music, check with your distributor whether they support the Gen AI disclosure field so your releases are labeled correctly.

The labels do not ban AI music or change monetization rules by themselves. They give listeners clearer information at the point of playback.

## Tips and limits

Prefer the original high-quality file or official stream when checking related tools such as SynthID Detector, because compression can affect some watermark reads. For YouTube Music itself, the label is designed to survive normal playback.

Artists using AI tools should declare Fully or Partly accurately. Over-declaring or under-declaring can lead to incorrect audience expectations. YouTube may still override or supplement based on detection.

Listeners who want to avoid AI-heavy tracks can use the label as one filter while browsing. The feature is rolling out, so absence of a label does not guarantee a fully human recording.

These labels follow the same transparency approach YouTube has applied to video. They expand the information available without removing choice.

## Conclusion

YouTube Music’s new AI labels make it easier to see when generative tools shaped a track’s core elements. Check the player page, tap for details, and understand the Fully versus Partly distinction. The system relies on partner input and platform detection, with expansion to the main YouTube app still ahead.

Stay updated through the official YouTube blog for further rollout notes. Combined with tools like the SynthID Detector, these features give more context around synthetic media across Google properties.
