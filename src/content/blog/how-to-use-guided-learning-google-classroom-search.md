Google rolled out Guided Learning globally in Google Classroom on November 3, 2026, and is adding the same step-by-step coaching approach to Search for student accounts. The feature coaches students through problems using open-ended questions and class materials instead of handing over final answers. Teachers keep control over when and how students use it.

This update builds on the Guided Learning mode already available in the Gemini app. Research from Google shows measurable learning gains when students work through topics this way rather than receiving direct solutions.

![Students gathered around a laptop in a classroom](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1758270705172-07b53627dfcb?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What Guided Learning does

Guided Learning supports productive struggle. Students stay challenged but receive enough guidance to continue. In Gemini it acts as an interactive study tool that breaks problems into steps, asks questions, and includes diagrams or videos when helpful.

In Google Classroom the experience is teacher-led. Teachers assign a Guided Learning activity grounded in course materials. Students work through the content with one-to-one coaching. Teachers receive insights on progress and engagement. Focus Mode keeps conversations on the assigned objectives.

For Search, when systems detect certain homework-help queries on student accounts, results walk users through the topic or problem step by step instead of jumping to the answer. A pilot for selected U.S. schools starts later in November 2026.

Google is also updating school controls. Access to Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search will be off by default for K-12 student accounts until administrators explicitly enable each product.

## How teachers assign Guided Learning in Classroom

Open Google Classroom and go to the Classwork tab for your course. Create a new assignment or material. Select the option to include a Guided Learning activity.

Attach or reference the class materials you want the activity to use, such as a reading, slides, or problem set. Set the learning objectives so the session stays focused. Publish the assignment to the class or to specific students.

Students see the activity in their stream. When they start it, Guided Learning coaches them through the material. They cannot switch to regular Gemini mode inside the Classroom activity. You can review student interactions and progress from the assignment insights.

The feature is available globally in Google Classroom starting November 3, 2026, for Workspace for Education accounts where the tools are enabled.

## How students use Guided Learning

In the Gemini app or at gemini.google.com, click the Guided Learning tool in the compose bar before entering your prompt. Ask a question such as “Help me understand how to solve this quadratic equation” or upload class notes and request a walkthrough.

Respond to the questions it asks. Request a diagram or video if a visual would help. You can also generate practice quizzes or study guides from the session.

In Google Classroom, open the assigned Guided Learning activity. Work through the steps using the provided materials. Answer the coaching questions. The session stays anchored to the teacher’s objectives.

On Search, enter a homework-related query on a student account. If the system recognizes the request type, it presents a step-by-step breakdown instead of a direct answer or overview.

Always review the guidance against your textbook or teacher notes. AI outputs can contain errors, so confirm key facts before submitting work.

## Research behind the feature

Google shared results from a randomized controlled trial with more than 300 students at the University of Tokyo. Students studied a statistics lesson for half a day using standard online resources. Those who also used Guided Learning improved by 8.1 percentage points on assessments, compared with 5.3 points for the control group.

A four-week pilot with Ark across three UK secondary schools tested the Classroom version in multiple subjects. Teacher and student feedback shaped the global rollout, emphasizing control, grounding in class materials, and progress insights.

These findings support the design choice to prioritize process over immediate answers.

## Tips for teachers and students

Teachers should ground activities in specific lesson materials so responses stay relevant. Review the insights after sessions to spot common sticking points and adjust instruction.

Students can upload their own notes or screenshots of problems when using Guided Learning in Gemini. Pair it with free textbooks in Gemini Notebook for cited sources. See our guide on [adding free textbooks to Gemini Notebook](/blog/add-free-textbooks-gemini-notebook/) for that workflow.

Start with one short activity to learn the flow. Check whether your school has enabled the tools under the new administrator controls before expecting full access.

## Limits and availability

Guided Learning in Classroom is available now for eligible Workspace for Education accounts. The Search version is in pilot for select U.S. schools later in November 2026, with broader expansion planned after feedback.

K-12 student accounts require explicit administrator enablement for Gemini products and Search. Details on the rollout timeline will be shared with schools in the coming months.

The feature does not replace teacher instruction. It provides structured coaching within the boundaries teachers set.

## Conclusion

Guided Learning gives teachers a way to assign AI coaching that keeps students actively working through material. Students receive step-by-step support grounded in class content rather than finished answers. The global Classroom rollout and upcoming Search capabilities, combined with tighter school controls, reflect Google’s response to educator feedback on AI in education.

Try assigning a short Guided Learning activity this week or open the mode in Gemini with a current homework problem. Review the research and controls with your school administrator to confirm availability for your students.
