Google published a short, practical guide on October 29, 2026, showing how to turn unstructured notes about past jobs into strong resume bullets with Gemini. The method takes four steps and works in the free Gemini app. It produces bullets that start with action verbs, stay concise, and stay friendly to applicant tracking systems.

This post expands the official steps with concrete examples, extra prompts, and tips so you can apply the process to real roles today. It also notes the new in-chat file editing tools that launched the same day, which let you refine a full resume PDF without leaving Gemini.

## Why This Method Works

Most people dump a list of duties into a resume. Recruiters and ATS software respond better to results. Gemini is good at converting a messy brain dump into short, outcome-focused lines when you give it a clear role and constraints.

The official prompt forces three things that matter:

- Strong action verbs at the start of each bullet.
- An executive, clear tone.
- ATS-friendly wording (no graphics, tables, or unusual formatting).

You stay in control. You supply the facts; Gemini only reshapes them.

## The Official 4-Step Process

Google’s guide is short. Here it is, expanded with practical notes.

### Step 1: Ramble about your past roles

Open the Gemini app (web or mobile) and start a new chat. Talk freely about one job or a cluster of related roles. Include:

- Job title and company (or industry if you prefer not to name the employer).
- Main responsibilities.
- Concrete outcomes, numbers, tools, or scope (team size, budget, time saved, revenue, users, etc.).
- Any awards, promotions, or unusual challenges.

Do not edit yourself. The point of “ramble” is to capture details you might otherwise forget. Gemini will organize them later.

Example raw input you might type or speak:

“I was a product marketing manager at a mid-size SaaS company for three years. I owned launch plans for three major releases. One feature increased free-to-paid conversion by 18 percent in the first quarter. I ran weekly stand-ups with engineering and design, wrote the positioning, and trained the sales team. We cut the average sales cycle by about two weeks after the second launch. Also managed a $40k content budget and worked with an agency.”

### Step 2: Add the official prompt

After your notes, paste this prompt exactly as Google recommends (replace the blank with the target role):

“Act as a professional resume writer. Use my raw, unedited notes about my past work and turn it into four high-impact resume bullets for a _______ role. Start each bullet with a strong action verb. Keep the tone executive, clear, and ATS-friendly.”

You can add a short job description or key requirements after the prompt if you want the bullets tailored to a specific posting. Keep any sensitive personal data out of the chat.

### Step 3: Iterate

Read the four bullets Gemini returns. Then chat back and forth:

- “Make the second bullet more quantitative.”
- “Shorten all bullets to under 20 words.”
- “Emphasize leadership and cross-functional work.”
- “Change the tone to be slightly more technical for an engineering manager role.”
- “Replace ‘led’ with stronger verbs and keep the metrics.”

Ask for alternatives: “Give me two different versions of the third bullet.”

This back-and-forth is where the quality improves. Gemini will adjust based on your feedback without requiring you to rewrite everything yourself.

### Step 4: Copy and paste

When the bullets look right, copy them into your resume document. Review them one last time for accuracy. Do not let Gemini invent metrics or responsibilities you did not provide.

## Extra Prompts That Improve Results

Once the basic four bullets exist, these follow-ups are useful:

- “Rewrite these bullets so they match the keywords in this job description: [paste relevant section]. Keep every metric I supplied.”
- “Turn the same notes into a short professional summary of 2–3 sentences.”
- “Create a skills section that lists tools and competencies mentioned in the notes, ranked by relevance to a [target role].”
- “Flag any bullet that lacks a measurable result and suggest where I should add one.”

If you have notes for several jobs, process them one role at a time so the context stays focused.

## Using the New In-Chat File Editing Tools

On the same day, Google added the ability to generate, preview, and edit files directly inside Gemini. You can ask Gemini to create a PDF resume from your notes and bullets, then expand the preview to full screen and type small corrections yourself, or highlight a section and ask Gemini to refine it.

PDF editing works on both web and mobile. Other formats (Google Docs, Sheets, etc.) are currently web-only. After you are happy with the preview, export or download the file.

This pairs well with the bullet method: generate the bullets first, then ask Gemini to assemble them into a clean one-page PDF resume. For full details on the file tools, see our guide to [generating, previewing, and editing files in Gemini](/blog/generate-preview-edit-files-gemini-app/).

## Tips for Better Resume Bullets

- Supply real numbers whenever you have them. Gemini cannot invent accurate metrics.
- Start every bullet with a verb such as Led, Built, Increased, Reduced, Launched, or Designed.
- Keep bullets to one line when possible. Recruiters scan quickly.
- Tailor the target role in the prompt for each application instead of using a generic “professional” label.
- After Gemini finishes, read the bullets aloud. If any sentence sounds unnatural or claims something you did not do, rewrite or delete it.
- Avoid uploading a full existing resume that contains personal contact details if you are concerned about privacy. Stick to the raw notes method.

## Conclusion

Google’s four-step method turns a casual brain dump into polished, ATS-friendly resume bullets in a few minutes. You keep ownership of the facts while Gemini handles structure, verbs, and clarity. The same-day file editing update lets you go one step further and refine a complete PDF resume inside the chat.

Open Gemini, paste your notes for one role, add the official prompt, and iterate. Most people finish a usable set of bullets in under ten minutes.
