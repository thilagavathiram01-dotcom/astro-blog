Google Photos shipped a practical set of editing and organizing tools on September 24, 2026. Product manager Simon Li described five updates aimed at trip photos and everyday screenshots: Photos in Gemini Spark, Wardrobe, Moods, 15 new Remix templates, and an upgraded Markup tool with a redact pen.

You do not need every feature. Moods and Markup are rolling out on Android worldwide. Wardrobe is limited to the United States, India, and Brazil. Remix templates are limited to select countries and to users over 18. This guide walks through what each tool does, who can open it, and the exact taps Google documents.

If you already search your library with natural language, start with [Ask Photos in Google Photos](/blog/ask-photos-google-photos/) and come back here for the new Create and Markup tools.

## What landed on September 24

Google's Photos blog grouped the updates into five jobs:

1. Ask Photos in Gemini Spark to select, brighten, and share family portraits from one prompt. That path is for eligible Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the U.S.
2. Build a digital closet with Wardrobe, which catalogs clothing from photos and lets you style and try on looks.
3. Apply Moods, styles that adapt to the photo, including 35mm film and 2000s digicam looks.
4. Reimagine a shot with 15 new Remix templates, including red carpet moments, birthday cards, and photo booth shots.
5. Hide sensitive details with the Markup redact pen, plus thickness sliders and new text fonts.

Rollout is staged. If a button is missing, update Google Photos from the Play Store or App Store and check again later. Google sends an in-app notice when Wardrobe is ready for an account.

## Apply a Mood on Android

Moods live in the Create tab, not in the standard color editor. Google's Android help page describes them as unique filters that adapt to the photo, rather than a single overlay pasted on every frame. The September 24 post names 35mm film and 2000s digicam as examples. Google lists Moods for Google Photos on Android globally. It does not list Moods for iPhone or iPad.

Steps from Google Photos Help:

1. Open Google Photos on your Android phone.
2. Tap **Create** at the bottom.
3. Tap **Moods**.
4. Select the photo you want to change.
5. Browse the templates and tap one to preview it.
6. Tap **Save**.

Save stores a creation. Your original file stays in the library. If Moods is not under Create, the rollout has not reached that account yet. The same Create tab also lists collage, highlight video, photo remix, and video remix, so scroll if the row is crowded.

Use Moods when you want a consistent look across a set of trip photos without manual curves. Preview two or three styles on the same frame before you save. Adaptive filters can push skin tones and night skies harder than a fixed preset, so check faces before you share.

## Build a Wardrobe from photos you already have

Wardrobe is a separate collection. Google Photos scans photos of you from the last four years, identifies clothing, and builds an interactive closet. You can sort by type, mix items into outfits, and virtually try a look on.

Eligibility, from Google Photos Help:

- An eligible Google Account that meets the age rules for your country.
- Location in Brazil, India, or the United States.
- Face Groups turned on, and your own face selected.
- More than 1,000 photos of yourself, unless you have Google AI Pro or AI Ultra. Subscribers are exempt from that photo count.
- Android 10 or later on Android. The feature is also available on iOS for eligible users in those three countries.

Google says the feature rolls out to AI Pro and Ultra subscribers and to other selected users. Availability is not instant for every account in those countries.

### Create the closet

1. Open Google Photos.
2. Tap **Collections**, then **Wardrobe**, then **Build my wardrobe**.
3. Wait for the scan. Google notifies you when the wardrobe is ready.

Open **Collections > Wardrobe** to view items, sort by recently worn, or filter by type such as tops, bottoms, and skirts. To remove a bad detection, open **Items**, tap the piece, and delete it. Deleting a wardrobe item does not delete the source photo.

### Make an outfit and try it on

1. Open **Collections > Wardrobe**.
2. Tap **Create**.
3. Tap up to six items.
4. Tap **Save**, name the outfit or skip the name, then save again.
5. Open **Outfits**, select a saved look, or tap **Create** for a new mix.
6. Tap **Try it on**.

The try-on result is a video. You can save it, share it, or tap **Regenerate** for another result. Google notes that saved outfits and try-on outputs count toward Google Account storage. Wardrobe use is covered by the Google Terms of Service and the Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy.

Wardrobe only knows clothes that appear in photos of you. A jacket that never made it into the library will not show up. Full-body shots and clear lighting produce cleaner item cuts than crowded group photos.

## Remix a photo with the new templates

Photo remix sits in the same Create tab. Google's September 24 post adds 15 templates, and names three of them: red carpet moments, whimsical birthday cards, and photo booth shots. The rest of the set is not listed by name in that post.

Remix is available in Google Photos on Android and iOS in select countries, and only for users over 18. Photo remix is subject to the same generative AI use policy as other AI creations in Photos.

Open **Create**, choose **Photo remix**, pick a photo, and try a template. Treat the output as a new creation, not a replacement of the original. If a template invents extra people or changes a face more than you want, discard it and pick another template instead of editing the original file.

## Hide details with the redact pen

The Markup update is the most useful change for screenshots. Google is rolling it out in Google Photos on Android globally. The upgraded tool adds a redact pen to blur sensitive information, thickness sliders for sketches, and new fonts for text.

Typical path once the update is on your phone:

1. Open the photo or screenshot.
2. Tap **Edit**, then **Markup**. On some builds the path is **Edit > Tools > Markup**.
3. Choose the redact pen.
4. Set the thickness so the stroke covers the detail without eating nearby text.
5. Paint over account numbers, addresses, boarding passes, or faces you do not have permission to publish.
6. Confirm the edit and save a copy.

Save a copy so the unmarked original remains in your library. If Markup still shows only pen, highlighter, and text, the redact pen has not reached that device yet.

Redaction in an image editor is not the same as removing metadata. Sharing from Google Photos can still include location unless you turn that off in share settings. Blur the visible text, then check the share sheet before you post a screenshot of a bank app, ticket, or ID.

## Photos in Gemini Spark, if you qualify

The first item in Google's September 24 list is Photos inside Gemini Spark. Eligible Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the U.S. can ask Spark to select, brighten, and share portraits in one prompt. Google pointed to that workflow as a way to pull the best family shots from a weekend without opening each file.

Spark is separate from Moods and Markup. You need the Gemini app, Photos connected, and Spark enabled. Edits Spark saves into Google Photos count toward storage, and originals stay intact because Spark writes a new copy. If you are outside the U.S. or not on Pro or Ultra, skip this step and use Create and Markup directly.

## Practical tips before you share

- Update the app, then force-close it once. Staged features often appear only after a fresh launch.
- For Moods, save two versions and compare them on another screen. Phone brightness hides crushed shadows.
- For Wardrobe, confirm Face Groups and that the correct face is marked as you. A wrong face selection builds the wrong closet.
- Cap outfits at the pieces you will actually wear. The limit is six items.
- For screenshots, redact first, then export. Do not rely on a crop if the sensitive text is still in the frame.
- Watch storage. Try-on videos and remix outputs are new files.

## Who should use which tool

Use Moods when you want a film or digicam look on Android and you do not want to leave Google Photos. Use Wardrobe if you are in the U.S., India, or Brazil, Face Groups is on, and you want outfit planning from clothes you already own. Use Remix when you need a card or photo-booth style frame and you are over 18 in a supported country. Use the redact pen any time a screenshot would expose an account number, address, or ticket barcode.

None of these tools replace a manual backup. Confirm backup is on for the albums you care about before you delete local copies from the phone.
