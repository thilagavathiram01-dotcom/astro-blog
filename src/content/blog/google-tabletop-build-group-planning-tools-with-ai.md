Group chats often turn planning into a mess of links, files, and half-finished ideas. Google Labs launched Tabletop on November 11, 2026, to fix that. It gives groups one shared space where an AI agent builds custom interactive tools on request.

You add links, files, and notes. Then prompt @tabletop or pick a starter template. The result is something everyone can use at the same time, like a Google Doc but with live polls, maps, brackets, and splitters.

Tabletop is currently rolling out through a waitlist to people aged 18 and older in the United States. Sign up at labs.google/tabletop. Google says it will let more people in each week. No pricing or timeline for other countries has been announced.

## What Tabletop Does

Tabletop creates a shared hub for a specific group or activity. Members drop in existing material from chats or drives. The built-in agent then generates interactive elements based on what is already there.

Examples from Google’s announcement include:

- Interactive trip itineraries that pull in maps and options.
- Dinner polls that let everyone vote in real time.
- Expense splitters that track who paid what.
- Tournament brackets for rec leagues, with live updates.

Starter templates speed up the process if you do not want to start from a blank prompt. Once the space is ready, share an invite link in your existing group chat. Everyone joins and works on the tools simultaneously.

Roman Nurik, Design & Engineering Lead at Google Labs, wrote that coordinating a family trip, running a rec league, or hosting a potluck works better when the whole group can pitch in from one place.

Access starts with a short waitlist form.

## Building a Space Step by Step

After you receive access, creating a space follows a simple flow.

First, start a new space and give it a clear name tied to the activity—Road Trip 2027 or Pickleball League, for example.

Next, add the material the group already has. Paste links to maps, hotel options, or restaurant lists. Upload files such as spreadsheets or photos. Type short notes that capture decisions already made in chat.

Then type a request that begins with @tabletop. Be specific. “Build an interactive map of brunch spots along our route with voting” works better than a vague “help us plan.” You can also choose a starter template and customize it.

The agent generates the tool. Review it, then ask for changes if needed. “Make the seating chart drag-and-drop so names can move between tables” is a valid follow-up.

Finally, copy the invite link and paste it into the group chat. Members open the link, join, and start using or editing the tools.

Because everything lives in one space, later changes stay visible to the whole group without another round of screenshots.

## Practical Tips for Better Results

Keep the initial material focused. Too many unrelated links can produce tools that miss the main goal.

Use short, direct prompts. Mention the exact format you want—poll, map, bracket, spinner, or chart.

Invite only the people who will actually use the space. Larger groups can still work, but smaller ones often reach decisions faster.

Revisit the space when plans change. A new request to @tabletop can update an existing tool instead of forcing you to start over.

## Limitations to Know

Tabletop is an experiment. Features, availability, and the underlying model can change. Google has not published details on data retention, export options, or how long a space stays active after the last edit.

It is limited to the United States and adults for now. People outside that group will need to wait for a broader rollout that has not been scheduled.

The agent generates tools from what you provide. It does not search the open web on its own unless that capability is added later.

## Why Shared Spaces Beat Group Chats

Traditional group chats scatter decisions across dozens of messages. Someone has to scroll back to find the latest hotel link or the final vote. Tabletop keeps the working version in one place. The tools themselves update as people interact with them.

This reduces the back-and-forth that usually happens when one person has to summarize the chat for everyone else. Because the space updates live, a late joiner sees the current state instead of an outdated screenshot.

Google compares the experience to collaborating in a Google Doc. The difference is that the content is interactive rather than static text. A poll collects votes. A bracket advances automatically when results come in. An expense splitter recalculates balances.

For groups that already share Google Drive folders or Docs, Tabletop can sit alongside those tools. The invite link works in any chat app, so you do not need to move the entire conversation into a new platform.

These small differences add up when the same group plans multiple events. Each activity can have its own space. Older spaces remain available if you need to look back at what worked last time.
