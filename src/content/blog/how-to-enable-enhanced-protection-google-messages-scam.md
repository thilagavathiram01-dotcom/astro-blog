Google announced new Android scam protections on October 14, 2026, during Cybersecurity Awareness Month. The headline addition is Enhanced protection mode in Google Messages. This opt-in setting uses cloud Gemini models via Private AI Compute for conversations with unknown senders, plus on-device Gemini Nano for link safety.

Existing Scam Detection already flags more than 5 million suspected scam calls and texts each month. The new mode adds deeper analysis for slow-burn conversations where intent emerges over multiple messages. It remains off by default and rolls out over the coming weeks.

## What Enhanced Protection Unlocks

Enhanced protection activates three layers for chats with numbers not saved in your contacts:

- Scam Detection with Private AI Compute. Cloud Gemini models evaluate complex message context to judge scammer intent faster. Analysis runs in an encrypted dedicated space that Google cannot access for other purposes.
- Smarter link safety checks with Gemini Nano. On supported devices, the on-device model combines real-time web signals with existing URL protections to flag brand-new malicious sites before you tap.
- Redesigned link checkpoints. Warnings give extra time and clearer context before opening links from unrecognized numbers. This expands availability beyond previous country or Advanced Protection limits.

These build on on-device detection. Users stay in control because the mode is opt-in. Privacy notes emphasize encrypted processing in Private AI Compute.

## Device Compatibility and Rollout

Support varies by feature and hardware. Google lists specific requirements in the announcement footnotes.

Cloud Scam Detection with Private AI Compute works on Pixel 6 and newer, Galaxy S24 series and newer, Galaxy Z Flip6 and Z Fold6 and newer, and other Android AICore devices. Gemini Nano link checks require Pixel 10 and newer, Galaxy S26 series, Galaxy Z Flip8 and Z Fold8, or other select devices. Basic link warnings appear on Android 6 and later.

The mode rolls out in stages starting in the coming weeks. Availability depends on device, Google Messages version, and region. Expanded notification scam alerts reach more OEMs including Honor, Motorola, OnePlus, OPPO, and Xiaomi later this year. Screen-share privacy in Chrome and WebView arrives on Android 15+ in October. Call-forwarding warnings start in early December on Pixel and select devices.

Check your phone’s settings after a Google Messages update. If the option is missing, wait for the staged rollout.

## How to Enable Enhanced Protection Mode

Follow these steps once the setting appears on your device:

1. Open the Google Messages app.
2. Tap your profile picture or the three-dot menu in the top-right corner.
3. Select Settings.
4. Tap Protection & safety (wording may appear as Spam and scam protection).
5. Look for Enhanced protection mode and toggle it on.
6. Review the privacy notice explaining Private AI Compute. Confirm if you agree.

The toggle may sit under Spam protection. Accepting the notice is required. You can turn the mode off at any time from the same menu. Existing spam filters stay active regardless.

If you do not see the option, force-stop Google Messages, clear cache, or wait for the next app update. Pixel and recent Galaxy devices receive it first.

## Privacy and Data Handling

Google states that cloud analysis for unknown-sender conversations uses Private AI Compute. Data is encrypted and processed in a secure dedicated space inaccessible to Google or third parties. On-device Gemini Nano link checks stay local. Reporting a message as spam can send content to Google for analysis, as with existing features.

The mode does not scan conversations with saved contacts. Users control the toggle. For deeper device-level protections, review [Android Advanced Protection setup](/blog/android-advanced-protection-setup/) which locks additional settings including Scam Detection.

## Tips for Staying Protected

Enable the mode on supported devices and keep Google Messages updated. Verify links from unknown numbers even with checkpoints. Use the notification scam alerts when they reach your OEM. Avoid sharing one-time codes or personal details in unexpected chats. Pair with call-forwarding warnings once available.

Test with known safe contacts first if the rollout reaches you. Report false positives so Google can refine detection.
