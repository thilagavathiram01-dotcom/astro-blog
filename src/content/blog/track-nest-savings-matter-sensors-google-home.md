Nest thermostat owners in the US and Canada gain a clearer view of energy costs this December. Google Home will display estimated cumulative savings from your Nest devices. The 4th-generation Nest Learning Thermostat also gains support for third-party Matter temperature sensors.

These updates arrive free through the Google Home app. They help you monitor heating and cooling efficiency without buying extra Google-branded hardware for every room.

![Nest Learning Thermostat on a wall](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1545259741-2ea3ebf61fa3?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What the savings dashboard shows

Starting in December 2026, open the Google Home app and check the Climate space, the thermostat controller, or the Favorites widget. You will see an estimated cumulative dollar amount saved by your Nest thermostat.

Google bases the figure on schedule changes and Eco mode use compared with a baseline. The number is an estimate, not a utility-bill guarantee. It appears for Nest thermostat owners in the United States and Canada only.

Homes with more than one Nest unit also receive Eco Mode Everywhere. You can switch all units to Eco mode at once from the app.

## Requirements for the 4th-gen Nest Learning Thermostat

The third-party sensor support applies only to the 4th-generation Nest Learning Thermostat. The standard Nest Thermostat does not gain this feature in the initial rollout.

You need the latest Google Home app on your phone. The thermostat must already be set up in Google Home and connected to the internet. A Matter-compatible hub is not required for the sensors themselves if they join through the Nest.

Compatible sensors include Matter temperature devices from IKEA, Eve, and Aqara. Examples mentioned in reports include IKEA TIMMERFLOTTE, Aqara W100, and Eve Weather. Check the Google Home app for the full list once the update arrives.

## How to view estimated savings

1. Update the Google Home app from the Play Store or App Store.
2. Open Google Home and select your home.
3. Tap the Climate card or your Nest thermostat device.
4. Look for the cumulative savings figure in the dashboard or Favorites widget.
5. Review the breakdown of schedule and Eco contributions if shown.

The feature rolls out gradually in December. If you do not see it immediately, check for app updates and try again later in the month.

## How to pair third-party Matter sensors

Once the December update reaches your thermostat:

1. Put the Matter sensor into pairing mode according to its instructions (usually a button hold).
2. In Google Home, tap + then Set up device.
3. Choose Matter device and follow the on-screen prompts to scan the QR code or enter the setup code.
4. Assign the sensor to a room.
5. Open your Nest Learning Thermostat settings and select temperature sensors.
6. Choose the new sensor or set an average across multiple sensors.
7. Optionally set time-of-day priorities so the thermostat follows the living room sensor during the day and the bedroom sensor at night.

The thermostat can average readings or prioritize one sensor. This balances hot and cold spots without Google’s own $40 sensors in every room.

## Tips for better results

Place sensors away from direct sunlight, vents, and exterior walls for accurate readings. Test one sensor first before adding several. Use the Nest leaf icon on the thermostat display to confirm energy-saving temperatures in real time.

Compare the new cumulative savings figure with your previous monthly utility bills. Adjust Eco temperatures if the estimate seems high or low after a few weeks.

## Limitations and rollout notes

Savings estimates start only in the US and Canada. Sensor support is limited to the 4th-gen Nest Learning Thermostat. Audio alerts for barking or glass break remain on the paid Google Home Premium Advanced plan.

Google announced the changes on November 17, 2026. Check the Nest Community or Google Home release notes for the exact December start date in your region.
