Google expanded the Gemini app to eligible Android (Go edition) devices on 18 November 2026. Hundreds of millions of people use lower-memory phones as their main link to the internet, school, and work. Those devices previously offered only basic voice commands. Google re-architected the app so the core Gemini experience runs on hardware with less memory.

This guide shows how to check eligibility, start a conversation, and use the features that work on Android Go. All steps come from Google’s official blog post and Gemini Apps Help Center.

## Check Device Requirements

The Gemini mobile app is available on Android (Go edition) devices with 2GB of RAM or more. You must sign in with a personal Google Account or a work or school account that has Gemini Apps access. The feature works in every language and country where Gemini is available.

On these devices, Gemini is available as part of the Google Search app. Update the Google Search app through the Play Store if needed. You can also download the standalone Gemini app from Google Play. Some newer devices from partners such as Transsion ship with it preloaded.

Family Link managers can turn off Gemini Apps access for users under 13 (or the applicable age in their country).

## Start a Conversation

On Android Go devices you start Gemini by touching and holding the Home button. On supported devices you can also press and hold the Power button.

“Hey Google” wake word and “Ask about this screen” are not supported on Android (Go edition) devices. Use the button methods instead.

Once the conversation starts you can type or speak. Gemini Live supports spoken conversations in more than 45 languages so you can brainstorm or practice for an interview out loud.

## What You Can Ask Gemini

Google lists these capabilities that work on lower-memory Android Go devices:

- Stay connected: ask Gemini to call or send text messages to contacts.
- Get help with images: take a photo to translate text or receive step-by-step help. You can also ask Gemini to create images with Nano Banana.
- Brainstorm: use Gemini Live for flowing conversations.
- Find local info: check drive time to work or ask for a ramen restaurant open for lunch on Tuesday that has an EV charger nearby.
- Plan your day: set an alarm or create a calendar event.
- Upload files: add docs, photos, and other files for more context.
- Play media: ask for music by mood or activity, for example “Play pop party jams!” or “Play quiet acoustic songs for a dinner party.”

These features replace the older Assistant Go experience with a more conversational assistant.

## Practical Tips for Smooth Use

Keep storage free. Lower-memory phones run better when you clear unused apps and files. Update Google Search or the Gemini app regularly through the Play Store.

Start simple. Try a calendar event or a music request first. Then move to camera translation or image creation once you are comfortable.

Confirm actions. Gemini can call contacts or create events, so review the confirmation screen before you proceed.

For more advanced multi-step work on standard devices, see our guide on [how to use free Gemini Tasks](/blog/how-to-use-free-gemini-tasks-for-daily-assistance/).

## Limitations to Know

“Hey Google” and screen context (“Ask about this screen”) are unavailable. Some advanced Gemini features that require higher memory or specific hardware may also be limited. Work and school accounts need the correct license for Gemini Apps access.

If the app does not appear after updating, check that your device meets the 2GB RAM threshold and that you are signed in with an eligible account. Availability can roll out gradually.

## Conclusion

The Gemini app now reaches eligible Android Go phones with 2GB of RAM or more. Hold the Home button to start, use Live for voice conversations, point the camera at notes for translation or help, and create images with Nano Banana. Google’s re-architecture puts the core assistant in the hands of more people without requiring a hardware upgrade.

Download the app from the Play Store or update Google Search, then try a simple request. Official details remain on the Google Blog post from 18 November 2026 and the Gemini Apps Help Center page for lower-memory devices.
