Google’s first branded tracker, Pixel Tag, is a stainless-steel finder that talks to Find Hub. Pairing is Fast Pair: pull the battery tab, hold the tag near an Android 9 or newer phone, and finish the on-screen steps.

This guide stays with what Google published on 12 August 2026 and with current Find Hub Help. It covers setup, nearby ringing, precision finding, sharing with up to 10 people, Left Behind alerts, and the network privacy switches.

## What Pixel Tag is

Pixel Tag is a small item tracker, not a phone accessory like Pixel Buds. Google’s product post says it is made of stainless steel, is IP67 water- and dust-resistant, and uses a replaceable CR2032 cell that lasts more than a year.

It sits on Google’s Find Hub network. Nearby, you can play a sound from the speaker. Out of Bluetooth range, other Android devices on the network can report an encrypted location.

Google also lists Ultra-Wideband with Bluetooth Channel Sounding for precision finding: on-screen distance and direction when the tag is close but hidden.

## What you need

- Pixel Tag with the battery tab still in place until you are ready to pair.
- An Android phone or tablet on **Android 9 or newer** signed into a personal Google Account.
- The **Find Hub** app (Play Store) and location services turned on.
- A screen lock (PIN, pattern, or password). Find Hub Help says the network requires one.

Precision finding needs Ultra-Wideband on both sides. Google’s Pixel Tablet Help page lists UWB on Pixel 8 series and newer **Pro** models, plus selected Galaxy S21+ / Ultra and some Motorola Edge and Razr phones, on Android 13 and up. Base Pixel 11 still pairs and rings the tag. Directional arrows may be limited if UWB is off or unsupported.

## Pair Pixel Tag with Fast Pair

Google’s setup copy is short. Follow it in this order.

1. Turn on Bluetooth and Location on the phone.
2. Pull the battery tab on the tag.
3. Hold the tag next to the phone. A Fast Pair card should appear.
4. Tap the prompt and stay signed into the Google Account you want as owner.
5. Open **Find Hub**, select the new accessory, and name it. Pick a category (keys, wallet, bag) or type a custom label.

If Fast Pair does not appear, open Find Hub and use the add-accessory path for tracker tags. Keep the tag within a few centimeters of the phone until pairing finishes.

Name the tag after the object, not after the person. “Front-door keys” is easier to scan in a list than “Dad.”

## Find the tag when it is nearby

Open Find Hub and tap the Pixel Tag.

- **Play sound** uses the built-in speaker when you are in Bluetooth range. Walk the room and listen. Mute notifications first so you do not confuse a chat ping with the tag.
- **Precision finding** (UWB + Channel Sounding, where supported) shows distance and direction. Point the phone and follow the on-screen cues under a sofa or a laundry pile.
- **Map view** shows the last reported spot when the tag is not next to you.

Google’s product page is explicit: if the tag is out of range, Find Hub uses the network of more than one billion Android devices. Those reports are encrypted. The helper phone does not see your name or the item label.

## Turn on Left Behind alerts

Left Behind alerts warn you when the tag and the phone separate. Enable them in the tag’s Find Hub settings after pairing.

Use them on items you carry every day: house keys, a work badge, a travel wallet. Skip them on a tag that stays in a drawer on purpose, or you will get noise when you leave the house.

Google lists Left Behind as an optional control on the Pixel Tag product page. Availability can still depend on the Find Hub app version on your phone. If the toggle is missing, update Find Hub from Play Store and reopen the accessory page.

## Share the tag with family

You can share a Pixel Tag with up to **10** people. Pixel Phone Help for Find Hub accessories is the source for the three-step flow.

**Owner sends the invite**

1. Open Find Hub and select the tag.
2. Tap **Share device**.
3. Send the link through Messages, email, or Quick Share.

The recipient has **24 hours** to accept.

**Recipient accepts**

1. Open the link on an Android device.
2. Install Find Hub if it is missing.
3. Tap **Accept**. Help notes a 4-digit PIN under the shared device for extra confirmation.

**Owner confirms**

1. Open Find Hub again and select the tag.
2. Open **Settings**, pick the person, and confirm sharing.

Help also warns that it can take several minutes after sharing before the new person sees a location. That delay is intentional for the shared owner’s privacy.

Do not share a tag that is clipped to something you would not want a roommate to ping at 2 a.m. Sharing grants locate and ring, not ownership transfer. Keep the original Google Account as the owner if you bought the pack.

## Privacy switches worth checking

Find Hub is more than a map. Pixel Help describes three network modes under Find Hub settings:

- **Off** — your phone does not help others find offline items and does not store encrypted recent locations with Google for offline finding. You can still find online devices and Fast Pair accessories that are connected.
- **Without network** — you store encrypted recent locations for your own phone and connected accessories, but you do not contribute to the crowdsourced network.
- The default network-on path — your device can help locate other people’s offline tags and phones, and you get the same help in return.

To reach the control on current Pixel Settings: **Settings → Google → All services → Personal & device safety → Find Hub**. Confirm **Allow device to be located** is on if you want remote maps at all.

If you later want the phone itself hidden from Find Hub, turn that allow switch off. Tags already paired stay in the app until you remove them.

For a separate privacy layer on apps rather than objects, use [Android 17 App Lock on Pixel](/blog/android-17-app-lock-pixel/) so a borrowed unlocked phone cannot open banking or photos.

## Limits Google actually stated

- Pairing target is Android 9+, not Pixel-only. Any current Android phone can own the tag.
- UWB precision finding is device-specific. Confirm UWB is enabled in Settings on a supported Pro phone.
- Sharing is capped at 10 people. Invites expire in 24 hours.
- Location after a new share can lag by several minutes.
- The Find Hub network needs a screen lock on the Android device.
- Pixel Tag ships as hardware. If your pack is still on preorder or a later retail window, pair it the day it arrives rather than treating this article as a software toggle.

Independent launch coverage put retail around $29.99 for one and $99.99 for four, with a later hardware window than Pixel 11 phones. Confirm live Store pricing before you buy. This article does not treat those figures as Google’s binding spec.

## A ten-minute first-day checklist

1. Update Find Hub and GMS from Play Store.
2. Pull the tab and Fast Pair the first tag.
3. Name it after the object and clip it on.
4. Walk into the next room and use **Play sound**.
5. If you have a Pixel 8 Pro or newer Pro phone, try precision finding once so you know the UI.
6. Enable Left Behind on keys only.
7. Share the house-key tag with one adult who already uses Android.
8. Open Find Hub settings and pick a network mode you accept.

## Conclusion

Pixel Tag is a Fast Pair accessory on the same Find Hub stack as phones, tablets, and earbuds. Setup is the battery tab plus a nearby Android 9+ device. Daily use is ring, map, optional UWB arrows, Left Behind, and a 10-person share list.

Treat the network switch as a real choice, not a footnote. Keep the owner account on a phone you control. Name tags after objects. Confirm UWB only on phones Google lists. Then the tag is a speaker and a map pin, not another account to babysit.
