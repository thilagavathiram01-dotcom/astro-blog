Android is adding automatic protections that hide login and payment fields during screen sharing and block apps from quietly turning on call forwarding. These features stop common social engineering tricks without extra apps.

Google announced the updates on October 14, 2026, as part of broader scam defenses. Screen sharing privacy is rolling out this month to Android 15 and later. Call forwarding protection arrives in early December on Pixel and select devices.

![Person sharing a phone screen safely](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1512941937669-90a1b58e7e9c?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What the new screen sharing privacy does

When you share your screen, Android already hides sensitive information in many native apps. The new protection extends the same behavior to Chrome and Android WebView. Login fields and payment screens blank out automatically during a live share session.

This stops a scammer who is “helping” you troubleshoot from seeing your password, card number, or security code. The feature works without you flipping any switch. It activates only while screen sharing is active.

The rollout covers devices running Android 15 or newer. Check your Android version under Settings > About phone if you are unsure.

## How call forwarding protection works

Scammers sometimes try to redirect your incoming calls so they can intercept one-time passcodes. Android now stops apps from enabling call forwarding in the background. If someone tricks you into dialing a forwarding code yourself, the system warns you before the change takes effect.

This protection starts in early December on Pixel phones and other select devices. Until then, avoid dialing any * or # codes that look unfamiliar.

## Step-by-step: stay protected today

1. Update your phone. Go to Settings > System > System update and install any available patches. Android 15 or later is required for the screen sharing change.
2. Confirm screen sharing is only used with people you trust. When a share starts, sensitive fields in Chrome should blank automatically once the update reaches your device.
3. Review call settings. Open the Phone app, tap the three-dot menu, then Settings > Calls > Call forwarding. Turn off any forwarding you did not set yourself.
4. Enable existing scam tools. In Google Messages, look for Scam Detection and turn it on if available. Pair it with the new enhanced protection mode when it rolls out in the coming weeks.
5. Use a strong lock screen and enable Find Hub. These basics make account takeover harder even if a code is intercepted.

## Tips for safer screen shares

- Start shares only from the system UI, not from unknown apps.
- End the session immediately after the help is finished.
- Watch for blanked fields. If a password box stays visible, the update may not have reached you yet.
- Avoid sharing while logged into banking or email sites.
