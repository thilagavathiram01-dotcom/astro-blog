Google has started rolling out refreshed icons for three of its most-used Android apps: Google Messages, Phone by Google, and Google Contacts. The update brings subtle gradients, sharper edges, and updated curvature that match the rest of the company’s recent design language.

The changes are purely visual. App functionality stays the same. You will still send texts, make calls, and manage contacts exactly as before. The new look simply makes these everyday apps feel more consistent with other Google services on your home screen.

![Close-up of a smartphone screen showing various app icons](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1738640679960-58d445857945?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What the New Icons Look Like

Google announced the redesign on November 2, 2026. The new icons use a blue color family with smooth gradients. Phone by Google keeps its familiar handset shape. Google Messages switches from flat interlocking pills to a rounded speech bubble with a second bubble behind it. Google Contacts shows a simplified person silhouette with a lighter lower half.

The company says the icons are sharper and clearer on home screens. Edges are more defined, and the curvature is updated so the icons sit better alongside other refreshed Google apps and the gradient Google logo.

These updates follow earlier icon refreshes across Workspace apps and select Pixel products. The goal is a more cohesive look across the Google ecosystem without forcing users to relearn where anything lives.

## How the Rollout Works

The new icons began rolling out on November 2, 2026. Google says they will appear on home screens over the coming weeks. Availability depends on when your device receives the app update through the Play Store.

Some users on the Google Messages beta (version 20261030) saw the new Messages icon earlier. The official rollout covers all three apps for stable users. The icons should also appear on the web versions at messages.google.com and contacts.google.com once the update reaches those surfaces.

No special action is required beyond keeping the apps updated. The change is automatic once the update lands.

## Step-by-Step: Get the New Icons

Follow these steps to check for and install the update on your Android phone.

1. Open the Google Play Store.
2. Tap your profile icon in the top right.
3. Select Manage apps & device.
4. Tap Updates available.
5. Look for Google Messages, Phone by Google, and Google Contacts.
6. Tap Update next to each one, or use Update all if available.
7. Return to your home screen and check the icons. You may need to wait a few minutes or restart the launcher if the old icons still appear.

If the apps do not show updates yet, the rollout has not reached your device. Check again in a day or two. Google is staggering the release, so not every phone receives the icons at the same time.

## Tips If the New Icons Do Not Appear

- Force-stop the app and reopen it. Long-press the icon, tap App info, then Force stop.
- Clear the launcher cache. Go to Settings > Apps > your launcher (Pixel Launcher, One UI Home, etc.) > Storage > Clear cache. Do not clear data unless necessary.
- Restart your phone. This refreshes the icon cache on many devices.
- Confirm you are on a recent app version. In Play Store, open the app page and check the version under About this app.

These steps resolve most cases where an updated app still shows the old icon. The new design does not require any settings change inside the apps themselves.

## What Stays the Same

Google has been clear that this is a visual-only update. Messaging features, RCS, spam protection, call screening, and contact management tools remain unchanged. If you rely on enhanced scam protection in Google Messages or other security features, those continue to work exactly as before. The redesign does not affect Android system icons, third-party launchers, or custom icon packs. If you use a third-party launcher that overrides app icons, you may need to reapply your theme after the update.

## Why Google Made the Change

The update aligns these core communication apps with Google’s broader design direction. Over the past year the company has introduced gradient treatments across many products, starting with the Google logo and expanding to Workspace and consumer apps. Sharper edges and clearer shapes improve visibility on busy home screens and different wallpapers.

User feedback influenced the curvature and spacing adjustments. The result is icons that feel familiar yet refreshed. They remain instantly recognizable while fitting the current Google aesthetic.

## Conclusion

The new gradient icons for Google Messages, Phone by Google, and Google Contacts are a small but visible polish. They make everyday tools look more consistent without changing how you use them. Check the Play Store for updates and apply the simple cache-refresh steps if the old icons linger. Within the coming weeks most Android users should see the refreshed look on their home screens.
