Short Video Overviews in Gemini Notebook turn the documents, notes, and links in a single notebook into roughly 60-second vertical videos. Google added the Short format so you can grasp one core idea quickly without watching a longer explainer.

These videos combine narration with simple animations drawn from your sources. They work well for review before a meeting, sharing a concept with a classmate, or creating a quick visual summary of a lecture recording. The feature sits in the Studio panel alongside quizzes, flashcards, and longer Video Overviews.

If you already use Gemini Notebook for research, this guide shows the exact steps to generate a Short Video Overview and how to steer it so the result stays useful. For broader study features including voice chat and quizzes, see our earlier walkthrough of [Gemini Notebook study tools](/blog/gemini-notebook-study-tools-2026/).

## What Short Video Overviews do

A Short Video Overview is one of three Video Overview formats available in Gemini Notebook. The others are Explainer, which produces a longer structured video, and Cinematic, which creates more immersive animated storytelling for users age 18 and older.

The Short format targets about 60 seconds and a vertical layout. It focuses on a single key concept rather than covering every detail in your sources. Google’s help documentation lists Short as a way to quickly grasp core ideas from the materials you add.

Because the video is generated only from the sources in that notebook, every claim stays tied to what you uploaded. Citations and the original documents remain available if you need to verify a point after watching.

## Prepare a focused notebook first

The quality of the video depends on the sources you select. Start with a narrow set that covers one topic well.

1. Open Gemini Notebook at notebook.google.com and sign in.
2. Create a new notebook and give it a specific name, such as “Photosynthesis key steps” instead of “Biology notes.”
3. Add three to five strong sources. These can be PDFs, Google Docs, lecture slides, web pages, or YouTube links. Avoid mixing unrelated topics.
4. Wait for the notebook to finish processing the sources. A summary appears in the chat panel when it is ready.

Selecting only the sources you want the video to use keeps the output tighter. You can uncheck sources in the Sources panel before generating if the notebook contains more material than needed.

## Generate a Short Video Overview

Once the notebook is ready, open the Studio panel on the right side of the screen.

1. Select Video Overview.
2. Choose the Short format from the options. If Short does not appear, confirm your account has access. The format rolled out first to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers and has expanded since.
3. Set the language. Short Video Overviews support a long list of languages, including English, Spanish, French, Hindi, Japanese, and many others. Check the full list in Google’s help center if you need a specific one.
4. Optionally add a steering prompt. Tell the system what to focus on. For example: “Explain the light-dependent reactions in simple terms for a high-school student and highlight the role of chlorophyll.”
5. Select Generate. The video builds in the background. You can continue working in the notebook or switch to another tab while it processes.

Generation can take several minutes. Google notes that some Video Overviews take more than 30 minutes in rare cases. Return to the Studio panel later to play the finished video.

## Customize and review the result

After generation finishes, play the video inside the Studio panel. You can change playback speed, enter full screen, or jump to sections with the slider.

If the video misses an important point, generate a new one with a tighter steering prompt rather than editing the finished file. Gemini Notebook does not offer frame-by-frame editing inside the tool. You can download the video and refine it in another editor if needed.

Share options include:
- Copy a link to the video (the notebook must be shared or set to anyone with the link).
- Share the entire notebook so collaborators see the video in Studio.
- Download the file and send it directly.

Public notebook sharing is available for consumer accounts. Workspace Enterprise and Education accounts may have different sharing rules.

## Tips for better Short Video Overviews

Keep the source set small and high quality. Three well-chosen documents usually outperform ten loosely related ones. The model has limited time to cover material in 60 seconds, so extra sources dilute the focus.

Write a specific steering prompt. Broad requests such as “summarize everything” produce generic results. Name the concept, the audience, and one or two points that must appear.

Use Short videos as an entry point, not a replacement for the original sources. After watching, open the cited passages in the chat panel and check the details yourself. AI-generated narration can simplify or occasionally misstate a point.

Pair the video with other Studio tools. Generate flashcards or a short quiz on the same concept after the video finishes. This turns passive viewing into active recall.

Check language and age requirements before relying on the feature for a class or team. Cinematic format has stricter age limits, and some visual styles are limited to users 18 and older.

## Limits and practical notes

Daily or compute limits apply depending on your plan. Gemini Notebook uses flexible usage limits that refresh every five hours rather than a strict daily cap for many features. Video generation consumes more of the budget than a short chat query.

The mobile app may have limitations for creating or fully managing Video Overviews. Google’s help page notes that the mobile app may not support every feature available on the web.

Videos are AI-generated and can contain inaccuracies or audio glitches. Always cross-check important facts against the original sources before sharing or studying from them.

If a book from Google Play Books is one of your sources, download restrictions may apply to the generated video depending on the publisher.

## Conclusion

Short Video Overviews give Gemini Notebook users a fast way to turn source material into a shareable 60-second visual summary. The process takes a few minutes once you have a focused notebook: select Video Overview, choose Short, add a steering prompt, and generate.

Use the feature for quick reviews, classroom sharing, or personal study reinforcement. Combine it with quizzes and flashcards from the same notebook for a complete active-learning loop. Start with one clear topic and a precise prompt, then verify the result against your sources.
