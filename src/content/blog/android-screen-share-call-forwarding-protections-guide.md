Scammers target more than just texts. They trick users into sharing screens during calls so they can see passwords and banking details, or they quietly forward calls to intercept one-time codes. Google announced protections against both tactics as part of its October 14, 2026 Android scam defenses.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="126" />

Automatic screen-share privacy now blanks sensitive fields in Chrome and WebView. Call-forwarding protection blocks background changes and warns before MMI codes take effect. These features arrive on different schedules and complement the Enhanced protection mode in Google Messages.

## Screen-Share Privacy for Chrome and WebView

Android already hides sensitive information such as login fields and payment screens in native apps during screen sharing. The new update extends the same automatic protection to Chrome and Android WebView.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="126" />

When you start a screen-share session, fields that collect passwords, credit card numbers, or similar data are blanked out so the remote viewer cannot see them. This reduces the risk if a scammer asks you to share your screen to "help" with a fake problem.

The feature rolls out this month to devices running Android 15 and later. No manual toggle is required for the core behavior; it activates during live screen-share sessions.

If you share your screen for legitimate help from family or coworkers, the protection still applies to sensitive inputs. You can temporarily show content if needed by switching apps or using other methods, but the automatic blanking remains the default for protected fields.

## Call-Forwarding Protection

Scammers often try to redirect incoming calls so they can receive one-time passcodes sent by SMS or apps. Android now blocks apps from quietly enabling call forwarding in the background.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="126" />

If a scammer instructs you to type an MMI forwarding code (such as *21* or similar) on the dial pad, Android warns you before the change takes effect. This gives you a chance to stop the action.

The protection starts in early December on Pixel phones and other select devices. Check system updates and Phone app settings for related options once available.

Combined with existing Scam Detection in the Phone app, this makes it harder for attackers to intercept verification codes without your knowledge.

## How These Features Work With Other Protections

These safeguards pair with the Expanded Scam Detection in Google Messages and chat notifications. For full coverage:

- Enable Enhanced protection mode in Messages once it appears (coming weeks).
- Keep the Phone app's Scam Detection active for calls.
- Use Android Advanced Protection if you face higher risk. See our [Advanced Protection setup guide](/blog/android-advanced-protection-setup/).
- Avoid sharing your screen with unknown callers and never enter codes dictated by someone who called you.

Google reports that existing AI-powered tools already flag more than 5 million suspected scam calls and texts monthly. Independent testing commissioned by Google found Android offered strong built-in protections compared with the evaluated iOS devices in several categories.

## Practical Steps to Stay Protected

1. Update your Android system and Google apps from the Play Store.
2. Open the Phone app, go to settings, and confirm Scam Detection or similar options are enabled.
3. For screen sharing, use the built-in system tools rather than third-party apps when possible, so the privacy protections apply.
4. If you receive a call asking you to enable forwarding or share your screen, hang up and contact the organization through its official app or website.
5. Monitor for the early December rollout of call-forwarding protection on supported devices.

These tools reduce the chance of accidental data exposure, but they do not replace caution. Scammers adapt, so verifying requests independently remains essential.

## Conclusion

Android's new screen-share privacy and call-forwarding safeguards close specific gaps that scammers exploit during calls and remote sessions. Screen-share blanking for Chrome and WebView arrives this month on Android 15+, while call-forwarding protection reaches Pixel and select devices in early December.

Enable available scam detection options, keep software updated, and treat unsolicited requests for screen sharing or code entry with skepticism. These built-in tools make everyday phone use safer without requiring extra apps.
