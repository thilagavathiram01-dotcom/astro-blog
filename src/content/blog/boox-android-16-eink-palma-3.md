On **15 September 2026**, BOOX announced three new E Ink devices that boot **Android 16**: the 10.3-inch **Note Air6 C**, the 8.52-inch **Note Mini C**, and the pocket-sized **Palma 3**. The official press release lists Google Play on all three, Bluetooth 6.0, an upgraded octa-core processor, and BOOX Super Refresh (BSR) for faster page turns on paper-like screens.

This is a buyer-and-setup guide, not a review of units we have not held. Specs and prices below come from BOOX’s announcement and from reputable hardware coverage that matches those numbers. Availability still differs: Note Air6 C is listed as available now; Note Mini C and Palma 3 are listed as “available soon.”

## What shipped, and what it costs

BOOX’s 15 September announcement (also carried on PR Newswire) publishes this list:

- **Note Air6 C** — 10.3-inch color E Ink notebook — **$579.99**, available now
- **Note Mini C** — 8.52-inch color notebook — **$579.99**, available soon
- **Palma 3** — 6.13-inch black-and-white reader — **$339.99**, available soon

All three use Android 16 rather than a locked reading OS. That is the reason they belong in an Android how-to: you install Kindle, Libby, Kobo, Outlook, or a notes app from Play the same way you would on a phone, then live with E Ink refresh instead of an LCD.

The Verge and 9to5Google both note that Android 16 is one generation behind the current phone OS (Android 17). That is still a jump from Palma 2 (Android 13) and the original Palma (Android 11).

## Pick a size before you pick apps

Use the form factor first. Software is shared more than the chassis.

### Palma 3 — phone-shaped reader

Keep Palma 3 if you want a second screen that fits a jacket pocket and is not a notification firehose.

Confirmed from BOOX and matching coverage:

- 6.13-inch HD black-and-white E Ink panel, **300 PPI**, dual-tone front light, ambient light sensor
- Anodized aluminum frame and paper-textured fiberglass back (replacing the older plastic shell)
- Qualcomm **Q-6690** octa-core (4 nm, up to 2.9 GHz), **6 GB RAM**, **128 GB** storage, microSD up to **2 TB**
- **3,950 mAh** battery
- **16 MP** rear camera with LED flash (document scan, not photography)
- Dual microphones for voice memos and speech-to-text
- Fingerprint reader in the power button
- Optional **InkSense Plus** stylus — supported, **not included**
- About **195 g** and **7.8 mm** thick (heavier than Palma 2, slightly thinner)

The screen size is the same 6.13-inch Carta panel used on earlier Palmas. The useful upgrades are Android 16, Bluetooth 6.0, the metal frame, and stylus support that used to live on Palma 2 Pro.

### Note Mini C — one-handed color notebook

Choose Mini C if you want color comics, marked-up PDFs, and a slate you can hold in one hand. BOOX prices it the same as the larger Air6 C at **$579.99**. Heise reports it does **not** support a keyboard cover. Confirm that on the product page before you buy if desk docking matters.

### Note Air6 C — full-size color notebook

Choose Air6 C if the device is a desk notebook first. BOOX positions the 10.3-inch Kaleido 3 color panel as a workflow machine (notes, split reading, stylus). Heise lists the familiar Air-class combo: 10.3-inch Kaleido 3 (300 PPI mono / 150 PPI color), 6 GB RAM, 64 GB storage, 3,700 mAh battery. Treat shop-page specs as the last word if those numbers change at ship time.

## What Android 16 actually changes on E Ink

You do not get Pixel-class Gemini Intelligence or Samsung One UI 9 on these devices. You get a current-enough Android base so Play apps install and stay signed in.

Practical effects:

- Play Store access is first-class, which closed e-readers still do not offer
- Bluetooth **6.0** pairs newer earbuds and keyboards with less fuss than Palma 2’s 5.1
- BSR refresh modes still matter more than the Android version for scrolling social apps — keep those apps off the home screen if the point is calm reading
- Security patches follow BOOX’s firmware cadence, not Google’s Pixel monthly drop. Check **Settings → About device → Android version** and BOOX’s firmware notes after setup

Do not buy a Palma 3 expecting Android 17 features such as Gemini Create My Widget or Pixel VIP widgets. Those are OEM overlays.

## First-run setup that actually helps

BOOX’s own “How to Set Up a New BOOX” tutorial is still the right sequence. Walk it once, then lock the device down.

1. Charge past 50 percent before the first update. E Ink firmware packages are not tiny.
2. Choose language, timezone, and Wi-Fi. Skip extra accounts until Play works.
3. Sign in with the **Google account** you want for books and notes. Use a dedicated account if this is a kid or travel device.
4. Open **Play Store**, update Google Play services, then install only the readers you will use this week (Kindle, Libby, Kobo, or your EPUB app).
5. In BOOX settings, set a **refresh mode** per app: normal for books, speed for notes, off for anything that flickers.
6. Turn on the **fingerprint** reader on Palma 3 and set a PIN. The power button sensor is easy to miss on first boot.
7. If you bought InkSense Plus, pair it in stylus settings and run the calibration sheet before annotating a real PDF.
8. Disable lock-screen notifications you do not want on a reading device. Android 16 will happily mirror phone chaos if you let Gmail and chat through.

## Apps that behave on E Ink

Start with apps that already think in pages:

- **Kindle, Kobo, Libby, Google Play Books** for licensed books
- **Moon+ Reader** or **ReadEra** for sideloaded EPUB and PDF
- BOOX native notes if you have a stylus; otherwise a simple handwriting app
- A scanner workflow that uses the 16 MP camera: scan, crop, send to Drive or the notes library

Avoid as daily drivers: short-form video, map navigation, and anything that needs 60 fps color. The hardware can open those apps. The screen will not make them pleasant.

Storage note: Palma 3 ships 128 GB plus microSD up to 2 TB. Air6 C coverage still lists 64 GB internal — budget a card if your library is scanned PDFs.

## Buying checklist

- Confirm the SKU on [shop.boox.com](https://shop.boox.com/) or Amazon matches **Android 16** and the September 2026 names (Air6 C, Mini C, Palma 3).
- Palma 3 and Mini C were **not** same-day retail at announcement. “Available soon” means wait for a ship date rather than a gray-market listing.
- Stylus is optional on Palma 3. Budget InkSense Plus separately if notes are the point.
- If you already own Palma 2, the screen size does not change. You are paying for Android 16, Bluetooth 6.0, metal build, and stylus support.
- Keep receipts. E Ink panels and official firmware support are the reason to buy from BOOX or Amazon, not an unlocked import with a mystery image.

## Conclusion

The September 2026 BOOX trio is useful because it puts **Android 16 and Play Store** on three different E Ink shapes instead of forcing everyone onto a 10-inch slate. Palma 3 is the interesting one for commuters: same 6.13-inch 300 PPI canvas, better chassis, optional pen, $339.99 when it lands. Air6 C is the one you can order now if you need a color notebook this week.

Set the device up like a quiet Android tablet: one Google account, a short list of reading apps, refresh modes per app, and notifications off. That is the whole product.
