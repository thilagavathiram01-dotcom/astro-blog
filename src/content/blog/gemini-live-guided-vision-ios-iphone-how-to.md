Google expanded Guided Vision in Gemini Live to iPhone users on November 25, 2026. The feature, first launched on Android on October 1, turns your phone camera into a conversational visual assistant. Point the camera at a label, an object, or a room, and Gemini describes what it sees while giving spoken cues to adjust the frame.

This guide covers the iOS rollout, how the feature works, and practical steps based on Google's official Android launch details and the November 25 announcement from the Gemini app account. Availability rolls out gradually, so check your Gemini app for the option.

## What Guided Vision adds on iPhone

Guided Vision builds on standard camera sharing in Gemini Live. When active, Gemini acts as a conversational partner. If the camera points too high, too close, or off to the side, it gives natural verbal prompts. Examples include asking you to pan slowly to the right, tilt downward, or step back for clearer context.

Google developed the feature with input from blind and low-vision communities. It partnered with Aira for tens of thousands of hours of visual interpretation training. More than 1,000 members of Aira’s Trusted Tester network helped test it. The same safety notes apply on iOS: it is an assistive utility, not a medical device, mobility aid, or navigation tool. Continue using established mobility aids for safe travel.

Android users already access it through Gemini app settings, accessibility shortcuts, or TalkBack. On iPhone the entry point is simpler: open Live and share the camera.

## How to start Guided Vision on iPhone

Google’s November 25 post on X states the basic flow: “Open Live, point your camera, and get real-time guidance right on screen.”

1. Open the Gemini app on your iPhone.
2. Start a Live session (tap the Live button or use the voice command if available).
3. Tap to share or turn on the camera.
4. Point the rear camera at the subject.
5. Speak your question or request. Gemini describes the scene and answers follow-ups.

If the view needs adjustment, Gemini coaches you with spoken reframing cues. Move slowly and keep the subject steady. Fast pans reduce usefulness.

The feature supports follow-up questions in the same conversation. It works across languages where Gemini Live is available, based on testing with communities in India, Brazil, Singapore, Indonesia, Japan, and other countries.

## Everyday uses that match Google’s examples

Google lists practical tasks for Guided Vision:

- Reading fine print and complex text, such as nutrition labels, appliance dials, washing machine cycles, or menus in low light.
- Finding and locating objects, such as a dropped earbud on the floor or an item in a crowded cabinet.
- Describing objects and matching details, such as colors, patterns, or whether a striped shirt pairs with trousers.
- Exploring an immediate environment, such as a room layout or objects on a table.

Try prompts like these:

- “Read the ingredients on this bottle and list any allergens.”
- “Pan across this spice rack and tell me when the black pepper is in frame.”
- “Describe the color and pattern of this jacket.”
- “Give me an overview of what is on this table.”

Verify important details yourself. Gemini can misread text or miss details, so treat answers as a helpful first pass.

## Differences from the Android version

Android received Guided Vision first, with extra access paths. Users can enable “Use Guided Vision in Live” in Gemini profile settings. They can also set an accessibility shortcut under Android Settings > Accessibility > Vision assistance > Guided Vision (floating button, two-finger swipe, or volume keys). TalkBack users can open it from the TalkBack menu.

On iOS the announcement focuses on the direct Live + camera flow. Google has not published a full iOS-specific help page with version requirements or country lists at the time of the November 25 announcement. The feature requires the Gemini app and a compatible iPhone that supports Gemini Live. Update the app from the App Store if the option does not appear.

For Android steps and more examples, see the existing guide on [How to Use Gemini Live Guided Vision on Android](/blog/gemini-live-guided-vision/).

## Tips for better results

- Hold the phone steady and move slowly when panning.
- Ask one clear question at a time, then follow up.
- Use good lighting when possible for finer text.
- End the Live session when finished so the camera does not stay active.
- Grant camera and microphone permissions if the system prompts you.
- Do not point the camera at sensitive information, passwords, or people without consent.

Google notes that Guided Vision is rolling out. If you do not see it yet, check for app updates and try again later. The same gradual rollout pattern applied on Android.

## Privacy and limits

Camera sharing in Live follows Google’s standard Gemini data practices. Review how Google handles Gemini Live data in the official help articles. You control when the camera is on. Ending the session stops the stream.

The feature is not intended for navigation, obstacle detection, or as a replacement for a white cane or other mobility tools. It can make mistakes, like any generative AI system. Use it as an assistive layer alongside other methods.
