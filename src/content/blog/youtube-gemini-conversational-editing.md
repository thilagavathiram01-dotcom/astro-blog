YouTube announced a new Gemini-powered editing partner at Made on YouTube 2026. You chat with the editor instead of dragging every clip by hand. The same event added Ask Studio feedback, video A/B tests, Shorts series, and custom homepage feeds.

This guide sticks to what YouTube and Google published on 23 September 2026. Some tools are live now. Conversational editing for Shorts and YouTube Create is scheduled for early 2027.

## What YouTube shipped at Made on YouTube 2026

YouTube CEO Neal Mohan framed the event as tools for the full creation path: discovery, editing, packaging, and protection. Official posts split the work into four buckets.

**Viewers** can build custom homepage feeds from a prompt, use Ask YouTube for product comparisons, and watch Shorts as numbered series on TV.

**Creators** get a conversational Gemini editor in Shorts and YouTube Create, Ask Studio draft feedback, channel-matched and dynamic thumbnails, and tests of up to three video cuts.

**Live and audio** tools include real-time auto-dubbing pilots, Live showdowns, Ask Music, and weekly Podcast Lineup summaries.

**Safety** work expands likeness detection to voice plus face later this year, and tests opt-in AI comment moderation that learns a channel’s style.

YouTube says more than 20 million videos go up every day. The new tools aim to cut production time without taking the timeline away from you.

## How conversational editing works

YouTube already put Gemini Omni into Shorts and the YouTube Create app earlier in 2026. Official posts say hundreds of thousands of channels used that path per day in August 2026. The next step is a chat layer on top of that model.

You start with suggested prompts or your own. The assistant builds a first draft. You keep chatting to reorder frames, trim pauses, add text overlays, and sync music to the beat. You can jump back to the manual timeline at any point.

Creator Happy Kelli demoed the flow on stage. She uploaded dance takes in different outfits, asked the tool to pick the best take per look, then asked it to add music and transitions. She still opened the timeline when she wanted a manual tweak.

YouTube’s own wording is useful: the partner edits *with* you, not *for* you. Treat every suggestion as a draft until you approve it.

TechCrunch, citing the event, reports availability for Shorts and YouTube Create in early 2027. Do not expect the full chat editor on every phone today.

## What you can do in YouTube Studio now

Studio changes start rolling out on web and mobile from the event date through 2027. TeamYouTube’s community post is the best checklist.

### Ask Studio feedback

Open a draft in YouTube Studio. Ask Studio can comment on pacing, structure, storytelling, title, and script before you publish. YouTube says the mobile version is coming soon to iPhone and Android.

Use it on a cut you already like, not a raw dump of files. Narrow questions work better than “make this better.” Example: “Does the hook in the first 15 seconds match the title?”

### Insights and research

New Insights and Research destinations surface past performance and outlier videos. Use an outlier as a brief for the next shoot, then run Ask Studio on the draft.

### Thumbnails

You can generate channel-matched thumbnails. Dynamic thumbnails let YouTube show the best of three options to different audience segments. Ask Studio can also review older videos in the background and suggest refreshed thumbnails for classic uploads.

YouTube says creators have run more than 40 million A/B experiments on titles and thumbnails since that feature launched in 2024. Treat that figure as YouTube’s own count, not an independent audit.

### Video A/B testing

Coming soon: upload up to three cuts of the same Short or long-form video and pick the winner from retention data. Use this when the hook is the variable, not when the stories differ.

## Set up Shorts series

Shorts series turns a pile of clips into seasons and episodes. You organize shows and upload custom show thumbnails in YouTube Studio on desktop.

Viewers in the Shorts feed see a **Watch series** button and play episodes in order on mobile, desktop, and TV. TeamYouTube says you can start adding show features to Shorts later this month (September 2026), with a wider TV and post-to-TV path into early 2027.

Practical setup:

1. Group related Shorts that already share a format.
2. Number episodes in titles so the series feed stays readable if the button is missing in a region.
3. Upload a show thumbnail that still works at TV size.
4. Keep episode length consistent so binge sessions do not stall.

Image posts and carousels also land in the Shorts feed, which is a separate discovery surface from the series button.

## Build custom feeds and Ask YouTube

Custom feeds let viewers pin homepage tabs from a prompt. Official examples include “Witty podcasts for my daily 30-minute commute,” “Evening wind down with long documentaries,” and “Help me discover new DIY creators.”

YouTube’s Made on YouTube FAQ says custom feeds start in the U.S. on web, mobile, and TVs this fall. You can save more than one feed. Each feed refreshes with new recommendations.

Ask YouTube expands into product guidance: comparison tables, follow-up questions, and voice commands on TV for tagged products. If you sell through YouTube Shopping, tag products cleanly so Ask YouTube has something to quote. YouTube says the Shopping affiliate program will reach 35 countries by year-end, with more than 1.3 million creators already enrolled.

If you research products in the browser on Android, the same habit pairs well with [Gemini in Chrome on Android](/blog/gemini-in-chrome-android/).

## Protect voice, face, and comments

Billions of channels can already opt into automated deepfake detection and manage visual likeness. Later this year YouTube will combine speaking-voice detection with facial detection to raise match accuracy. Likeness controls will also open in the YouTube mobile app so you can review matches away from a desktop.

Opt-in AI comment moderation is in test. When you turn it on, it learns the tone you already enforce. Review the first week of actions. Do not treat it as a fire-and-forget filter.

## A practical weekly workflow

Use this order so you do not wait on features that are still rolling out.

1. **This week:** Turn on likeness alerts in Studio. Sketch two or three custom-feed prompts you want viewers to use. Group existing Shorts into a series if the desktop control is on your account.
2. **On the next draft:** Run Ask Studio on title, hook, and pacing before you publish. Generate two extra thumbnails and let dynamic thumbnails split traffic if the control is present.
3. **When video A/B testing appears:** Test only the first 15 seconds or the cold open. Keep the rest of the cut identical.
4. **When conversational editing ships in 2027:** Import selects, ask for a first assembly, then lock picture on the timeline yourself.
5. **After publish:** Check Insights for outliers. Feed those titles back into the next brief.

## Tips that keep you in control

Write prompts with a job and a constraint. “Trim dead air, keep every product name on screen for two seconds” beats “edit this.”

Never skip the timeline pass on anything that uses licensed music or on-screen text you must spell correctly.

Label series episodes before you enable the show feature so older Shorts do not land out of order.

Keep a human moderation pass on comments while the AI filter is in test.

Watch rollout notes. TeamYouTube said some items ship this month, others through 2027, and live auto-dubbing starts as a small pilot.

## Conclusion

Made on YouTube 2026 is a production kit, not a single button. Ask Studio and thumbnail tests can change how you ship this quarter. Shorts series and custom feeds change how people find the work. Conversational Gemini editing is the piece that waits until early 2027.

Use the tools that are on your account today. Keep the timeline as the last word. Check the official YouTube blog when a control appears in Studio that this article listed as “coming soon.”
