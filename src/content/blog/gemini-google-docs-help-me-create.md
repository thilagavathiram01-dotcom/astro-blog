A blank Google Doc used to mean a blinking cursor and a first paragraph you would rewrite anyway. Gemini in Docs is now a workspace collaborator: it can draft a formatted document from Drive, Gmail, Chat, and the web; edit a section without regenerating the whole file; keep tone consistent across co-authors; and summarize comment threads.

These capabilities sit behind an eligible **Google Workspace** or **Google AI** plan and Workspace smart features. Google documented the reimagined Docs experience in April 2026 (Workspace Updates) and followed with custom instructions (May 2026), expanded language support (July 2026), Gemini-powered comments (July 2026), and cross-app creation from Gmail, Chat, Drive, and Slides (rollout started 2 September 2026).

This guide covers what you need, how to generate a first draft, how to edit in place, how to add sources, and what not to expect.

## What you need

- A computer browser. Google’s Help Center marks **Help me create** as desktop-only at this time.
- An eligible plan. Official availability for Gemini in Docs includes Workspace Business and Enterprise editions that include Gemini, Education Plus and Google AI Pro for Education, consumer **Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra**, plus listed Frontline and AI add-ons. Exact SKUs change; check [Gemini features and plans](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/13952129) if the spark icon is missing.
- **Workspace smart features** turned on for your account. Admins also need Gemini for Workspace in Drive enabled where that control exists.
- Edit access on the document for comment workflows and for generating or deleting Studio-style artifacts in related products.

If Ask Gemini does not appear at the top right of Docs, confirm the plan and smart features first. Do not assume a free consumer Gmail account has the same tools.

## Open Gemini in a document

1. On your computer, open a file in [Google Docs](https://docs.google.com).
2. At the top right, click **Ask Gemini** to open the side panel.
3. Pick a suggested prompt, or type your own in the box at the bottom of the panel and press Enter.

You can also work from the **bottom bar**: hover the spark near the bottom of an existing doc and type there. Suggested edits stay private to you until you accept them.

Clear history from the panel’s more-options menu if you do not want the current conversation to linger. Refreshing the tab, closing the file, or going offline also drops unsaved Gemini history in Docs.

## Create a formatted first draft (Help me create)

Use this when the page is empty and you want structure, headings, and sourced context—not a single paragraph.

1. Open Docs.
2. Go to **File → New → Help me create** (or start from a new doc’s create prompt).
3. Describe the document in natural language. Instructions work better than questions. Example: “Write a one-page launch brief for our Q4 Android tutorial series. Audience is junior developers. Include goals, outline, owners, and risks.”
4. Optional: type **@** next to the prompt and attach related Drive files so Gemini can pull structure and facts from them.
5. Click **Create** and wait for the formatted draft.

Google’s product write-up for Help me create states that Gemini can synthesize files, email, Chat, and the web when Workspace Intelligence is in play. Treat every citation and number as something you verify before you share the doc.

**Match doc format** can mirror fonts, colors, headings, and table structure from a source document when you ask Gemini to follow that file. Use it when the team already has a branded brief or template.

## Edit what is already on the page (Help me write)

1. Click where you want new text, or select the passage to change.
2. Open Help me write from the left margin, the bottom bar, or Ask Gemini.
3. Give an instruction: “Make this more concise,” “Rewrite for a non-technical VP,” “Turn this section into a numbered checklist.”
4. Review the suggestion. Insert, retry, or discard. Suggestions are not visible to collaborators until you accept them.

Built-in rewrite options documented in Google’s Help Center include tone (formal or casual), summarize, bulletize, and elaborate. You can also ask Gemini to proofread grammar and punctuation.

**Match writing style** analyzes the current document and proposes edits so mixed co-author voices read as one. Run it after a review cycle, not before people have added their sections.

## Add sources and ask about the file

In the side panel, use **Add sources** when the draft should stay grounded:

- **Document links** already in the file
- **Add from Drive** for extra Docs, Slides, or PDFs you can access

Then ask concrete questions:

- “Summarize this document in three bullets.”
- “What decisions are still open?”
- “Draft a short email to the design lead based on the Risks section.”

Gemini respects the signed-in user’s sharing permissions. If you cannot open a Drive file, Gemini cannot use it either. Workspace documentation states that this Workspace data is not used to train Gemini models and is not reviewed by humans for that purpose.

## Custom instructions that persist

Since May 2026 you can store standing rules for Gemini in Docs instead of repeating them every session. Open Gemini in the side panel and add instructions such as:

- “Always respond in bullet points.”
- “When summarizing, start with three bullets.”
- “Use a concise, professional tone.”

Google’s Workspace Updates post notes a cap of **1,000 active instructions**. Keep the list short and specific. Conflicting rules (“always be brief” plus “always add examples”) produce mushy drafts.

## Comment workflows

If you have **edit access**, you can ask Gemini from the bottom bar or side panel to work on comments, not only body text:

- “Summarize all comments from Priya.”
- “What unresolved issues are blocking publish?”

Docs may also nudge you to summarize comments when you open a busy file, or offer a draft reply when you enter a thread. You still send the reply. Google rolled this out starting 28 July 2026 for domains with Gemini for Workspace in Drive enabled.

## Create Docs without leaving Gmail or Chat

From 2 September 2026, Gemini can generate a formatted Doc, Sheet, or Slides deck in the background from another Workspace app and save it to Drive. You can also ask it to draft mail, propose meeting times, or drop items into Google Tasks.

Availability at launch (Workspace Updates, 9 September 2026):

- Business Standard and Plus; Enterprise Standard and Plus
- Consumer **Google AI Pro** (scheduling excluded) and **Google AI Ultra**
- Frontline Plus for scheduling only; Google AI Pro for Education; AI Expanded Access
- English only at launch, with usage limits on advanced features

Actions that send mail or book calendar time show a preview card. Review and confirm before anything leaves your account.

## Practical prompt patterns

Write instructions, not trivia questions.

```text
Create a 1,000-word how-to outline for transferring passkeys on Android.
Audience: people who already use Google Password Manager.
Use H2 headings, a short intro, numbered steps, and a sources list.
Pull structure from @android-passkey-brief and do not invent product names.
```

```text
Rewrite the Executive summary so a legal reviewer can scan it in two minutes.
Keep every date and proper noun. Flag any claim that is not in the attached sources.
```

```text
Match writing style to the rest of this doc. Do not change the table of owners.
```

If the first draft is wrong, add constraints and retry. Regenerating the entire file to fix one section is slower than selecting that section and using Help me write.

## Limits and safety notes

- Gemini suggestions are not Google’s official views and can be wrong. Do not treat them as legal, medical, or financial advice.
- Generated images inside Workspace apps are meant for use in those editors.
- Conversation history in Docs is separate from Gemini Apps Activity. Clearing one does not wipe the other.
- Feature names and eligibility differ by domain rollout. Rapid and Scheduled Release domains saw the cross-app wave over up to 15 days from 2 September 2026.

## Conclusion

Use **Help me create** when you need a structured first draft with Drive context. Use **Help me write** and **Match writing style** when the words are already on the page. Store a few custom instructions so you stop repeating house style. Point Gemini at comments when a review thread is longer than the draft. Keep a human in the loop for anything that emails a person or books time.

Start in a new Doc on desktop, open Ask Gemini, and write one specific instruction plus an @-mentioned source file.
