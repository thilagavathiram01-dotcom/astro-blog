Google updated Gemini Notebook on September 15, 2026 with tools meant for actual study sessions, not generic chat. You can talk to a notebook in nearly 100 languages, record a lecture inside the mobile app, and generate quizzes, flashcards, and ~60-second Video Overviews from sources you add yourself.

This guide walks through the Android workflow using Google’s official product posts. Features roll out in waves. Voice chat starts with Google AI Ultra subscribers age 18+, then Pro and other plans. Interactive learning overviews and extra quiz formats are listed as arriving for all users over the following weeks.

## What Gemini Notebook is now

Gemini Notebook is the renamed NotebookLM product. It is still a standalone research tool at [notebook.google](https://notebook.google/), with Android and iOS apps. Answers are supposed to stay grounded in the sources you attach. If a fact is not in those files, the product is designed to say so rather than invent one.

That is the difference from a normal Gemini chat. Treat the notebook as a closed stack of PDFs, slides, notes, photos of handwriting, Drive files, links, and now audio you record in the app.

Google also ships a separate “notebooks in Gemini” space inside the Gemini app. That feature organizes chats and files in Gemini. This article is about the standalone Gemini Notebook product and its September study tools.

## Set up one notebook before you turn on voice

Do this once so later features have something to work with.

1. Install [Gemini Notebook for Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.labs.language.tailwind) or open the web app.
2. Create a notebook for a single course or project. Keep one subject per notebook.
3. Add sources: syllabus, lecture slides, assigned chapters, your notes, and any Play Books title Google supports in Expert Intelligence if you own it.
4. Confirm citations appear when you ask a basic question in chat.

A messy pile of unrelated PDFs produces messy quizzes. A tight source list produces better voice answers and shorter videos.

## Talk through a topic with real-time voice

Google’s headline study feature is a live conversation with the notebook on Android and iOS, in nearly 100 languages. Responses stay tied to your sources. You can ask for a step-by-step explanation and interrupt while the model is speaking.

Google compares the feel to Gemini Live: spoken turn-taking, not a one-shot voice memo.

How to use it when the control appears in your app:

1. Open the notebook that holds the unit you are studying.
2. Start the real-time conversation from the mobile app (the control is in the Gemini Notebook app, not the generic Gemini overlay).
3. Ask for one concept at a time: “Explain Kirchhoff’s laws using only my lecture slides.”
4. Interrupt when a step is unclear and ask for a slower walkthrough or a different example from the same sources.
5. Switch back to text chat if you need a citation you can copy into notes.

Availability from Google’s footnotes: Ultra subscribers 18+ first, then Pro and others. If you do not see voice yet, update the app and check the plan on the account.

## Record lectures and thoughts in the app

You could already photograph handwritten notes and worksheets. The September update adds an audio recorder in the mobile app so lectures and spoken thoughts land next to those sources.

Google says recordings live with your other materials. You can chat about them, cite them, and keep editing. Official support for this capture path is listed for web and mobile when English is the selected output language, including users under 18.

Practical recording rules:

- Start the recorder before class, not after the first example.
- Keep one recording per session and name it with the date and topic.
- Ask the notebook questions about that file only when you want a recap of that hour.
- Do not treat the transcript as a substitute for the official slides. Use both.

If your campus restricts recording, follow that policy. The feature is a capture tool, not permission to record other people.

## Build an interactive learning overview

Under Reports, Google is adding interactive learning overviews. These stitch a summary together with Studio outputs such as infographics, quizzes, and flashcards so a study block stays in one place.

Google says this report type rolls out to all users over the next few weeks after the September 15 post.

A clean workflow:

1. Finish adding sources for one exam unit.
2. Generate the interactive overview from Reports.
3. Work the quizzes and flashcards in the same session.
4. Return to notebook chat and ask how you did on the completed set.
5. Edit weak questions yourself. Google says you can add or change quiz and flashcard items.

New quiz formats on the way: short answer, multiple select, and fill in the blank. Until those land, use whatever quiz type the notebook already offers and follow up in chat.

## Make a 60-second Video Overview

Short Video Overviews run about a minute. Google describes them as a narrative plus simple educational animation, available in more than 80 languages. You can share the clip with classmates.

Use them for formulas, labeled diagrams, and process steps. Do not use them as the only study pass. A 60-second video is a recap, not the source of record.

Prompt the notebook toward one idea: “Make a short video of the citric acid cycle using only my uploaded chapter.” Then watch it once, pause on each label, and quiz yourself from the same sources.

## Add purchased books when Expert Intelligence is on

On September 17, 2026, Google Workspace announced Expert Intelligence for Gemini Notebook. Compatible ebooks bought on Google Play Books can sit in the same notebook as your notes. Answers are supposed to stay grounded in that book’s text.

A student can put a syllabus, lecture notes, and a purchased book in one notebook, then generate an Audio Overview or practice quiz. Workspace customers, Workspace Individual subscribers, and personal Google accounts with Gemini Notebook, Play, and Books enabled are in the listed audience. Rapid and Scheduled Release domains were marked available at announcement.

Only add books you have the right to use. The feature is not a free library.

## A 20-minute study loop that actually uses the new tools

1. Record the lecture or drop photos of handwritten notes into the course notebook.
2. Ask three targeted questions in text and check citations.
3. Start a voice session and talk through the one concept you missed.
4. Generate an interactive overview or a short Video Overview for that unit only.
5. Take the quiz, then ask chat where you slipped.
6. Edit two weak flashcards before you close the app.

If you already use September’s Android Drop tools, keep the same phone setup: updated Gemini and related Google apps. The Find Hub and Motion Assist walkthrough lives in [How to Use Android’s September 2026 Drop](/blog/android-september-2026-drop-guide/).

## Limits worth knowing

Voice chat is age-gated at 18+ in Google’s footnote and starts on Ultra. Interactive overviews and new quiz types were not same-day for every account. Audio capture is tied to English as the output language in the official note.

Grounding is only as good as the files you give the notebook. A missing chapter will not appear in a quiz. A bad recording will produce a bad recap.

Gemini Notebook is not the same product as notebooks inside the Gemini app. Shared notebooks, Gemini-app notebooks, and standalone notebooks have different sharing rules. Check which surface you opened before you send a link to a classmate.

## Conclusion

The September 2026 Gemini Notebook update is useful when you keep sources narrow and finish the loop: capture, ask, speak, quiz, fix the weak cards. Voice is the new piece. Recording and short videos only help if they point back to the same files.

Build one course notebook this week. Add this week’s materials only. Run one voice session and one quiz. That is enough to see whether the rollout has reached your account.
