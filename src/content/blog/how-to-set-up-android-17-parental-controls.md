Android 17 brings built-in parental controls to every device that receives the update. Parents can set screen time limits, block apps, schedule downtime, and filter content right from Settings. No extra app is required for the basic controls.

These tools first appeared on Pixel phones. Google expanded them so any Android 17 phone or tablet can use the same PIN-protected menu. You manage everything on the child’s device or combine it with Family Link for remote oversight.

## What Android 17 Parental Controls Include

The on-device controls live under Settings > Parental controls. After you create a PIN, you get access to:

- Daily limit: Cap total device use each day. The phone locks when the limit is reached.
- Downtime: Schedule automatic lock periods, such as bedtime or school hours.
- App limits: Restrict time on specific apps or block them completely.
- Web content filters: Block explicit sites and filter search results in Chrome and the Google app.
- App store filters: Limit Google Play (and other stores) by age rating for new downloads.

A recovery account lets you reset the PIN if you forget it. Without one, a forgotten PIN requires a factory reset. The PIN stays the same across users on the device, and some settings become restricted while controls are active to prevent bypasses.

These controls work without signing a Google account into the child’s device for the basic features. Family Link adds options like School Time, purchase approvals, and location alerts, which you manage from the parent’s phone.

## Set Up the Parental Controls PIN

Confirm the device runs Android 17. Open Settings > About phone > Android version. If it shows 17 or higher, proceed. Older versions do not include these expanded controls.

1. Open the Settings app on the child’s device.
2. Scroll and tap Parental controls.
3. Tap Set up Parental controls PIN.
4. Enter a PIN of at least four digits and confirm it.
5. Optionally add a recovery account. Go to Manage parental controls PIN later to verify it with the account password. Your Google Account is not signed in on the device.

Keep the PIN private. Children cannot change settings without it. If the device has multiple profiles (work profile or private space), on-device controls cannot be turned on.

Once the PIN is set, the Parental controls menu appears. You can return anytime and enter the PIN to adjust settings.

## Configure Daily Limits and Downtime

Daily limits help establish healthy habits. 

1. Open Settings > Parental controls > Daily limit.
2. Turn on Use daily limit.
3. Select a day and set hours and minutes.
4. Tap Apply to all days if you want the same limit every day.

When the daily limit is reached, the device locks. The lock screen shows an option to add more time. Enter the PIN, choose the bonus amount, and confirm. Only emergency calls remain available otherwise.

Downtime works the same way for scheduled blocks.

1. Go to Parental controls > Downtime.
2. Turn on Use downtime.
3. Pick a day, then set start and end times.
4. Apply to all days if needed.

During downtime the device locks. The same bonus-time option appears if you need to grant temporary access. Use this for bedtime or homework periods so the phone does not stay available all night.

## Limit or Block Specific Apps

App limits let you target high-use apps without restricting the whole device.

1. Open Settings > Parental controls > App limits.
2. Select the app.
3. Tap Set time limit and choose the duration.
4. Or turn off Allow app to block it completely.

Blocking an app hides it from the All apps list. Avoid blocking the Google app, as other system features may stop working correctly. Supported apps appear in the list only if they are installed. You can still find blocked apps in the supported list later.

App store filters stop age-inappropriate downloads before they start.

1. Go to Parental controls > App store filters > Google Play Store.
2. Enter your PIN.
3. Choose categories: Apps & games, Movies, TV, or Books.
4. Select the highest age rating allowed (for example, Everyone or Teen).
5. Save.

Filters apply to new installs from Google Play, the device manufacturer’s store, and carrier stores. Already-installed apps are not removed, but you can block them separately under App limits. The device-level filter overrides less restrictive account settings.

## Set Web Content Filters

Web filters reduce explicit content in search and browsing.

1. Open Settings > Parental controls > Web content filters.
2. Turn on Block explicit sites to restrict adult websites in Chrome and supported browsers.
3. Turn on Filter explicit search results to apply SafeSearch-style filtering in the Google app and Chrome.

These filters work on Google app version 16.42 or later, Chrome 140 or later, and Android System WebView 139 or later. Other browsers may support them if the developer integrates the feature. Explicit content can still appear in other apps. Sites visited in Chrome are checked by Google when Block explicit sites is on.

## Combine with Family Link for More Control

On-device controls handle the basics on one phone. Family Link adds remote management and extra tools.

Install the Family Link app on your own phone. Create or link a supervised Google Account for your child. For children under the age of consent, you must manage their account. Follow the prompts to supervise the device.

Family Link lets you:

- Approve or block Play Store downloads from your phone.
- Set School Time schedules that lock the device during school hours.
- View location (with appropriate permissions).
- Manage multiple devices from one place.

You can switch from pure on-device controls to Family Link. The Parental controls menu on the child’s device also offers a direct path to set up Family Link. ## Tips for Effective Use

Test the limits before handing the phone over. Set a short daily limit and confirm the lock screen appears. Check that blocked apps stay hidden. Verify web filters on a known explicit search.

Update Chrome and the Google app so filters apply. Keep the recovery account current. Review settings monthly as your child grows and needs change. Talk with your child about why the limits exist; tools work better alongside conversation.

If controls do not appear, confirm the Android version. A security patch alone is not Android 17. Search Settings for “parental” if the menu is not obvious on a non-Pixel device. Manufacturer skins may move the entry slightly, but it remains under Settings.

## Conclusion

Android 17 parental controls put practical limits in one PIN-protected place on any updated device. Set a daily cap, schedule downtime, block distracting apps, and filter content without installing extra software. Add Family Link when you need remote approval or School Time.

Start on the child’s phone today if it runs Android 17. Create the PIN, configure the limits that match your family’s routine, and adjust as needed. These tools give parents clearer control while kids use their devices.
