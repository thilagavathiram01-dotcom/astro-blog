Competitive analysis used to mean hours of tab-switching and note-taking. Gemini Deep Research changes that. It builds a research plan, searches the web, and delivers a cited multi-page report in minutes.

You stay in control. Edit the plan before it starts. Add your own files or connect Gmail and Drive. The result is a structured brief you can share or refine with follow-up questions.

## What Deep Research Does

Deep Research is an agentic feature in the Gemini app. It turns a prompt into a multi-step plan, browses sources, reasons over findings, and writes a report with citations.

Google Search is on by default. You can add Workspace sources or upload files. Reports typically take 5-10 minutes. Complex ones take longer. Google AI Pro and Ultra users get higher limits and can use the Pro model for higher quality.

## Start a Competitive Analysis Report

Open gemini.google.com and sign in. You must be 18 or older.

1. In the text box, click Tools, then select Deep Research.
2. Optional: Click Add files to upload competitor decks, internal memos, or images.
3. Optional: Click Sources and select Gmail, Drive, or a Gemini Notebook. Deselect Google Search if you want only your materials.
4. Enter a clear prompt. Example: "Research the top three competitors in the eco-friendly paint market. Focus on color trends, pricing, and marketing channels for the next five years. Include public financials where available."
5. Click Submit.

Gemini shows a research plan. Review the steps. Click Edit plan if you need to add markets, remove sources, or change the scope. Use natural language. Then click Start research.

When the report finishes, click Open. It includes an executive summary, key findings, and citations.

## Edit the Plan and Choose Sources

The plan appears before research begins. This is the best moment to steer the work.

Add specific competitors by name. Ask it to prioritize recent sources or exclude certain sites. If you connected Workspace, tell it to cross-reference your strategy docs with public data.

Google notes that Deep Research shows its thinking and the sites it browses while it works. Check those if you want to verify direction early.

## Review the Report and Ask Follow-ups

Open the finished report. Scan the citations. Hover or click them to see the original sources.

Ask follow-up questions in the same chat. Deep Research can answer from the material it already gathered or go back out for more. Examples: "Compare pricing tables across the three brands" or "Pull any mentions of sustainability claims."

You can turn the report into an Audio Overview for listening later. Export sections to Docs if you need to share with a team.

## Tips for Better Competitive Reports

- Start with the outcome you need. "I need a one-page brief for a product launch meeting" works better than a vague topic.
- Upload one internal document early. It helps the agent match public data to your context.
- Keep the first plan narrow. You can always run a second report on a sub-topic.
- Check limits. Free users can run reports. Pro and Ultra users get more capacity and higher-quality model options.
- Verify critical numbers yourself. Citations point to sources, but you still own the final decision.

## Conclusion

Gemini Deep Research turns competitive analysis into a guided process. You set the scope, review the plan, and receive a cited report you can act on. Start with one clear question about your market, edit the plan, and test the output against a source you already know.

The feature is available to all Gemini users. Higher plans raise the number of reports you can run and unlock better models. Use it for the research phase, then bring your own judgment to the strategy.
