Google Play now lets you organize games into custom playlists and compete in short Sprints. These tools help you discover titles that match your style and add quick competition without long commitments.

Game Playlists let you curate collections of games you like. Sprints match you with a small group for a 48-hour challenge in a supported game. Both features rolled out as part of the October 2026 Google Play updates.

![Person playing a mobile game on a smartphone](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1762769152062-9581ad081c4c?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What are Game Playlists?

Game Playlists are custom collections you build inside the Google Play Store. You pick games, give the playlist a name, and organize them by theme or mood.

Google announced the feature alongside Game Finder. You can create playlists today. Sharing them and browsing other players’ lists will arrive later.

This works well if you already use Game Finder to find titles based on your Gamer DNA. See our guide on [using Game Finder and Ask Play](/blog/how-to-use-game-finder-and-ask-play-on-google-play/) for more on personalized recommendations.

## How to create a Game Playlist

Open the Google Play Store app on your Android device. Make sure you are signed in with your Google account.

1. Go to the Games section or your library of installed games.
2. Look for the option to create a new playlist. Select it.
3. Add at least three games. Search for titles or pick from your library.
4. Give the playlist a title and optional description. Choose a color if the option appears.
5. Save the playlist.

You can edit it later. Remove games, change the name, or delete the whole list if you change your mind.

Playlists stay private for now. Google plans to add sharing so you can explore collections from other players.

## What are Sprints?

Sprints are 48-hour competitive challenges. Google Play matches you with one to four other players in the same game. You race for the highest score on a specific in-game action.

The timer starts once the group is formed. Only one Sprint can run at a time. You join a new one after the current Sprint ends.

Sprints are available in select games. You need a personal Google account, a Play Games profile at least 21 days old, and the latest version of the game installed from Play.

## How to join a Sprint

1. Open the Google Play Store app.
2. Tap the You tab.
3. Find Challenges and look for available Sprints.
4. Select Join Sprint on a supported game.
5. Wait for matchmaking. You can cancel before the group forms.

You can also join from the Sidekick overlay while playing a supported game. Sign into the game with your Play Games account so scores track correctly.

Once the Sprint starts you cannot leave or pause it. The player with the highest score at the end wins. Ties go to whoever reached that score first. If everyone else leaves and your score is above zero, you win.

## Rewards and fairness

Winners receive a temporary avatar effect and a “Won Sprint” highlight on their Gamer Profile for the next 48 hours. Tap the avatar to see which Sprint you won.

Google Play checks scores for suspicious activity. Cheating can lead to disqualification or bans from competitive features. Keep play fair so the challenges stay fun.

If technical issues arise, Play may pause or cancel a Sprint. You will see a message under the You tab.

## Tips for better results

Update your games regularly so you stay on the latest version that supports Sprints. Create your Play Games profile early if you plan to compete.

Use playlists to group games by category, such as puzzle titles for short sessions or action games for longer play. This makes it faster to jump into a Sprint later.

Check the Challenges section often. New Sprints appear as more games support the feature.

Family Link, school, or work accounts cannot join Sprints. Stick to a standard personal Google account.

## Conclusion

Game Playlists give you a simple way to organize the titles you enjoy. Sprints add short, matched competitions that fit into a busy schedule. Together they make Google Play more useful for finding and playing games.

Start with a playlist of your current favorites. Then join a Sprint in one of those games and see how the 48-hour challenge feels. Both features continue to expand as Google rolls them out more widely.
