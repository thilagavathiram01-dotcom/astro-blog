Cars with Google built-in already put Maps and Gemini on your dashboard without a phone. On 13 October 2026 Google announced a redesigned Google Play experience that makes the existing library of more than 700 apps easier to browse and download. The same update adds a parked-to-drive handoff for YouTube and brings new apps such as STARZ, Firefox, Ludo King, and JioHotstar.

The changes roll out automatically over the coming weeks. You do not install a separate update. Once the new layout appears on your vehicle display, the steps below show how to use it.

## What the redesign changes

The homepage now surfaces featured apps, new releases, and key details so you can download with a single tap. Navigation is streamlined. Curated collections change depending on whether the car is parked or in drive. The layout itself adapts to the shape, orientation, and size of your particular screen.

Google notes that cars with Google built-in are available across 16 brands and more than 100 models. The redesign reaches those vehicles, including the next-generation 2027 Chevrolet Silverado 1500 and GMC Sierra 1500.

## How to open and browse the new Play Store

1. Sit in the driver’s seat with the vehicle powered on. The update arrives over the air, so check after a few days if the old layout is still present.
2. Tap the Google Play icon on the home screen or in the app tray. If you do not see it, confirm you are signed in with a Google account that has an active data connection.
3. On the homepage, scan the featured row and the “What’s new” section. Tap any title to view details, then tap the install button once to download.
4. Use the left-side (or top, on vertical screens) navigation to switch between Apps, Games, Search, and More. Collections that appear only while parked disappear or change when you shift into drive.
5. Search by name if you already know the app. Results load faster because the interface prioritizes the most relevant matches for the current vehicle state.

The same single-tap install works for the newly added titles. STARZ, Firefox, Ludo King, and JioHotstar appear in the store for parked use. They stay restricted while the car is moving, consistent with existing safety rules for interactive apps.

## Use the YouTube parked-to-drive feature

Starting with YouTube, you can watch video on the dashboard while the car is parked. When you shift into drive, playback continues as audio only. This requires an active YouTube Premium subscription.

1. Park the vehicle and open YouTube from the Play Store or your home screen.
2. Start a video while stationary. Confirm video plays on the dashboard.
3. Shift into drive. The visual stops and audio continues in the background.
4. If audio does not continue, verify the signed-in Google account holds YouTube Premium and that the app is updated.

Google says it will keep expanding this parked-to-drive pattern with partners including Audible, Pandora, SiriusXM, Amazon Music, and others.

## Tips for the best experience

- Keep the vehicle connected to cellular or a known Wi-Fi network so the store and media apps stay current.
- Sign in with the same Google account you use on your phone so purchases and subscriptions carry over.
- Check the parked-only collections when you stop for charging or pickup. Those lists disappear once you start driving.
- If an app you installed earlier does not appear after the redesign, open the library and look for an update prompt. The new layout does not remove existing installs.
- For Gemini voice control while driving, see our earlier guide on [Gemini in cars with Google built-in](/blog/gemini-cars-google-built-in/).

The redesign does not change the core safety model. Interactive apps remain limited while the vehicle is in motion. Video stays parked-only except for the Premium audio continuation on YouTube.

## Conclusion

The updated Google Play in cars with Google built-in reduces the steps needed to find and install apps from a catalog that already exceeds 700 titles. Single-tap downloads, adaptive layouts, and state-aware collections make the store usable on both wide and tall vehicle screens. The YouTube parked-to-drive handoff extends listening time without requiring you to stop playback manually.

Once the update reaches your vehicle, the changes appear automatically. Open Play, browse the new homepage, and test the YouTube transition on your next stop.
