Guided Vision in Gemini Live is now rolling out on compatible Android phones. Share the camera during a Live session and Gemini speaks descriptions, answers follow-up questions, and tells you when the frame is too high, too close, or off to the side.

Google launched the feature on 1 October 2026 after building it with blind and low-vision testers. It is an assistive tool inside the Gemini app, not a separate download. Access is still staggered, so the toggle can be missing even on a recent Pixel or Samsung phone.

This guide follows the Android Accessibility Help page and the Gemini Live launch post. For volume keys, the floating button, and the two-finger swipe, use the [Guided Vision accessibility shortcut guide](/blog/android-accessibility-shortcut-guided-vision/).

## What Guided Vision actually does

With the setting on, a Gemini Live camera share becomes a spoken conversation about what is in front of the lens. If the shot is bad, Gemini asks you to pan slowly, tilt down, or step back so it can see the object clearly.

Google lists five everyday jobs:

- Read fine print, appliance dials, washing-machine cycles, and menus in dim rooms.
- Find an object nearby, such as a dropped earbud or black pepper in a crowded cabinet.
- Describe color, pattern, and shape, including whether a striped shirt matches a pair of trousers.
- Give an overview of a room layout or items across a table.
- Continue the same conversation in more than one language. Google cites testing with communities in India, Brazil, Singapore, Indonesia, Japan, and other countries.

The same help page also lists reading or translating text, identifying object position, and describing surroundings. Older adults and anyone squinting at small print in poor light can use the same flow. You do not need TalkBack for the Gemini app path.

Google is explicit about limits. Guided Vision can make mistakes. It is not a medical device, a mobility aid, or a replacement for a white cane. Do not use it for navigation, safe-travel guidance, or obstacle detection. Keep your usual mobility aids and travel habits.

Training context comes from a partnership with Aira. Google says the teams visually interpreted data for tens of thousands of hours, and more than 1,000 members of Aira’s Trusted Tester network stress-tested daily routines. Aira specialists also helped set safety checks. That is background, not a promise that every label will be read correctly.

## Turn on Use Guided Vision in Live

Google’s help article says the feature rolls out slowly. If you cannot find the switch, update the Gemini app and wait. A VPN does not add a country to the availability list.

1. Open the Gemini mobile app on your Android phone.
2. At the top, tap your profile picture or initial.
3. Open Settings.
4. Turn **Use Guided Vision in Live** on.
5. On the on-screen notification, tap **Start Live** if you want a session immediately.

Once the switch is on, start Gemini Live and share the camera. End the session with **End Live session** so the camera is not left sharing.

If Live itself is unfamiliar, open Gemini, start a Live chat, and use the camera control at the bottom of the Live screen. Google’s Gemini Live help page covers camera and screen sharing separately. Guided Vision sits on the camera path, not on screen share.

Sally Khoo, Deputy Director (Innovation) at SG Enable, described the gap this way in Google’s launch post: phones are typically built for sighted users, and real-time descriptions with verbal camera guidance can help with home tasks such as reading fine print and locating daily essentials. Prashant Verma of the National Association of the Blind, India, noted testing with blind and low-vision users across Indian languages.

## Run a first session on a known object

Practice at a table before you need a label in a shop.

1. Start Live from the notification after you enable the setting, or open Gemini Live and share the camera.
2. Point the rear camera at a cereal box, a bottle, or a printed page.
3. Hold still. If Gemini asks you to pan right, tilt down, or step back, follow that cue instead of sweeping the phone.
4. Ask a narrow follow-up: “Read the ingredients,” “What color is this jacket?” or “Where is the black pepper?”
5. End the Live session when you are done.

Broad prompts such as “What is in front of me?” produce weaker answers than a single object or a line of text. Confirm anything that would change a medical, legal, or payment decision with a second source.

## Three more ways to launch it

The Gemini settings switch is the on-ramp. Google also documents three quick starts under **Settings → Accessibility → Vision assistance → Guided Vision**:

- **Accessibility button:** tap the floating button.
- **Accessibility gesture:** swipe up from the bottom with two fingers.
- **Volume keys:** press and hold both volume keys.

Turn **Guided vision shortcut** on, then pick one of those options. TalkBack users can tap the screen with three fingers, open the TalkBack menu, and choose Guided Vision. Update TalkBack from Play Store first.

Step-by-step shortcut setup, including OEM menu names, is in the [accessibility shortcut article](/blog/android-accessibility-shortcut-guided-vision/). Use one launcher. Two gestures for the same tool are easy to forget.

## Prompts that match the official examples

Keep the request inside the jobs Google published.

**Labels and dials.** “Read the nutrition panel.” “What cycle is this dial set to?” “Read the menu item under the photo.”

**Finding an item.** “Is there an earbud on the floor?” “Point me toward the black pepper in this cabinet.” Wait for a reframing cue before you move the phone again.

**Matching clothes.** “Is this striped shirt a close match for these trousers?” “What color is the jacket on the left?”

**A room or a table.** “Describe the layout of this room.” “What is on the table from left to right?”

Ask follow-ups in the same Live session. Guided Vision is built as a conversation, not a one-shot caption.

## If the toggle is missing

Work through this list before you assume the phone is unsupported.

- Update Gemini from the Play Store, then force-close and reopen it.
- Sign in with a personal Google Account in a country where Gemini is available.
- Check profile Settings again after a reboot. The help page says rollout is gradual.
- Confirm you are in the Gemini app, not only the Google app assistant overlay.
- Search Settings for “Guided Vision.” On some builds the row appears under Accessibility before the Gemini switch does.

The September 2026 Android Drop previewed the feature for Android 9 and later. The October post says it is launching across compatible Android devices. Treat both as a rollout, not a same-day switch on every handset.

## Tips before you rely on it

- Wipe the rear lens. A smudge becomes a wrong description.
- Use a lamp for small print. Dim menus are a supported case, not a guarantee.
- Follow the reframing cue once, then ask the question. Sweeping the camera while talking drops the frame Gemini just corrected.
- End Live when you finish. A shared camera should not stay open in a pocket.
- Do not use it to cross a street or detect obstacles. Google’s warning is part of the product, not a footnote you can ignore.
- Pair “what is this?” with Find Hub remembered items if you also need “where did I put it?” on Android 16 and later, where both Gemini and Find Hub are available.

## Conclusion

Enable **Use Guided Vision in Live** from your Gemini profile settings, share the camera, and ask one specific question about a label, an object, or a room. Bind a shortcut only after that first session works.

Treat the spoken answer as assistance. Check critical details yourself, and keep established mobility tools for travel.
