Google refreshed Gemini Notebook on September 15, 2026 with tools aimed at real study sessions, not just chat summaries. You can talk through a notebook out loud, record a lecture into the same source list, and build interactive overviews that mix quizzes, flashcards, and short videos.

This guide walks through what shipped, who gets it first, and a setup you can finish in one sitting. Facts below come from Google’s official Notebook post and the product footnotes on that page.

If you also use Android’s September Drop features such as Find Hub memory or Guided vision, pair this with our [September 2026 Android Drop guide](/blog/android-september-2026-drop-guide/).

## What changed in the September refresh

Gemini Notebook (the product formerly branded NotebookLM) now emphasizes three study loops:

- Voice chat grounded in the sources you uploaded
- On-device capture of lectures and spoken notes in the mobile app
- Reports that combine summaries with Studio outputs such as infographics, quizzes, and flashcards

Google also added short video overviews of about 60 seconds in more than 80 languages, plus extra quiz formats (short answer, multiple select, and fill in the blank) rolling out in the weeks after the post.

None of these replace reading the source. They sit on top of the files, links, photos, and recordings you choose.

## Who can use each feature

Availability is not one switch. Google split the rollout:

- **Voice conversations** with a notebook: mobile app on [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.labs.language.tailwind) and [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gemini-notebook/id6737527615), in nearly 100 languages. First wave is Google AI Ultra subscribers age 18 and older. Google AI Pro and other plans follow.
- **Audio recorder** in the mobile app: web and mobile, including users under 18, when English is the selected output language in settings. Google said this would start the week after the September 15 announcement.
- **Interactive learning overviews** under Reports: rolling out to all users in the following weeks.
- **New quiz formats**: same “next few weeks” window for all users.

Student pricing is separate from feature flags. Eligible U.S. college students can claim one year of Google AI Pro (listed at $19.99 per month). That offer raises Gemini Notebook usage limits 4x versus non-AI subscribers. Eligible students in 140+ other markets can claim one year of Google AI Plus, which Google says gives 2x higher Notebook limits. Redeem through December 31, 2026. A payment method is required at sign-up, and the paid plan starts after the year unless you cancel.

## Step 1: Build a notebook from real sources

Open [notebook.google](https://notebook.google/) or the Gemini Notebook mobile app and create one notebook per course or project. Do not dump every subject into a single pile.

Add sources you trust:

1. PDFs of lecture slides and assigned chapters
2. Links to official course pages or papers
3. Photos of handwritten notes and worksheets (already supported before this drop)
4. After the recorder ships on your account, lecture audio and spoken memos

Keep the source list tight. Grounded answers only help if the files match the exam or brief you care about.

Name the notebook after the syllabus unit, not a vague label like “School.” You will search this list later.

## Step 2: Talk through a hard section

When voice chat is on your account, open the notebook in the mobile app and start a spoken session. Google’s design is a real-time conversation you can interrupt.

Use it for:

- “Walk me through the derivation in source 3, step by step.”
- “Quiz me out loud on the terms I highlighted.”
- “Explain this paragraph as if I already know last week’s lecture.”

Every reply is supposed to stay grounded in your sources. If the model cites a file you did not mean to include, remove that source and ask again.

Speak in the language you study in. Google lists nearly 100 languages for this mode.

Treat voice as a first pass. After the session, type follow-ups so you have a written trail of what you still miss.

## Step 3: Record lectures and thoughts in the same notebook

The new mobile audio recorder puts captured audio next to your other sources. Google says you can chat with those notes, cite them, and keep editing them.

A practical capture habit:

1. Start the recorder before class, not after the first slide.
2. Stop between topics if the lecture shifts units. Shorter clips are easier to cite.
3. Add a one-line note: date, course code, and lecture title.
4. After class, ask the notebook to list claims that do not appear in the slides. Those are the gaps to check against the textbook.

This path requires English as the selected output language for the first release. Switch the setting if recordings refuse to attach.

Do not record private conversations or sessions where recording is banned. The tool stores what you capture.

## Step 4: Build an interactive learning overview

Under **Reports**, create an interactive learning overview. Google describes this as a single study surface that weaves a summary together with Studio outputs such as infographics, quizzes, and flashcards.

Suggested order:

1. Generate the overview after you have at least three solid sources.
2. Open the quiz and flashcard blocks and delete items that cite a weak source.
3. Add your own questions for anything the overview skipped.
4. After you finish a quiz or flashcard set, ask notebook chat how you scored and where to study next. Google explicitly supports that follow-up.

New formats arriving after the announcement: short answer, multiple select, and fill in the blank. Until they appear on your account, use the formats you already see and edit questions by hand.

## Step 5: Make a 60-second video overview

Short Video Overviews combine a spoken narrative with educational animations. Google says they run about 60 seconds and work in more than 80 languages. You can share them with classmates.

Use a video overview when a formula, diagram, or process is hard to hold in text. Do not use it as the only study pass. Watch it once, then return to the cited sources.

## A one-hour study block that uses the new tools

1. **10 minutes:** Add this week’s slides, one chapter PDF, and any new recording.
2. **15 minutes:** Voice-chat the two concepts you failed last quiz. Interrupt when an answer skips a step.
3. **20 minutes:** Run an interactive overview. Take the quiz. Edit two weak questions so they match the syllabus wording.
4. **10 minutes:** Generate a short video overview in the language you will use in the exam.
5. **5 minutes:** Ask chat which sources the missed quiz items came from. Open those pages before you close the notebook.

Repeat the block per unit, not per semester. A bloated notebook slows citations and hides gaps.

## Tips that keep answers honest

- One notebook per exam unit or project brief.
- Prefer official PDFs over random blog posts.
- Delete a source that keeps pulling the chat off syllabus.
- Customize quizzes instead of accepting the first draft.
- Confirm student-plan limits before you rely on heavy video and quiz generation during finals week.
- Voice chat is adult Ultra-first. Do not plan a class workflow around it until Pro and standard accounts show the mic.

## Conclusion

The September 2026 Gemini Notebook update is useful when you treat the notebook as a closed set of sources, then speak, record, quiz, and watch against that set. Voice chat helps you rehearse. The recorder keeps lectures next to the slides. Interactive overviews and short videos compress review time without hiding citations.

Start with one course notebook this week. Add three sources, run one overview, and take the quiz before you add another class. That is enough to see whether the new tools earn a place in your routine.
