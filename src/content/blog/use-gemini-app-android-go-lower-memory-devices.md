Google expanded the Gemini app to eligible Android (Go edition) devices on November 18, 2026. These phones serve as the main internet connection for hundreds of millions of people. Hardware limits previously restricted them to basic voice commands. Google re-architected the app so the core Gemini experience runs on devices with less memory.

This guide covers eligibility, setup steps from official help pages, available features, and current limits. It draws only from Google’s announcement and support documentation.

## Check Device Eligibility First

The Gemini mobile app works on Android (Go edition) devices with 2GB of RAM or more. You need a personal Google Account or a work or school account that has access to Gemini Apps. Availability follows the same languages and countries where Gemini is offered.

On these devices, Gemini is available as part of the Google Search app. It replaces Assistant for Android (Go edition). Some newer phones from partners such as Transsion may ship with Gemini preloaded.

Confirm your phone meets the RAM requirement in Settings under About phone or System. Sign in with an eligible account before proceeding.

## Update Apps and Start a Conversation

Update the Google Search app or install the Gemini app from the Play Store.

1. Open the Google Play Store on your Android Go device.
2. Search for “Google” or “Google Search.”
3. Tap Update if the button appears. If it says Open, the app is already current.
4. Alternatively, search for the Gemini app and install it where available.

To start a chat:

- Touch and hold the Home button.
- On supported devices, press and hold the Power button.

“Hey Google” voice activation and “Ask about this screen” are not supported on Android Go devices. Use the button methods instead.

Conversations work through the re-architected app that prioritizes core functions on constrained hardware.

## Core Features Available on Lower-Memory Phones

The app supports practical tasks without requiring higher-end hardware. Google’s help page lists these capabilities:

- Stay connected: Ask Gemini to call or send text messages to contacts.
- Work with images: Photograph notes, forms, or signs for translation or step-by-step help. Request custom images via Nano Banana.
- Brainstorm and practice: Use Gemini Live for natural spoken conversations, such as interview practice, in more than 45 languages.
- Find local information: Check drive times or ask detailed questions like locating a restaurant with specific amenities.
- Plan your day: Set alarms or create calendar events.
- Add context: Upload documents, photos, or other files.
- Play media: Request music by song, mood, or activity.

These features run by sending requests to Google’s systems. The phone handles speech, camera input, and the interface while keeping memory use low.

## Limits You Should Know

Two features remain unavailable on Android Go: the “Hey Google” wake word and “Ask about this screen.” Both require the button-based launch methods described earlier.

The experience focuses on the core Gemini capabilities listed above. It does not include every advanced option available on higher-memory devices. Performance depends on available storage and background apps. Close unused apps before longer sessions for smoother results.

Family Link managers can control access for users under the applicable age in their country.

## Tips for Better Results

Keep the Google Search app or Gemini app updated through the Play Store. Clear cache if responses slow down. Use short, clear requests when memory is tight. Upload only the files needed for context.

For developers building related Android experiences, see our guide on [building Android apps with Google AI Studio](/blog/build-android-apps-google-ai-studio/).

Test basic tasks first—such as setting an alarm or asking for a local recommendation—before relying on camera or Live features.

## Conclusion

The Gemini app now reaches eligible Android Go devices with 2GB of RAM or more. Setup involves updating the Google Search app or installing Gemini, then launching via the Home or Power button. Core features cover calls, messages, camera help, Live conversations, image creation, calendar tasks, and media playback. Official limits exclude hands-free wake words and screen questions.

Check your device specifications and account access, then follow the Play Store steps to begin. The change extends personal AI assistance to more people who rely on lower-memory phones for daily tasks.
