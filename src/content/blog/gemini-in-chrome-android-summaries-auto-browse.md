Gemini in Chrome puts page summaries, contextual answers, and limited agentic browsing into the browser on your Android phone. You open it from the toolbar or overflow menu, keep the current page as context, and (on eligible accounts) hand multi-step web chores to Auto Browse after reviewing the plan.

This guide walks through the exact steps Google documents for Android, the requirements that actually gate the feature, and the safety controls that keep you in charge.

## What Gemini in Chrome does on Android

On supported Android devices, tapping the Gemini icon (or choosing it from the three-dot menu) opens a sheet that mirrors the desktop experience. You can:

- Summarize the page you are viewing.
- Ask questions that use the page as context.
- Connect Calendar, Keep, and other Google apps via Personal Intelligence.
- Generate or edit images with Nano Banana.
- (AI Pro / Ultra) Start Auto Browse tasks that navigate sites, fill forms, or collect information, with confirmation prompts before sensitive actions.

Availability is still rolling out by region and account. Core features are free for eligible users; Auto Browse requires Google AI Pro or Ultra. Device language is typically set to English, and managed work/school accounts need admin approval.

## Requirements before you start

Confirm these before troubleshooting:

1. Update Chrome from the Play Store.
2. Run Android 12 or higher with at least 4 GB RAM (Google’s stated baseline for the rollout).
3. Set the device language to English (US or UK English in current waves).
4. Sign in to Chrome with a personal Google Account that is eligible for Gemini in Chrome.
5. For Auto Browse: active Google AI Pro or Ultra subscription, and opt-in to Gemini in Chrome.

If the icon is missing, the feature has not reached your account yet—there is no manual force-enable flag on stable.

## Open Gemini in Chrome and summarize a page

1. Open Chrome and load the page you want help with.
2. Tap the Gemini sparkle icon to the right of the address bar, or open the three-dot menu and choose the Gemini entry.
3. The sheet appears. The current page is already available as context; you can turn this off in settings if you prefer.
4. Tap the **Summarize page** chip (or type “Summarize this page”).
5. Expand the result or ask a follow-up in the same sheet.

You can slide the sheet down so it stays docked at the bottom while you continue reading.

## Ask questions that stay grounded on the page

After the summary, type a specific question:

- “What is the main claim in the second section?”
- “List the three action items mentioned.”
- “Does this article contradict the earlier statement about pricing?”

Gemini uses the visible page content plus any connected apps you have enabled. For broader research, open additional tabs and add them as context the same way you do on desktop.

Personal Intelligence (Calendar, Keep, etc.) is controlled from the tools menu inside the Gemini sheet. Turn individual apps on or off as needed. See our earlier walkthrough on [connecting apps to Gemini on web and Android](/blog/connect-apps-gemini-web-android/) for the full settings path.

## Start an Auto Browse task (Pro / Ultra only)

Auto Browse is the agentic layer. On Android it appears for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in supported regions (including recent expansion to India).

1. Open the Gemini sheet as above.
2. Describe one clear multi-step outcome, including constraints. Example: “Find three hotels in Mumbai for 12–14 November under ₹12,000 a night with free cancellation. Compare ratings and location. Do not book anything.”
3. Submit. Gemini returns a plan that lists the sites it intends to use and any personal details it may share.
4. Read the plan. Edit if dates, budget, or sites are wrong.
5. Tap **Start Task**. A dedicated Auto Browse tab opens, marked so you can see activity.
6. Approve or correct each confirmation prompt. Gemini pauses before form submission, payment, or other sensitive steps.

You remain responsible for the final action. Pause or stop the task at any time from the sheet.

Daily limits apply (Google has stated 20 tasks for Pro and higher for Ultra on desktop; mobile follows the same account tiers). Safe Browsing must be set to Standard or Enhanced.

## Safety and control tips

- Review every plan before starting. The most common failure is an incorrect site or date that you could have corrected in the plan step.
- Keep the Auto Browse tab visible for high-stakes tasks.
- Turn off page-context sharing if you do not want the current tab included by default.
- Replace the toolbar shortcut if you prefer a different action: long-press the Gemini icon and choose Edit shortcut.
- For image generation inside the sheet, use the Nano Banana entry; results stay in the conversation until you save them.

Auto Browse is designed to detect known prompt-injection patterns and still asks for explicit confirmation on sensitive steps. Treat it as an assistant that can make mistakes, not an unsupervised operator.

## Conclusion

Gemini in Chrome on Android is the fastest way to get a page summary or grounded answer without switching apps. For eligible Pro and Ultra accounts, Auto Browse extends that to multi-step web tasks once you approve the plan. Update Chrome, confirm language and account eligibility, and start with a simple summarize request before moving to longer Auto Browse jobs.
