Google rolled out a substantial October 29, 2026 update for Gemini for Home and the Google Home app. The most noticeable change fixes speakers and displays that randomly said “Sorry, I didn’t understand” even when nobody had spoken.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="196" />

This early-access patch also improves music room transfers, timer handling, YouTube TV commands, and volume controls. Updates are rolling out now for eligible households.

If you already have Gemini for Home enabled, follow these steps to confirm the update and test the fixes. For initial setup, see our [Gemini for Home Nest speakers guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-gemini-for-home-nest-speakers/).

## What the October 29 Update Fixes

Google’s official changelog lists six targeted Gemini for Home improvements.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="196" />

- Unprompted “Sorry, I didn’t understand” responses on speakers and displays are stopped.
- Music transfers between rooms work more reliably. Phrases such as “move Mozart to the living room” or “bring the music here” are understood better.
- Gemini distinguishes media sleep timers, regular timers, and alarms more accurately.
- YouTube TV voice commands on smart displays start the requested content more consistently.
- Volume changes aimed at another room no longer affect the device you are speaking to when the target is unavailable.
- Zero-volume timers and alarms are corrected so they match the device’s normal volume.

The same release adds Matter energy data and improved door/window icons on Android, plus camera and firmware fixes. Gemini for Home remains in early access, so not every household receives it immediately.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="196" />

## Confirm the Update on Your Devices

### Step 1: Update the Google Home app

Open the Google Play Store or App Store and update the Google Home app to the latest version. The changelog notes that some app improvements ship with recent versions.

Restart the app after updating.

### Step 2: Check firmware on speakers and displays

In the Google Home app:

1. Tap a speaker or display.
2. Tap the settings gear.
3. Look for a firmware or software update option and install if available.

Compatible devices include the Google Home Speaker (2026), Nest Hub (2nd gen), Nest Audio, Nest Mini (2nd gen), Nest Hub Max, and earlier Nest Hub and Home models for most features. Gemini Live requires newer hardware and Google Home Premium.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="192" />

Rollouts can take hours to days. If the bug still appears, wait and check again later.

### Step 3: Verify early-access status

Gemini for Home is still early access. In the Google Home app go to your profile → Home settings and look for Early Access or Gemini options. Eligible users in supported countries (including the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and several European nations) can opt in or receive invitations.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="192" />

## Test the Key Fixes

After the update appears active, try these commands:

**Unprompted replies**  
Leave the room quiet for several minutes. The speaker or display should stay silent instead of randomly saying “Sorry, I didn’t understand.”

**Music transfer**  
Play music on one speaker, then say: “Hey Google, move the music to the living room” or “bring the music here.”

**Timers**  
Set a regular timer, a media sleep timer, and an alarm. Confirm each is handled correctly and that volume is audible (not zero).

**Cross-room volume**  
From one room say: “Hey Google, turn the volume down on the kitchen speaker.” Only the target device should change if it is online.

**YouTube TV** (on smart displays)  
Say: “Hey Google, play YouTube TV” or a specific show name. The content should start reliably.

## Additional App and Matter Improvements

The October 29 notes also cover the Google Home app:<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="196" />

- Google Home Premium subscribers can view features and benefits from the profile menu.
- Android users see more accurate icons and controls for Matter garage doors, gates, windows, blinds, and cabinets.
- Supported Matter smart plugs and energy monitors now show real-time power (mW) and cumulative energy (kWh) on device tiles.
- Camera live view is smoother on foldable Android phones; event timeline scrolling is more reliable on iOS.

Firmware updates for Chromecast with Google TV, Nest Wifi, and various speakers include security patches, better Wi-Fi 7 compatibility, and audio improvements.

## Tips If the Bug Persists

- Restart the speaker or display by unplugging it for 30 seconds.
- Confirm the device is on the same Wi-Fi network and linked to the correct Google account.
- Check that microphone access is enabled and that Do Not Disturb or similar modes are off.
- Wait for the staged rollout; Google notes that features may take time to reach everyone even after the app is updated.
- Submit feedback in the Google Home app under Help & feedback if the unprompted replies continue after a few days.

Households that have not yet switched to Gemini for Home can still benefit from the app and firmware side of the update. The voice-assistant fixes apply specifically to early-access Gemini for Home users.

## Conclusion

The October 29, 2026 Google Home update directly addresses one of the more unsettling Gemini for Home behaviors—speakers talking when no one has spoken—while tightening music, timer, volume, and YouTube TV reliability. Update the app, check firmware, and test the listed commands to confirm the changes on your devices.

For the full official notes, visit Google’s [What’s new in Google Home](https://support.google.com/googlehome/answer/15962877) page. Early-access users should see the improvements continue to roll out over the coming days.
