Google Labs launched Tabletop on November 11, 2026. It is a new experiment that turns prompts into shared interactive tools groups can edit together in real time.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="242" />

Friends planning a road trip, a rec league setting brackets, or a family organizing a potluck often bounce between chats, spreadsheets, and polls. Tabletop puts those pieces in one space. You add links, files, and ideas. Then you prompt @tabletop or pick a starter template. The result is a custom tool everyone can use at once, much like a Google Doc.

![Friends planning a trip with a map](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1758272960281-46b65da6336c?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What Tabletop Does

Tabletop creates customizable collaborative spaces. Groups generate trip itineraries, dinner polls, expense splitters, and tournament brackets. Examples from Google include an interactive pickleball league bracket, brunch options along a road-trip route, and a spinner that decides who walks the dog.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="242" />

Users can also build seating charts where name badges drag between tables, chore charts, or even simple games. Once built, the tool stays editable. Anyone in the space can ask @tabletop to change rules, add elements, or update details without starting over.

The experience is real-time. After you share an invite link in a group chat, members join and work simultaneously. Google describes it as working together in one place instead of coordinating across multiple apps.

Access is limited. Tabletop rolls out via waitlist to people aged 18 and older in the United States. Sign up at labs.google/tabletop. Google has not announced pricing, a global launch date, or which model powers the tool.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="243" />

## How to Join the Waitlist

1. Open a browser and go to labs.google/tabletop.
2. Confirm you are 18 or older and located in the United States.
3. Enter the required details to join the waitlist.
4. Wait for access. Google is expanding invitations each week. Early reports indicate accepted users receive a limited number of invites they can share.

Work and school accounts may not be supported. Keep an independent copy of important plans outside Tabletop while it remains an experiment.

## How to Set Up and Use a Space

Once you have access:

1. Create a new space.
2. Add links, files, and ideas the group has already collected. These can include maps, restaurant lists, player names, or expense records.
3. Prompt @tabletop with a clear request, or select a starter template. Examples:
   - "Make an interactive bracket for our pickleball league."
   - "Find some brunch options along our road trip route."
   - "Build a tool we can use to decide who walks the dog."
4. Review the generated tool. Ask @tabletop to revise it if needed.
5. Share the invite link in your group chat. Members can join and edit at the same time.

The space supports live updates. Changes appear for everyone without manual syncing. You can continue prompting for adjustments as plans evolve.

## Practical Examples

**Recreational sports league.** Prompt for a bracket that includes team names, matchups, and scores. Members can update results live. Google showed this with pickleball teams such as Smash Kings and Baseline Bandits.

**Road trip.** Drop in a route map and restaurant links. Ask for brunch stops that fit the path. The group can vote or adjust stops together.

**Household tasks.** Create a spinner or chart for chores. One person prompts for the initial tool; others spin or mark tasks complete.

**Event seating.** Build a drag-and-drop seating chart. If someone ends up next to the boss, prompt to rearrange badges.

These tools remain interactive. The group does not rely on one person to update a static document.

## Tips for Better Results

- Write specific prompts. Include the names of people, teams, or places involved.
- Start with a template if you are unsure how to phrase the request.
- Add source material first. Links and files give @tabletop better context.
- Share the invite early so the group can test and refine before the event.
- Export or copy key details elsewhere. Labs experiments can change.

Tabletop works best when the whole group participates. One person sets up the space; everyone else contributes ideas and votes.

## Limitations to Know

Tabletop is a Labs experiment, not a finished product. Features may change. Access is US-only for now and restricted to adults. Google has not disclosed the underlying model or any usage limits. Pricing is unconfirmed.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="246" />

It does not replace every planning app. For complex budgets or legal records, keep official copies outside the space. For quick group decisions, it reduces the usual friction of scattered chats and abandoned polls.

## Conclusion

Tabletop offers a practical way for groups to build the exact tools they need on the spot. Sign up for the waitlist at labs.google/tabletop if you are eligible. Test it on a small plan first—such as a weekend potluck or club matchup—to see how the shared space performs.

The experiment shows Google applying real-time collaboration to AI-generated tools. For groups tired of coordinating across five different apps, it provides a single place to plan and decide together.
