Google is shifting how K-12 schools control AI tools. Starting in early 2027, Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search will be off by default for student accounts. Administrators must explicitly turn them on.

This change follows feedback from schools, teachers, researchers, and policymakers. It also introduces Guided Learning in more places so students work through problems instead of receiving direct answers.

School leaders now face clearer choices. They can enable individual products or keep AI off. The trade-off is that Search cannot be used without its AI features.

## What changes for K-12 student accounts

Access to Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search will stay off until an administrator turns each one on. Google said this applies to K-12 student accounts.

Chris Phillips, Vice President and General Manager of Education at Google, wrote that the company is updating controls after listening to schools and educators. The goal is to give administrators more control over AI in classrooms.

Schools can turn on one, two, or all of the services individually. However, Google Search for students will include AI Mode and AI Overviews. Phillips told The New York Times that “Search and AI are deeply integrated.” Schools that fully restrict AI will not have Search. Students will see a message that it is unavailable from their school.

Google later clarified on X that the changes give schools more choice. Administrators control which services are active. AI features remain part of Search when it is enabled.

These controls roll out after Google shares more details with schools in the coming months. The timing gives districts time to prepare.

## Guided Learning arrives in Classroom and Search

Guided Learning is now available globally in Google Classroom. Teachers assign activities that act as one-to-one study tools. The experience is anchored in class materials.

It coaches students with open-ended questions and step-by-step breakdowns. Students stay on task. Teachers receive insights into progress.

Google is also building Guided Learning into Search for student accounts. When a query looks like homework help—such as a pasted physics problem or a request for help with a book report—Search will walk the student through concepts. It does not jump straight to the final answer.

A pilot starts in select U.S. schools later this month. Google will collect educator feedback before wider expansion.

Research supports the approach. In a University of Tokyo study of more than 300 students studying statistics, those using Guided Learning gained 8.1 percentage points. Students using only standard web tools gained 5.3 points. That is roughly 1.5 times higher learning gains in a single half-day session.

A separate UK pilot with Ark across three secondary schools also informed the Classroom version.

## How administrators and teachers can prepare

School administrators should review current Google Workspace for Education settings. Confirm which student accounts currently have Gemini or Search access.

1. Check the Admin console for Gemini, Classroom, and Search controls. Note which services are on by default today.
2. Decide policy for each product. Options include turning Gemini on with Guided Learning, enabling Search with AI Mode, or leaving services off.
3. Communicate with teachers and families. Explain what students will see if a service is off and how Guided Learning works when it is on.
4. Monitor the pilot and early Classroom rollout. Collect feedback from teachers on Guided Learning assignments.
5. Prepare for the early 2027 default-off change. Document decisions so the transition is smooth when the controls fully take effect.

Teachers can start using Guided Learning in Classroom now. Assign activities based on existing materials. Review student chats for insights into misconceptions or areas that need extra support.

Parents and students outside school accounts can still try Guided Learning in the Gemini app. It works as an interactive study partner that asks questions rather than handing over answers.

## Practical tips for effective use

- Treat Guided Learning as a coaching tool. Prompt it with “help me understand” rather than “give me the answer.”
- Ground Classroom activities in specific lesson materials so responses stay relevant.
- Use teacher insights to adjust instruction. Look for common points where students struggle.
- Combine with existing NotebookLM workflows for research. See our guide on [adding free textbooks to Gemini Notebook](/blog/add-free-textbooks-gemini-notebook/) for related organization tips.
- Test the Search pilot if your school is selected. Provide feedback on whether step-by-step guidance supports learning without removing necessary research tools.
- Document school policy clearly. Students need to know when Search or Gemini will be available and what message they will see if it is restricted.

These steps help schools balance access and learning goals. The controls do not remove AI from Search when the service is on, but they restore administrator choice on whether the products are available at all.

## Conclusion

Google’s November 2026 updates give K-12 schools explicit opt-in control over Gemini, NotebookLM, Gemini in Classroom, and Search. The change takes effect after further details are shared, with full default-off behavior expected in early 2027.

Guided Learning expands to Classroom globally and enters a Search pilot. It aims to support productive struggle rather than shortcut answers. Research from the University of Tokyo and UK school pilots shows measurable learning gains when students engage step by step.

Administrators should review settings, set clear policy, and prepare communications now. Teachers can begin assigning Guided Learning activities immediately. The result is more control for schools and tools designed for deeper understanding when AI is enabled.
