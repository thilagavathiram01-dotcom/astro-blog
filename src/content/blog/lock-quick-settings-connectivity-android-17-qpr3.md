Pixel owners on Android 17 QPR3 Beta 2 now have a practical way to stop unauthorized changes to key connectivity settings from the lock screen. The new toggle blocks Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Airplane mode, and mobile data tiles until you unlock the device. This closes a long-standing gap that let anyone swipe down and disable tracking on a locked phone.

Google rolled the beta out to eligible Pixel devices on October 20, 2026. The setting is off by default, so you enable it manually. It works with your existing unlock method, including fingerprint or face unlock. Other Quick Settings tiles such as flashlight and battery saver stay available without unlocking.

## Why this feature matters

Thieves have long used the Quick Settings panel on locked phones to turn on Airplane mode. That action takes the device offline and prevents Find My Device or similar tools from locating it. The new lock requires an unlock before those connectivity controls respond. It adds a small but meaningful barrier without changing how you use the phone day to day.

The option appears in Android 17 QPR3 Beta 2 builds. Reports from early testers confirm the toggle lives under lock screen or device unlock settings. Google’s official release notes focus on bug fixes and do not list the feature by name, so expect the label or location to shift in later builds.

## How to enable Lock Quick Settings connectivity

Follow these steps on a Pixel running Android 17 QPR3 Beta 2.

1. Open the Settings app.
2. Go to Display & touch, then Lock screen. Alternatively open Security & privacy, then Device unlock.
3. Look for the toggle labeled Lock Quick Settings connectivity or Lock connectivity settings in Quick Settings.
4. Turn the switch on.
5. Test it: lock the phone, swipe down to open Quick Settings, and try to toggle Airplane mode. The phone should ask you to unlock first.

The change takes effect immediately. You can turn it off the same way if you prefer the previous behavior.

## Get the beta on your Pixel

Android 17 QPR3 Beta 2 is available through the Android Beta for Pixel program. Supported devices include the Pixel 6a through the Pixel 11 Pro Fold series, plus the Pixel Tablet and Pixel Fold.

Visit [google.com/android/beta](https://www.google.com/android/beta), sign in with the Google account on your phone, and opt in the eligible device. After enrollment, open Settings > System > Software updates and check for the update. Back up your data first. Beta software can contain bugs, and leaving the program later usually requires a data wipe.

Build number DP11.261002.005 carries the October 5, 2026 security patch. Developers can also flash factory images or use the Android Flash Tool if they prefer a clean install.

## Tips for beta use

Keep the feature enabled if you want the extra protection. It does not affect normal unlocked use. If you frequently hand your locked phone to others for quick checks, leave it off so they can still adjust connectivity.

Report any issues with the toggle through the Android Beta feedback tools or the issue tracker. Early builds often refine the exact wording and menu placement.

## Limitations and next steps

This remains a beta feature. The toggle may move, rename, or disappear before the stable Android 17 QPR3 release expected in early 2027. Test it on a secondary device if possible.

The lock applies only to the listed connectivity tiles. Other Quick Settings controls remain accessible from the lock screen. Combine this setting with a strong screen lock, Find My Device, and the Android Beta feedback loop for the best result.

## Conclusion

Lock Quick Settings connectivity on Android 17 QPR3 Beta 2 gives Pixel users a simple way to protect tracking and connectivity controls on a locked device. Enable the toggle in Lock screen settings, test the behavior, and decide whether it fits your daily habits. Stay enrolled in the beta if you want to follow refinements through the QPR3 cycle.
