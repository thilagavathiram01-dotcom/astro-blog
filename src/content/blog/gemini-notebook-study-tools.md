Gemini Notebook (the product formerly called NotebookLM) is useful when you already have sources and want answers that stay inside those sources. Google's September 15, 2026 product update adds study tools that work on that same idea: talk to a notebook, record a lecture into it, then turn the material into reports, quizzes, and short videos.

This guide walks through the consumer workflow using Google's official announcement and Workspace help pages. Features are rolling out in stages. Treat availability notes as part of the setup, not as optional fine print.

## What changed in September 2026

Google is rolling out a refreshed Gemini Notebook experience plus these study tools:

- **Real-time voice conversations** with a notebook in the Android and iOS apps, in nearly 100 languages
- **Mobile audio recording** for lectures and spoken notes, stored next to other sources
- **Interactive learning overviews** under Reports, combining a written summary with studio outputs such as infographics, quizzes, and flashcards
- **More quiz formats** soon: short answer, multiple select, and fill in the blank
- **Short Video Overviews** of about 60 seconds in more than 80 languages

Voice chat responses are grounded in the sources you added to that notebook. You can interrupt and ask follow-up questions while speaking.

## Who gets which feature, and when

Google published rollout notes with the announcement:

- **Voice chat:** rolling out this week to Google AI Ultra subscribers who are 18 or older; coming to Google AI Pro and other users later
- **Audio recorder:** supported on web and mobile for users (including under 18) when English is the selected output language in settings; Google said recording starts rolling out the week after the announcement
- **Interactive learning overviews:** rolling out to all users in the following few weeks
- **New quiz formats:** same window as interactive overviews

If a control is missing on your account, check the plan, age gate, language setting, and whether the mobile app is updated. Do not assume a missing button is a bug on day one of a staged rollout.

Workspace admins can still turn Gemini Notebook on or off for their organization. Personal accounts use [notebook.google.com](https://notebook.google.com/) and the official mobile apps.

## Step-by-step: build a study notebook that can talk back

### 1. Create one notebook per course or topic

1. Open [Gemini Notebook](https://notebook.google.com/) and sign in.
2. Create a new notebook named after a single course, exam, or project.
3. Add only sources you want the model to use: PDFs, websites, YouTube videos, audio, Google Docs, or Google Slides.

Keep one subject per notebook. Mixing calculus homework with a travel itinerary makes citations harder to trust and quizzes less useful.

Google's Workspace help pages list current notebook and source caps by access tier. Free and standard access is tighter (for example, fewer sources per notebook and fewer chats per day) than paid AI plans. Limits can change; check the live help article if you hit a cap.

### 2. Capture lectures and handwritten notes

On mobile you can already photograph worksheets and handwritten notes. When the audio recorder arrives on your account:

1. Open the Gemini Notebook app on [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.labs.language.tailwind) or [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gemini-notebook/id6737527615).
2. Start a recording during a lecture or while you talk through a problem.
3. Save the clip into the notebook so it sits beside PDFs and Docs.

Google says those recordings can be edited later, cited in chat, and used as source material for other studio tools. Confirm local classroom or workplace rules before you record other people.

### 3. Use voice chat for source-grounded explanations

When voice chat is enabled on your mobile app:

1. Open the notebook that holds the lecture notes or textbook chapters.
2. Start a voice conversation instead of typing.
3. Ask for a step-by-step walkthrough of one concept, then interrupt when a term is unclear.
4. Ask the notebook to point back to the source it used.

A useful prompt pattern is narrow: "Explain section 3 of the uploaded syllabus in simpler language, then give me two practice questions from that section only." Broad prompts such as "teach me biology" waste chat quota and pull from the whole pile.

Voice chat is not a substitute for reading the primary source. It is a way to quiz yourself against material you already collected.

### 4. Build an interactive learning overview

Under **Reports**, create an interactive learning overview when the control appears on your account. Google describes these overviews as a way to weave a summary together with studio outputs such as infographics, quizzes, and flashcards.

A practical sequence:

1. Finish adding the week's sources before you generate the report.
2. Generate the written overview first.
3. Add only the studio pieces you will actually use (a quiz plus flashcards is enough for most exam weeks).
4. After you complete a quiz or flashcard deck, ask notebook chat what you missed and where to study next.
5. Edit weak quiz questions. Google notes that you can add or change questions instead of accepting the first draft.

New formats (short answer, multiple select, fill in the blank) are scheduled after the first interactive-overview rollout.

### 5. Share a Short Video Overview when a slide deck is too much

Short Video Overviews are roughly one-minute clips that mix a spoken overview with educational animation. Google says they work in more than 80 languages and can be shared with classmates.

Use them for formulas, diagrams, and lab concepts that are easier to see than to read. Do not treat a 60-second clip as a complete study guide. Keep the notebook and the citations as the source of truth.

## A concrete study loop

Suppose you have a midterm on introductory circuits.

1. Create a notebook called "Circuits midterm."
2. Upload the lecture slides, the problem set PDF, and a public YouTube recitation that matches the syllabus.
3. Record office-hours notes into the same notebook when the recorder is available.
4. On the commute, use voice chat to walk through Kirchhoff's laws using only those sources.
5. Generate an interactive overview with a quiz and flashcards.
6. After the quiz, ask chat which source pages cover the items you missed.
7. If a classmate needs a recap, share a Short Video Overview rather than the entire notebook.

That loop stays inside material you chose. It is the same reason to pick Gemini Notebook over a general chatbot for exam prep.

## Student plan notes (verify before you rely on them)

Google's announcement includes time-limited student offers:

- Eligible U.S. college students can claim one year of Google AI Pro (listed at $19.99 per month after the trial unless cancelled). Redeem by December 31, 2026. A valid payment method is required at sign-up. Google says this unlocks 4x higher Gemini Notebook usage limits versus non-AI subscribers.
- Eligible college students in 140+ markets outside the U.S. (with listed exclusions) can claim one year of Google AI Plus. After the trial the plan bills $4.99 per month or local equivalent unless cancelled. Google says this unlocks 2x higher notebook limits versus non-AI subscribers.

Offer terms live on Google's student AI page. Eligibility, price, and exclusions can change. Cancel before the trial ends if you do not want the paid plan.

## Limits that keep the notebook honest

- **Grounding is only as good as the upload.** A missing chapter will not appear in a voice explanation.
- **Age and plan gates are real.** Voice chat starts with Ultra users 18+; some studio tools stay limited for users under 18.
- **Language settings matter** for recording.
- **Daily caps apply** to chats, reports, quizzes, flashcards, audio overviews, and video overviews.
- **AI can still be wrong.** Open the cited source before you memorize an explanation.

## Conclusion

Gemini Notebook's September 2026 study tools are useful when you treat the notebook as a closed set of sources. Add the week's materials, record what you need to keep, talk through hard sections on the phone, then generate an interactive overview and a short quiz from the same pile.

Start at [notebook.google.com](https://notebook.google.com/) or the official mobile apps. Check your plan and language setting if voice chat or recording is not visible yet. Use the report and video tools as study aids, not as replacements for the original slides and problem sets.
