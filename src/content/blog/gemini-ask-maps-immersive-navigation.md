Google Maps no longer stops at a pin and a blue line. In March 2026, Google launched **Ask Maps**, a Gemini-powered chat that answers messy, real-world questions about places, and **Immersive Navigation**, a rebuilt driving view that uses Street View and aerial imagery to show the road ahead.

Both features started in the U.S. (Ask Maps also launched in India). In August 2026 Google expanded Ask Maps in English to Australia, Brazil, Canada, Indonesia, Japan, Mexico, and more than 150 other countries and territories, and added food ordering, real-time transit, and optional Personal Intelligence.

This guide covers what Google has documented: how to open Ask Maps, prompts that match official examples, how Immersive Navigation changes a drive, and which permissions to leave off until you need them.

## What Ask Maps actually does

Ask Maps is a conversation inside Google Maps, not a separate Gemini chat. You tap **Ask Maps** (under the search bar on mobile) and type or speak a question that a keyword search cannot answer well.

Google’s own examples include:

- “My phone is dying — where can I charge it without having to wait in a long line for coffee?”
- “Is there a public tennis court with lights on that I can play at tonight?”
- “I’m headed to the Grand Canyon, Horseshoe Bend and Coral Dunes — any recommended stops along the way?”
- “My friends are coming from Midtown East to meet me after work. Any spots with a cozy aesthetic and a table for 4 at 7 tonight?”

Maps then builds a customized map of options. Google says the answers draw on information about more than 300 million places and reviews from a community of more than 500 million contributors. Results can also reflect places you have searched for or saved.

From a result you can book a reservation, save a place to a list, share it, or start navigation. You still confirm bookings and payments yourself.

## How to turn on Ask Maps

Availability is region- and language-dependent. If the button is missing, update the app and wait for the rollout rather than hunting for a hidden flag.

1. Update **Google Maps** from the Play Store or App Store.
2. Sign in with the Google account you use for saved places.
3. Open Maps. Look for **Ask Maps** under the search bar.
4. Grant location permission while using the app if you want “near me” answers.
5. Start with one concrete question that includes time, constraint, and place type.

Ask Maps is on Android and iOS. Google said desktop would follow the original U.S. and India launch.

Personal Intelligence is separate and **off by default**. In the August 2026 update, connecting Gmail lets Ask Maps factor in an upcoming flight or dinner reservation. Calendar support was listed as coming next. Connect Gmail only if you want Maps to read those messages; you can disconnect the apps later.

## Prompts that work

Vague prompts produce generic lists. Official and regional posts point to prompts that name a constraint and a next action.

**Constraint + place type**  
“Quiet cafe near Union Station that is open after 8 p.m. and has indoor seating.”

**Trip with stops**  
“Plan a drive between the Grand Canyon, Horseshoe Bend, and Coral Dunes. Suggest one lunch stop and one short hike.”

**Group logistics**  
“Four people, table at 7, midway between Midtown East and my saved office pin, cozy indoor seating.”

**Travel gap (needs Personal Intelligence + Gmail)**  
“Give me ideas on how to spend a few hours near the hotel before my flight tomorrow.”

**Transit**  
Google’s example: “Is there a ferry to get to Taronga Zoo with views of the Opera House?” Ask Maps can show a virtual departure board that updates if a service is delayed.

**Food order (agentic, partner-limited)**  
“Order spicy pad kee mao with seafood for me to pick up on my way home.” Google says Ask Maps finds open restaurants along the route, can use saved places and dietary notes, and can add the dish to a cart for you to review. Food ordering rolled out with Square and Toast; Uber Eats was listed as coming soon.

Follow-up questions stay in the same thread. Google also documented conversation memory: you can ask “Remind me, what activities did you suggest for my trip to Seattle?” and pick up an earlier plan.

Review every cart and reservation screen. An agent that can add a dish still cannot know if the restaurant accepted the order or if the price is what you expected.

## Immersive Navigation: what changes on the drive

Immersive Navigation is the driving UI overhaul Google called its biggest navigation update in more than a decade. It started rolling out in the U.S. on 12 March 2026, with expansion planned to eligible iOS and Android phones, CarPlay, Android Auto, and cars with Google built-in.

Gemini models help Maps read fresh Street View and aerial photos so the 3D view can show buildings, overpasses, terrain, lanes, crosswalks, traffic lights, stop signs, landmarks, and medians when those details help a turn or merge.

Google listed three practical upgrades:

**A wider look ahead.** Smart zooms and transparent buildings show the next junction earlier. Voice guidance is more specific. Google’s example: if you should skip an exit, you may hear “Go past this exit and take the next one for Illinois 43 South,” with the next step highlighted on the map.

**Clearer tradeoffs.** Maps already ingests more than 5 million traffic updates per second, according to Google. Immersive Navigation can explain an alternate: longer with less traffic, or faster with a toll. Community reports — Google cites more than 10 million driver contributions per day — feed alerts for construction and crashes.

**The last block.** Before you leave you can preview the destination in Street View and get parking suggestions. On approach, Maps can highlight the building entrance, nearby parking, and which side of the street to use.

## How to use Immersive Navigation on a real trip

1. Start a driving route in Google Maps as usual.
2. Keep the app in the foreground until the 3D immersive view appears on an eligible device.
3. Glance at highlighted lanes and signs before the turn, not only at the distance-to-turn number.
4. When Maps offers an alternate, read the tradeoff (time, traffic, toll) before you tap it.
5. Near the destination, watch for entrance and parking callouts instead of hunting for the address number on the facade.

If the 3D view never appears, the device or region is not in the current wave. Classic turn-by-turn still works. Do not disable location or background traffic if you want disruption alerts.

Use the same caution you would with any navigation overlay. Immersive visuals are extra context. They do not replace road signs or local traffic law.

## Privacy and control

Ask Maps gets more useful as you connect more context. That is also when you should slow down.

- Saved places and search history already personalize recommendations. You do not need Gmail for basic prompts.
- Personal Intelligence is opt-in. Google states that connecting apps is off by default and that you choose which apps to connect.
- Food ordering stops at a reviewable cart. Complete payment in the partner flow yourself.
- Location access should stay “while using the app” unless you have a separate reason for always-on location.

If you share a phone, keep Personal Intelligence off. A brief that mentions a flight from Gmail is helpful on a personal device and a leak on a shared one.

Ask Maps sits next to other Gemini surfaces on Android. If you already use multi-step tasks or Chrome Auto Browse, treat Maps the same way: give a narrow command, watch the confirmation, and keep payment steps in your hands. See the setup notes in [How to Use Gemini Intelligence on Android](/blog/gemini-intelligence-android/).

## Tips that save time

- Name the clock time and party size in the first prompt so Ask Maps does not guess.
- Save Home and Work so “on my way home” has a real endpoint for food and route prompts.
- Use lists. After Ask Maps suggests stops, save the keepers instead of screenshotting the chat.
- Ask one follow-up at a time (“only places with bathrooms and outdoor seating”) instead of restating the whole trip.
- For transit questions, include the destination name Maps already knows. A well-known landmark beats a nickname.
- On a drive, preview parking before you leave if the last block is a one-way street or a garage-only venue.

## Conclusion

Ask Maps is Gemini grounded in Maps data: places, reviews, your saves, and — if you opt in — a connected inbox. Immersive Navigation is the same stack applied to the drive, with a wider 3D view, spoken tradeoffs, and last-block cues for parking and doors.

Start with one local constraint question and one familiar commute. Confirm that the Ask Maps button exists in your country and language, then decide whether Gmail is worth connecting. Leave food ordering and Personal Intelligence off until you have watched a full confirmation flow on a low-stakes order.
