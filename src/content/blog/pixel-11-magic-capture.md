Magic Capture is a Camera mode on Pixel 11 phones. You start a short recording. The phone keeps a video and, at the same time, picks stills from the action so you do not have to time a single shutter press.

Google announced it with the Pixel 11 lineup on 13 August 2026. Official help lists it on Pixel 11, Pixel 11 Pro, Pixel 11 Pro XL, and Pixel 11 Pro Fold. It is not documented on older Pixels.

This guide follows Google’s Camera help article and the Pixel blog posts. It is a user walkthrough, not a review of every sample photo on the internet.

## What Magic Capture actually does

Think of it as video-first capture with stills as a second product.

Google’s product posts say a typical session analyzes around **400 frames**. On-device processing and Gemini models look for a usable smile, a laugh, or a peak action frame. Output stills are described as **12-megapixel** photos. If you opt in, the phone can apply light edits such as crop, straighten, or unblur.

You also keep a video of the same session. You do not flip between Photo and Video mid-scene.

Google’s Made by Google demo used a basketball sequence: point the phone, start Magic Capture, and let the phone propose stills after the play. That is the intended job. It is a weak fit for a posed portrait where you already know the exact frame.

## Before you start

Confirm four things:

- You are on a **Pixel 11, 11 Pro, 11 Pro XL, or 11 Pro Fold**
- The **Camera** app is updated from Play Store
- The phone has **free storage**. Google’s help page says low storage can stop the session from saving smoothly
- You know whether you want **Video Boost** (Pro models only; details below)

Magic Capture writes both a clip and extra stills. A long session fills space faster than a normal photo burst.

## How to run a session

These steps match [Use Magic Capture on a Pixel phone](https://support.google.com/pixelcamera/answer/17402963) in Pixel Camera Help.

1. Open the **Camera** app.
2. On the mode slider at the bottom, select **Magic Capture**.
3. Point the phone at the scene. Hold it as steadily as you would for video.
4. Tap **Shutter** to start. The shutter shows a color animation while automatic capture is running.
5. If you see a frame you want right now, tap the extra shutter control on the right for a **manual snapshot**. Automatic picks still run.
6. Tap **Stop** when the moment is over.

Open the thumbnail or Google Photos to review the video and the stills the mode saved.

Start the recording *just before* the action (candles, kickoff, a pet jumping), then stop soon after. Leaving Magic Capture running for minutes burns storage and gives the picker more empty frames to sort.

## Make Magic Capture the default mode

If you use this more than Photo, Google’s help points to Camera control customization.

1. Open **Camera**.
2. Open Camera settings (gear, or the path your build uses for controls).
3. Find the option that sets the **starting mode** when Camera opens.
4. Choose **Magic Capture**.

You can still swipe back to Photo or Video for a posed shot or a long interview clip.

## Video Boost on Pro models

On Pixel 11 Pro, Pixel 11 Pro XL, and Pixel 11 Pro Fold you can pair Magic Capture with **Video Boost**. Google’s help says boosted video is recorded in **4K 10-bit HDR**.

Turn it on from inside the mode, not from a buried system menu:

1. Open Camera and switch to **Magic Capture**.
2. Tap **Magic Capture Settings** at the bottom left, or swipe up.
3. Turn on **Video Boost**.

Google’s tip on that page: Video Boost **must be enabled each time** you use Magic Capture. It does not stay on across sessions. Check the toggle before a clip you care about.

Video Boost is a quality path for the *video*. It is not required for the 12MP stills Magic Capture already produces on every Pixel 11.

## What to shoot — and what to skip

**Good first tests**

- A short burst of motion: kids, pets, sport, someone blowing out candles
- A scene where faces turn toward the lens for a second
- Action you would normally miss because you were watching the subject instead of the shutter

**Skip it for**

- A posed headshot you can time yourself
- A landscape with no motion
- A 20-minute recital where you only need one continuous video file and no extra stills

If the AI stills miss the frame you wanted, you still have the video. Pull a frame in Photos or keep the clip. Google does not claim Magic Capture replaces every manual shutter press.

## Storage, edits, and review habits

- Free space before a long outing. Help text is explicit that low storage hurts capture.
- Treat auto crop / unblur as optional. If a crop feels tight, keep the unedited still or the video frame.
- Review faces and timing before you share. A “peak action” pick can still be the wrong person in focus.
- Do not expect the same results on a Pixel 8 or 9. Official device list starts at Pixel 11.

## A 10-minute practice loop

1. Stand in good daylight with a moving subject (a friend walking, a pet).
2. Start Magic Capture, record 8–12 seconds, stop.
3. Compare the suggested stills with a frame you pick from the video yourself.
4. Run a second clip with a manual snapshot mid-session so you see both paths.
5. On a Pro phone, enable Video Boost for one clip and note that you must turn it on again next time.

That loop shows whether you prefer Magic Capture for events or whether Photo + a short Video is enough.

## Conclusion

Magic Capture is a Pixel 11 Camera mode that records a clip and proposes stills from the same take. Google documents it on every Pixel 11 model, with optional Video Boost (4K 10-bit HDR) on the Pro phones if you turn the toggle on for that session.

Use it when the moment is short and unpredictable. Keep Photo mode for shots you can time. Check storage, stop the recording when the action ends, and look at the stills before you send them.
