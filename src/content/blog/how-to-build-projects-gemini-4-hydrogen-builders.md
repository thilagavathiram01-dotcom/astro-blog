Google introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12, 2026 as a fast model for everyday coding and agentic work. Builders quickly put it to use. On November 23 the official blog highlighted six early projects that show what the model can produce in Google Antigravity, the Gemini app, and multi-model setups.

These demos range from Three.js cityscapes to interactive animations. They demonstrate Hydrogen’s speed on visual and code tasks when used alone or paired with Gemini 4 Carbon. This guide walks through each example, the tools involved, and practical steps you can take to start similar projects.

If you are new to the model, start with the [setup guide for Gemini 4 Hydrogen in the app, AI Studio, and API](/blog/how-to-use-gemini-4-hydrogen-app-studio-api/).

## Sci-Fi Journey Through a Rainy Skyline

@kimmonismus built a Three.js scene of a neon-lit futuristic city under rain. The project used Hydrogen at its high thinking level inside Google Antigravity. The result is a navigable skyline with reflections, lights, and atmospheric effects generated from prompts and iterative coding.

High thinking gives the model more steps to refine geometry, lighting, and camera paths. Antigravity’s agentic environment lets Hydrogen edit files, run previews, and adjust based on feedback without constant manual intervention.

![Neon-lit rainy city street at night](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1760165276068-22913e126b24?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

To try a similar build:
1. Open Google Antigravity (available to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers).
2. Select Gemini 4 Hydrogen and set the thinking level to high.
3. Prompt for a Three.js scene: describe camera movement, weather, and visual style.
4. Iterate by asking the model to fix lighting, add rain particles, or adjust colors based on the live preview.

The official demo video is available on the Google blog. Community videos on YouTube also compile similar early Hydrogen builds.

## Seaplane Flight from Morning to Night

@LuminaBench created a 3D seaplane that flies across water while the lighting shifts from daytime to night. The scene shows continuous time progression with corresponding changes in sky, reflections, and overall atmosphere.

Hydrogen handled the visual coding and timing logic. This type of project benefits from the model’s ability to generate coherent WebGL or Three.js code that maintains consistent world state across frames.

You can experiment with time-of-day effects in AI Studio or Antigravity by prompting for progressive lighting and then refining the animation loop.

## Blocky Voxel Koi Pond

@IamEmily2050 used Hydrogen directly in the Gemini app to build a 3D voxel-style koi pond. The scene includes a red pagoda, cherry blossoms, lantern-lit bridges, and swimming fish. Working in the app shows that Hydrogen can produce interactive 3D results even without a full coding IDE for some users.

Voxel aesthetics are forgiving for early iterations. The model can generate the geometry, materials, and simple animation scripts in a single conversation thread.

![Koi fish swimming in a pond](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1677293602097-d084a1507f6f?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

Try it:
- Open the Gemini app and select Hydrogen (or let auto-selection choose it when appropriate).
- Describe the scene in detail: “Create a 3D voxel koi pond with a red pagoda, cherry blossoms, and lantern bridges. Make the fish swim.”
- Ask for interactive controls or export the code for further editing in AI Studio.

## Jelly-Like Koi Pond

@notjazii took a softer approach with a squishy, gel-like koi pond instead of hard voxel edges. The project highlights Hydrogen’s flexibility with material properties and shading. Soft-body or fluid-like visual effects can be requested by specifying deformation, transparency, and lighting response.

Comparing the two pond styles shows how small prompt changes produce different aesthetics while the model handles the underlying 3D structure.

## Interactive Ruby-Red Moth

@thtbee_ paired Gemini 4 Carbon with Hydrogen in Google Antigravity. Carbon orchestrated a /swarm of Hydrogen workers to build an interactive crimson moth animation complete with atmospheric visuals and sound. Hydrogen then recorded the demo video.

This multi-model pattern uses Carbon for higher-level planning and coordination while Hydrogen executes the detailed coding and asset work. The /swarm feature distributes tasks across multiple Hydrogen instances under Carbon’s direction.

![Red moth on flowers](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1767967404154-9681ac526552?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

Steps for a multi-model build:
1. In Antigravity, start with Carbon for the overall plan.
2. Invoke /swarm and assign Hydrogen workers specific subtasks (geometry, animation, audio, interaction).
3. Review the combined output and ask Hydrogen to capture a walkthrough video.

## Desert Ranch Comparison Across Models

@Bhavani_00007 ran the same animated 3D desert ranch prompt on Gemini 3.8 Flash and on Hydrogen. Hydrogen completed the scene in under one minute and produced a more polished result. The comparison illustrates the speed and quality jump from the previous Flash-class model.

Desert environments test lighting, terrain, and object placement. Rapid iteration lets you refine cactus placement, building geometry, or day/night cycles quickly.

## Tips for Your Own Hydrogen Projects

- Choose the thinking level deliberately. Low or medium works for simple prompts; high or xhigh helps with complex 3D scenes and multi-step animation.
- Start in the Gemini app or AI Studio for quick prototypes, then move to Antigravity for agentic editing and multi-model workflows.
- Pair Hydrogen with Carbon when you need orchestration. Use Carbon to plan and Hydrogen to implement.
- Provide reference images or describe camera paths, lighting, and interaction clearly.
- Export code early and test in a local browser or Three.js setup to catch issues the preview might miss.
- Monitor token use. Hydrogen is priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens on the paid tier, with a 1 million-token context window.

Google notes that free users in the Gemini app receive Hydrogen via auto model selection when it fits the prompt. Paid Pro and Ultra subscribers get direct access in Antigravity and more consistent model choice.
