Google Finance left its long beta period on **June 25, 2026** with a dedicated **Android app**. The app is built for people who check markets several times a day: a watchlist, live quotes, a financial news feed, an AI research tool, and **Key Moments** that annotate why a ticker moved.

This is a research and tracking product, not a brokerage. You cannot place trades in it. Treat every AI summary as a starting point you still verify against filings and primary news.

Availability: the Android app and the redesigned web experience are live globally. An iOS app is planned later in 2026. Portfolio dashboards and scheduled AI tasks launched on the web first; Google said those pieces would follow on mobile in later months.

## Install and sign in

1. On Android, open [Google Finance on Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.finance) or search **Google Finance**.
2. Install the official app from Google LLC.
3. Sign in with the Google Account you already use for Search.
4. Grant notifications if you want headline and watchlist alerts.

If Play Store search is noisy, go to [google.com/finance](https://www.google.com/finance) in Chrome and follow the app banner when Google shows it.

## Build a watchlist you will actually open

The app is useful only if the first screen is *your* list, not the default market mosaic.

1. Search a ticker or company name (include the exchange when names collide, for example `NASDAQ:GOOG`).
2. Open the quote page.
3. Tap **Follow**.
4. Choose the default watchlist or a custom list.

Keep lists small. One list for holdings, one for research candidates, one for indexes and FX is easier than a single dump of 80 names.

On the web you can turn a custom watchlist into a **portfolio** (More → View as portfolio) and add share counts. That workflow is documented in [Google Search Help for portfolios](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/11330383). Until the same controls land in the Android app, use the website for cost basis and allocation views.

## Read Key Moments instead of staring at a line

**Key Moments** sit on the price chart. They are short AI notes that try to explain a daily move — an earnings print, a rate decision, a sector headline — instead of leaving you to reconstruct the story from a news tab.

How to use them well:

- Open a ticker you already follow, not a random mover.
- Scan Key Moments for *dates that matter to you*, then open the linked sources.
- Compare two days: a Key Moment after earnings should mention the print; if it only restates the percentage move, treat it as incomplete.

Key Moments are not a substitute for the 10-Q or the official press release. They compress public news. They can miss quiet sessions, over-weight one headline, or lag after-hours filings.

## Use the AI research tool with a tight prompt

Google’s June 2026 product post lists an **AI research tool** in the Android app. On the web, the same family of features includes longer answers and Deep Search for harder questions.

Useful prompts (grounded in *your* list, not generic market chat):

- “Which sectors on my watchlist moved more than the S&P 500 today, and what headlines are attached?”
- “Summarize the last earnings call themes for [ticker] using only linked sources.”
- “What is underrepresented if this watchlist were a portfolio?” (better on web once holdings exist)

Avoid: “What should I buy?” The product is not licensed advice, and the model will happily sound confident on a weak prompt.

## Scheduled tasks live on the web first

Google added **AI tasks**: you describe a recurring briefing in plain language, and Finance runs it in the background. Official example from the product post: a daily pre-market note on overnight moves.

Google said notifications can arrive in the **Google app** and in the Finance insights panel on the web. As of the June launch, creating and editing those tasks was a **web** workflow, with mobile support promised later. If you do not see a task composer in the Android app, that is expected — use [google.com/finance](https://www.google.com/finance) on desktop or Chrome.

A practical task to start with:

> Weekday 7:30 local time: summarize overnight moves for tickers on my default watchlist. Include only items with a published headline. Skip unchanged names.

Edit or pause tasks from the web insights panel so you do not accumulate stale alerts.

## What stays on the website for now

Google was explicit about the split:

| Feature | Android app at launch | Web (out of beta) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watchlists | Yes | Yes |
| Live quotes and news feed | Yes | Yes |
| AI research tool | Yes | Yes (plus longer Deep Search) |
| Key Moments | Yes | Yes |
| Custom portfolios and holdings | Coming later | Yes |
| Scheduled AI tasks | Coming later | Yes |
| Live earnings audio and transcripts | Coming later | Yes |

If you need allocation charts or earnings-day audio, bookmark the web app. Do not assume a missing button is a bug on your phone.

## Storage, privacy, and what not to paste

- Watchlists and portfolios are tied to your Google Account.
- Do not paste brokerage account numbers, tax IDs, or full trade blotters into the research box. Ticker + share *count* is enough for a portfolio on the web.
- Quotes can be delayed depending on the exchange. Confirm the timestamp on the quote page before you act anywhere else.
- Turning off notifications does not delete the watchlist.

## A 15-minute setup

1. Install the app and follow five names you already own or track.
2. Open one ticker that moved this week and read its Key Moments against the original article.
3. On the web, create a named portfolio and add share counts for those same names.
4. Create one scheduled task on the web with a narrow scope.
5. Disable the default market-wide push alerts if they drown the useful ones.

## Conclusion

The Google Finance Android app is the pocket version of a rebuilt research desk: lists, news, an AI research box, and Key Moments on the chart. Portfolios, scheduled briefings, and live earnings still belong on the website until Google finishes the mobile pass.

Use Key Moments to jump to a date. Use the research tool to summarize *your* list. Keep trading in the app that actually holds your cash.
