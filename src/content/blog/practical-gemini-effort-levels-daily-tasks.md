Google rolled out Low, Medium, and High effort levels across the Gemini app in October 2026. Free users now default to Auto model selection, while everyone gains these three controls that replace the old Extended toggle. The levels let you decide how thoroughly Gemini reasons before it answers.

Most free prompts still route to Flash-Lite for speed. Harder ones can reach Flash or Pro when Auto detects the need. Effort levels give you a practical lever on top of that routing. Higher effort improves depth but burns more of your compute-based limit. This guide shows concrete ways to apply the levels to everyday tasks so you waste less quota and get better output.

## What the Three Effort Levels Actually Do

Low prioritizes speed and efficiency. Gemini spends less internal reasoning time. Use it for straightforward requests where latency matters more than extra analysis.

Medium balances depth and speed. It is the practical default for most work. Responses take a bit longer than Low but stay responsive.

High is extra thorough. The model takes more time to plan and check its work. Answers are more careful, but they consume more of your limit and arrive slower. These options match the thinking levels already available in Google AI Studio and Antigravity.

Free accounts cannot manually pick Flash or Pro. Auto decides. Leaving smart model selection enabled is the only way harder prompts can escalate. If you turn it off, every response stays on Flash-Lite.

## Step-by-Step: Set Effort Levels in the Gemini App

Open the Gemini app on Android, iOS, or gemini.google.com and start a new chat. Confirm the model picker shows Auto if you are on a free account.

Look for the effort or thinking level control near the model area. Select Low, Medium, or High before you send the prompt. The control is per model or per conversation depending on the current rollout.

Send the prompt. Note the response time and quality. Change the level and resend a similar prompt to compare. Update the app if the control is missing.

Keep smart model selection on unless you specifically want every answer locked to Flash-Lite.

## Daily Tasks and the Right Effort Level

**Email and message drafts.** Use Low for quick replies and short rewrites. Switch to Medium when tone, structure, or multiple recipients matter. High is rarely needed unless the email is a complex proposal.

**Research summaries.** Medium works well for most article or document summaries. Move to High when you need the model to cross-check claims, note uncertainties, or produce a structured comparison. Break very long documents into chunks so one High prompt does not exhaust your limit.

**Code and technical fixes.** Start with Medium for typical debugging or snippet generation. Escalate to High only for multi-file logic, edge cases, or when the first answer misses the bug. Clear prompts that describe the expected behavior help Auto route correctly.

**Brainstorming and lists.** Low is usually enough. The model still produces useful options without deep reasoning. Medium helps if you want ranked suggestions or short justifications.

**Learning a new topic.** Medium for initial explanations. High when you want step-by-step reasoning, worked examples, or the model to flag common mistakes.

## Tips to Stay Within Free Limits

Write precise prompts. State the format, length, and what to avoid. Specific instructions reduce the need for High effort on simple tasks.

Test the same prompt at two levels. You will quickly learn which one is sufficient for each type of work.

Split large jobs. A series of Medium prompts often costs less total compute than one High prompt that rambles.

Check your usage in the app. Free limits are compute-based and refresh on a rolling window. Higher effort reaches the cap faster.

Update regularly. Google continues to refine Auto routing and the effort controls.

If your daily volume requires consistent Pro-level answers, review Google AI plan options. AI Pro multiplies standard limits by four. See the related guide on [how to use Gemini Auto model and effort levels](/blog/how-to-use-gemini-auto-model-effort-levels/) for the full plan breakdown.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Setting High for every prompt wastes quota and slows simple tasks. Most daily work succeeds on Medium or Low.

Turning off smart model selection removes the only path free users have to stronger models. Leave it on unless you have a specific reason to lock to Flash-Lite.

Expecting High to guarantee correctness. Extra reasoning helps, but you still need to verify important facts, code, or decisions.

Ignoring prompt quality. Effort levels amplify good instructions. Vague prompts produce vague answers regardless of the setting.

## Conclusion

The new effort levels give free Gemini users real control over response depth even when model choice is limited to Auto. Low keeps everyday lookups fast. Medium covers most productive work. High is reserved for the cases where thoroughness is worth the extra time and quota.

Match the level to the task, write clear prompts, and leave smart selection enabled. These habits extract better answers from the free plan without constant limit hits. Test the controls on your next few chats and adjust based on what you actually need.

The October 2026 changes are live for most accounts. Open Gemini, pick an effort level, and see the difference on a real task today.
