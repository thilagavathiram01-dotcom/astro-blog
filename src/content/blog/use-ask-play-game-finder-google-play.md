Google Play is no longer just a catalog. On 13 October 2026 the company added conversational tools that compare apps, recommend games based on how you actually play, and let anyone launch a private Android app without a developer account.

This guide walks through Ask Play comparisons, Game Finder with Gamer DNA, personal app launching, and the other discovery features rolling out now.

## Compare apps side-by-side with Ask Play

Ask Play already answered natural-language questions about apps. Starting 13 October it can generate a comparison between the apps it recommends. The comparison pulls from store listings and user reviews to highlight differences such as subscription models, offline support, connectivity requirements, and overall sentiment.

To use it:

1. Open the Google Play Store app.
2. Tap the Ask Play entry (usually near the top of the home or search screen, or via the search bar).
3. Type a question such as “compare budgeting apps that work offline” or “which note-taking app is better for teams?”
4. Review the results. When a compare option appears, tap it to see the side-by-side summary.
5. Tap any app name to open its full listing and install if it fits.

The feature is available today in supported markets. If the compare button is missing, update the Play Store app and try again later. Results stay inside Play; you do not leave the store to get the summary.

## Find games that match how you play with Game Finder

Game Finder recommends titles based on Gamer DNA rather than pure popularity. Gamer DNA is Google’s analysis of your gameplay habits, ratings, and the preferences you share. You can refine it by specifying genres, mechanics, art styles, and games you already like.

Once available (rolling out in English to U.S. users first):

1. Open Google Play and navigate to the Games section or search for Game Finder.
2. Review the initial recommendations and the explanation for each title.
3. Open the Gamer DNA settings (usually under your profile or the Game Finder panel) and add or remove preferences.
4. Check which followed profiles are playing a recommended game and read the summarized reviews.
5. Install directly or add the game to a Game Playlist.

Game Playlists let you curate collections. Sharing those lists and browsing others’ collections is coming soon. Game Finder expands to more markets after the U.S. English rollout.

## Launch a personal app without a developer account

Anyone 18 or older in select markets (including the U.S.) can now vibe-code an app in Google AI Studio and run it privately on their own device through Google Play. No professional developer account is required. The app stays separate from the public catalog.

Steps:

1. Open Google AI Studio and create or import your Android project.
2. Follow the prompts to generate or refine the app with AI assistance.
3. When ready, choose the option to launch as a personal app on Google Play.
4. Sign in with your regular Google account and install it on your device.
5. The app remains private. In the coming months you will be able to share it with a small circle of friends and family by invitation.

If you later want to publish publicly, Google Play guides you through upgrading to a professional developer account. At that point the app goes through the same Trust & Safety reviews as every other public listing.

## Other discovery and play updates

- Play in Gemini: Google Play apps, games, and developer content appear inside Gemini conversations, with direct install links. Rolling out to all Gemini users soon.
- Sprints: 48-hour competitive micro-challenges that match small groups of similar-skill players. Available in all markets.
- Sidekick Playbacks: On select devices the in-game overlay can record 30-second clips you can share without leaving the game.
- Community Posts: Ask and answer questions on any game’s detail page, with automatic translations. Developer posts arrive over the coming weeks.
- PC titles and Game Trials: Popular PC games such as Dave the Diver arrive on Play with cloud saves; Game Trials expand so you can sample full premium titles before buying.

Sports Topic Pages now cover major soccer leagues and link to live scores and streaming apps.

For developers building the apps that appear in these recommendations, see the [Android Studio Gemini guide](/blog/android-studio-quail-4-agent-mode/) if you want agent-assisted coding, or the official Play developer blog for listing best practices.

## Tips for better results

- Be specific in Ask Play questions. “Compare two offline map apps for hiking” produces clearer comparisons than “best maps.”
- Update Gamer DNA after you finish a few new games so recommendations stay accurate.
- Keep the Play Store and Gemini apps updated so new toggles and panels appear promptly.
- Personal apps are private by default; do not share sensitive data through them until you review permissions.
- If a feature is missing, check your country and language settings. Many of these tools start in the U.S. English and expand later.

## Conclusion

Ask Play comparisons and Game Finder shift discovery from scrolling ranked lists to asking questions and matching real preferences. Personal app launching removes the developer-account barrier for private experiments. Update the Play Store, try a comparison or Game Finder search if it has reached you, and watch for the remaining features as they expand.

The tools stay inside Google Play, so you keep the same security and update path you already use.
