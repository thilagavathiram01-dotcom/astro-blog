Googlebook reached shelves in the United States on October 4, 2026, and in Canada, the U.K., Ireland, France, Germany, and Australia on October 5. Magic Pointer is the feature Google built to replace a plain click with a short Gemini task. Wiggle the cursor, point at what is on screen, and ask for the next step without saving files and pasting them into a chat window.

Google describes Magic Pointer as a cursor that understands text, images, and context, and that stays off until you summon it. If you are still choosing a model, start with our [Googlebook launch comparison](/blog/choose-googlebook-october-4-launch/) and come back here for the pointer workflow.

## What Magic Pointer does

A quick wiggle wakes the pointer. Google says it then brings Gemini to the spot where you are working. Official examples cover three jobs:

- Highlight a training plan on a web page and ask Gemini to map the runs onto Google Calendar.
- Hover a suspicious email and ask Gemini to check the text and images for spam versus a real message.
- Select several images and ask Gemini to visualize them together, without a download-upload-prompt loop.

In the Android launch demo, pointing at a date in an email surfaces actions such as setting time with friends, drafting a reply, or finding places to meet. Selecting a room photo and a product image offers a combined visual. Those are prompts into Gemini, not a second app you install.

Privacy is part of the product claim. Google says Magic Pointer only starts when you wiggle the cursor, and it stays off when you are not using it. Alexander Kuscher, senior director for laptops and tablets, later described the split this way: after you activate the pointer, the laptop identifies interactable items on screen locally. Once you select something and ask Gemini to act, that selected content goes to a cloud model. Recognizing an email is local. Asking whether that email is spam sends the selection to the cloud.

## Turn it on and run the first task

Magic Pointer ships with Googlebook. You do not install a separate Chrome extension. Use it on the laptop, not on a phone mirroring the screen.

1. Sign in with the Google account you use for Calendar, Gmail, and Gemini. Pre-orders include 12 months of Google AI Pro, which raises Gemini usage limits.
2. Open the page, email, or image set you want to act on. Keep the source visible. The pointer works on what is on screen.
3. Wiggle the cursor. Wait for the pointer to come alive and for suggestion chips tied to the item under the cursor.
4. Point at one object first: a date, a paragraph, or a single image. Read the chips before you type. They are shortcuts into Gemini, not automatic actions.
5. Select the region if you need more than a hover. Ask a specific request in plain language, such as “Add these runs to Calendar on weekday mornings” or “Is this message spam?”
6. Review the result before you save, send, or accept a calendar event. The pointer starts the task. You still confirm it.

If nothing happens on a wiggle, check that you are on Googlebook OS rather than a browser tab on another laptop. Magic Pointer is a Googlebook feature. Gemini in Chrome has a related pointer rollout for web pages, but that is a separate surface.

## Three tasks worth trying first

**Schedule from a page.** Open a plan that already lists dates and distances. Wiggle, highlight the block, and ask Gemini to place each session on Calendar. Name the calendar and the time window in the same sentence so the draft does not guess.

**Check a suspicious email.** Hover the message and ask for a spam check that cites the signals it used: sender domain, odd links, or mismatched images. Do not forward the mail or click links while you wait. Local recognition only tells the pointer that the item is an email. The analysis itself uses the cloud model.

**Combine images.** Select the photos you want in one view, then ask for a single visualization. Google’s example is a set of images brought together without a manual upload. Keep the selection small so you can tell whether the result matches the files you pointed at.

DeepMind’s earlier notes on the same capability add a few more patterns: point at a PDF and ask for bullets you can paste into an email, hover a statistics table and ask for a chart, or highlight a recipe and ask for doubled ingredients. Treat those as prompts you type after the wiggle, not as guaranteed chips on every page.

## What stays on the device

Do not assume the whole screen is uploaded because the cursor moved. Google’s published rule is narrower. The pointer is inactive until you wiggle. On-device recognition then marks items you can interact with. Cloud processing starts when you select an item and ask Gemini to do something with it.

That split matters for shared screens and for mail you would not paste into a public chat. Summon the pointer only when you intend to ask. Dismiss it when you are done. If a suggestion chip would send content you do not want analyzed, ignore the chip and do not select that region.

The same laptop also includes Rambler, which cleans a spoken brain dump into structured notes and can switch languages mid-sentence. That is a dictation tool, not a pointer. Use Magic Pointer when the source is already on screen. Use Rambler when the source is your voice. Create My Widget is a third path: describe a home-screen widget in words, with no code. None of those replace the wiggle gesture.

## Limits to expect on day one

Magic Pointer does not run on every Android device. It is part of Googlebook’s built-in intelligence system, alongside task automations, Gemini Live, and on-screen suggestions. You can close the lid while Gemini Spark continues a longer background task, but that is a separate feature from the cursor wiggle.

Retail timing is country-specific. U.S. buyers could pick up devices on October 4. Canada, the U.K., Ireland, France, Germany, and Australia follow on October 5. Pre-orders are on the Google Store, Best Buy, and selected retailers. List price starts at $899, with Googlebook OS updates for up to 10 years and the included year of Google AI Pro.

Suggestion chips only cover tasks Gemini can perform from the selection. If a chip is missing, type the request after you point. If the result is wrong, undo it in Calendar, Gmail, or the image tool rather than running the same vague prompt again.

## What to do after unboxing

Wiggle once on a page you already trust, read the chips, and complete one small Calendar or email check before you point at anything sensitive. Confirm the result yourself. For model choice and what else ships in the box, use the [October 4 Googlebook buyer notes](/blog/choose-googlebook-october-4-launch/). Magic Pointer is the shortest path from something already on screen to a Gemini action, and it stays idle until you ask.
