You just pulled off a perfect clutch in your mobile game, but the moment is already gone. Android now lets you grab the last 30 seconds of that action without starting a recording in advance.

Google added Playbacks to Play Games Sidekick in the October 2026 Google System Updates. The feature buffers recent gameplay frames and saves them only when you choose to capture. It keeps the clip on your phone as an MP4 so you can review or share it later.

If you have not set up the Sidekick overlay yet, start with our guide on [enabling Play Games Sidekick](/blog/enable-play-games-sidekick-android/).

## What Playbacks Does

Playbacks works only inside games installed from Google Play that support Sidekick. It stores a rolling buffer of the last 30 seconds of video frames from the current game surface.

Nothing is written to storage until you tap Capture Playback. If you never tap the button, the buffer is deleted. The saved file goes to the Play Games Media folder on your device. Playback records video only. It does not capture audio.

The feature is on by default on compatible phones. You can turn it off from Sidekick settings if you prefer not to buffer gameplay.

## Open Sidekick and Capture a Clip

Sign in to a Play Games profile in the Google Play Store app, or create one. Launch a supported game installed from Play. A small floating handle appears on the edge of the screen.

1. Drag the handle toward the center of the screen to open the Sidekick panel. If you switched to the notification entry point, swipe down and tap the persistent Play Games notification instead.
2. Play normally. When a moment you want to keep happens, open Sidekick again.
3. Tap Capture Playback. The buffered frames convert into an MP4 and save locally.
4. Close Sidekick and keep playing. The clip is already stored.

You can also take a regular screenshot or start a full screen recording from the same panel. Capture Playback is the retroactive option that only saves the previous half-minute.

## Find and Share Your Clips

Open your phone’s Files app or Google Photos and look for the Play Games Media folder. The MP4 files appear there after you capture them. You can rename, edit, or share the clips like any other video on the device.

Google also added the ability to share Play Shorts via a link. Recipients can view those clips on Android, iOS, and the web. Playbacks clips themselves stay local until you choose to upload or send them.

## Turn Playbacks On or Off

The buffer is active by default on compatible devices. To change that:

1. Open Sidekick while in a game.
2. Tap the three-dot menu and select Settings.
3. Under Playbacks, toggle the switch to OFF if you want to stop buffering, or back to ON to resume.

Turning it off stops new buffering. Existing saved clips stay in the Play Games Media folder until you delete them.

## Requirements and Limits

Playbacks is available on compatible mobile phones. Sidekick itself requires Android 13 or higher, a Play Games gamer profile, and a game installed from the Google Play Store. Sideloaded copies do not show the overlay.

The buffer covers only the current game surface. It does not record other apps or the system UI. Because it is a short rolling buffer, you must capture within roughly 30 seconds of the moment you want. Longer highlights still need a manual screen recording started in advance.

## Tips for Better Clips

Position the Sidekick handle where it does not cover important controls. You can drag the entry point up or down the edge or move it to the other side of the screen.

If the floating handle gets in the way, switch to the notification entry point in Play Store settings under Google Play Games. You lose the on-screen drag handle but keep access without covering the game.

Capture right after a key moment. The buffer is short, so waiting too long drops the earlier frames. Check free storage before long sessions. Each captured clip takes space once you save it.

## Keep the Highlight Without Leaving the Game

Playbacks solves the common problem of missing a great moment because recording was not already running. Open Sidekick, tap Capture Playback, and the last 30 seconds become a local MP4 in the Play Games Media folder.

Update the Play Store and Play services if the option does not appear yet. Then open a supported game, confirm the Sidekick handle, and try a capture. The clip stays on your phone until you share or delete it.
