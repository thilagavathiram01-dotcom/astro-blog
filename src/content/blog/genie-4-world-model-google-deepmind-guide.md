Google DeepMind announced Genie 4 on October 27, 2026, its latest world model for real-time interactive simulation. It builds on earlier Genie versions by adding native audio, stronger photorealism, and tighter integration with Gemini models for coherence.

World models let AI simulate physical environments that respond to actions. Genie 4 generates scenes that include sound, movement, and speech while users steer them with text prompts and keyboard controls. The research preview is limited, but a waitlist is open for Project Genie access.

This guide covers the official capabilities, practical limits, and current access paths based on Google DeepMind’s announcement and supporting reports.

## What Genie 4 Adds

Genie 4 is natively multimodal. It produces audio—ambient sounds, music, and interactive dialogue—alongside the visual world. Previous versions focused mainly on visuals.

Gemini Omni anchors the appearance and behavior in real-world detail. Other Gemini models handle reasoning so the scene stays consistent as it evolves. Users can change the weather, move the camera, or introduce characters with natural language while the simulation continues.

Google DeepMind describes four advances: photorealism, advanced physics including natural human movement, real-time steering with prompts, and native audio. The model can generate longer continuous experiences than Genie 3, which was limited to roughly one minute in the public Project Genie prototype.

## How the Simulation Works

Users navigate with standard keyboard controls. Text prompts modify the running scene. Gemini monitors continuity between inputs so objects and people behave consistently for short periods.

The official DeepMind post shows examples such as a saxophone player on a floating platform over a lake. The prompt preserves the original composition while the camera moves freely and ambient lake sounds plus saxophone audio play in real time. Another demo features flowing pink fabric across a meadow.

These are research recordings, not the final consumer interface. Object permanence and fine detail can still drift over longer sessions, a limitation noted in coverage of the announcement.

## Access and Availability

Genie 4 is currently a research preview for trusted testers. Google is gathering feedback before wider release.

It will come first to Google AI Ultra subscribers inside Project Genie, the existing web prototype that previously used Genie 3. Early access partners will also receive it through the Gemini API and Google AI Studio.

A waitlist form is available from the DeepMind blog post. No public pricing or exact timeline has been published. AI Ultra plans start higher than standard Gemini subscriptions, so broad consumer access will remain limited initially.

Developers should watch AI Studio and the Gemini API changelog for early access invitations. Consumer users can join the Project Genie waitlist and ensure their Google account is eligible for Ultra features.

## Practical Uses and Limits

Google positions Genie 4 for filmmaking previsualization, game prototyping, and safe training environments for robots and AI agents. The ability to generate interactive dialogue and ambient audio makes it more useful for narrative or training scenarios than earlier visual-only models.

It is not a replacement for traditional game engines or video tools yet. Sessions remain short, compute is expensive, and access is gated. Real-time interaction still depends on the underlying Gemini reasoning stack staying within its context window.

If you already use Project Genie with Genie 3, the upgrade path is automatic once Genie 4 rolls out to your account. The interface is expected to stay similar: prompt, sketch, and explore.

## Comparison to Earlier Genie Models

Genie 3 powered the public Project Genie experience launched in January 2026 for Ultra subscribers in the US. It supported real-time navigation and image or text prompts but lacked native audio and had shorter coherent sessions.

Genie 4 improves visual fidelity, adds sound, and uses more advanced Gemini models for better continuity. The core loop—prompt a world, explore it with controls, steer with language—remains the same.

## Next Steps

1. Read the official announcement on the Google DeepMind blog and the shorter post on blog.google.
2. Join the Genie 4 waitlist linked from the DeepMind post if you want Project Genie access.
3. Check Google AI Studio periodically for API early access if you are a developer partner.

The model is early. Feedback from the research preview will shape the consumer and developer versions. Expect gradual expansion rather than an immediate wide launch.
