OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on 13 November 2026. It lets your dots AI agents finish online purchases on your behalf without ever seeing your real card number. You review every order and approve it with Face ID or a fingerprint scan. The card network then issues a one-time payment token for checkout.

This guide covers eligibility, setup steps, how purchases work, and practical tips. It draws from OpenAI’s official announcement and Help Center.

## Who can use ChatGPT Wallet

ChatGPT Wallet is rolling out gradually to ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States. You must be 18 or older, live in the US, and hold an eligible US-issued credit card from American Express, Chase, Mastercard, or Visa. Debit cards and virtual cards are not supported at launch. Chase cards require you to be the primary cardholder.

Wallet works only inside conversations with your dot, not in regular Chat, Work, or standalone Codex threads. You need a supported device for biometric approval. Set up a dot first if you have not already. See our guide on [setting up dots for apps and tasks](/blog/chatgpt-dots-setup-apps-tasks-controls/).

Even eligible users may not see the feature yet. When it appears, your dot can help you add a card.

## How ChatGPT Wallet protects your card details

You add your card once through a secure form. OpenAI stores the details separately from your ChatGPT subscription payment method. Before any purchase, your dot shows the merchant, items, total, and selected card. You approve with biometrics.

After approval, the payment network generates a payment token. This substitute has its own card number, security code, and expiration date. The merchant and the agent receive only the token, not your actual card details. You keep your card’s rewards and protections for eligible disputes.

OpenAI built the system with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa so it works with existing checkout flows. Users transact directly with businesses, which handle pricing, fulfillment, and returns.

## Step-by-step: Add a card to ChatGPT Wallet

Ask your dot: “Help me add a card to Wallet.” It will present a secure link or form. You can also go to Settings > Wallet > Add Card on mobile.

1. Enter your card number, expiration date, and security code only in the secure form—never in chat. For American Express, use the 4-digit code on the front.
2. Review the ChatGPT Wallet terms and Privacy Notice, then select Add card.
3. Complete any bank verification your issuer requests. This may involve a code or approval in your bank app. For eligible Chase cards, approve the connection in the Chase mobile app (scan a QR code if on desktop).
4. Wait for confirmation that the card is added. Ask your dot to list saved cards if you are unsure.

Cards in Wallet are separate from merchant-stored payment methods. Your bank may ask you to verify ownership during setup. Enter verification codes only in the secure flow.

## Approve a purchase

Once your card is saved, ask your dot to help find and buy something. Example prompts: “Help me find a new sweater” or “Book me a hotel for my trip to Chicago next weekend.”

When checkout time arrives:

1. Review the merchant, items, total, selected card, shipping details, and any recurring terms shown.
2. Select Approve and complete biometric verification (Face ID, Touch ID, or fingerprint). If your current device cannot do this, follow the link or QR code to a supported phone signed into the same account.
3. Your dot continues checkout and reports the result.

“Ready to check out” means your approval is done. It does not confirm the merchant has accepted the order. Check for a merchant confirmation before retrying. If you change your mind, decline the request. To cancel after approval, contact the merchant.

The virtual card number used at checkout differs from your physical card number. Recurring charges follow the terms you agreed with the merchant.

## Manage cards and get help

Ask your dot to show saved cards. It lists nicknames, last four digits, and expiration dates. To set a default, say “Make the card ending in 1234 my default.” To remove one, identify it by the last four digits and confirm.

Removing a card does not cancel existing orders or close the card with your bank. Contact the merchant for refunds, returns, or recurring-charge changes. For unrecognized charges or declines, contact your card issuer promptly. For Wallet-specific errors or account issues, contact OpenAI Support with device details and the exact error (never share full card numbers or codes).

Some websites may block agent traffic or flag agent purchases in risk systems. OpenAI publishes a Commerce Partner Playbook with guidance for businesses on allowing access and recognizing legitimate agent transactions.

## Tips for safe use

- Always review the full purchase details before approving. Decline anything you do not recognize.
- Never enter card details, security codes, or verification codes in chat or support messages.
- Confirm merchant order status before repeating a failed checkout to avoid duplicate charges.
- Keep your ChatGPT account secure. Report suspected unauthorized access immediately.
- Remember the limited availability. Features expand over time; OpenAI plans future options for spending limits and category pre-approvals.

Over time, users may delegate more purchases within set parameters while retaining biometric safeguards and authorization records.

## Conclusion

ChatGPT Wallet brings agentic shopping closer by letting dots complete purchases under your direct control. The combination of per-purchase biometric approval and network payment tokens keeps real card details out of agent and merchant hands. Setup is straightforward once the feature reaches your account, and the same security model applies to every transaction.

If you are a US ChatGPT Pro subscriber, check your dots conversations for Wallet availability and try adding a supported credit card. Start with small, familiar purchases while you learn the flow. For the latest details, refer to OpenAI’s official pages.
