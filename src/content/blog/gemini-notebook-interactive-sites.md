Google added a new way on November 16, 2026 for Gemini Notebook to turn the sources in a notebook into interactive sites. Type a short prompt that starts with “make me a site,” describe what you need, and the tool builds a clickable page grounded only in your materials. The result can be a visual explainer, a data dashboard, or a project tracker that you open in a new tab or share.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="40" />

This feature is available on the web for users age 18 and older, including Google Workspace subscribers. Mobile app support is coming soon. You need edit access to the notebook to create or delete a site.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="80" />

## What you can build

Visual explainers help with study. An interactive periodic table or a historical timeline lets you click elements or events and see related details from your sources. Dashboards turn numbers from spreadsheets or reports into filters and charts you can explore. Project trackers list tasks and deadlines so you can check progress without flipping through documents.

The sites stay tied to the notebook. Only checked sources are used. Answers and content inside the site cite back to those materials.

## Prepare your notebook

Open Gemini Notebook at notebook.google.com and sign in. Create a new notebook or open one you can edit. Add sources on the left panel. Supported types include PDFs, Google Docs and Slides, web URLs, YouTube videos, Google Sheets, images, and audio.

Select only the sources you want the site to use. Unchecked items stay out of the generation. If you already added free textbooks from the catalog, they work the same way. See the guide on [adding free textbooks to Gemini Notebook](/blog/add-free-textbooks-gemini-notebook/) for the catalog steps.

Give the notebook a clear name so you can find it later. The site will appear in the Studio panel once it is ready.

## Write the prompt and generate

In the Chat panel, type a prompt that begins with “make me a site” or “make a site.” Add details about sections, interactive elements, and audience. A vague request still works, but specific instructions produce a closer result.

Example for a study site: “Make me a site that explains cellular respiration. Include a section for each stage with a short diagram, key terms as clickable cards, and a five-question quiz at the end.”

Example for work: “Make me a site that is a sales dashboard from the attached sheet. Add region filters, a bar chart of revenue versus quota, and a table of open deals.”

Gemini Notebook generates the site in the background. You can keep working in the notebook while it runs. When finished, the site appears in the Studio panel on the right. Generation can take a few minutes depending on the number and size of sources.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="80" />

## Open, interact, and revise

Select the site in the Studio panel. Choose More, then Open in new tab for a full-screen view. Click elements, enter text in fields, or toggle filters. The site remembers your selections and inputs when you reopen it.

To change the site, return to the chat and ask for revisions. New versions are created while the original stays available. Describe the change clearly: “Add a timeline section at the top” or “Change the color scheme to blue and gray.”

Check facts before you share. Open cited passages in the original sources and confirm numbers against the sheet or document. The tool stays grounded in what you uploaded, but verification keeps important claims accurate.

## Share or download

From the Studio panel, select More next to the site, then Share. Viewers need access to the full notebook. You can share the notebook with specific people or set it to Anyone with a link, then copy the artifact link. On shared sites, each person sees only their own inputs; other users’ changes stay private.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="80" />

You can also download the site from the same More menu. Delete a site the same way if you no longer need it. Previously shared links stop working after deletion.

Usage limits apply to site creation. Check your plan details inside Gemini Notebook for current quotas.

## Tips for stronger results

Name sections and interactive features in the prompt. Request a slider, quiz, filter, or expandable cards when you know the interaction you want. Mention the audience so the language and detail level match.

Start with a small set of high-quality sources. Too many conflicting documents can dilute the output. Turn off unused sources for the generation step.

After the first version, iterate in chat rather than regenerating from scratch. Ask for one change at a time so you can compare results.

Pair the site with other Studio outputs. Generate an Audio Overview or study guide from the same sources, then link related materials in your workflow.

## Limits and availability

The feature rolls out on the web first for users 18 and older. Workspace subscribers receive it as well. Mobile support arrives later. You must have edit access to generate or remove a site. Shared viewers need notebook access to open the site.

Sites do not pull from the open web unless you added those pages as sources. Content stays within the materials you provided.

## Conclusion

Type “make me a site” in a Gemini Notebook chat, describe the explainer, dashboard, or tracker you need, and open the result from the Studio panel. The page is interactive, grounded in your sources, and ready to share once you confirm the details. Start with a notebook that already holds the documents or data you care about, add a precise prompt, and review the output before you send the link.

The capability launched on November 16, 2026 and continues to roll out. Check your notebook today to see if the option is available on your account.
