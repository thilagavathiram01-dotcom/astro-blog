You copy an address, switch apps for a minute, and the text vanishes from Gboard. That one-hour limit has frustrated Android users for years. Google is now testing a longer window in the Gboard beta.

The new setting lets you keep unpinned clipboard items for up to 24 hours. This guide shows how to turn it on, what still requires pinning, and how to use the feature safely.

## What changed in Gboard beta

Android Authority reported that Gboard version 18.8.0 beta adds a “Keep clipboard history for” control under Clipboard settings. You can choose one hour, two hours, three hours, six hours, 12 hours, 18 hours, or 24 hours. A “Use default” button restores the previous behavior.

Google’s official Gboard Help still describes the stable version as clearing history “shortly after you last use it.” Pinned items remain permanent. The 24-hour option is currently limited to the beta channel and is rolling out gradually.

## Join the Gboard beta and update

1. Open the Google Play Store and search for Gboard.
2. Scroll to the bottom of the app page and tap Join beta (or Join the beta program).
3. Confirm the request. Wait a few minutes, then pull down to refresh the page.
4. Tap Update if a new version appears. Look for version 18.8.0 or later.

Not every account receives the feature immediately. If the new option is missing after the update, check again in a day or two. You can leave the beta later from the same Play Store page.

## Turn on and configure clipboard history

1. Open any app that accepts text input, such as Messages or Chrome.
2. Tap a text field so Gboard appears.
3. Tap the clipboard icon in the toolbar above the keys. If you do not see it, tap the four-square grid icon and select Clipboard.
4. If this is your first time, tap Turn on clipboard (or the equivalent toggle).
5. Open Gboard Settings (gear icon) → Preferences → Clipboard, or look for Clipboard settings inside the clipboard panel.
6. Find “Keep clipboard history for” and move the slider to your preferred duration. 24 hours is the maximum.
7. Return to the keyboard and copy a few items to test.

Items appear in the Recent section of the clipboard panel. Tap any item to paste it at the cursor. Long-press an item for options such as Pin, Delete, or Edit.

## Pin items that must last longer

Even with a 24-hour window, Google still treats pinned clips differently. Official documentation states that pinning exempts an item from automatic clearing.

1. Open the clipboard panel.
2. Touch and hold the text or image you want to keep.
3. Tap Pin.

Pinned items move to a separate section and stay until you delete them. Use this for addresses, email signatures, or short templates you paste often. ## Privacy and practical tips

Clipboard history stores whatever you copy, including passwords, verification codes, and personal data. Longer retention increases the window during which someone with physical access to your phone could see those items.

- Delete sensitive clips immediately after use. Open the clipboard panel, long-press the item, and choose Delete.
- Prefer pinning only non-sensitive text.
- On Android 13 and later the system shows a brief confirmation when you copy something; use that as a reminder to clear the clipboard if needed.
- Clear the entire recent history from the clipboard panel’s edit mode when you finish a session.

Google has not published an official maximum number of stored items. Most users see a handful of recent clips plus any pinned ones.

## Conclusion

The 24-hour clipboard option in Gboard beta removes a long-standing annoyance for people who copy text across multiple apps. Enable the beta, set the duration that matches your workflow, and keep pinning for anything that must survive beyond a day. Always clear sensitive items promptly. When the feature reaches the stable channel, the same steps should apply for everyone.
