Google Pixel Tag gives Android users a simple way to track keys, wallets, bags, and other everyday items. It launched on November 11, 2026, as part of the November Pixel Drop and works with the Find Hub network of more than one billion Android devices.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="296" />

The tag is slim (5.4 mm thick), IP67-rated, and uses a user-replaceable CR2032 battery that lasts over a year under typical use. It pairs via Fast Pair with any Android phone running Android 9 or later, supports Ultra-Wideband (UWB) Precision Finding on compatible devices, and includes a button that can ring your phone when the phone is nearby.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="300" />

This guide walks through setup, everyday use, and practical tips based on Google’s official details.

## What You Need Before You Start

Confirm these requirements:

- An Android phone running Android 9 or newer with Bluetooth and Location enabled.
- The Find Hub app (download from the Play Store if it is not already installed).
- A Google account signed in on the phone.
- The Pixel Tag itself, with the battery pull tab still in place until you are ready to pair.

For the best Precision Finding experience (directional arrows and distance), use a phone that supports UWB, such as recent Pixel Pro models. Basic tracking and sound playback work on any compatible Android phone.

## How to Set Up Your Pixel Tag

Setup takes about two minutes.

1. Pull the battery tab from the back of the Pixel Tag to power it on. You should hear a short sound confirming it is active.
2. Bring the tag close to your unlocked Android phone. A Fast Pair prompt appears on screen. Tap to connect.
3. Follow the on-screen steps in the Find Hub flow. Choose a category (keys, wallet, bag, etc.) or enter a custom name.
4. Accept the terms to link the tag to your Google account. The tag now appears in the Find Hub app.

You can adjust volume, enable “Notify when left behind,” and set up “Ring this phone” during or after setup. Double-press the physical button on the tag to ring a nearby phone, even if the phone is on silent.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="295" />

## Finding Your Item with Find Hub

Open the Find Hub app and select the Pixel Tag.

- **Play sound**: Rings the tag when it is within Bluetooth range so you can locate it by ear (for example, under couch cushions).
- **Find nearby / Precision Finding**: On supported phones, shows visual distance and directional guidance using Bluetooth Channel Sounding and UWB as you get closer.
- **Map view**: Shows the last known location when the tag is out of direct range. The Find Hub network updates this location when other Android devices detect the tag.

If you mark the item as lost, the system can help others who find it contact you (depending on your privacy settings). Unknown tags traveling with you also trigger an alert on your phone so you can identify and ring them.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="300" />

You can share a single tag with up to 10 people through the Find Hub app. Shared users see the location after a short delay and can ring the tag. This works well for family keys or shared luggage.

## Practical Tips for Daily Use

Enable “Notify when left behind” so your phone alerts you if you walk away from the tagged item while still within range. This pairs well with other Find Hub features covered in our [left-behind reminders guide](/blog/find-hub-left-behind-reminders-guide/).

Replace the CR2032 battery yourself when it runs low. Twist the battery door, swap the cell, and close it—no tools required. Google’s testing indicates more than a year of typical use that includes occasional sound playback and Precision Finding sessions.

The tag works with Pixel Watch through the Find Hub app on Wear OS and can be rung by voice via Pixel Buds and Gemini. Attach it with a third-party keyring or sleeve if you need a secure mount; the tag itself does not include a built-in loop.

Keep Bluetooth and Location on for the most reliable updates. The network uses end-to-end encrypted location data, so other devices help locate the tag without exposing your personal information.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="295" />

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

- No Fast Pair prompt: Confirm Bluetooth is on, move the tag closer, and restart the phone. Remove and reinsert the battery tab if needed.
- Precision Finding unavailable: Your phone may lack UWB or the required Android version for Channel Sounding. Basic map and sound features still work.
- Tag not updating on the map: Wait a few minutes for the network to report a new location, or ensure the tag has a clear view of other Android devices.
- Battery life shorter than expected: Heavy use of sound and Precision Finding drains the cell faster. Replace it with a quality CR2032.

If the tag was previously paired to another account, reset it by removing the battery for 30 seconds and starting the setup process again.

## Conclusion

Google Pixel Tag turns everyday items into trackable assets inside the Find Hub app. The Fast Pair setup is straightforward, the replaceable battery removes a common pain point of sealed trackers, and the combination of local Bluetooth, UWB Precision Finding, and the large Android network covers both nearby and distant searches.

Whether you attach it to keys, slip it into a wallet, or clip it to luggage, the process is the same: power it on, pair once, and manage everything from Find Hub. Check the Google Store for current availability and pricing ($29.99 single or $99.99 four-pack at launch).
