Google’s Pixel Tag went on sale November 11, 2026, giving Android users a first-party tracker that works with the Find Hub network of more than one billion devices. It costs $29.99 for one or $99.99 for a four-pack, measures roughly 28 × 46.1 × 5.4 mm, weighs about 11.8 grams with the battery, and carries an IP67 rating.

Unlike many third-party tags, it includes Ultra Wideband (UWB) for directional guidance on supported phones, Bluetooth 6.0 Channel Sounding for better distance readings, a physical button that can ring your phone, and a user-replaceable CR2032 battery rated for over a year. Setup uses Fast Pair and works on any phone running Android 9 or later.

## What you need before starting

Confirm your phone meets the basic requirements. Android 9 or newer is required, along with Bluetooth turned on, an internet connection, and a signed-in Google account. Install the Find Hub app from the Play Store if it is not already present. Location permission should be set to “While using the app” during setup.

The tag ships with a plastic pull tab over the battery contacts. Removing it powers the device on and starts the pairing process. Avoid placing the tag inside metal luggage or RFID-blocking wallets, as these can weaken the signal. Exterior fabric pockets work better for checked bags. The CR2032 battery complies with airline rules for both carry-on and checked luggage.

## Pair the Pixel Tag with Fast Pair

1. Unlock your phone and leave it on the home screen.
2. Turn on Bluetooth and confirm the phone has internet access.
3. Hold the Pixel Tag right next to the phone.
4. Pull out the plastic battery tab completely. You should hear a short sound confirming the tag is active.
5. A Fast Pair pop-up appears. Tap **Connect**.
6. Read the Acceptable Use Policy and tap **Continue**.
7. Accept the Terms and Conditions.
8. Grant Location permission when prompted (“When using this app”).
9. The tag appears in the Find Hub app.

If the pop-up does not appear, wait five minutes (or up to 24 hours if you dismissed it twice). You can force a retry by toggling Bluetooth or restarting the phone. Alternatively, open Settings → Connected devices and look for the tag at the top, or open Find Hub → Add Device and follow the on-screen steps.

After pairing, open Find Hub, select the tag, and assign a clear name and category (Keys, Backpack, Wallet, Luggage, or Other). The default name combines your account name with the category, for example “Alex’s keys.” You can edit it anytime.

## Locate items with Find Hub

Open the Find Hub app and tap your Pixel Tag. When the tag is nearby, choose **Find nearby**. Supported phones show an on-screen arrow, distance readout, and directional guidance. UWB phones provide the most precise arrow once you are close; Bluetooth Channel Sounding improves distance accuracy from farther away on compatible hardware.

If the item is out of direct Bluetooth range, Find Hub shows the last reported location on a map. The Find Hub network of more than one billion Android devices helps crowdsource that location. Location data is end-to-end encrypted; only you and people you explicitly share with can see it.

To make the tag ring, tap **Play sound** in the app. Volume can be adjusted in the tag’s settings. When you have the tag but cannot find your phone, double-press the physical button on the tag. Your nearby phone rings even if it is on silent. Press the button once or unlock the phone to silence it.

Left Behind alerts notify you if you walk away from the tagged item. You can customize these in Find Hub settings so they do not trigger at home or work. For more on managing those alerts, see our guide to [Find Hub left-behind reminders](/blog/find-hub-left-behind-reminders-guide/).

## Share the tag with others

You can share a Pixel Tag with up to 10 people so family or colleagues can also locate the item in their own Find Hub apps. In the app, select the tag → Share ownership (or Share device) → choose contacts. They receive a link and have 24 hours to accept. After acceptance they can track the item without triggering unwanted-tracking warnings. You can stop sharing at any time.

When you mark a tag as lost, people who find it can see limited contact information (email, phone, or a short message you set). The tag automatically clears the lost state when it reconnects to your phone.

## Replace the battery

The CR2032 battery is designed to last over a year. To replace it, hold the circular battery door and twist counterclockwise. No tools are required. Insert a fresh CR2032, reclose the door, and the tag reconnects. Low-battery alerts appear in Find Hub so you know when to swap it.

## Tips for reliable tracking

Attach the tag securely. Because it lacks a built-in keyring hole, most people use a compatible holder or case (available from third parties such as Bellroy or Spigen). Place it where the signal is not blocked by metal.

Test immediately after setup: walk into another room, open Find Hub, and confirm the location updates and the sound plays. On UWB phones, practice the “Find nearby” arrow so you recognize the guidance.

Keep the Find Hub app updated. The network improves as more Android devices participate, so performance generally increases over time.

## Conclusion

Pairing a Pixel Tag takes under two minutes on any Android 9+ phone with Bluetooth and internet. Once added to Find Hub you can see last-known locations via the billion-device network, get directional guidance on UWB phones, play a sound, reverse-ring your phone with the physical button, and share access with up to 10 people. The slim IP67 design, tool-free battery swap, and one-year battery life make it practical for keys, wallets, bags, and luggage.

Start by removing the battery tab next to an unlocked phone, accept the prompts, name the tag clearly, and test the sound and map view before you rely on it.
