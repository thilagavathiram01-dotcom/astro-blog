Google Pixel Tag is now available, giving Android users a first-party way to track everyday items like keys, wallets, and luggage. It works with the Find Hub network of more than one billion Android devices and pairs quickly via Fast Pair.

This guide covers eligibility, setup steps from official support pages, how to find items nearby or on a map, sharing with family, and practical tips. Availability started November 11, 2026, at $29.99 for one tag or $99.99 for a four-pack from the Google Store in supported markets.

## What is Pixel Tag?

Pixel Tag is Google’s first item tracker. It is thin enough to fit in a wallet, made from durable stainless steel and polycarbonate, rated IP67 for dust and water resistance (up to 1 meter for 30 minutes), and uses a replaceable CR2032 coin battery that lasts up to one year under typical use.

It supports Bluetooth 6.0 with Channel Sounding and Ultra-Wideband (UWB) on compatible phones for precise distance and direction guidance when nearby. When farther away, the crowdsourced Find Hub network helps locate it. Location data is end-to-end encrypted. You can also press the button on the tag twice to ring your phone if you have the tag but lost the phone.

It requires an Android phone running Android 9 or later with Bluetooth and internet. Pixel Watch users can also locate or ring the tag from the Find Hub app on Wear OS. For the broader November Pixel Drop context that includes other features, see our guide on [November 2026 Pixel Drop features](/blog/november-2026-pixel-drop-gemini-intelligence-pixel-tag/).

## Step-by-step setup

Setup takes about two minutes. Follow these steps based on Google’s official Pixel Phone Help:

1. Ensure Bluetooth is on and your phone is unlocked on the home screen with internet access.
2. Place the Pixel Tag next to your phone.
3. Remove the plastic battery tab completely. The tag should power on.
4. A Fast Pair pop-up appears. Tap Connect.
5. Read the Acceptable Use Policy and tap Continue.
6. Accept the Terms and Conditions.
7. Grant Location permission when prompted (choose “When using this app”).
8. The tag is added to the Find Hub app.

If the pop-up does not appear, wait a few minutes, toggle Bluetooth, or restart the phone. You can also open the Find Hub app, tap Add Device, and follow the prompts. After setup, rename the tag by choosing a category such as Keys, Backpack, or Other. It defaults to something like “Your name’s keys.”

Note: Carbon-fiber or aluminum luggage and some RFID-blocking wallets can block the signal. Place the tag in an exterior fabric pocket for checked bags. The battery is airline-compliant for checked and carry-on luggage.

## Finding your items

Open the Find Hub app to see all your tracked items.

**Nearby items:** Select the tag and use Find nearby. On phones with UWB (such as certain Pixel Pro models), you get visual arrows and a distance meter. Bluetooth Channel Sounding provides distance on more devices. Play a sound from the app to make the tag chime if it is within range.

**Out of range:** The map shows the last reported location from the Find Hub network. Google cannot see your location data.

**Left Behind alerts:** Enable these in the tag settings so your phone warns you if you walk away from the item.

**Reverse finding:** Double-press the physical button on the tag (located on the back, flush with the body) to ring your phone when the devices are nearby.

You can adjust volume levels for the tag’s speaker in Find Hub. Precision finding works best on supported hardware; check your phone’s capabilities if distance or direction cues are limited.

## Sharing and managing tags

Share a tag with up to 10 people so family or colleagues can also locate shared items like house keys or a car remote.

From Find Hub:

1. Select the tag.
2. Tap Share device or Share ownership.
3. Send the invitation via message, email, or Quick Share.
4. The recipient accepts on their Android device (they may need to install Find Hub).
5. Confirm the share on your side if required.

Invitations expire after 24 hours. Shared users see a PIN for extra security. Location updates for shared tags can take a few minutes to appear.

You can also mark an item as lost so a finder who taps the NFC area (below the battery door) sees contact details or a message. Unknown tracker alerts notify you if someone else’s tag is traveling with you, with options to ring it.

## Tips and limitations

- Update your phone and the Find Hub app for the best experience.
- Battery life varies with how often you play sound or use precision finding. Replace the CR2032 yourself by twisting the battery cover.
- Precision finding (UWB + Channel Sounding) requires compatible hardware. Base model phones may offer less precise guidance.
- The tag works with Android 9+ devices, not just Pixels.
- Rollout of the November Pixel Drop features is gradual; ensure your system is up to date.
- Avoid relying solely on the network in very remote areas with few Android devices nearby.

## Conclusion

Pixel Tag brings first-party tracking to Android with Fast Pair setup, a large Find Hub network, precise nearby finding on supported phones, reverse phone ringing, and easy sharing. At $29.99 it matches competitor pricing while adding a replaceable battery and reverse-find button.

If you frequently misplace keys, bags, or luggage, pair one today and test it in everyday spots around your home before traveling. Check the Google Store for current availability in your region and review official support for any model-specific notes.
