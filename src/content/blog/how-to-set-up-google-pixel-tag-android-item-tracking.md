Google Pixel Tag gives Android users a first-party way to track everyday items like keys, wallets, and luggage. Announced in August 2026 and available starting November 11, the slim tracker works with Google’s Find Hub network of over a billion Android devices.

Unlike third-party options, it integrates directly with the Find Hub app on phones running Android 9 or later. The design is thin enough to slip into a wallet or attach to keys without much bulk. It uses a user-replaceable CR2032 battery rated for over one year and carries an IP67 rating for dust and water resistance up to 1 meter for 30 minutes.

This guide walks through setup, everyday use, and precision finding so you can start locating items right away.

## What You Need Before Setup

You need an Android phone running Android 9 or newer with Bluetooth enabled and an internet connection. The Find Hub app should be installed or will appear during pairing. Pixel Tag comes in a single Fog color and measures about 1.1 by 1.8 by 0.2 inches, weighing 0.4 ounces with the battery.

A single tag costs $29.99. A four-pack is $99.99. Both are available from the Google Store and Amazon in supported regions including the United States, United Kingdom, India, Japan, Australia, Canada, Singapore, and Taiwan.

## How to Set Up Your Pixel Tag

Setup uses Google Fast Pair and takes under two minutes in most cases. Follow these steps from the official Pixel Phone Help page.

1. Turn on Bluetooth on your Android phone and unlock it to the home screen.
2. Make sure the phone has an internet connection.
3. Place the Pixel Tag next to your phone.
4. Remove the plastic battery tab completely from the tag. You may hear a short beep.
5. A Connect popup appears. Tap Connect.
6. Review the Acceptable Use Policy and tap Continue.
7. Accept the Terms and Conditions.
8. Grant Location permission when asked (choose When using this app).
9. The tag appears in the Find Hub app.

After pairing, open Find Hub and select a category such as Keys, Backpack, or Other. The app suggests a name like “Your name’s keys.” You can edit it. You can also adjust the tag’s speaker volume and enable “Notify when left behind” or the reverse ring feature.

If the popup does not appear, open Settings > Connected devices or open Find Hub and tap Add Device. Restarting Bluetooth or the phone clears temporary delays if you dismissed the prompt earlier.

## Everyday Tracking with Find Hub

Once paired, the tag shows up in the Find Hub app on your phone and, with the Wear OS app, on compatible Pixel Watches. The network of Android devices helps update the location when the tag is out of Bluetooth range. Location data is encrypted and not stored on the network itself, according to Google.

From the app you can:

- View the last known location on a map.
- Play a sound on the tag when it is nearby (speaker reaches about 80 dB at max volume).
- Mark the item as lost so you receive an alert when the network sees it again.
- Share the tag with up to 10 people for shared items like a family backpack.
- Leave a contact message (email, phone, short note) for someone who finds a lost tag.

Double-press the physical button on the back of the tag to ring your phone even if it is on silent. A single press stops the sound. The button is flush, so it is not obvious by feel.

## Precision Finding with UWB and Channel Sounding

When you are close to the tag, precision finding improves accuracy. Ultra Wideband (UWB) on supported phones shows distance and direction with an arrow in the Find Hub app. Bluetooth Channel Sounding, available on phones with Bluetooth 6.0 and Android 16 or later, provides distance only.

Pixel Pro models since the Pixel 6 support UWB. Base Pixel models generally do not. Channel Sounding works more broadly on newer Android phones that meet the Bluetooth requirement. Google notes that some UWB phones may have limitations, so test with your specific device.

Once within range, tap Find Nearby in the app. The combination of sound, distance readout, and directional guidance makes it practical to locate a tag under cushions or inside a bag.

## Practical Tips for Daily Use

Attach the tag with a third-party keychain case if you want a more secure loop; Google sells Made for Google options from Bellroy and Spigen. Place luggage tags in exterior fabric pockets because metal or RFID-blocking materials can weaken the signal. The CR2032 battery is TSA/FAA/IATA compliant for air travel.

Replace the battery by holding the circular door and twisting counterclockwise—no tools required. Google rates battery life at over one year under typical use.

NFC support lets you tap the tag to a compatible phone to open its details page in Find Hub. This is useful for quick settings access.

For shared households, enable the reverse ring feature so anyone with the tag can locate a misplaced phone. Combine this with Android’s existing theft protection features for broader device security. ## Conclusion

Pixel Tag fills a long-standing gap for Android users who wanted a simple, first-party tracker that works with the large Find Hub network. Setup is fast, the battery is replaceable, and precision finding helps once you are nearby. Availability is currently limited to select countries, and performance still depends on network density in your area.

If you frequently misplace keys or travel with checked bags, the $29.99 price is competitive with similar trackers. Start with one tag, test the precision features on your phone, and expand as needed.
