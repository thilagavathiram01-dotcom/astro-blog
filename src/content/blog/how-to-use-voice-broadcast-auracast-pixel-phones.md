Android 17 QPR2 arrived on Pixel phones in early November 2026 and brought Voice Broadcast to Quick Settings. This feature turns your Pixel into a personal PA system. You speak into the microphone and nearby listeners hear you through Auracast-compatible earbuds, hearing aids, or speakers.

Voice Broadcast builds on Bluetooth LE Audio and Auracast. It streams live audio from your phone's mic or a wired headset to any number of receivers in range. No one-to-one pairing is required. You can make the broadcast public or lock it behind a password. Listeners join via QR code, NFC tap, or the Audio streams tile.

The feature first appeared in beta builds under the name Voicecast. It lived in Developer Options. Android 17 QPR2 Beta 4 moved it to Quick Settings and renamed it Voice Broadcast. The stable QPR2 update that rolled out starting November 10, 2026 made it available to everyone on supported Pixels.

## What You Need

You need a Pixel phone running Android 17 QPR2 or later. The update covers devices from the Pixel 6a through the Pixel 11 series, including the Pixel Tablet and Pixel Fold models. Auracast reception works best on Pixel 8 series and newer phones, though Voice Broadcast itself appears on the broader QPR2 device list.

Listeners need Bluetooth LE Audio devices that support Auracast. Pixel Buds Pro 2 are a reliable choice. Other compatible earbuds, headphones, and hearing aids also work. Both the broadcasting phone and the receiving devices must support the standard.

Bluetooth must be on. The feature does not require an internet connection once the broadcast starts.

## How to Start a Voice Broadcast

Open Quick Settings by swiping down from the top of the screen. If you do not see the Voice Broadcast tile, tap the pencil or edit icon and drag the tile into your active layout.

Tap the Voice Broadcast tile. A setup screen appears. Enter a broadcast name. Toggle Private channel if you want to require a password. Set the password when prompted.

Choose the audio source. Use the phone's built-in microphone or switch to a connected wired headset. Tap Start broadcast. The phone begins transmitting. You will see controls to mute or end the session.

Share the join method with listeners. The screen shows a QR code. You can also use NFC tap-to-join where supported. Listeners open their own Quick Settings Audio streams tile or scan the code to connect.

## How Listeners Join

On a compatible device, open Quick Settings and tap the Audio streams tile, or go to Settings > Connected devices and look for the stream option. Select the named broadcast. Enter the password if it is private.

Scan the QR code with the Camera app or an NFC tag if the broadcaster provides one. Once connected, audio routes to the listener's earbuds or hearing aids. Live Caption and Live Translation can run alongside the stream on supported devices.

Multiple people can join the same broadcast at the same time. Range depends on the environment, typically a room or small outdoor area.

## Practical Uses

Tour guides can speak once and have an entire group hear them through their own earbuds. Teachers can address a class without raising their voice. Presenters can share audio with attendees who prefer private listening. Families can share commentary during events without external speakers.

Accessibility benefits stand out. Hearing aid users can receive clear audio from a single source. Public venues that already deploy Auracast transmitters can complement phone-based broadcasts for smaller groups.

The feature differs from regular Audio sharing. Audio sharing streams media playback such as music or video. Voice Broadcast specifically transmits microphone input in real time.

## Tips for Better Results

Keep the phone close to your mouth or use a wired headset for clearer audio. Reduce background noise when possible. Test with one listener before relying on it for a larger group.

Use a private channel and a strong password in public spaces to prevent unwanted listeners. End the broadcast when finished so the channel does not stay open.

Check that listener devices are updated and support LE Audio. Pixel Buds Pro 2 work well with recent Pixel phones. If audio sounds distorted, move closer or restart Bluetooth on both devices.

## Troubleshooting

If the Voice Broadcast tile is missing, confirm you are on Android 17 QPR2 or later. Check Settings > System > System update. Restart the phone after the update installs.

Listeners who cannot find the stream should verify Bluetooth is on and their earbuds support Auracast. Move closer to the broadcaster. Ensure the password matches exactly if the channel is private.

Some older Pixel models support LE Audio but have limited Auracast broadcast capability. Pixel 8 series and newer offer the most consistent experience.

## Conclusion

Voice Broadcast with Auracast gives Pixel owners a simple way to share live voice audio with a group. The Quick Settings tile, password protection, and QR or NFC join options make it practical for everyday use. Update to Android 17 QPR2, add the tile, and test it with compatible earbuds.

The feature expands what Pixel phones can do without extra hardware. Tour guides, educators, and anyone who needs clear group audio now have a built-in option.
