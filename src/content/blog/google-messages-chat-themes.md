Google’s September 2026 Android Drop treated chat styling as a first-class feature, not a hidden beta toggle. **Chat theme** in [Google Messages](https://messages.google.com/) lets you pick a wallpaper and bubble colors for one conversation at a time — a family group can look like a vacation album while a work thread stays plain.

The control is local. The person on the other end does not inherit your wallpaper. That is the opposite of the older **Change colors** option on RCS chats, which used to restyle bubbles for everyone in the thread.

This guide follows Google’s [September Android Drop](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/android-drop-september-2026/) announcement, the [android.com/drop](https://www.android.com/intl/en_us/new-features-on-android/featured/september-2026/#chat-theme-in-google-messages) feature page, and how the menu actually appears in Messages in mid-September 2026. Where Google Help still says “Change colors” and “RCS only,” that page is older than the Drop.

## What Chat theme actually changes

Inside one conversation you can set:

- **Wallpaper** — a curated still from Google’s categories (Animals, Architecture, Black & White, Cityscapes, Landscapes, Macro, Space, Sunsets, Textures) or a photo from your gallery
- **Bubble colors** — a static palette, or **Dynamic Color** that pulls from the wallpaper and your system theme
- **Reset to default** — remove the custom look without deleting the chat

Google’s Drop copy is explicit: pick a wallpaper or your own photo and the text bubbles adjust to match, or you can pick colors separately. The example they used is a latte palette for a coffee friend and a beach photo for a trip group.

Chat theme is **not**:

- A shared wallpaper the other person sees
- The same as dark theme for the whole Messages app
- Required for RCS to work
- Related to Google Keep notes inside Messages (that is a separate Drop feature on the + menu)

## Update Messages first

Chat theme is a server-side rollout on current Messages builds. If you only see **Change colors**, update and reopen the thread.

1. Open Play Store → **Messages** → **Update**.
2. Force-stop Messages and open it again so the conversation list reloads.
3. Confirm you are using Google Messages, not Samsung Messages or a carrier SMS app.
4. Open any thread. Tap the name at the top or the **More** (⋮) menu. Look for **Chat theme**. A red-dot badge on More is how the first rollout flagged the new item.

If the row is still missing after an update, wait. Google listed Chat theme as available in the 1 September 2026 Drop, but individual accounts still catch up over days.

## How to apply a theme (step by step)

These steps match the flow 9to5Google documented when Google announced the feature and the menu that shipped with the Drop.

1. Open **Google Messages** and tap the conversation you want to style.
2. Tap the contact or group name in the top bar, **or** tap **More** (⋮).
3. Tap **Chat theme**.
4. Under **Colors**, swipe the palette. Try Dynamic Color first if you already picked a photo; try a static swatch if you want the same bubbles on every wallpaper.
5. Under **Wallpapers**, pick a category or tap **Choose a photo** and select an image from Gallery or Photos.
6. Tap **Next** to open the preview with dummy bubbles over your image.
7. If the photo is too busy behind text, go back and pick a darker crop or a simpler category image.
8. Tap **Apply**.

Repeat per conversation. There is no global “apply this wallpaper to all chats” switch.

## RCS vs SMS: what still matters

When Google first shipped Chat theme in late August 2026, coverage and an older Help article said the feature needed **RCS**. That was true of the first build.

By **18 September 2026**, [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-messages-chat-theme-text-rcs-3712851/) confirmed Chat theme on ordinary SMS threads as well. The likely reason is the privacy model: the wallpaper is only on *your* device, so Google no longer has to keep the feature inside RCS.

Practical takeaway:

- Try Chat theme on any thread, RCS or SMS.
- If SMS still lacks the menu, the account is on the older gate. The conversation still works; you just cannot decorate it yet.
- Turning RCS off does not delete a theme you already applied, but deleting the chat and starting a new one with the same number *does* drop the custom look — that behavior is documented on Google’s older appearance Help page and still matches what testers see.

To check RCS: Messages → your profile → **RCS chats** (wording varies by build). You do not need to turn RCS on solely for a wallpaper.

## What the other person sees

Nothing of your wallpaper. Their bubbles, their dark mode, their own theme if they set one.

The old **Change colors** control on RCS could restyle the thread for every Google Messages user in that chat. Chat theme replaced that shared experiment with a local skin. Do not text “I changed our chat to the beach photo” and expect them to see sand.

Group chats work the same way: each member who has the menu can style their own copy.

## Reset, delete, and privacy notes

- **Reset to default** lives in the same Chat theme sheet. Use it when a photo makes outgoing bubbles hard to read.
- **Deleting the conversation** clears the theme. Starting a new thread with the same number starts from default colors.
- **Photos you upload** stay on the device as the chat background. They are not sent as an attachment and are not a new shared album.
- Avoid using a screenshot of a one-time password, boarding pass, or ID as a wallpaper. The image sits on screen every time you open the thread.

## When the menu is missing

Work through this list before assuming a bug.

- Wrong app. Samsung Messages and many carrier apps do not have Chat theme.
- Stale build. Update Messages from Play Store.
- Cached thread. Force-stop, reopen, pull to refresh.
- Work profile. A managed Messages install can lag or hide personalization.
- Very old Android. The Drop targets current Messages on supported Android versions; it is not a new system API you enable in Settings.

Keep notes and lists from the same Drop live under **+** in a thread. They do not depend on Chat theme.

## A 5-minute setup checklist

1. Update Google Messages.
2. Open one personal thread and one group thread.
3. Apply a curated wallpaper to one and a gallery photo to the other.
4. Confirm bubbles stay readable in both light and dark system themes.
5. Ask the other person whether they see anything different. They should not.
6. Reset the work thread if the photo is distracting.

## Conclusion

Chat theme is a small, local customization. Use it to tell threads apart at a glance: family photo for family, muted color for work, default for anything you skim in public. Update Messages, open **Chat theme** from the thread header, preview before you apply, and remember the look is yours alone.

The September Drop bundled this with Keep-in-Messages, Motion Assist, and Find Hub remembered items. Theme the chats you open every day; skip the rest.
