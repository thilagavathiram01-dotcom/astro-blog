Google and Samsung opened pre-orders for Intelligent Eyewear on November 12, 2026. These Android XR audio glasses from Warby Parker and Gentle Monster start at $395 and ship on December 10. They pair with Pixel, Galaxy, or iPhone for hands-free Gemini help, music, calls, and photos.

This guide covers compatibility checks, pre-order steps, and first-time setup. You get real-time assistance without pulling out your phone.

![Person holding eyeglasses in front of an eye chart](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1517948430535-1e2469d314fe?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## Check Phone Compatibility First

Intelligent Eyewear requires a supported phone. Google lists these devices at launch:

- Pixel 7 series and newer running Android 17 with the September 2026 update or later.
- Samsung Galaxy S, Galaxy S FE, Galaxy Z Fold, Flip, and Fold Ultra series on One UI 9.0 or higher.
- iPhone 13 Pro and newer with at least 6GB of RAM on iOS 26.6 or later.

Guided Vision descriptions, Aira visual interpreter, and Audible work only with Android phones at launch. iPhone users get a smaller feature set. Confirm your phone meets the requirements before you order. Availability is limited to Google accounts in the United States and South Korea, and users must be 18 or older.

## Choose Your Frames and Pre-Order

Warby Parker offers three styles: Alden, Reid, and Wren. Optical or sun versions start at $395 in Pine, Saddle, and Jet Black. Prescription, width, and lens options are available. Visit warbyparker.com or a retail location for demos at more than 360 stores.

Gentle Monster designs start at $449. Six bold frames come in dark red, pink, gray, and yellow, with tinted, clear, blue, or brown lenses. Most support prescriptions. The collection is available in the US and South Korea. Pre-orders run online and in-store at both brands. Full retail availability begins December 10. Each pair includes a charging case that recharges the glasses up to seven times.

Frames weigh around 41 grams and deliver up to nine hours of battery life. An off switch lets you use them as regular glasses.

## Set Up the Glasses Companion App

Once your glasses arrive, download the official companion app on your supported phone. Sign in with your Google account. Place the glasses in the charging case, open the case lid, and follow the on-screen pairing prompts. The app walks you through Bluetooth connection and permissions.

Grant camera and microphone access so Gemini can respond to what you see and hear. You control which apps use these sensors and can revoke access at any time. Adjust Gemini History settings to review or delete chats.

Say “Hey Gemini” or tap the frames to start. “Hey Google” also works. Test basic commands: ask about an object in view, request turn-by-turn Google Maps directions, or translate speech. The open-ear speakers deliver audio while you stay aware of surroundings.

## Use Key Features Day to Day

Gemini handles multi-step tasks. Look at a mural and ask for its history. Check your Calendar against an event poster. Add items you see to a grocery list. Google Translate provides real-time speech translation across more than 70 languages with automatic detection.

Capture photos and videos with the 12-megapixel ultra-wide camera. Press the button or ask Gemini. HDR processing and image stabilization keep results sharp. Spatial audio records with the clip. An indicator light shows when you are recording, and the glasses stop if the light is covered.

Play music from YouTube Music, Spotify, or Apple Music. Handle calls and messages hands-free. On Android phones, Guided Vision offers conversational audio descriptions for low-vision users, and Aira streams the camera feed to a human interpreter.

## Privacy and Safety Notes

Camera visuals used by Gemini are not saved in your Gemini History. They are not used to train AI models or target ads. The glasses automatically redact bystanders’ faces before processing. Data on the device is encrypted, and data in transit is encrypted. You can turn the glasses fully off with the hardware switch.

For developers building experiences, see our guide on [Android XR emulators in Android Studio](/blog/android-xr-emulators-studio-rabbit-2-guide/).

## Tips for Best Results

Keep the glasses charged in the case overnight. Update the companion app and your phone software for the latest features. Practice short voice commands in quiet settings first. Clean the lenses and camera regularly. If pairing fails, restart Bluetooth and the app, then re-pair from the case.

Battery life varies with camera and microphone use. Store the glasses in the case when not in use to protect the frames.
