You collect research, notes, and data in Gemini Notebook. Now you can turn that material into an interactive site with one chat prompt.

Google added the feature on 16 November 2026. Type "make me a site" plus a short description. Gemini Notebook builds a clickable page from the sources already in the notebook. The result can be a study explainer, a data dashboard, or a project tracker. You open it in a new tab or share the link.

The tool stays grounded in what you uploaded. It does not search the open web for new facts while building the site. That makes it useful when you already have the PDFs, Sheets, or notes you trust.

## What the feature creates

Google lists three common outcomes:

- Visual explainers for study, such as an interactive periodic table or a historical timeline.
- Interactive dashboards that let you filter and explore numbers from your sources.
- Project trackers that show tasks and deadlines in one place.

The finished site appears in the Studio panel. You can open it full screen, interact with its controls, and share it. On shared sites, each person sees only their own inputs. Your selections do not appear for other viewers.

The feature is available on the web at notebook.google.com for users 18 and older, including Google Workspace accounts. Mobile support is coming later.

## Step-by-step: build your first site

You need a Google account and edit access to a notebook. The process takes a few minutes once sources are ready.

1. Go to [notebook.google.com](https://notebook.google.com) and open an existing notebook or create a new one.
2. Add sources. Upload PDFs, paste website links, connect a Google Sheet, or add Docs and other files. Check only the sources you want the site to use.
3. In the Chat panel, type "make me a site" followed by a clear description. Example: "Make me a site that turns these notes into an interactive timeline with a short quiz at the end."
4. Wait while Gemini Notebook generates the page. You can keep working in the notebook. The site shows up in the Studio panel on the right.
5. Click the site, then choose "Open in a new tab" for full-screen view.
6. Interact with the controls. Check a few facts against the original sources before you share.

Google's Help Center confirms the same flow: ask to "make a site" with a description, then open from Studio.

## Write prompts that produce better sites

A short phrase works, but detail improves the result. Include:

- The main purpose (study aid, dashboard, tracker).
- Specific sections or interactive elements you want.
- The audience (yourself, a team, students).

Strong examples:

- "Make me a site with a dashboard of the sales numbers in my sources. Include filters by region and a chart of revenue versus target."
- "Make me a site that tracks the tasks and deadlines in this project plan. Show status and due dates."
- "Make me a site that visually explains cellular respiration with interactive diagrams and a five-question quiz."

After the first version appears, you can ask for changes in the same chat. Gemini Notebook builds a new version and keeps the previous one.

## Share and manage the site

In the Studio panel, open the three-dot menu next to the site. Choose Share, set who can access the notebook, then copy the link to the artifact. Viewers need access to the notebook to open the shared site.

Your site remembers the selections and text you enter. When you reopen it, checkboxes, notes, and filters return to the last state you left.

Usage limits apply. Free plans allow fewer sources and notebooks than paid Google AI plans. Creating sites counts toward those limits. Check the current caps in Gemini Notebook Help if you hit a restriction.

## Tips for reliable results

Keep sources focused. One notebook per topic produces cleaner sites. Mixing unrelated files weakens the grounding.

Verify numbers and claims. Dashboards pull from the Sheets or tables you added. Open the source file if a chart looks off.

Test interactivity yourself before sharing. Click filters, complete a quiz, and confirm the behavior matches what you asked for.

Gemini Notebook now turns the sources you already collected into interactive sites. Type "make me a site," describe the outcome, and open the result from Studio. The feature works for study guides, dashboards, and trackers without leaving the notebook.
