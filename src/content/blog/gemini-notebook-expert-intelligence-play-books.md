Owning a book used to mean you could highlight it, search a chapter, or reread a paragraph. **Expert Intelligence** extends that to Gemini Notebook: if you bought an eligible ebook on Google Play Books, you can add the full title as a source and ask questions that are grounded in that text, with citations back to the book.

Google announced the feature on 27 August 2026 and confirmed Workspace availability on 17 September 2026. It is not a free “ask anything about any book” mode. You must own the Play Books copy, the title has to be in the eligible set, and answers are supposed to stay inside that source rather than drifting to a generic web summary.

This guide walks through eligibility, the two official add-source paths, and a few workflows that are actually worth the extra step.

## What Expert Intelligence is (and is not)

Google describes Expert Intelligence as a cross-product effort to let people work with **trusted sources they already have**, starting with Play Books inside Gemini Notebook. At launch it covers more than **100,000** English-language ebooks from publishers including Bloomsbury, De Gruyter Brill, Johns Hopkins University Press, Macmillan, O’Reilly Media, and Penguin Random House.

Once a book is in a notebook you can:

- Ask questions and get answers cited from that book
- Generate Gemini Notebook outputs such as **Audio Overviews**, **Infographics**, and **Quizzes**
- Combine several owned titles in one notebook
- Read the book text inside the notebook when Google surfaces it as a source

It is **not**:

- A way to chat with books you only borrowed, pirated, or bought elsewhere
- Automatic training access for everyone who shares a notebook (collaborators must own their own copy)
- A guarantee that every title in your Play library is supported

Google has also worked with a small group of authors on **Featured Notebooks** that bundle extra sources around a title. Those are optional starting points, not a requirement.

## Check whether a book is eligible

Do this before you rebuild a research notebook around a title.

**On the Play Books product page**

1. Open the book in [Google Play Books](https://play.google.com/store/books).
2. Look for a **Tools** badge near the page count on the detail page.
3. Open Tools. If **Gemini Notebook** is listed, the ebook is in the eligible set.

**For a title you already own**

1. Open the Play Books app or the web library.
2. On mobile, long-press the cover to open About the Book. On web, use the overflow menu.
3. Look for a **Use with Gemini Notebook** control near the Read button.

Google’s help page also notes that only **select English-language ebooks** are included so far. Greyed-out titles in the Gemini Notebook picker are in your library but not eligible.

## Add a Play Book from Gemini Notebook

Use the same Google Account for Play Books and Gemini Notebook. Mixed accounts are the most common reason the library looks empty.

1. Open [Gemini Notebook](https://notebooklm.google/) (the product Google rebranded from NotebookLM).
2. Confirm you are signed in to the account that holds the ebook.
3. Choose **Create new notebook**, or open an existing one.
4. On the left, select **+ Add sources**.
5. Choose the **Play Books** icon.
6. Select one or more eligible titles. Ineligible books stay visible but greyed out.
7. Select **Add Source(s)**.

Wait until the source finishes processing before you ask detailed questions. Large books take longer than a short PDF.

## Add a book from the Play Store instead

If you are shopping rather than mining an existing shelf:

1. Browse Google’s featured Expert Intelligence titles in Play Books.
2. Buy an eligible ebook so it lands in that account’s library.
3. Either use **Use with Gemini Notebook** on the book page, or switch to Gemini Notebook and add it with the steps above.

Google said it offered a complimentary eligible book for people 18 or older in the United States while supplies last. Treat that as a limited promo, not a standing perk.

## Useful ways to use the book as a source

The value is in **narrow questions**, not “summarize this entire book.”

**Apply one framework to a real situation.** Google’s briefing example used Kim Scott’s *Radical Candor* against a specific workplace conversation. Ask for the relevant chapter ideas, then ask what the book does *not* cover so you do not treat a citation as a complete playbook.

**Turn a short practical book into a working plan.** The Verge described generating a recipe-style plan from Michael Pollan’s *Food Rules*. Pair the book with your own source — a fridge photo, a grocery list Doc, or class notes — so the notebook is combining owned text with your context.

**Study, then test yourself.** Generate a quiz from one section, then ask for citations for every missed answer. That is closer to spaced review than a one-shot summary.

**Keep publisher and personal material separate.** If you upload a journal or work notes, label those sources clearly. Expert Intelligence is for the purchased book; your private files are still just notebook sources with whatever sharing rules you set.

## Who can use it

According to Google’s 17 September 2026 Workspace update:

- Available now on Rapid Release and Scheduled Release domains
- Available to Google Workspace customers, Workspace Individual subscribers, and personal Google accounts
- You need Gemini Notebook plus Play Books / Google Books enabled
- There is **no separate end-user admin toggle** for the feature itself

If Play or Books is turned off by an administrator, the Play Books source picker will not help.

## Limits worth knowing before you rely on it

- **Ownership is required.** Sharing a notebook does not grant collaborators the book. They are prompted to buy their own Play Books copy.
- **Not every purchase qualifies.** Print, audiobook-only, or storefronts other than Play Books do not count.
- **Language is limited at launch.** Google’s help text currently points to select English ebooks.
- **Citations can still miss tone or exceptions.** Grounding reduces invented quotes; it does not replace reading the passage.
- **More Google surfaces are promised later.** Google said Expert Intelligence will expand to the Gemini app and AI Mode. Until that ships, treat Gemini Notebook as the working surface.

## A compact checklist

1. Sign in to the same account in Play Books and Gemini Notebook.
2. Confirm the Tools badge or “Use with Gemini Notebook” button.
3. Create or open a notebook and add the Play Books source.
4. Ask questions that name the situation you care about, then open the cited passage.
5. Use Audio Overview or a quiz only after the source has finished indexing.
6. Do not share a notebook expecting others to inherit your purchase.

If a title never appears, it is usually eligibility or account mismatch — not a hidden setting.

## Conclusion

Expert Intelligence is a practical upgrade if you already buy technical, business, or reference ebooks on Play Books and you want those pages inside the same notebook as your notes and docs. It is less useful as a discovery tool: you still have to purchase an eligible title, and the first wave is English-only.

Set it up once on a book you actually reread. The feature earns its keep when a cited paragraph saves you from flipping through a 400-page file, not when it produces another generic summary of a book you have not opened.
