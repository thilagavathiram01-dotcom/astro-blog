A digital ID in Google Wallet is a verified copy of a US driver’s license or state ID stored on one Android phone. You can present it at some TSA checkpoints and in a small set of apps. Google’s Help Center is explicit: it is **not** a replacement for the physical card. Keep the plastic ID with you.

Ohio joined the Wallet state-ID list in late August and early September 2026, according to reporting from 9to5Google. Availability still depends on your issuing agency. The current list lives in [Google Wallet Help](https://support.google.com/wallet/answer/12436402) and in the Wallet app itself. If your state is missing from the picker, you cannot add the card yet.

## What you need before you start

Google documents these requirements:

- A phone running **Android 9 or higher**
- A **screen lock** (PIN, pattern, password, or biometric)
- **Bluetooth** and **Nearby devices** turned on
- The current **Google Wallet** app from Play Store
- A valid US driver’s license or state ID from a participating issuer
- Time to photograph both sides of the card and record a short selfie video

The issuer — not Google — approves the request. A still from your video is sent to that agency. Approval can take a few minutes. Some agencies ask for extra steps if the first scan fails.

The ID is a **private pass**. It is encrypted on the device, not copied into Google Takeout, and it lives on **one phone only**. If you switch phones, remove it first, then add it again.

## Add the ID in Google Wallet

Google’s official steps:

1. Open the **Google Wallet** app.
2. Tap **Add to Wallet**, then **ID card** (some builds label this **ID & personal info** → **Driver’s license or state ID**).
3. Choose the state that issued the card.
4. Photograph the **front** and **back**. Hold the card still on a dark surface in good light. Zoom so the barcode is sharp.
5. Record the short face video the app requests.
6. Consent to send the application to the issuer.
7. Wait for the Wallet notification that the card is approved.

If the scan fails, Google’s tips are practical: flatten the card, improve lighting, update Wallet, then restart the phone and try again.

## Where you can use it

Google currently lists two everyday uses:

- **TSA at supported airports.** The digital ID must be REAL ID compliant. TSA still expects you to carry the physical card. Check the [TSA digital ID map](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/digital-id/map) and [TSA digital ID page](https://www.tsa.gov/digital-id) before you fly.
- **Some apps**, such as certain car-rental flows, that ask Wallet to share identity or age attributes. You review what will be sent and authenticate on the phone before anything leaves the device.

More venues are described as coming later. Do not assume a hotel desk or a bank teller will accept the phone instead of plastic.

### Present the ID at TSA with NFC

1. Unlock the phone.
2. Hold the back of the phone to the NFC reader for a few seconds.
3. Review the data Wallet will share.
4. Tap **Continue** or **Cancel**.
5. Authenticate when Wallet asks.
6. Wait for the blue check mark.

If the read fails, change grip. NFC antennas sit in different spots on different phones. Hold closer and a second longer. If it still fails, use the physical ID.

### Present the ID with a QR code

1. Open Wallet and scroll to the ID.
2. Tap the ID so the QR code is on screen.
3. Let the agent scan it.
4. Review the share sheet, tap **Share** or **Cancel**, then authenticate.
5. Wait for the blue check mark.

## Manage, log, and delete the ID

**View details:** open Wallet, tap the ID, then **More** → **ID or license info**.

**Activity history** is off by default. Turn it on if you want a local log of who received data and when. The log stays on the device and requires your unlock. Turning history off again deletes that log. You can download it from **More** → **View and manage activity** → **Download**.

**Remove on the phone**

1. Open Wallet and tap the ID.
2. Tap **More** → **Remove** → **Remove**.

**Remove remotely** if the phone is lost, sold, or in repair:

1. Go to [myaccount.google.com](https://myaccount.google.com).
2. Open **Security & sign-in**.
3. Under **IDs**, tap **Manage IDs**.
4. Select the ID and tap **Delete**. Confirm.

Do this before you factory-reset or hand a phone to a technician. The credential cannot ride along to a second device.

## Problems Google documents

- **Issuer rejected the application.** Rescan using the lighting and barcode tips. If it keeps failing, use the state’s motor-vehicle site rather than guessing a Wallet setting.
- **Issuer deleted the ID.** The agency can revoke the digital card. Check that site for the reason.
- **Issuer needs more info.** Complete the extra verification in the app. You cannot skip it.
- **Offline too long.** The phone must reach the internet. After **more than 30 consecutive days offline**, the digital ID is unavailable until you reconnect.
- **You moved.** Repeat setup with the new state’s license if that state is supported. If the new state is not supported, the digital ID stops working when the physical card is no longer valid.
- **Not REAL ID.** A non–REAL ID digital card will not clear TSA domestic checkpoints even if it sits in Wallet.

## Privacy in plain terms

When you apply, you give Google photos of both sides of the card and a short face video. With your consent, Google sends ID data and a face still to the issuing agency. If approved, the encrypted credential stays on that phone. You choose each share. Google records that you consented. Analytics follow [Google’s Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

A passport-based **ID pass** is a separate Wallet flow, with its own Help article and country limits. Do not mix those steps with the state-license path above.

## Practical checklist

- Confirm your state appears in Wallet’s ID picker or on the official Help page.
- Update Wallet, set a screen lock, enable Bluetooth and Nearby devices.
- Scan on a dark, well-lit surface; get the barcode in frame.
- Carry the physical card anyway — Google and TSA both say so.
- Test NFC grip at home against any NFC tag so you know where the antenna sits.
- Know the remote-delete path on myaccount.google.com before you travel.
- If you change phones, delete the old copy first.

Digital IDs in Wallet are useful at a growing list of TSA lanes and in a few apps. They are still a companion to the card in your pocket, not a reason to leave that card at home.
