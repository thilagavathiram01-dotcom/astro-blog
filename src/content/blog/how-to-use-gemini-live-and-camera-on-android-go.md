Google re-architected the Gemini app so core features run on eligible Android (Go edition) phones with at least 2 GB of RAM. Hundreds of millions of people who use these devices as their main internet connection can now hold spoken conversations, get help with paper notes, and create images.

This guide shows how to start Gemini Live, point the camera at documents for translation or explanation, and generate images with Nano Banana. It focuses on the features that matter most on limited hardware.

For the full eligibility and setup steps, see our existing guide on [using the Gemini app on lower-memory Android devices](/blog/gemini-app-lower-memory-android-go-guide/).

## Confirm Your Device and Account

Your phone must run Android (Go edition) and have 2 GB of RAM or more. Sign in with a personal Google Account, or a work or school account that has Gemini access. Family Link managers can restrict the feature for younger users.

The rollout is gradual. Update the Google Search app or download the standalone Gemini app from the Play Store. On supported phones, press and hold the Home button or the Power button to open Gemini. Hey Google and Ask about this screen do not work on these devices.

## Start a Gemini Live Conversation

Gemini Live lets you talk through a task instead of typing a long prompt. It supports more than 45 languages on these phones. Practising for an interview out loud is one example Google highlights.

1. Open Gemini by holding the Home or Power button.
2. Tap the microphone icon or start speaking once the interface appears.
3. Speak naturally. Pause when you finish a thought so the system can respond.
4. Continue the conversation by speaking again. The history stays available when you sign in on other devices.

Keep the phone charged and close other apps before a long session. Short, clear spoken requests work better on limited RAM than complex multi-part questions.

## Use the Camera for Notes and Forms

Point the camera at handwritten notes, printed forms, or signs. Gemini can explain the content, translate it, or walk you through the next steps. This works on the spot without needing a high-end processor.

1. Open Gemini.
2. Tap the camera icon in the interface.
3. Frame the note or form clearly. Good lighting helps the phone read the text.
4. Ask a specific question, such as “Explain this form” or “Translate this to English.”
5. Review the response. You can ask follow-up questions about the same image.

Take one clear photo rather than several. Upload only what you need so storage stays free. The feature replaces the older basic voice commands with practical help for everyday paperwork and study materials.

## Create Images with Nano Banana

Nano Banana lets you generate custom images from a short description. A bakery sale flyer is one sample use case. The tool runs as part of the core experience on eligible devices.

1. Open Gemini.
2. Describe the image you want in plain language. Include style, colours, and purpose.
3. Wait for the result. Generation uses the re-architected path that fits lower memory.
4. Download or share the image if it matches what you need. Adjust the prompt and try again if it does not.

Start with simple requests. Complex scenes with many details can take longer or use more resources. Save finished images to free storage space.

## Tips for Reliable Performance

Update the Google app regularly through the Play Store. Free RAM by closing unused apps before you start Live or camera tasks. Use Wi-Fi when possible for faster responses and image generation.

Sign in with the same Google Account across devices so chat history stays in sync. If a feature has not appeared yet, wait a day or two and check for updates. The rollout continues gradually.

Test basic actions first: set a timer, find a nearby place, or ask a short question. Then move to Live conversations and camera work. This builds confidence without stressing the limited hardware.

## What Is Not Available

Hey Google voice activation does not work on Android (Go edition) devices. Ask about this screen is also unavailable. Some advanced agent features that require higher resources or specific regions remain limited to other phones.

The core conversational experience, Live voice, camera assistance, and Nano Banana image creation are the features Google prioritised for these devices.

## Conclusion

Eligible Android Go phones with 2 GB of RAM or more now run the core Gemini experience. Hold the Home or Power button, start a spoken conversation in Gemini Live, point the camera at a note or form, or create an image with Nano Banana.

These tools turn a basic voice command device into a practical AI assistant for education, work, and daily tasks. Update your apps, confirm your account, and try one feature today.
