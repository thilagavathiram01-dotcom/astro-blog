Linear math fails in a kitchen. Multiply a family shawarma recipe by six and the spice mix will drown the meat. Google published a 28 September 2026 case study on how Brooklyn grocer Edy Massih uses Gemini to scale Lebanese recipes for catering, then turn those batches into shopping lists and dietary swaps.

This guide turns that official post into steps you can run in the Gemini app or at gemini.google.com. It stays inside the three jobs Google documented: scale a recipe, build an aisle list, and adapt a dessert for restricted diets.

Pair this with the broader home workflow in [How to Use Gemini for Household Chores and Meals](/blog/gemini-household-chores-meals/) if you also scan a fridge or connect Instacart.

## What you need

- A signed-in Gemini session on the web app or the Gemini app for Android or iOS
- The original recipe with serving count, units, and any spice notes you already trust
- Guest count, diet rules, and the date of the event
- Connected Apps such as Instacart only if you want Gemini to hand items to a cart (Keep Activity on for web and iOS, per Gemini Apps Help)

Gemini will not taste the dish. Treat every scaled gram as a draft. Taste a small batch before you commit a full tray.

## 1. Scale a recipe without a straight multiply

Edy told Google that you cannot just multiply ingredients by 50. Delicate spices and pastry ratios break first. The official try-this prompt is the right shape: name the dish, the start count, the target count, and the rule for spices.

Open Gemini and paste a prompt like this:

“Scale a recipe for chicken shawarma wraps from 8 servings up to 50 for a large event. Adjust spices so they do not overpower the dish. Group ingredients by grocery aisle. Keep yogurt-based sauces on a separate line.”

Then attach or paste the original recipe. Include:

- Serving count on the card you already use
- Weight and volume units as written (do not convert first)
- Marinade time and oven temperature
- Any “to taste” lines, so Gemini flags them instead of inventing a number

Ask for a second pass: “Show a column for 8, 25, and 50 servings. Mark spices that should scale slower than the meat.” That matches Edy’s warning without adding extra product claims.

If you cook from a handwritten card, photograph it in even light and attach the image. Confirm every number Gemini reads. A smudged 3 can become an 8.

## 2. Turn the menu into an aisle-by-aisle list

Google’s second job is the shopping run. Edy uses Gemini to combine recipes, total shared ingredients, and structure prep days in advance. The official sample prompt is a mezze platter for 10 to 12 people.

Use this structure:

“Create a grocery shopping list for a large mezze platter serving 10 to 12 people. Include hummus, baba ganoush, tzatziki, warm pita, and vegetable crudités. Organize aisle by aisle with exact quantities. Deduplicate items that appear in more than one dip.”

Add constraints that matter in a real store:

- Brand or fat level if your recipe depends on it (full-fat yogurt, tahini by weight)
- Produce that can sit two days versus items you buy the morning of service
- A “already in pantry” list so Gemini subtracts salt, oil, and garlic you own

Ask Gemini to output three blocks: shop list, prep schedule by day, and a short waste note (what spoils first). Edy told Google the lists help keep food waste and cost down. Check prices in your own store app. Gemini does not see your local flyer unless you paste it.

If Instacart is connected, ask Gemini to add only the missing items, then open the Instacart cart and review quantities before you pay. Connected Apps do not replace the checkout screen.

## 3. Adapt desserts and mains for diet rules

The third official job is swaps that keep flavor. Google’s sample: adapt pistachio baklava and salted tahini brownies for guests who are strictly gluten-free and nut-free, and keep flaky texture plus a rich, nutty taste.

Prompt pattern:

“Adapt a dessert spread of pistachio baklava and salted tahini brownies for guests who are strictly gluten-free and nut-free. Suggest ingredient swaps that preserve the flaky texture and nutty, rich flavor. List allergens that remain after the swap.”

Rules that keep the output usable:

- Name the restriction as a hard constraint, not a preference
- Ask Gemini to separate “safe for the whole table” from “plated only for that guest”
- Demand a leftover-allergen line. Sesame, dairy, and gluten hide in wrappers and spice mixes
- Require a method change if the structure of pastry changes (phyllo substitutes do not bake like phyllo)

Do not serve a swapped dessert from a shared tray if cross-contact matters. Gemini can rewrite a formula. It cannot sanitize a bakery bench.

For mains, reuse the same pattern: “Keep the Lebanese flavor profile. Remove peanuts. Flag every sauce that still contains sesame.” Taste a test portion. If a guest has a medical allergy, follow their clinician and the packaged labels, not a chat reply.

## A working session from start to service

Run the three jobs in one notebook-style chat so the numbers stay consistent.

1. Paste the family recipe and the guest count.
2. Ask for the scaled formula and the slower spice curve.
3. Ask for the aisle list and a two-day prep calendar.
4. Ask for diet variants as separate recipes, not as footnotes on the original.
5. Export or copy the tables into Docs or Keep so the line cooks are not reading a long chat on a phone.

If the event grows from 50 to 70 after you shop, do not multiply the shopping list by 1.4 in your head. Ask Gemini to rebase from the original 8-serving card again. Compounding two scaled sheets is how salt and yeast get away from you.

## Limits to respect

- Gemini can misread handwriting and faded cards. Recheck weights.
- Spice intensity is not linear. Google’s source quotes Edy on that point. Keep a small test batch.
- Diet language in a chat is not a certification. Read every package.
- Connected shopping apps need your review before payment.
- Work and school Google accounts may hide some consumer connectors. Check the Apps page in Gemini settings.

Edy told Google that Gemini freed time from administrative kitchen math so he could spend more of the day on food and guests. That is the bar for this workflow: fewer scrap-paper conversions, same recipes, same taste checks.

## Quick checklist

- Start from the family card, not from a scaled sheet you already distorted
- Name start servings, target servings, and the spice rule in one prompt
- Deduplicate the grocery list across dishes
- Split diet variants into their own recipes
- Taste a small batch before the full tray
- Review any Instacart or Calendar action Gemini proposes
