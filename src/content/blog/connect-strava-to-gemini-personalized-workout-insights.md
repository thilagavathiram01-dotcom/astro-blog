Strava subscribers in the US can now connect their activity history directly to Google Gemini. The integration turns months of runs, rides, and workouts into conversational insights without scrolling feeds or exporting data.

Gemini answers questions grounded in your own Strava records. It is optional, read-only, and available on any Gemini plan as long as you hold a Strava subscription. International rollout is not yet announced.

![Runner sprinting on a sunlit track](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1744060204728-f68e434a3edf?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What the Strava–Gemini connection unlocks

Once linked, Gemini can examine volume, consistency, sport mix, heart-rate trends, and even weather data recorded with each activity. Useful prompts include:

- Am I getting faster at the same heart rate?
- How is my strength training impacting my running?
- Give me a detailed analysis of my last race, mile by mile.
- What’s my fastest 10K this year versus all time?
- How long is my current run streak?
- How much did the heat slow me down on Saturday’s long run?

The connection stays read-only. Gemini cannot edit activities, add kudos, or change privacy settings. You can disconnect at any time from Gemini’s Connected Apps menu.

This builds on Strava’s earlier Claude integration via Model Context Protocol. Google’s version lives inside the Gemini app and web experience under Personal Intelligence.

## Step-by-step: connect Strava to Gemini

The steps are the same in the Gemini mobile app and on gemini.google.com. Availability is rolling out in the United States first.

1. Open the Gemini app or visit gemini.google.com and sign in with the Google account you want to use.
2. Go to Personal Intelligence or open the sidebar and select Customize, then Connectors (or Connected Apps).
3. Find Strava in the list. On mobile, check the Inactive section if it does not appear immediately.
4. Tap Add or Connect, then sign in with your Strava credentials.
5. Review the permissions screen. Gemini receives access to workout activities, performance metrics, training plans, gear, clubs, and basic profile information. Approve the connection.
6. Return to a new Gemini chat and ask a question that references your training data.

If the option is missing, force-close the app, check for updates, and confirm your account is in the US. Strava subscription status is required; free accounts cannot connect.

You can also start the flow from the Strava side: Settings > AI Assistants or Your Strava Subscription > Tools on the web or mobile app.

## Tips for better answers

Be specific with dates and locations when asking about older activities. Some users report that pagination limits how far back Gemini can see without extra context.

Ask follow-up questions about weather, terrain, or fatigue instead of accepting the first summary. Treat the responses as a fast data pull, not coaching advice.

Combine the connection with other Gemini tools. For example, after reviewing a race breakdown, ask Gemini to draft a recovery plan or suggest route ideas based on your usual pace.

![Group of runners on a city street](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1539966903171-89770f33f468?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

Privacy note: the consent screen indicates that parts of the conversation, plus your name and email, may be shared with Strava and used according to its privacy policy. Review both companies’ policies before authorizing if data handling is a concern.

## Disconnect or manage the link

Return to Gemini Settings > Connected Apps (or Personal Intelligence > Connected Apps). Select Strava and choose Disconnect. The link removes immediately. Your historical Strava data remains unchanged.

On the Strava side, visit Settings > AI Assistants to review or revoke access from that end as well.

## Conclusion

Connecting Strava to Gemini gives US subscribers a simple way to query their training history in plain language. The setup takes a few minutes, the connection is optional and read-only, and the insights cover pace trends, race analysis, streaks, and weather effects.

Start with one clear question after you authorize access, then expand from there. If you already use Personal Intelligence for other apps, this fits the same pattern.
