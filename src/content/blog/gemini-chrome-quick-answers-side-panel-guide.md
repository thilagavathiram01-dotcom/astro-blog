Google rolled out clearer guidance in November 2026 for using Gemini right inside Chrome. The side panel lets you ask questions about the page you are viewing without switching tabs or copying text.

This keeps your place on the site while Gemini handles summaries, comparisons, or plain-language explanations. The official post from November 12 outlines the two basic steps.

## Why the side panel helps

Researchers and shoppers often bounce between tabs. One holds the source page. Another holds a chatbot. Context gets lost. Scroll position resets. The Gemini side panel sits beside the page and uses the current tab by default.

You stay on the original site. Gemini reads the content you share and answers in the panel. This works for dense reports, product pages, and technical articles.

## Requirements before you start

You need a signed-in personal Google account in Chrome. The feature is available on supported desktop platforms including Windows, macOS, and ChromeOS. Availability depends on your region and language settings. Update Chrome to the latest version first.

Work or school accounts may need admin approval. Incognito mode does not support it. If the Gemini icon is missing, check Chrome Settings under AI innovations and confirm the toolbar button is enabled.

## Step-by-step: open the side panel

1. Open the webpage you want help with in Chrome.
2. Click the Gemini icon in the Chrome toolbar (usually near the top right).
3. The side panel opens beside the page. Opt in if this is your first time.
4. Type your request in the prompt box. Gemini uses the current tab as context by default.

You can keep the panel open while you scroll or switch tabs. It stays attached to your session.

## Practical prompts that work

Google highlights three main uses in the November 12 post.

- Summarize a dense report: "Summarize the key findings and any caveats in this document."
- Compare product details: "Compare the specs, price, and reviews for this item versus the previous model."
- Explain complicated topics: "Explain this concept in plain language with one simple example."

Start with a short request. Add follow-ups if you need more detail. You can also share additional open tabs by typing @ and selecting them, up to a set limit.

## Tips for better results

Be specific in your prompt. Mention what you want extracted, such as tables, key dates, or pros and cons. If the page has multiple sections, ask Gemini to focus on one part first.

For shopping pages, request a comparison table. For research papers, ask for the main claim plus supporting evidence. Always verify important facts against the original page, as AI summaries can miss nuance.

You can pop the panel out into a floating window if you prefer. Dock it back when you want it fixed to the side. Keyboard shortcuts become available after the initial opt-in on supported systems.
