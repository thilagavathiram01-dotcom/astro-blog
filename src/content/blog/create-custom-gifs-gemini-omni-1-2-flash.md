Google added native GIF creation to the Gemini app on 20 November 2026. The feature uses the Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash video model to turn text prompts, images, or short video references into looping animations about three seconds long.

You no longer need to search Giphy or Tenor for reaction clips. Describe the motion you want, and Gemini produces a ready-to-share GIF. The rollout covers Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Japan, Korea, the United Kingdom, and the United States, and it is expanding gradually.

## What the feature offers

Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash powers both longer video generation and these short GIFs. Full videos can run from 3 to 15 seconds with optional audio and resolutions up to 4K. GIFs stay at roughly three seconds, remain silent, and loop cleanly.

You can start from a text prompt alone, attach up to five reference images, or include one video. The model keeps visual consistency across frames so characters and objects do not drift. After generation you copy the GIF into chat apps, documents, or email, or download the .gif file.

Official Google Help documents confirm the ~3-second length and the country list. The Gemini App X account noted that the feature is free for users in the listed markets, though a Google AI plan is required for broader video features in some cases.

## Requirements and access

Sign in to the Gemini app on the web or mobile. Personal accounts in the supported countries can generate GIFs; availability is rolling out, so the option may not appear for every user yet. Users under 18 cannot access the feature. Work or school accounts need a qualifying Google Workspace licence.

You must hold rights to any images or videos you upload. Google’s Terms of Service and Prohibited Use Policy apply. Generated content can be removed if it violates those rules.

## Create a GIF step by step

1. Open gemini.google.com or the Gemini mobile app and sign in.
2. Start a new chat or open the image/video tool from the menu (Create video or the format picker).
3. Choose GIF from the format selector if it appears. You can also simply prompt for a GIF in the main chat.
4. Type a clear description. Example: “A three-second looping GIF of a cat waving its paw against a plain background, smooth motion, consistent lighting.”
5. Optionally attach one to five images or one video as reference. Upload only content you own.
6. Submit the prompt. Generation takes a few minutes. While it runs you can start another chat and return later.
7. Preview the result. Copy it for immediate paste into messages or docs, or download the .gif file.

If the first result needs changes, continue the conversation and ask for edits such as “make the background blue” or “slow the wave slightly.” Multi-turn editing works within the same chat.

## Tips for better GIFs

Keep prompts specific about motion, style, and background. Mention “looping,” “smooth motion,” and “consistent character” to reduce frame jumps. Reference images help when you want a particular look or existing photo animated.

Test simple ideas first—waving, blinking, or light camera moves—before complex scenes. Because output is short, focus on a single clear action.

## Limitations to know

GIFs are fixed at about three seconds. Longer clips require the full video generation path. Audio is not included in GIF output. Geographic and account restrictions still apply, and the gradual rollout means some eligible users will wait for the option to appear.

## Conclusion

Gemini’s GIF tool brings quick custom animations into everyday chat without extra software. Users in the supported countries can describe a scene or supply a reference and receive a shareable looping clip in minutes. Start with a simple prompt, refine if needed, and drop the result into any app that accepts GIFs.
