Casting YouTube used to fail for a boring reason: the phone was on guest Wi-Fi, 5 GHz, or mobile data while the television sat on the home router. On **1 September 2026**, Team YouTube said that pairing no longer depends on a shared network. Sign in with the same account on both devices and the phone becomes a **companion** for the TV app.

This is not a new streaming protocol and it is not a replacement for every Chromecast session. It is an account-based control layer on top of the YouTube apps. Official help still documents local Cast and a **TV code** path for devices that are not signed in together.

This guide walks through what changed, how to start a session, how to send a video from the home feed, and where the new model still falls short — for example, a guest who wants to throw a clip onto someone else’s TV without sharing a login.

## What companion mode actually changes

The announcement on the [YouTube Community forum](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/463959026/use-your-phone-as-a-companion-for-watching-youtube-on-tv) lists four product changes:

- **No shared Wi-Fi requirement.** You do not have to join the TV’s network or tap a Cast icon just to keep the two screens in sync.
- **Engage from the phone.** Like, comment, and subscribe on the phone while the long-form video keeps playing on the television.
- **Multitask in the mobile app.** Search or open Shorts on the phone without stopping the TV playback.
- **Play on different device.** From the home feed, open the three-dot menu next to a title and send that video to the signed-in TV.

When both apps use the same account, opening YouTube on the phone can detect what the TV is already playing and show a **companion panel**. You can reopen that panel from the **devices** icon at the top of the mobile home screen.

The old Cast icon is no longer the only way in. Reporting from [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/09/01/youtube-overhauls-app-to-tv-experience/) and [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-tv-companion-mode-3706108/) describes a more generic devices control instead of the classic Cast badge.

## Set up the same account on phone and TV

Companion mode only appears when the identity matches. Do this once per household TV you control.

1. On the smart TV or streaming stick, open the **YouTube** app (Google TV, Android TV, Fire TV YouTube app, or a similar living-room client).
2. Sign in with the Google account you use on your phone. If the TV already has a family profile, pick **your** profile, not a shared “Living room” guest.
3. On Android or iPhone, update YouTube from Play Store or the App Store, then sign in with **that same account**.
4. Start a video on the TV with the remote so there is something for the phone to attach to.
5. Open YouTube on the phone. Wait for the companion panel. If it does not appear, tap the **devices** icon on the home screen.

You can be on mobile data, a different SSID, or a travel hotspot. Both devices still need their own working internet connection. Removing the shared-Wi-Fi rule does not make YouTube work offline.

## Send a video from the phone feed

You do not have to start playback on the phone first.

1. Stay signed in on both devices.
2. On the phone home feed, tap the **three-dot menu** next to a video title — not only the player overflow once a clip is already open.
3. Choose **Play on different device**.
4. Select the TV that shows your account.
5. Confirm that the television takes over. The phone should stay free for search, comments, or Shorts.

If the TV is not listed, it is usually signed into a different account, sleeping, or running an older YouTube build that has not received the companion rollout yet.

## What you can do from the companion panel

Use the phone as a keyboard and a remote, not as a second player that fights the TV.

- Pause, skip, and change volume for the TV session.
- Open the description and links that are painful on a D-pad.
- Type a comment or a search query with a real keyboard.
- Subscribe without hunting through the TV sidebar.
- Open Shorts on the phone while a long video continues on the set.

That last point is the practical win. Older Cast sessions often treated the phone as a controller that died if you left the video. Companion mode is designed so the mobile app remains a normal YouTube client.

## How this sits next to classic Cast and TV codes

YouTube Help still documents three related paths in [Watch YouTube on your smart TV](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7640706):

- **Cast** from the YouTube app when devices can see each other on the local network.
- **Companion** when the same account is signed in on phone and TV.
- **TV code** when you need to link a phone to a TV that is not on the same Wi-Fi and you are not relying on the new automatic companion attach.

Do not assume local Cast is gone. Google has not published a retirement date for network discovery. Shared-room situations still need it: a visitor should not have to sign into the house account just to send one clip.

If several people want to queue videos on one television without sharing passwords, keep using Cast on the same LAN, or sign the TV into a dedicated viewing account that everyone in the room already uses.

## Troubleshooting when the TV never appears

Work through this list before reinstalling apps.

- **Account mismatch.** Check the avatar on the TV and on the phone. Brand accounts and YouTube Premium family members are easy to mix up.
- **Stale session.** Force-close YouTube on the phone, open it again, and tap the devices icon.
- **TV asleep.** Wake the YouTube TV app so it can register as online for the account.
- **App version.** Update both clients. The companion change began rolling out after the 1 September 2026 forum post; older TV firmware can lag.
- **Login bugs.** Mid-September 2026, YouTube confirmed and later [fixed sign-in errors](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-tv-console-login-issue-fixed-3711218/) on some TVs and consoles. If QR or code login fails, try again after an app update.
- **Shared TV privacy.** If the living-room set is a family device, consider YouTube’s newer **account lock** PIN so one person’s recommendations and history stay closed when the remote is idle.

## Privacy and household etiquette

Account-based pairing is convenient and easier to leak. Anyone holding an unlocked phone that is signed into the house YouTube account can send videos to that TV from another network.

Practical habits:

- Sign the TV into the profile that should own recommendations, not a child’s account.
- Sign out of YouTube on a phone you lend.
- Use account lock on shared living-room TVs when YouTube offers the four-digit PIN in your region.
- Do not treat companion mode as a way to drive a hotel TV. Hotel sets rarely run your Google account, and using a public display with your login is a bad idea.

Playback still counts against the signed-in account. Likes and subscriptions you tap on the phone attach to that same identity, even if the picture is on a different screen.

## A short checklist for tonight

1. Update YouTube on the phone and on the TV.
2. Sign both into one account you control.
3. Play anything on the TV, then open the phone app and look for the companion panel.
4. From the phone feed, use **Play on different device** on the next video you want on the big screen.
5. Leave Shorts and search on the phone; leave the long video on the television.
6. Keep classic Cast for guests who should not touch the household login.

## Conclusion

YouTube’s September 2026 companion update fixes the most common living-room failure: two devices that could not see each other because they were on different networks. Same account, two internet connections, and the phone becomes a usable keyboard and second screen.

Treat it as a control layer, not as a reason to share your Google login with every visitor. When the people in the room do not share an account, Cast on the local network is still the honest tool. When they do, skip the Wi-Fi dance and send the video from the three-dot menu.
