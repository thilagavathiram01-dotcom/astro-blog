Students and professionals often leave lectures with pages of messy handwriting. Sorting those notes into clean study material used to take hours. Gemini now handles the conversion and lets you edit the result without leaving the chat.

Google rolled out the ability to generate, preview, and edit files directly in the Gemini app on 29 October 2026. PDF preview and editing works on both web and mobile. Other formats such as Google Docs, Sheets, slide decks, Markdown, TXT, and code remain web-only for now. The feature is available to all users in every language where the Gemini app runs.

This guide walks through one high-value workflow: turning handwritten lecture notes into a polished PDF study guide.

## What You Need Before You Start

Open the Gemini app on the web at gemini.google.com or on your phone. Sign in with your Google account. Make sure you can upload photos or files. The feature works for free users and paid subscribers alike.

Take clear photos of your handwritten pages. Good lighting and a flat surface help Gemini read the text accurately. You can upload multiple images in one prompt.

## Step 1: Upload Your Notes and Ask for a Study Guide

In a new Gemini chat, attach your photos of the handwritten notes. Then type a clear instruction:

"Create a PDF study guide from these handwritten lecture notes. Organize the content into clear sections with headings, key points, and a short summary at the end. Highlight important terms."

Gemini processes the images, extracts the text, and builds a structured PDF. The file appears in the chat as a preview you can open immediately.

## Step 2: Preview the PDF in Chat

Tap or click the PDF preview. On mobile and web you can expand it to full screen. Review the layout, headings, and accuracy of the transcribed content. If a word was misread, note the section so you can correct it next.

Because PDF editing works on both platforms, you do not need a computer for this step.

## Step 3: Edit Directly or Ask Gemini to Refine

Three editing options are available:

- Expand the file to full screen and type changes yourself.
- Highlight a specific passage and ask a question about it.
- Tell Gemini to revise a section, for example “Make the second section more concise and add a bullet list of formulas.”

Gemini updates the file in place. You can iterate several times until the study guide matches what you need.

![Clean desk setup with laptop and notebook](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1758876021859-bd2371d8f0a2?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## Step 4: Export or Save the Finished Guide

Once the content looks right, use the export option in the preview window. You can download the PDF or send it to Google Drive. The same process works for other file types on the web, including Google Docs and Sheets.

## Practical Tips for Better Results

Write your initial prompt with the desired structure in mind. Mention whether you want definitions, practice questions, or diagrams described in text. If the notes contain diagrams or equations, describe them briefly in the prompt so Gemini can recreate or explain them.

Check transcription accuracy on technical terms or names. Gemini does well with clear handwriting, but dense or messy pages may need one round of corrections.

For longer courses, generate separate study guides per lecture and later combine them in a Google Doc on the web.

If you also use Gemini Notebook for deeper research or flashcards, you can upload the finished PDF there. See our guide on [Gemini Notebook study tools](/blog/gemini-notebook-study-tools-how-to-guide/) for complementary workflows.

## Limitations to Know

PDF editing is the most flexible option because it works on mobile. Creating or heavily editing Google Docs, Sheets, or slides still requires the web version. Large batches of pages may take longer to process. Always verify critical facts against your original notes before exams or presentations.

## Conclusion

The new in-chat file tools turn Gemini into a practical companion for students and anyone who captures information by hand. Upload notes, generate a clean PDF, and refine it in the same conversation. The result is a study guide you can review on any device without switching apps.

Try the workflow with your next set of lecture notes. Small adjustments in the prompt produce large differences in usefulness.
