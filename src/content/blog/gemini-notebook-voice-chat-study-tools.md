Gemini Notebook (the product formerly called NotebookLM) can now talk back. Google’s September 2026 update adds real-time voice conversations on the mobile app, an in-app audio recorder, interactive Learning Overviews, extra quiz formats, and short Video Overviews.

This guide walks through what shipped, who can use it, and a setup path that keeps answers tied to sources you uploaded. Facts below come from Google’s product blog, Workspace release notes, and the Gemini Notebook Help Center.

## What Google shipped in mid-September

On 15 September 2026, Google published [Sharpen your study routine with new Gemini Notebook tools](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-notebook/new-study-tools-september-2026/). Workspace followed on 18 September with the same feature list and rollout notes.

The practical additions:

- Real-time spoken conversation with a notebook on Android and iOS, in nearly 100 languages
- Audio recorder in the mobile app for lectures and spoken notes
- Interactive Learning Overviews under Reports, combining summaries with infographics, quizzes, and flashcards
- More quiz formats, plus follow-up chat about quiz results
- Short Video Overviews (~60 seconds) in more than 80 languages

Google says chat answers stay grounded in the sources inside that notebook. That is the difference between this voice mode and a general Gemini Live chat that can roam the open web.

## Who can use voice chat right now

Google’s Help Center article [Use chat in Gemini Notebook](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16179559) is the strictest source for access:

- Live Voice Chat is currently limited to **Google AI Ultra** subscribers
- Users must be **age 18+**
- It runs on the **Gemini Notebook mobile app**, not the desktop site

Android Authority’s coverage of the same announcement says Ultra gets the feature first, then Google AI Pro and broader accounts. Treat Help Center as the live gate. If you do not see an Audio control on the chat box, you are still in the typed-chat tier.

Workspace’s 18 September note says the wider study-tool package (recorder, Learning Overviews, video overviews, quizzes) is available to Google Workspace customers, Workspace Individual subscribers, and personal Google accounts. Rollout for Rapid and Scheduled Release domains started 15 September 2026 and may take longer than 15 days.

U.S. college students can claim one year of Google AI Pro at no charge under the student offer described in Google’s product post. That plan is not the same as Ultra, so voice chat may still wait behind the Ultra queue.

## Step 1: Build a notebook with real sources

Voice chat is only useful if the notebook already holds the material you want to quiz yourself on.

1. Open [notebook.google.com](https://notebook.google.com/) or the Gemini Notebook app.
2. Create a notebook for one course, project, or exam unit. Keep the scope narrow.
3. Add sources you trust: PDFs, slides, Docs, copied notes, or links Google accepts in that notebook.
4. Skim the auto-generated summary so you know what the model actually ingested.

If a lecture recording or PDF is missing pages, voice answers will miss them too. Chat uses quotes, text, and images from sources as citations. Hover a citation on web to read the quote; tap it to jump back into the source.

Google also warns that creative prompts such as “rewrite the ending of my short story” can return “Gemini Notebook can’t answer this question.” Stay inside the source set when you need citations.

## Step 2: Set chat style before you speak

On the web chat panel:

1. Select **Configure Chat**.
2. Pick a style: **Default** for research, **Learning Guide** for teaching, or **Custom** for a role you type yourself.
3. Set response length.
4. Save.

Learning Guide is the better default for study sessions. Default fits briefing a teammate. Custom works when you want a fixed persona, such as “quiz me like a teaching assistant and stop after two hints.”

Pin useful replies with **Save to note** so tables and inline citations stay on the noteboard. Clear history from the three-dots menu in the chat panel when a session should not linger.

## Step 3: Start Live Voice Chat on the phone

From Google’s Help Center steps:

1. Open the Gemini Notebook mobile app on Android or iOS.
2. Open the notebook you prepared.
3. At the bottom right of the chat box, tap **Audio**.
4. Speak. Ask for a definition, a worked example, or a recap of one section.
5. Interrupt when an explanation is too fast or too long. Google designed the session so you can cut in and redirect.
6. Tap **Mic** to mute. Tap **Close** to end the Live Voice Chat session.

Transcripts appear in chat after you talk. Review them the same way you review typed answers, including citations.

Google’s use policy still applies: ask people before you record them or drop their voice into a notebook.

## Step 4: Record lectures into the same notebook

Google says an audio recorder arrived in the mobile app the week after the 15 September post. Record a lecture or a voice memo. The file sits next to your other sources so later chat and citations can use it.

A clean workflow:

1. Start the recorder at the beginning of class or a meeting you are allowed to capture.
2. Stop when the session ends. Do not leave a two-hour file if only 20 minutes matter.
3. Add a short typed note with the date, course code, and topic.
4. After processing finishes, ask voice chat to list claims you should verify against the slide deck.

English is the language Google called out first for some note-plus-source flows on web and mobile. Confirm language support in your app build before you rely on a non-English lecture as the only source.

## Step 5: Build a Learning Overview instead of rereading

Under **Reports**, create an interactive Learning Overview. Google describes this as a bundle of the summary plus Studio outputs: infographics, quizzes, and flashcards.

Use it after the notebook has at least one solid source set:

1. Generate the overview for a single unit, not the entire semester notebook.
2. Run the quiz. Google added more formats and lets you add or edit questions.
3. After you finish, ask notebook chat how you did and where to study next.
4. If you need a fast recap, generate a Short Video Overview (~60 seconds) in one of the 80+ supported languages and share it only with people who should see those sources.

Video Overviews are study aids, not a substitute for reading the original paper or problem set.

## Prompts that stay inside the sources

Voice works best with short, checkable asks:

- “Explain section 3 of the uploaded syllabus in two minutes, then quiz me.”
- “What does source 2 say about the deadline? Quote it.”
- “Give me three flashcard pairs from the lecture recording. Stop after three.”
- “I missed the derivation. Walk through it from the PDF only.”

Avoid “what should I believe about this debate?” unless every side is in the notebook. The model will not invent a missing paper, and it may refuse creative rewrites that leave the source set.

## Limits and safety checks

Google documents several hard limits:

- Mobile chat can lag desktop features. Check the [mobile supported-features article](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16296687) when a button is missing.
- Agentic actions (web search, code, downloadable files, charts, Word, Excel, PowerPoint) are experimental. Google tells you to supervise them and double-check outputs.
- You can stop a running request, revise the prompt, or keep generating.
- Guided study is not the same as accessibility tools on the phone OS. For camera descriptions and motion comfort on Android, see the existing Yum News walkthrough of the [September 2026 Android Drop](/blog/android-september-2026-drop-guide/).

Do not upload work documents you are not allowed to store in a Google account. Notebook content follows your account’s Workspace or consumer data controls.

## A 20-minute study loop

1. Add this week’s PDF and one lecture recording (4 minutes).
2. Set chat to Learning Guide (1 minute).
3. Speak for five minutes: summary, then two interruptions on weak spots.
4. Generate a Learning Overview and one short quiz (5 minutes).
5. Save two cited answers as notes (2 minutes).
6. Skim citations against the PDF so a wrong quote does not stick (3 minutes).

Repeat the loop per unit. A single mega-notebook makes voice answers vaguer and citations harder to check.

## Conclusion

Gemini Notebook’s September 2026 tools matter because the voice layer still has to point at your files. Ultra subscribers on the mobile app can talk through a unit, cut in, and read the transcript later. Everyone else can still use Learning Overviews, quizzes, recordings, and Video Overviews as they reach each account type.

Load fewer, better sources. Turn on Learning Guide. Talk, then verify the citations. That is the whole product, minus the slogan.
