OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on 13 November 2026. It lets eligible ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States save a payment card once and have their dot agent complete purchases on their behalf. You review every transaction and approve it with Face ID or Touch ID. The agent never sees your full card details.

This guide walks through eligibility, setup, approving a purchase, and what to do if something fails. All steps come from OpenAI’s announcement, Wallet terms, privacy notice, and help center article.

## Who can use ChatGPT Wallet

ChatGPT Wallet is limited at launch.

You need a ChatGPT Pro subscription. You must be 18 or older, live in the United States, and have a US billing address. The card must be a US-issued American Express, Chase, Mastercard, or Visa card that belongs to you or that you are authorized to use. For Chase cards, you must be the primary cardholder and complete verification in the Chase app.

Wallet works only inside conversations with your dot. It is not available in regular Chat, ChatGPT Work, or standalone Codex threads. You must set up a dot first.

If you do not yet have a dot, follow the steps in our earlier guide to [set up ChatGPT dots](/blog/chatgpt-dots-setup-apps-tasks-controls/).

## How a purchase works

You add a card once. When your dot finds something to buy, it shows the merchant, items, total, and selected card. You review the details and approve with a biometric check. After approval, the payment network creates a payment token—a one-time substitute with its own card number, security code, and expiration date. The charge hits your real card, but neither the agent nor the merchant receives your actual card number or CVV.

You keep your existing card rewards and protections for eligible disputes. OpenAI does not take a cut of transactions at launch. Virtual cards are not supported yet.

## Add a card to ChatGPT Wallet

You can add a card from a conversation with your dot or from Settings.

**From your dot**  
In a conversation with your dot, say: “Help me add a card to Wallet.” Open the secure card-setup request that appears. Do not type the card number in the chat.

**From Settings**  
On mobile, open the left navigation, tap Settings, then Wallet, then Add Card. On desktop, go to Settings and select Wallet.

In the secure form:

1. Enter the card number, expiration date, and security code. For American Express, use the four-digit code on the front.
2. Review the ChatGPT Wallet terms and privacy notice, then select Add card.
3. Complete any bank verification your issuer requests. This may involve a code or approval inside your bank’s app. For eligible Chase cards, open the Chase app, sign in with biometrics, and approve the connection (scan a QR code if you started on a computer).
4. Wait until you see the confirmation that the card was added.

Cards saved in Wallet are separate from the payment method used for your ChatGPT subscription and from any gift-card balance.

## Approve a purchase

Once the card is saved, ask your dot to find or buy something. Example prompts from OpenAI: “Help me find a new sweater” or “Book me a hotel for my trip to Chicago next weekend.”

When a purchase request appears:

1. Review the merchant, items, total, selected card, shipping, and any recurring terms.
2. Select Approve and complete Face ID, Touch ID, or the equivalent biometric check on a supported device. If your current device cannot do biometrics, follow the link or QR code to a phone or computer signed into the same account.
3. After approval, the dot finishes checkout and reports the result.

“Ready to check out” means your approval succeeded. It does not guarantee the merchant accepted the order. Check the merchant’s confirmation. If you decline, the purchase does not proceed. After approval, contact the merchant to cancel or request a refund.

If you agree to a recurring plan, the merchant may store the payment credential and charge it again under the terms you accepted. Manage those charges with the merchant, not OpenAI.

## Manage cards and get help

Ask your dot to show saved cards. The list shows the nickname, last four digits, and expiration. To change the default, say “Make the card ending in 1234 my default.” To remove a card, identify it by the last four digits and confirm. Removal does not cancel existing orders or close the card with your bank.

For order status, returns, or refunds, contact the merchant. For declined or unrecognized charges, contact your card issuer. For Wallet errors or suspected unauthorized access to your ChatGPT account, contact OpenAI Support. Never send full card numbers, security codes, or verification codes in chat or support messages.

## Tips and limitations

- Rollout is gradual. If Wallet does not appear, confirm you meet every eligibility rule and that your dot is set up.
- Not every merchant accepts agent purchases yet. OpenAI published a Commerce Partner Playbook so businesses can allow signed agent requests and work with risk providers.
- Future updates may let you set spending limits or pre-approve categories, but today every purchase still requires explicit biometric approval.
- Wallet does not create a stored balance or bank account. It only tokenizes a card you already have.

## Conclusion

ChatGPT Wallet is the first official way for a ChatGPT dot to pay without ever handling your real card details. Eligible US Pro users can add a supported card once, review each purchase, and approve it with biometrics. The feature is new and limited, so check the official help article and Wallet terms before you start.
