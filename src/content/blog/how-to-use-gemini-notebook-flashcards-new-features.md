Google added three practical upgrades to flashcards inside Gemini Notebook on November 24, 2026. The tool now pulls relevant images from your uploaded sources, adds an optional stopwatch for timed review, and lets you schedule study reminders in the mobile app.

These changes sit on top of the existing flashcard generator. You still upload sources, open Studio, and generate a deck. The new pieces make the cards more visual and help you keep a consistent review habit. They are available now to personal Google accounts, Workspace Individual subscribers, and all Workspace customers. No extra paid plan is required.

If you already use Gemini Notebook for research or class notes, this update turns the same sources into stronger study material. For related study tools such as short video overviews, see our guide to [short video overviews in Gemini Notebook](/blog/gemini-notebook-short-video-overviews-guide/).

## What the three upgrades do

**Images on cards.** Gemini Notebook scans the PDFs, slides, or documents you added as sources. It places matching diagrams, maps, or photos next to the question or answer text. The images come only from your materials, so the visual stays tied to the same content you uploaded.

**Built-in stopwatch.** During a review session you can turn on a timer. It tracks how long the session lasts. Google notes this helps simulate timed exam conditions and lets you measure how quickly you recall information.

**Mobile study reminders.** In the Gemini Notebook app on your phone you can set a notification to revisit a deck you already created. The reminder appears at the time you choose so the cards do not sit unused after the first generation.

All three work without an administrator toggle. They rolled out to Rapid Release and Scheduled Release domains at the same time.

## Step-by-step: generate and use the new flashcards

1. Open [notebook.google.com](https://notebook.google.com) or the Gemini Notebook mobile app and sign in with your Google account.
2. Create a new notebook or open an existing one that already contains your study sources (PDF notes, lecture slides, a textbook chapter, or a Google Doc).
3. Confirm the sources you want are selected in the left panel. Uncheck anything that should not influence the cards.
4. In the Studio panel on the right, choose Flashcards.
5. Optionally set the number of cards, difficulty level, and a steering prompt if the controls appear. Then generate the deck.
6. Once the cards appear, review them. Relevant images from your sources should now sit beside the text on many cards.
7. Start a review session and look for the stopwatch control. Turn it on if you want to time the round.
8. On mobile, open the same notebook, find the flashcard deck, and set a reminder notification for a future review.

The process takes a few minutes once your sources are already in the notebook. Generate a fresh deck after you add new material so the images stay current.

## Practical tips for better results

Upload sources that already contain clear diagrams or labeled figures when visual learning matters. Anatomy slides, geography maps, or circuit diagrams produce the most useful image cards. Text-only notes still work; the tool simply falls back to text when no matching image exists.

Keep the steering prompt short and specific. “Focus on key definitions and include any labeled diagrams” usually yields cleaner cards than a long request. After generation, mark cards you know versus those you miss so you can re-generate or review the weaker set later.

Use the stopwatch for short, focused sessions rather than long blocks. A five-minute timed run on a 20-card deck quickly shows where recall slows down. Pair the mobile reminder with a fixed daily slot—after dinner or during a commute—so the habit sticks.

If a card shows the wrong image, regenerate the deck after tightening the source selection. The system draws only from the checked sources, so removing an unrelated PDF often fixes the mismatch.

## When the features help most

Students preparing for exams that include diagrams benefit immediately from the image cards. Language learners can pair vocabulary text with any illustrations that appear in their source PDFs. Workplace training teams can turn internal manuals that contain process charts into review decks without extra design work.

The stopwatch is useful for anyone who needs to build speed under pressure—certification tests, classroom quizzes, or simply measuring progress over several days. The reminders solve the common problem of generating cards once and never opening them again.

These upgrades do not replace quizzes, audio overviews, or video overviews inside the same Studio panel. They complement them. Generate a deck, review it with the timer, then listen to a short audio overview of the same sources while you commute.

## Limits and what to expect

Images appear only when the uploaded sources contain them and the system finds a relevant match. Pure text documents produce text-only cards. The stopwatch is optional and session-based; it does not store historical times across days. Reminders exist only in the mobile app; the web version does not send push notifications.

Google has not published exact generation limits for flashcards on free accounts. In practice most users can create multiple decks from a single notebook without hitting a hard wall on the free tier. If you hit a temporary limit, wait a few hours or switch notebooks.

Citations remain available when you chat with the sources, but the flashcards themselves do not display inline citation numbers on every card. Spot-check important facts against the original source if the topic is high-stakes.

## Conclusion

The November 2026 flashcard updates make Gemini Notebook more useful for active recall. Automatic images give visual context from materials you already own, the stopwatch adds a simple speed metric, and mobile reminders help you return to the decks. All three are live for free accounts today.

Upload a set of notes that contain a few diagrams, generate a deck, turn on the timer for one session, and set a reminder on your phone. That sequence shows the full value in under ten minutes.
