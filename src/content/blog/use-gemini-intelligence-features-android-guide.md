Google expanded Gemini Intelligence to more Android devices in November 2026. Pixel 10 series phones now receive features previously limited to Pixel 11, while task automation reaches users in the UK, India, and Japan. Compatible Samsung Galaxy and Motorola devices gain the same system-level AI capabilities.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="1" />

Gemini Intelligence brings proactive help, cleaner voice typing, and app automation directly into Android. You stay in control because Gemini acts only on explicit requests and stops when the task ends.

This guide shows how to check compatibility, enable the core features, and put them to work.

## Check Device Compatibility

Gemini Intelligence requires specific hardware and software. Supported devices currently include Pixel 10 and 11 series (except Pixel 10a), Samsung Galaxy S26 series, Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra, Z Fold8, Z Flip8, and the new motorola signature 27.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="11" />

Your phone needs Android 17 or later for full Device Assistance support. Confirm your system version under Settings > About phone. Features also vary by country and language. Task automation currently works in select markets including the US, UK, India, and Japan for users aged 18 and older.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="51" />

Update your Gemini app and Gboard from the Play Store before starting. On Pixel devices, install the latest Pixel Drop if available.

## Enable Gemini as Your Assistant

Most recent Pixel phones already use Gemini as the default assistant. On other Android devices, open the Gemini app from the Play Store and sign in with your Google account.

To set it as the digital assistant:

1. Open Settings.
2. Go to Apps > Default apps > Digital assistant app.
3. Select Google, then choose Gemini.

You can also long-press the power button or say “Hey Google” once Voice Match is trained. Open the Gemini app, tap your profile, then Settings > Talk to Gemini hands-free to enable the wake phrase.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="16" />

## Use Rambler for Cleaner Voice Typing

Rambler turns spoken thoughts into polished text. It removes filler words such as “um” and “uh,” fixes self-corrections, and formats grammar automatically. The feature now works on Pixel 10 series phones as well as Pixel 11 and the motorola signature 27.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="5" />

To activate Rambler:

1. Open any app where you type, such as Messages or Gmail.
2. Tap a text field to bring up Gboard.
3. Tap the microphone icon.
4. If prompted, select Start to enable Rambler. Otherwise, open Gboard settings (gear icon), go to Voice typing, and choose Rambler.

Speak naturally. Tap the checkmark when finished. The cleaned text appears in the field. You can continue speaking to edit the previous output, for example by saying “make it shorter” or “add a bullet list.”<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="49" />

Rambler requires an internet connection and works best in supported languages including English. It processes audio temporarily on Google’s servers, so review the result before sending.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="49" />

## Automate Multi-Step Tasks

Task automation lets Gemini open apps and complete steps such as ordering food, booking a ride, or making a reservation. It runs in a background virtual environment so you can watch or stop it at any time. The feature expanded to the UK, India, and Japan in November 2026.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="1" />

Open the Gemini app and describe the task clearly. Examples include “Order my usual coffee from the Starbucks app” or “Book a ride to the airport for 6 pm.” Gemini navigates the app, fills details, and stops before payment or final confirmation so you can review.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="14" />

Availability depends on the app and region. Samsung Galaxy devices currently support a smaller set of apps than Pixel phones. Always confirm the final screen before approving charges.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="14" />

For more on connecting apps, see our guide to [automating multi-step tasks with Gemini on Android](/blog/automate-multi-step-tasks-gemini-android/).

## Get Help with Device Assistance

Device Assistance turns Gemini into a personal tech support tool. Ask it to free storage, review location permissions, mute notifications while keeping important calls, or explain a setting. It understands your device configuration and acts on explicit requests.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="51" />

Enable it under Gemini app Settings > Personal Intelligence > Connected Apps > Device Assistance. Then try prompts such as “Clear cache for apps I rarely use” or “Which apps can access my location?”

This feature requires Android 17 or later on supported devices.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="51" />

## Tips for Better Results

- Speak or type specific requests. “Order lunch from my usual place for two people” works better than “order food.”
- Review automation steps on the progress screen. You can take over at any moment.
- Check Gemini app privacy settings to control chat history and model training. New controls let you keep history while opting out of training.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="2" />
- Update Gboard and the Gemini app regularly for the latest Rambler and automation improvements.
- Create custom widgets with Create My Widget on supported Pixel devices for glanceable information. See our related post on [creating custom widgets with Gemini](/blog/create-my-widget-gemini-android-guide/).

## Conclusion

Gemini Intelligence reduces routine work on compatible Android phones. Rambler speeds up messaging, task automation handles logistics across apps, and Device Assistance answers phone questions without menu hunting. Start with the features available on your device and region, then expand as updates arrive.

Check official Google support pages for the latest availability, since rollout continues over the coming weeks.
