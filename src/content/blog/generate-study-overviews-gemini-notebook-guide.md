Exam season or a dense reading list can overwhelm anyone. Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) now offers tools that turn your own materials into structured study aids. Short Video Overviews and Interactive Learning Overviews help you review faster without inventing information outside your sources.

This guide shows exactly how to create both, based on Google’s official announcements from September and October 2026.

![Student studying with laptop and notebook at a desk](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1758612898312-708f2ffdcd53?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What the New Study Features Do

Google rolled out three study improvements in Gemini Notebook. Two stand out for most learners:

Short Video Overviews create roughly 60-second videos. They combine a short narrative with educational animations. These help with formulas, diagrams, or concepts that benefit from visual explanation. You can watch them on the go or share them.

Interactive Learning Overviews appear under Reports. They walk you through your materials step by step. The overview mixes summaries with Studio outputs such as slides, quizzes, and flashcards. Everything stays grounded in the sources you uploaded.

Expanded quiz formats (short-answer, multi-select, fill-in-the-blank) complement these, but this guide focuses on the two overview types.

These features build on earlier Video Overviews. They remain source-grounded, so responses cite your documents rather than general web knowledge.

## Prepare Your Notebook

Start at notebook.google or the Gemini Notebook mobile app. Create a new notebook or open an existing one.

Upload or link your sources: PDFs, Google Docs, Slides, websites, YouTube videos, or audio recordings of lectures. Select only the sources relevant to the topic you want to study. Fewer, higher-quality sources usually produce clearer results.

Confirm the notebook language settings match your preferred output language. Short Video Overviews support more than 80 languages according to Google’s September 2026 update.

If you are a student, check eligibility for the Google AI student plan. It raises usage limits in Gemini Notebook.

## Create a Short Video Overview

Open the Studio panel in your notebook.

Locate the Video Overview option. Select it, then choose the Short format if available (some accounts show Explainer, Cinematic, and Short variants).

Add a custom prompt if the interface offers one. Examples that work well:

- Focus on the main formula and one real-world example from the sources.
- Explain this concept for a first-year student with simple language and one diagram callout.
- Highlight the three most important points from the uploaded lecture notes.

Submit the request. Generation typically takes a few minutes. When finished, the video appears as a card in Studio. Play it, download it if the option appears, or share the link with classmates.

Short videos work best for a single concept. If your sources cover many topics, create separate notebooks or deselect unused sources before generating.

## Create an Interactive Learning Overview

In the same Studio panel, open Reports.

Choose Interactive Learning Overview (or the closest matching report type if the exact label varies by rollout stage). Provide a brief instruction such as "Create a step-by-step review of the key concepts in these sources for exam preparation."

The tool weaves together summaries and existing or newly generated Studio items: slides, quizzes, and flashcards. It keeps every section tied to your uploaded materials.

Review the outline when prompted. Adjust the order of sections or ask to expand a specific part before final generation. Once complete, the overview becomes a navigable report you can revisit during study sessions.

After using quizzes or flashcards inside the overview, return to the chat and ask the notebook to identify weak areas based on your answers. This keeps the next study block targeted.

## Tips for Better Results

Select sources carefully. A focused set of lecture slides plus one textbook chapter produces tighter overviews than a large mixed folder.

Use the customization box on Video Overviews to set audience and learning goal. Vague prompts yield generic output.

Generate the Short Video first for a quick refresher, then the Interactive Learning Overview for deeper review.

Check citations. Click source links in the overview or video description to verify the original passage.

If a feature is missing, the rollout may still be reaching your account. Refresh the page or check again after a day. Google noted staggered availability in the September 2026 post.

For related ways to shape slides and longer videos, see our guide on [co-creating slides and videos in Gemini Notebook](/blog/co-create-slides-videos-gemini-notebook/).

## Limitations to Keep in Mind

Outputs stay limited to the sources you provide. They do not pull in external facts unless you add those sources yourself.

Short videos run about 60 seconds, so complex topics need multiple clips or a full Interactive Overview.

Usage limits apply, especially on free plans. Higher-tier Google AI plans increase daily allowances.

Visual quality depends on the clarity of diagrams already in your sources. Hand-drawn notes may need cleaner uploads for the best animation results.

## Conclusion

Gemini Notebook’s Short Video Overviews and Interactive Learning Overviews turn your existing materials into focused study tools. Upload sources, select the relevant Studio or Reports option, add a clear prompt, and review the result against the original documents.

Start with one difficult concept today. Generate a 60-second video, then build the interactive overview for the full chapter. You will spend less time organizing notes and more time testing what you actually know.
