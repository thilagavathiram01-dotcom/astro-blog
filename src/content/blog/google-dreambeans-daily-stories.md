Google Labs built Dreambeans as a short, finite stack of stories instead of another infinite feed. On 10 September 2026 the experiment opened to all U.S. Google accounts age 18 and over on Android and iOS. You no longer need Google AI Ultra to try it.

The app uses Personal Intelligence, with your permission, to pull context from the Google apps you choose. It then writes a small daily collection: topics you might miss, places you might visit, and follow-up actions you can take the same day.

This guide follows Google’s Labs posts and the Android help page so you can install the app, pick sources, and tune the next day’s pack.

## What Dreambeans is (and is not)

Dreambeans is a Google Labs experiment, not a replacement for Gmail, Calendar, or the Gemini app. Google’s June launch post describes it as a daily collection of personalized stories that “cut through the clutter.” The September expansion post adds Gemini as a connected source and confirms the U.S. 18+ rollout on both phone platforms.

Each “dreambean” mixes:

- **Personalized topics** such as places to explore, shows to watch, or a reminder of an upcoming event.
- **Connected apps** you select: Calendar, Gmail, Photos, Search, YouTube, and now Gemini.

If you connect Google Photos, stories can include generated illustrations that reflect people and places you already photograph. Google names **Nano Banana 2** as the image model used for those unique pictures.

The feed is meant to end. Google’s product copy repeats that the point is a finite set of stories, not a scroll that never stops. When a card is useful, you tap through for web-backed next steps, bookmark it to a library, or share it.

Dreambeans is also not the same product as Gemini Daily Brief. Daily Brief is a morning triage list inside Gemini that leans on Gmail, Calendar, and Gemini chats. Dreambeans lives in its own app and writes narrative cards. Use both if you want a task list *and* a short reading pack. See our [Gemini Daily Brief setup guide](/blog/gemini-daily-brief-setup/) if you want the inbox-first agent first.

## Check who can use it today

Treat the **10 September 2026 Labs blog** as the current access rule, then use the help page for device steps.

You need all of the following:

- Age **18 or older**.
- A **U.S.** location and a personal Google Account.
- An **Android** phone on Android 10 or later, or a supported **iOS** device.
- English as the supported language. Labs Help still lists English only.

At launch in June 2026, Dreambeans required **Google AI Ultra** plus a waitlist. The September post changed that: the app is available to **all accounts in the U.S. (18+)** on Android and iOS. The official YouTube clip from Google Labs repeats “no subscription required.”

The Android help article may still mention Ultra and a waitlist form. If Play Store lets you install and sign in without Ultra, follow the September announcement. If the app still blocks the account, submit the Labs waitlist form linked from [labs.google/dreambeans](https://labs.google/dreambeans) and retry after the account is approved.

Work or school Workspace logins are not described as supported. Use the personal account you already use for Gmail and Photos.

## Install Dreambeans on Android

Google documents these steps for Android:

1. Open the Play Store on a phone running **Android 10 or higher**.
2. Search for **Dreambeans** and pick the official Google Labs listing (package `com.google.android.apps.labs.lotus`).
3. Tap **Install**, then open the app.
4. Sign in with your **personal** Google Account.
5. Choose which Google apps to connect. You must connect **at least one** app or the product will not generate stories.

On iPhone, search the App Store for Dreambeans (Apple lists bundle id `id6748778630` on the June blog) and complete the same sign-in and app-connect screens.

Connect only sources you are willing to let a Labs experiment read. Google states that the choices you make *inside Dreambeans* do not change Personal Intelligence settings in Gemini Apps or AI Mode. You can add or remove connected apps later without rebuilding your Gemini setup.

## Pick sources that produce useful cards

Google’s examples are concrete. A Gmail delivery note for puppy treats produced training tips. A Calendar reminder that a friend was coming to town produced nearby dog-friendly restaurants. Those cards work because the source events were dated and specific.

Use this source map:

- **Calendar** — trips, birthdays, and visits. Best first connection if you want same-week recommendations.
- **Gmail** — order confirmations, tickets, and school or client threads you already act on.
- **Photos** — people and places that should appear in the generated illustrations.
- **YouTube and Search** — hobbies and research threads that should show up as “things you might love.”
- **Gemini** — added in the September expansion. The Labs video says Dreambeans can use nuance from your Gemini chats so later stories track topics you already discussed.

Start with Calendar plus one more app. Turn on Photos only if you want faces and locations in the art. Turn on Gmail only if you accept that order receipts and travel mail will feed story ideas.

Dreambeans “works best when they are all enabled,” according to the June post, but that is a quality note, not a requirement beyond the one-app minimum.

## Read, save, and correct a day’s pack

Open the app in the morning. You should see a short stack, not a timeline.

On each card:

- Read the story and the illustration.
- Tap through to the deeper view when you want maps, class listings, or other web-backed actions. Google’s example actions include nearest dog parks and puppy training classes.
- Bookmark keepers to the **library** so you can reopen them after the daily pack refreshes.
- Share a card if a classmate or travel partner needs the same prompt.
- Leave feedback when a recommendation is wrong, or when the app missed a hobby you care about. Google says that signal changes later collections.

Do not treat a story as a calendar event until you confirm the date in Calendar. Dreambeans can invent a useful outing from a real reminder; it is still an experiment and can miss context.

If a card cites a photo or a chat you did not expect, disconnect that source in the in-app app list. That change stays inside Dreambeans.

## Privacy and safety limits

Keep three official limits in view.

First, permission is per product. Connecting Photos in Dreambeans is not the same toggle as Personal Intelligence in Gemini.

Second, the experiment is U.S.-only and English-only in the published help. There is no official global client or extra language pack to install.

Third, Labs experiments can change or close. Save anything you need into Calendar, Keep, or a shared doc instead of treating the library as long-term storage.

Skip sideloaded APKs and VPN tricks. The app checks the Google Account and region. An unofficial package does not create a supported login.

## Tips that improve the next pack

- Put real dates on Calendar. Vague “sometime this month” events give the model less to work with than a Friday dinner.
- Keep Gemini chats on topics you want reflected after you connect Gemini as a source.
- Use Photos albums you already curate if you care about who appears in illustrations.
- Give negative feedback once, then wait a day. Repeated taps on the same bad card teach less than a single clear “not right” plus a note about the missing hobby.
- Pair Dreambeans with [Gemini Daily Brief](/blog/gemini-daily-brief-setup/) if you also want a checkbox list of mail and meetings. One product narrates; the other triages.

## Conclusion

Dreambeans is worth ten minutes of setup if you already live in Gmail, Calendar, and Photos and you want a short morning read instead of another social feed. Install the official app, connect at least one source, and judge the experiment after two or three daily packs—not after the first cute illustration.

If the cards track real trips and hobbies, leave the sources on and keep using the library. If they wander, disconnect the noisy app or delete the experiment. Google published the off-ramps; use them.
