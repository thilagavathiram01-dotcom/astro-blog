Google has made its agentic Tasks feature free for hundreds of millions of people. On November 17, 2026, the company expanded Tasks in the Gemini app to users in the U.S., Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India without requiring a paid Google AI subscription. The feature runs on the new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model and turns the chatbot into a background worker that can complete multi-step jobs while you do other things.

If you already use Gemini for quick answers, Tasks changes the relationship. You describe a goal once. Gemini plans the steps, works in the cloud, and checks back when it needs approval. This guide shows you how to turn it on, what it can do today, and how to avoid common limits.

## What Gemini Tasks Actually Does

Chat mode answers your question and stops. Task mode keeps working after you close the app. You can ask it to research three flight options under a price limit and put the results in a spreadsheet. You can ask it to scan school emails and add every date to your calendar. You can set a recurring schedule so it delivers a morning briefing every day.

In the United States, Tasks can also place phone calls to businesses, complete purchases through Google Pay with a spending cap you set, accept instructions by text message, and act on emails you forward to gemini@google.com. These advanced features are rolling out starting in the U.S. Google states that Gemini discloses it is an AI on calls, businesses can opt out, and it will not share payment or private account details unless you approve.

All of this runs on Gemini 4 Hydrogen. Google says the model finishes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash and cuts fabricated claims by more than 5x. Free users reach Hydrogen through automatic model selection when it fits the prompt.

## Requirements Before You Start

Tasks is not available to everyone. Google’s help documentation lists clear requirements:

- You must be 18 or older.
- You must sign in with a personal Google Account. Work and school accounts cannot use Tasks.
- Gemini History must be turned on.
- You can use the Gemini mobile app (Android or iOS), the Gemini app on Mac, or gemini.google.com.

Outside the six free countries, Tasks requires a Google AI Pro or Ultra subscription. The feature is not offered at all in the European Economic Area, Nigeria, Switzerland, or the United Kingdom.

You can run up to 15 tasks at the same time on every plan. Free and AI Plus users can keep 10 active schedules that run at most once a day in a rough time window. Pro and Ultra users get 50 schedules with more precise timing plus Gmail and topic monitors. Usage is compute-based and refreshes on a rolling window. Long research tasks consume more of your allowance than short ones.

## How to Turn On and Start a Task

The toggle is built into the main Gemini interface.

1. Open the Gemini app or go to gemini.google.com and sign in with your personal account.
2. Look for the Chat / Task toggle. On mobile it sits above the prompt box. On the web it sits below.
3. Tap or click Task. The prompt area changes to something like “What can I get done for you?” and the model switches to Hydrogen.
4. Type a clear description of the job. Include budget, deadline, constraints, and what you want the final output to look like. Example: “Compare three USB-C chargers under $40 that support 65W. Put the top options in a Google Sheet with price, wattage, and a link.”
5. Submit. Gemini confirms once that you want it to take on tasks. After that it works in the background.

You can jump back into a running task at any time and add instructions. Gemini is designed to pause and ask before critical steps such as clicking Buy. For routine actions you trust, you can set “Always allow” so it does not keep interrupting.

To stop a task, use the Stop button in the interface.

## Practical Examples That Work Today

Google’s announcement and user reports highlight several patterns that succeed.

**Calendar and email cleanup.** Forward a batch of school or work emails to gemini@google.com (U.S. feature) or paste the content into a Task. Ask Gemini to extract every date, create calendar events, and invite the relevant people. It can also flag conflicts.

**Research with a deliverable.** “Find the three cheapest flights from my city to Tokyo in March under $900 and put them in a Sheet with airline, times, and total price.” Gemini browses, compares, and returns a structured file.

**Shopping with a cap.** “Find a 65W USB-C charger under $35 that has good recent reviews. Set a $40 spending limit and stop before checkout.” In the U.S. it can complete the purchase via Google Pay after you confirm, and receipts land in Google Wallet.

**Recurring briefings.** Set a schedule for “every weekday morning, summarize unread Gmail, today’s calendar, and any weather alerts.” Free users get one rough daily window; paid users get tighter control.

If you have already experimented with multi-step automation on Android, the [automate multi-step tasks guide](/blog/automate-multi-step-tasks-gemini-android/) shows older power-button and workflow patterns that still complement the new Task mode.

## Safety and Limits You Should Know

When you assign a task, Gemini runs it inside Google Cloud’s Agent Compute Engine. Each task gets a hardware-isolated virtual machine dedicated to your account. The environment is sandboxed, scanned for prompt injection, and limited by Google Safe Browsing so Gemini cannot open arbitrary links.

Gemini is designed to ask for confirmation before purchases and to ask clarifying questions when instructions are ambiguous. You can review or delete history at any time and disconnect Connected Apps in settings.

Calling is currently limited. Google notes it is not available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, and Nebraska. Texting requires a U.S. phone number on an eligible carrier. Shopping uses the Agent Payments Protocol with tokenized cards from supported networks so you still earn points and can set a hard spending cap.

Tasks can fail or hand the final click back to you on some websites. Google recommends giving a thumbs-down when results miss the mark so the system improves. Do not paste passwords or full card numbers into the task prompt.

## Tips for Better Results

Write the job, not a question. “Book an early Friday service appointment at the two nearest Honda dealerships and add it to my calendar” works better than “my car needs service.”

Connect the apps you need first. Gmail, Calendar, Drive, and Password Manager (for portal logins) all improve success rates.

Start with one or two tasks while you learn the limits. Fifteen concurrent tasks is the hard ceiling, and a schedule will not fire if that number is already running.

Check the Stop button and the task list regularly until you trust the pattern. You remain in control.

## Conclusion

Tasks in Gemini is the first widely available free agent that can keep working after you close the app. In the six launch countries it requires only a personal account, age 18+, and History turned on. The Hydrogen model handles the reasoning. You handle the approvals.

Open Gemini, flip the toggle to Task, and hand off one concrete job today. The busywork that used to sit on your list can move into the background while you stay in control of the final steps.
