Googlebook reached US store shelves on October 4, 2026. Google says you can close the laptop while Gemini Spark keeps working on a complex request in the background. That is the feature worth setting up on day one, before you spend the afternoon hunting for cables.

This guide covers what Google has actually published about Spark on a closed lid, plus the on-device tools that feed it work: Magic Pointer, Rambler, and Create My Widget. Phone pairing is a separate step. If you have not linked your Android phone yet, start with [Googlebook launch-day phone pairing](/blog/googlebook-launch-day-phone-pairing/).

## What Google confirmed for launch day

Alexander Kuscher, senior director for laptops and tablets, described Googlebook as a laptop built so Gemini sits on the device, not only in a browser tab. The October 4 US retail date, and October 5 dates for Canada, the UK, Ireland, France, Germany, and Australia, come from the same September 21 product post.

Google lists a starting price of $899. Every Googlebook includes 12 months of Google AI Pro, which Google says provides 5TB of cloud storage and higher usage limits in Gemini, plus 3 months of YouTube Premium, Adobe Photoshop, and more. Googlebook OS is scheduled for regular feature drops and updates for up to 10 years.

Spark is not a separate download in that post. Google says the Gemini features already used on Chrome and Android ship on Googlebook, including task automations, Gemini Live, and proactive suggestions based on what is on screen. The closed-lid line is specific: you can close the laptop while Gemini Spark processes complex requests and finishes tasks.

## Sign in before you close the lid

Spark needs an account that can run Gemini on the device. On first boot, sign in with the Google account you want tied to the included AI Pro period. Google states that the 12-month AI Pro bundle comes with every Googlebook. Treat the retail box and the first-run setup screens as the source of the redemption prompt. Do not assume a second Google account on the same laptop inherits that year of Pro.

Connect to Wi-Fi before you hand Spark a long job. A closed lid does not create a network. If the task needs Gmail, Drive, Calendar, or the web, those calls still need a connection Google has already described for Gemini on the device.

If you already schedule Spark jobs on Android, the same idea applies here. The [Gemini Spark task schedules guide](/blog/gemini-spark-task-schedules/) covers recurring work on the phone. On Googlebook, the new piece is that a job can continue after the display is shut.

## Hand Spark a job you can verify later

Google has not published a click-path for a “finish after close” toggle. Use a task whose result you can check when you open the lid again.

1. Open Gemini on the Googlebook and start a request that takes more than a quick reply. Good candidates from Google’s own examples include turning a training plan on a web page into Calendar events, or checking whether an email looks like spam.
2. Ask Spark to finish the work and leave the result in a place you already use, such as a Calendar event or a note. Avoid jobs that require you to approve a purchase or send a message while the lid is shut.
3. Close the lid only after Gemini has accepted the request. Google’s claim is that Spark can keep processing in the background, not that a half-typed prompt survives a shutdown.
4. Open the laptop later and read the result. If the event, note, or reply is missing, rerun the request with the lid open so you can see where it stopped.

Treat the closed lid as background processing, not as a promise that every agent step will complete offline. Google has not said Spark ignores sleep, low battery, or a dropped network.

## Use Magic Pointer to give Spark a precise target

Magic Pointer is the fastest way to point Spark at the thing on screen. Google says a quick cursor wiggle brings Gemini to where you are working. It stays off until you summon it. It understands text, images, and context.

Google published three patterns:

- Highlight a marathon training plan on a web page and ask Gemini to map the runs onto Google Calendar.
- Hover a suspicious email and ask Gemini to review the text and images for spam versus a real message.
- Select several images and ask Gemini to visualize them together, without a separate download and upload step.

Wiggle, point, ask, then decide whether the follow-up belongs in Spark. A Calendar mapping job is a better closed-lid test than a one-line definition, because you can see the events when you return.

Privacy note from Google: Magic Pointer only starts when you wiggle the cursor. It is not described as an always-on screen watcher.

## Turn a Rambler brain dump into the task brief

Rambler is the voice path into that same workflow. Google says ordinary dictation writes every stutter and filler. Rambler is meant to take a messy verbal dump, clean errors, turn action items into bullets, and add emoji. It is natively multilingual, including a language switch mid-sentence.

Use it when you would rather talk than outline:

1. Open Rambler while you draft notes or a task list.
2. Talk through the work in the order you remember it. Name the output you want, such as bullets in a doc or events on a calendar.
3. Read the structured notes before you send them to Spark. Rambler organizes speech. It does not replace a check of dates and names.
4. Pass the cleaned list to Gemini Spark if the remaining work is multi-step, then close the lid.

That split keeps the voice step short and leaves the slower organizing job for background processing.

## Add a widget only after the first Spark job works

Create My Widget lets you describe a widget with no code. Google’s examples are a sports-score tracker and a countdown to a family trip. Build one after Spark is returning results, so you are not debugging two new surfaces at once.

Developers get a second path. Google Antigravity ships on every Googlebook. You can write apps and deploy them on the device. A full Linux terminal is available for tools such as Claude Code or the Antigravity CLI. That environment is for building software, not for the closed-lid Spark test above.

## Tips before you rely on a closed lid

- Plug in if the task may run for a while. Google has not published a battery floor for background Spark.
- Keep the first closed-lid job reversible. Calendar drafts and notes are easier to undo than sent email.
- Use Magic Pointer on content you are allowed to share with Gemini. A wiggle sends that context when you ask.
- Confirm the included AI Pro period on the account you use daily. Higher Gemini limits are part of the bundle Google lists with the laptop.
- Retail timing differs by country. US shelves are October 4. Canada, the UK, Ireland, France, Germany, and Australia follow on October 5.

## What to do with the result

Open the lid, read the Spark output, and correct anything the model inferred. Google’s examples are assistance tasks: scheduling from a page, checking an email, combining images, structuring a voice note. None of those replace your own confirmation before you share the result.

If the background job did not finish, run it again with the display open and a narrower ask. Spark on Googlebook is useful because the laptop can keep working after you walk away. It is still a Gemini request, and the finished note or calendar block is what you should trust only after you read it.
