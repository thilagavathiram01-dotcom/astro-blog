Google rolled out Genie 4 inside Project Genie on November 18, 2026. Eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers can now generate interactive worlds in the browser, edit them while exploring, and share the results.

This is the first public access to Genie 4 after the October research preview. The update also adds a conversational agent, a community gallery, and longer sessions for Genie 3. Google AI Pro members can join a waitlist on the same page.

If you followed the earlier waitlist steps in our [How to Access Google Genie 4 Interactive Worlds](/blog/access-google-genie-4-interactive-worlds/) post, this guide covers what changed once the model went live.

## Who Can Use Genie 4 Right Now

Access is limited to eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers (18+). The experience runs entirely in the browser at labs.google/projectgenie — no separate app or download is required.

Google AI Pro subscribers see a waitlist option on the same page. Free accounts are not included in the initial rollout. Exact country eligibility is not listed in the announcement, so check the Project Genie page while signed into your Ultra account.

All generated video and audio carries SynthID watermarking, and inputs and outputs pass safety screenings.

## What Genie 4 Adds in Project Genie

Genie 4 is Google DeepMind’s most realistic and interactive world model to date. It builds on Gemini models: Gemini Omni anchors appearance and behavior, while other Gemini models help maintain coherence.

Key capabilities now available:

- Mid-session prompting. Type a new prompt while you navigate to change weather, add characters, move the camera, or redirect action.
- Native audio. Real-time ambient sound, music, and character dialogue are generated with the visuals and adapt as the scene changes.
- Higher-fidelity visuals and physics compared with earlier Genie models.

Genie 3 remains available in the model picker for longer runs. Its updates include sessions up to 3 minutes (previously 60 seconds), world chaining, 360-degree panorama image grounding, and real-time in-world events.

## Step-by-Step: Create and Explore a World with Genie 4

Follow these steps once you have access:

1. Go to labs.google/projectgenie and sign in with the Google account tied to your AI Ultra subscription.
2. Start a new world. You can describe the scene in text, upload an image, or use the Project Genie agent (covered below).
3. Choose Genie 4 from the model picker if multiple options appear.
4. Enter the world. Use standard keyboard controls to move and look around. The environment generates in real time as you navigate.
5. Edit mid-session. Type a short, specific prompt while inside the world — for example, “start heavy rain,” “add a dog that barks in the distance,” or “pull the camera back.” The change applies without restarting the session.
6. Listen for native audio. Sound is generated with the visuals and responds to events and prompts.
7. End or save the session when finished. You can publish to the gallery if the option is available.

Keep prompts short and concrete for better results. Describe the desired change clearly rather than rewriting the entire scene.

## Use the Project Genie Agent

The new agent turns a conversation into a starting world. Describe the story or simulation you want. The agent orchestrates multimodal models to create initial visuals and a matching background score.

During the session, Gemini can observe what is happening and surface suggestion chips or trigger events automatically. This reduces the need to write every detail yourself.

You can also remix existing gallery worlds by adjusting structure, soundtrack, or rules through the agent.

## Explore and Remix the Community Gallery

The gallery collects curated experiences and community creations. Examples mentioned in the announcement include “Living Memory” (personal photos turned into explorable spaces), “Dino Field Guide” (prehistoric habitats from 360-degree panoramas), and “Rock Paper Scissors” (themed objects on a desk with Gemini as referee).

Collaborations shown include “Villa Giallo” (gothic manor mystery with Will aka BLVCKL!GHT) and “The Life of Ants” (colony navigation with filmmaker Anand Gandhi). You can jump into these, remix them, or publish your own worlds for others to explore.

## Practical Tips for Better Results

- Start with a clear opening prompt that includes environment, lighting, and any important text (signs or labels). Clear text usually only appears if specified early.
- Prefer short mid-session edits over long rewrites. Specific requests such as “change to sunset lighting” or “introduce a flying character” work more reliably.
- Switch to Genie 3 when you need longer continuous exploration or 360-degree panorama seeding.
- Test object permanence and fine detail. Research notes highlight ongoing challenges with camera latency, intricate physical interactions, real-time audio precision, and long-term scene permanence.
- Review outputs. Worlds remain research outputs and can contain inconsistencies.
- Publish selectively. Once a world is in the gallery, others can explore and remix it.

## Current Limitations

Genie 3 and Genie 4 are still frontier research models. Google notes active work on camera latency, complex physical interactions, precise real-time audio, and long-term scene permanence. Session length for Genie 4 itself is not stated in the November 18 announcement (Genie 3 now supports up to 3 minutes).

Access remains gated to eligible Ultra subscribers. No public API model identifier or general pricing for Genie 4 has been published.
