Google rolled out the built-in audio recorder widely in the Gemini Notebook mobile app for Android and iOS on October 27, 2026. You can capture lectures or your own thoughts on the go and turn them into sources you can chat with, cite, and edit later.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="25" />

The feature first appeared in Google's September 15 announcement. It is now reaching most accounts. If you do not see the microphone icon, force-stop the app and reopen it.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="45" />

## What the Mobile Recorder Does

A new microphone button sits between the camera icon and the Create New floating action button at the bottom of the app. Tap it to start recording.

Starting from the home screen creates a fresh notebook. Starting from the Sources tab of an open notebook adds the recording to that notebook. Recordings continue in the background if you switch apps or lock the screen. Each session is limited to one hour.

Google states that your notes live directly alongside your sources. You can chat about them, cite them, and keep editing.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="56" />

## Step-by-Step: Start a Recording

1. Open the Gemini Notebook app on your Android or iOS device. Make sure you are signed in.
2. Look for the microphone icon at the bottom of the screen.
3. Choose your starting point. Tap the microphone on the home screen to begin a new notebook. Open an existing notebook, go to Sources, and tap the microphone there to add to that notebook.
4. Grant microphone permission if prompted.
5. Speak or place the phone where it can hear the lecture clearly. A waveform and live timer appear.
6. Use the pause button if you need to stop temporarily. Tap the checkmark when you finish. This starts the upload.

The transcript becomes a source in the notebook. You can then ask questions, generate quizzes, or create other outputs based on it.

## Manage Recordings and Sources

After upload, the recording appears as a source next to any documents, slides, or photos you already added. You can open it, review the transcript, and edit notes.

Combine a live recording with photos of handwritten notes or the board for a fuller set of sources. Google highlighted this pairing in its official post.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="56" />

The Create New button now starts a notebook immediately instead of opening the add-source panel. This small change arrived around the same time as the wider recorder rollout.

## Tips for Better Results

Place the phone closer to the speaker and avoid covering the microphone. Background noise reduces transcript quality.

For lectures longer than one hour, stop and start a second recording. You can add both to the same notebook.

Check local rules before recording other people. Many schools and workplaces require permission.

The September announcement noted English as the selected output language for full support, including for users under 18. Confirm your settings if results seem limited.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="45" />

Once the source is ready, try prompts such as “Summarize the main points of this lecture” or “Create three quiz questions from the second half.”

## Conclusion

The mobile audio recorder turns Gemini Notebook into a practical tool for capturing spoken material without a separate app. Record from the home screen or an existing notebook, finish with the checkmark, and treat the transcript as any other source.

Update the app if the microphone does not appear, then test a short recording to confirm the workflow on your device.
