Google’s Pixel Tag gives Android users a reliable way to track keys, wallets, bags, and other everyday items. It works through the Find Hub app and the network of more than one billion Android devices. The tag is slim enough for a wallet, carries a replaceable CR2032 battery that lasts over a year, and supports Ultra Wideband for precise nearby location.

This guide walks through official setup steps, how to find items on a map or nearby, sharing with family, and practical tips drawn from Google’s support documentation.

## What You Need Before Starting

Your Android phone must run Android 9 or later and have Bluetooth turned on plus an active internet connection. The Find Hub app should be installed from the Play Store. Place the Pixel Tag next to your phone and fully remove the plastic battery tab so the tag powers on.

Avoid placing the tag inside carbon-fiber or aluminum luggage or RFID-blocking wallets, as these can block the signal. For checked baggage, use exterior fabric pockets. The battery itself meets airline safety rules for both checked and carry-on bags.

## Connect the Pixel Tag with Fast Pair

Follow these steps from Google’s official support page:

1. Confirm Bluetooth is on and the phone has internet access.
2. Hold the Pixel Tag right next to the unlocked phone on the home screen.
3. Remove the plastic tab covering the battery contact.
4. Wait for the Fast Pair pop-up, then tap Connect.
5. Review the Acceptable Use Policy and tap Continue.
6. Accept the Terms and Conditions.
7. Grant Location permission when prompted by choosing “When using this app.”

The tag appears in the Find Hub app. You can rename it by choosing a category such as Keys, Backpack, or Other. The app combines your name with the category, for example “Alex’s keys.”

If the pop-up does not appear, open Settings > Connected devices and look for the tag at the top. Alternatively, open Find Hub, tap Add Device, and follow the on-screen prompts. If you dismissed the prompt once, wait five minutes; a second dismissal requires a 24-hour wait unless you toggle Bluetooth or restart the phone.

## Locate Items with Find Hub

Open the Find Hub app and select your Pixel Tag. Two main options appear depending on distance.

For nearby items within Bluetooth range, tap Find nearby. Supported phones show directional guidance and a distance meter. On devices with Ultra Wideband (including many Pixel 8 Pro and newer models), an arrow and precise distance reading help you walk straight to the item. You can also play a sound so the tag chimes if it is under a cushion or in a bag.

When the item is farther away, the map displays its last reported location. Find Hub encrypts location data end-to-end, so only you and people you share the tag with can see it. Google cannot access the position.

The tag also has a physical button. Press it twice to ring your phone if you have the tag but cannot find the handset. Unlock the phone or press the power button to stop the ringing.

## Share Ownership and Set Reminders

You can share a Pixel Tag with up to ten other people. In Find Hub, open the tag and tap Share ownership. Select contacts and send the invitation through any messaging app. Recipients have 24 hours to accept on an Android device. After acceptance, the owner confirms the share in the tag’s settings. Shared users see a 4-digit PIN for extra security.

Left Behind Reminders notify you if you walk away from the tag. Enable the option in the tag’s settings so you receive an alert when the phone and tag separate. This pairs well with other Find Hub features such as marking an item as lost so finders can contact you.

For more on left-behind alerts, see our related guide on [Find Hub left-behind reminders](/blog/find-hub-left-behind-reminders-guide/).

## Replace the Battery

The CR2032 battery is user-replaceable without tools. Press the back cover, twist counterclockwise to open, swap the cell, and twist clockwise to close. Find Hub warns you every two weeks once the battery runs low, so you have time to replace it before the tag stops reporting.

## Practical Tips for Daily Use

Attach the tag securely to keys with a keyring or slip it into a wallet or bag pocket. Test the sound volume in settings; three levels are available, with the loudest around 80 dB. Check that your phone supports UWB if you want the full Precision Finding arrow; not every UWB-equipped device enables the feature for third-party tags.

Keep the Find Hub app updated. If location updates seem delayed, confirm the phone’s Location services are on and that the tag is not blocked by metal or thick materials. The network relies on other Android devices nearby, so dense urban areas usually report faster than remote trails.

You can also use a Pixel Watch with the Find Hub Wear OS app to check location or trigger the chime without pulling out your phone.

## Conclusion

Setting up a Pixel Tag takes under two minutes with Fast Pair and immediately adds your item to Find Hub. From there you can locate it on a map across the global Android network or walk right up to it with directional guidance when nearby. Sharing with family and left-behind alerts add extra peace of mind, while the replaceable battery keeps the tag useful for years.

Google’s official support pages cover the exact connection flow, map and nearby finding, and sharing steps. Start with the Find Hub app, follow the prompts, and you will have a simple, private way to keep track of the things you use every day.
