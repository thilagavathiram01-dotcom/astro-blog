Google Play Store version 53.5, dated 5 October 2026, adds a way to see your global influence on reviews. The official system release notes say you can now find total views and helpfulness votes on the reviews you have posted.

That pairs with an earlier change in Play Store v53.2, dated 14 September 2026. That release added a Contribution Hub cluster on the You tab so you can submit ratings and reviews and track the social impact of your contributions. Together, the two updates give reviewers a clearer picture of whether their notes are being read.

This guide covers how to update, where to look, what the numbers mean, and how to write a review that other people can actually use.

## What Google shipped in October

Google publishes system services notes for Play services, the Play Store, and related system apps. The [October 2026 notes](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/14343500) list two phone changes for Google Play Store v53.5:

- AI-generated images in India now include an AI label, with some exceptions. That label is covered in our guide to [Play Store AI image labels in India](/blog/label-ai-images-play-store-india/).
- You can find your global influence, including total views and helpfulness votes on your reviews.

The same notes also list Play services v26.39, which adds NFC shortcuts for Find Hub tags and a registration flow that can set a category, a name, and Left-Behind Reminders during setup. Those tracker changes are separate from review stats. If you use network tags, the [Find Hub NFC registration guide](/blog/find-hub-tags-nfc-registration-october-2026/) walks through that side of the update.

A line in the release notes does not guarantee the control is on every phone the same day. Google rolls Play Store versions through the store, and some surfaces appear for a subset of accounts first. Check the app version before you hunt for a missing panel.

## Update the Play Store to v53.5

Play Store updates arrive through Play itself. On a phone or tablet:

1. Open the Google Play Store app.
2. Tap your profile picture, then tap **Manage apps & device**.
3. Under **Updates available**, look for Google Play Store. If it is listed, tap **Update**.
4. If the store is not listed, open **Manage**, search for Play Store, open the listing, and tap **Update** if the button is there.
5. Confirm the version from the store listing or from **Settings > Apps > Google Play Store > App info**. You want **53.5** or later, released 5 October 2026.

On Pixel phones, Google also points people at system services from Settings: tap your name at the top for Google services, open the All services tab, then Privacy & security, then System services. That path refreshes Play services and related packages. The review influence numbers live in the Play Store app, so the store update is the one that matters for this feature.

Restart the Play Store after the update if the You tab still looks like the September layout.

## Open Contribution Hub on the You tab

Play Store v53.2 introduced the Contribution Hub cluster on the You tab. Google describes it as a place to submit ratings and reviews while you track the social impact of your contributions. Version 53.5 adds the global influence view on top of that, with total views and helpfulness votes.

To get there:

1. Open the Play Store and sign in with the Google Account you use for installs.
2. Tap **You** in the bottom bar.
3. Scroll to the Contribution Hub cluster.
4. Open the influence or contributions entry. The notes do not publish a pixel-level label beyond global influence, total views, and helpfulness votes, so the wording on your phone may be short.
5. Read the totals for views and helpful votes across the reviews tied to that account.

If the cluster is missing, you are either still on an older Play Store build or the cluster has not reached your account yet. Update again in a day or two rather than clearing app data, which can sign you out and drop local store state.

Helpfulness votes are the marks other people leave when a review was useful. Views are how often the review was seen. Google has not published a formula that converts those two numbers into ranking weight, so treat them as feedback on your writing, not as a score that moves an app in search.

## What a public Play review already shows

Google’s ratings and reviews policy explains what other people see when you post. Most reviews are public. Public reviews show your name and profile image from your Google Account, plus past edits, to anyone using Play, including the developer. Private reviews are usually limited to early access or beta tests, and only the developer can see those.

App and game star ratings on the store listing are weighted toward current quality, not a simple lifetime average, unless the app has very few ratings. Play still shows the lifetime review count. Ratings for books, movies, and TV shows use the lifetime average instead.

You can only review apps and games you have downloaded. Reviews from a work or school account are blocked. If any account on the device is in a beta for that app, you cannot leave a public review for it. New public ratings are generally held for about a day so Play can check for suspicious activity. Developers can still see and reply during that window.

## Write a review that earns helpful votes

Play’s posting policy asks reviewers to be useful, cover both strengths and drawbacks, stay readable, and stay on the product. Fake reviews, repeat posts, multi-account campaigns, and off-topic political commentary can be removed.

A short review that names a concrete version of the app is easier to vote helpful than a one-word rating:

1. Open Play Store, search for an app you installed, and open the listing.
2. Under **Rate this app**, pick a star rating.
3. Tap **Write a review**.
4. State what you did in the app, what worked, and what failed. Mention the device only if the bug is device-specific.
5. Tap **Post**.

You can edit or delete a review later from the same listing or from your profile. Edits are visible on public reviews. Deleting a review removes it from the public listing.

If you want the new counters to move, post on apps you still use. A review of an app you uninstalled two years ago rarely gets fresh views.

## Tips if the numbers do not appear

- Confirm Play Store is 53.5 or newer before you assume the feature is missing.
- Sign in with the same account that posted the reviews. Influence is tied to that account’s contributions, not to every profile on the phone.
- Enterprise accounts cannot post public app reviews, so they will not accumulate public helpful votes.
- Beta-only feedback stays with the developer. Do not expect those notes in a public influence total.
- A views count can rise without helpful votes. People often read a review and leave without tapping the helpful control.
- Developers see ratings and clustered review topics in Play Console. That dashboard is separate from the user-facing influence totals in the store app.

## Why the counters matter

Play already tells developers that ratings reflect recent quality and that review text is public. Until October 2026, the person who wrote the review had little official feedback beyond a star they chose themselves. Total views and helpfulness votes close that gap without publishing a ranking formula.

Use the numbers to edit weak reviews, not to chase volume. A single specific note about a crash, a subscription price, or a missing offline mode is more useful than five generic five-star posts. If a developer replies, update the review when the issue is fixed so the public record matches the current app.

## Conclusion

Play Store v53.5, released 5 October 2026, lets you see global influence on your reviews, including total views and helpfulness votes. The You tab Contribution Hub from v53.2 is the place to submit reviews and track that impact. Update the store app, open You, and read the totals for the account that posted the reviews. Write specific notes, stay within Play’s posting rules, and treat the counters as a readership signal rather than a ranking lever.
