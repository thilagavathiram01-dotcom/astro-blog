You open a long product page or a dense report and need a quick summary. Switching to another tab or pasting text into a chatbot breaks your flow. Gemini in Chrome solves this by placing an AI assistant in a side panel next to the page you are reading.

Google’s official blog describes the feature this way: click the Gemini icon in the Chrome toolbar to open the side panel, then ask Gemini to summarize a dense report, compare product details, or explain complicated topics in plain language while you keep your place on the page.

This guide covers the exact steps, what you can ask, how to share multiple tabs, and the settings that control the experience. All details come from Google’s Gemini Apps Help and blog.google.

## What you need before you start

Gemini in Chrome works on Chromebook Plus, Mac, and Windows computers. You must use the latest version of Chrome and be signed in with a Google account. The feature is not available in Incognito mode.

You need to be 13 or older (or the applicable age in your country) and located in a supported region. Your device language must be one of the supported languages listed in Google’s help pages. For work or school accounts, an administrator must enable access.

Parents can turn off the feature for supervised teens through Family Link. The feature is rolling out gradually, so the Ask Gemini button may not appear for every user yet.

Update Chrome through the three-dot menu → Help → About Google Chrome. Restart the browser after the update finishes.

## Open the Gemini side panel

1. Open Chrome and go to any webpage.
2. Click the Ask Gemini button at the top of the browser window (usually the top-right toolbar).
3. The first time you use it, follow the on-screen instructions to opt in.
4. The side panel opens beside the page. Type your question or request in the text box and press Enter.

By default, Gemini uses content from the current tab. After you opt in, Gemini is added to the Mac menu bar or Windows system tray. Keyboard shortcuts become available: typically Alt+G on Windows and Ctrl+G on Mac to open or close the panel.

You can right-click the Gemini button and choose Unpin Gemini if you want to hide the toolbar icon later.

## Summarize a page and ask follow-up questions

With the side panel open, type a clear request such as “Summarize the key takeaways from this page in five bullet points” or “Explain the main technical terms in plain language.” Gemini reads the shared page content and replies in the panel.

Common useful prompts from Google’s examples include:

- Summarize key takeaways from an article
- Explain a complex topic in a different way
- Help you test your knowledge of a new subject
- Modify a recipe to meet dietary needs
- Make recommendations based on your preferences

You stay on the original page the entire time. The panel remains open as you switch tabs if you enable the related setting.

## Compare information across multiple tabs

You can share up to 10 open tabs with Gemini in addition to the current one. This is useful when you research products, news stories, or documentation across several sites.

To share tabs:

1. In the Gemini side panel text box, click Add tabs or Update tabs.
2. Select the open tabs you want to include. A check mark appears on shared tabs.
3. You can also type @ followed by part of a tab title to add it quickly.

A glowing underline appears on tabs that Gemini is actively using. Ask something like “Compare the price, battery life, and weight of these three laptops in a table” or “List the main differences between these three articles.”

Gemini consolidates the information from the shared page content. For Google Workspace pages (Docs, Sheets, etc.), it may access more of the document when you connect the Workspace app.

## Control what Gemini can see and do

Open Chrome Settings → AI innovations → Gemini in Chrome. Under Preferences you can turn these on or off:

- Show Gemini at the top of the browser
- Show Gemini in the Mac menu bar or Windows system tray and enable the keyboard shortcut
- Keep the Gemini sidebar open when you open a new tab

Under Permissions:

- Share current tab by default (turn this off if you prefer to share tabs manually each time)
- Precise location
- Microphone (required for Live mode)
- Let Gemini browse for you (auto browse; not available to users under 18)

Auto browse lets Gemini navigate sites and complete multi-step tasks such as research or form filling. It requires a Google AI Pro or AI Ultra plan and is still rolling out. You can pause or take over a task at any time.

You can also pop the chat out into a floating window or dock it back to the side panel from the panel’s top menu.

## Tips for better results

Write specific prompts that state the desired format. “Summarize this in three short paragraphs” works better than “summarize.”

Start a new chat when you change topics. The current tab is shared by default in a new chat unless you disable that setting.

Use the model selector in the text box if you want to switch Gemini models. Connect Google Workspace apps when you need Gemini to draft emails or create calendar events based on page content.

Check important answers against the original page. Gemini can make mistakes, so verify numbers, quotes, and critical details.

If the Ask Gemini button is missing, confirm you are signed in, update Chrome, disable any VPN, and check that your region and language are supported. Restart the browser after changes.

## Conclusion

The Gemini side panel in Chrome lets you summarize pages, compare multiple tabs, and ask questions without leaving the site you are reading. Open it with the toolbar button or keyboard shortcut, share up to 10 tabs when needed, and adjust the share-current-tab and auto-browse settings to match your privacy preferences.

Update Chrome, sign in, and try a summary on the next long article you open. The feature continues to expand by region and account type, so check Google’s help pages if the button does not appear yet.
