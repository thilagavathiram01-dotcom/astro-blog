Google Chat users can now schedule meetings without leaving the conversation. Gemini detects when someone asks to meet, underlines the request, and offers up to three available times from Google Calendar. You pick one and click Schedule. The meeting appears on calendars, and a confirmation lands in the chat.

This feature started rolling out on November 9, 2026, for eligible Google Workspace plans. It works in direct messages and small groups on the web version of Chat.

## What the Gemini scheduling card includes

When Gemini spots a clear meeting request, it underlines the relevant text. Hover over the underline and a card opens. The card shows:

- Up to three suggested times based on Calendar availability. Conflicts are flagged.
- Attendees pulled from the conversation, including people mentioned by name.
- A suggested title drawn from the chat context. You can edit it right on the card.

Select a time and click Schedule. The event is created. A short confirmation appears in the chat so everyone sees it. Need more details? Click Open in Calendar to finish the invite there.

Gemini only underlines messages with a clear request for a new meeting. Casual mentions of dates do not trigger it.

## Who can use it and where

The feature is available on Google Chat for the web at chat.google.com and in the Chat panel inside Gmail. At launch it works in English. It applies to direct messages and group chats with up to five people.

Eligible plans include Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus, Frontline Plus, and AI Ultra Access. The feature is on by default for users who have smart features and personalization enabled. There is no separate admin toggle for this specific capability. Admins control access to Gemini features and smart features more broadly.

Rollout began November 9, 2026, and may take more than 15 days for full visibility across Rapid Release and Scheduled Release domains.

## Step-by-step: schedule a meeting from Chat

1. Open Google Chat on the web or the Chat panel in Gmail.
2. Make sure smart features and personalization are turned on for your account. Check Settings in Chat or Gmail if the underline never appears.
3. In a direct message or small group (five people or fewer), have someone ask clearly for a meeting. Examples: “Are you free tomorrow afternoon to go over the proposal?” or “Let’s grab 15 minutes to discuss this.”
4. Look for the underlined phrase. Hover over it.
5. Review the suggested times, attendees, and title on the card. Edit the title if needed.
6. Choose a time and click Schedule.
7. Confirm the message that appears in the chat. Check your Calendar if you want to verify the event.

If you prefer full control, click Open in Calendar instead of Schedule. This opens the full event form so you can add a description, location, or video link before sending.

## Tips for better results

Phrase the request clearly. Vague language such as “we should talk sometime” is less likely to trigger the underline. Include a rough time window when you can.

Keep the group small. The feature only works in chats of five people or fewer. Larger spaces still use the normal Calendar workflow.

Check Calendar permissions. Suggested times depend on visible free/busy information. If someone has a fully private calendar, fewer accurate slots appear.

Use it alongside other Gemini tools. Pair this with skills in Google Workspace or meeting notes in Google Meet for a smoother end-to-end flow. See our guide on [building Gemini skills in Workspace](/blog/build-gemini-workspace-skills-google-docs/) for related automation ideas.

## Current limits

The feature is web-only at launch. Mobile Chat does not show the underline yet. It is English-only for now. It does not appear in large spaces or rooms. Gemini ignores unclear or already-scheduled meeting mentions to avoid noise.

If the card does not appear, confirm your plan, that smart features are on, and that the conversation meets the size limit. Contact your Workspace admin if Gemini features are restricted.

## Why this helps teams

Scheduling is one of the most frequent interruptions in chat. Opening Calendar, checking availability, and copying times back into the thread adds friction. The one-click card removes that step for the most common cases. Teams stay in the conversation, pick a slot, and move on.
