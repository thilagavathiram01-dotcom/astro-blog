You need a ride to the airport or a quick grocery order, but you do not want to open three apps and tap through the same screens. Gemini screen automation on Android now handles those multi-step tasks inside supported apps on Pixel 10 and Pixel 11 devices in more countries.

Google expanded the beta feature in the November 2026 Pixel Drop. Task automation (powered by screen automation) is now available in the UK, India, and Japan, in addition to the US. Pixel 10 series phones also receive the full Gemini Intelligence suite, including this capability.

This guide shows the exact requirements, steps, and limits based on Google’s official Pixel support documentation.

## What Screen Automation Does

Gemini opens the relevant app inside a secure on-device virtual window. It navigates menus, selects items, and builds a cart or reservation. You watch progress in a live notification or by tapping View progress. Gemini stops before the final confirmation or payment so you complete that step yourself.

Supported everyday tasks include booking rides, ordering food or groceries, buying tickets, and (on some Samsung devices) making travel reservations. The feature works only with select apps that Google has allowlisted. It runs in English, Japanese, and Korean for now.

## Devices, Regions, and Requirements

You need:

- Pixel 10, Pixel 10 Pro, Pixel 10 Pro XL, Pixel 10 Pro Fold, or any Pixel 11 series model
- Or Samsung Galaxy S26 series, Z Flip 8, Z Fold 8, or Tab S12 series
- Age 18 or older
- Personal Google Account (work, school, and supervised accounts are not supported)
- Location in the US, UK, India, Japan, or South Korea (Pixel 10 series is not supported in South Korea)
- Gemini mobile app on Android (including Hey Google activation)

The feature is rolling out gradually. Check that your device has the November 2026 Pixel Drop or later. Screen automation is different from Gemini Tasks (scheduled workflows) and Connected Apps.

## Step-by-Step: Start a Screen Automation Task

1. Open the Gemini app on your phone, or say “Hey Google” / long-press the power button.

2. Describe the task clearly. Examples from Google’s help page:
   - “Book a ride to the airport.”
   - “Reorder my last coffee.”
   - “Order pizza for delivery.”
   - “Add milk and eggs to my grocery cart.”

3. The first time you use screen automation (or a specific app), follow the on-screen prompts to grant the Google app permission to interact with other apps’ screens.

4. Review Gemini’s action plan if it appears. You can reply with changes.

5. Gemini works in the background. A notification shows progress. Tap View progress to see the virtual window.

6. When Gemini reaches a sensitive step (payment, final confirmation, sign-in), it pauses and asks you to take control or finish the action yourself.

On Pixel 10 and Galaxy S26 devices you can stop a task but cannot take full control mid-process the same way as on some foldables. Use the Stop task button in the chat or notification at any time.

## Manage Permissions and Limits

Open the Gemini app → profile icon → Settings → Screen automation. For each app you can choose Always allow, Ask every time, or Do not allow.

Google limits the number of screen automation requests per day and concurrent tasks. The exact numbers appear in the app when you approach the cap. Free accounts receive a smaller daily allowance than AI Pro or Ultra subscribers.

Do not type passwords, payment details, or other sensitive information into the Gemini chat. Enter those directly in the app after you take control.

## Tips for Reliable Results

Be specific with location, quantity, and preferences. “Order a large pepperoni pizza from the place I used last week” works better than “order dinner.”

Keep the target apps updated from the Play Store. Only the official signed versions of allowlisted apps qualify.

You can start some tasks with a photo or shared screen. For example, share a Maps view of a landmark and ask Gemini to book a nearby hotel.

Monitor the live notification, especially for time-sensitive requests. You remain responsible for any purchases or actions Gemini performs.

If you already use scheduled Gemini Tasks, note that screen automation is a separate beta limited to on-device app interaction. See our earlier guide on [automating multi-step tasks with Gemini](/blog/automate-multi-step-tasks-gemini-android/) for the initial US rollout details.

## Safety and Data Notes

Gemini can make mistakes: it might select the wrong item, quantity, or address. Always review the plan and the final screen. Prompt injection risks exist if malicious content appears inside an app, but Google has designed safeguards that pause for confirmation on payments, address changes, and messages.

The virtual window isolates the session. Gemini does not gain broad access to the rest of your device. Activity is subject to Gemini’s standard data handling; check the linked help page for details on what is stored.

## Conclusion

Gemini screen automation turns a voice or text request into a prepared cart or reservation on supported Pixel 10, Pixel 11, and select Galaxy devices. With the November 2026 expansion to the UK, India, and Japan, more users can offload routine food, ride, and shopping steps while keeping final confirmation in their own hands.

Update your Pixel to the latest Drop, open Gemini, and try a clear request. Check Settings → Screen automation if the option does not appear immediately.
