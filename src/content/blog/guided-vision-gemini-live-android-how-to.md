Google launched Guided Vision in Gemini Live on October 1, 2026. It turns your phone camera into a conversational visual partner that describes what it sees and coaches you on framing. The feature targets people who are blind or have low vision, but anyone can use it for reading labels, finding objects, or checking details.

You share the live camera feed during a Gemini Live session. Gemini responds with spoken descriptions and natural prompts such as “pan slowly to the right” or “tilt downward” when the view needs adjustment. It supports everyday tasks without requiring perfect framing from the start.

## What Guided Vision does

Guided Vision was built with input from the blind and low-vision community, including testing with Aira specialists and over 1,000 trusted testers. It provides real-time audio interpretation of your surroundings while you talk to Gemini.

Core capabilities include:

- Reading fine print on nutrition labels, appliance dials, or restaurant menus in low light.
- Locating everyday objects such as a dropped earbud or a specific jar in a cabinet.
- Describing colors, patterns, and shapes for tasks like matching clothing.
- Giving overviews of rooms or table layouts.
- Supporting conversations in multiple languages based on community feedback from regions including India, Brazil, and Japan.

Google stresses that Guided Vision is an assistive tool. It can make mistakes. It is not a medical device, mobility aid, or white-cane replacement, and it should not be used for navigation, obstacle detection, or safe travel guidance. Continue using established mobility practices.

The feature works on Android devices running Android 9 or later in regions and languages where Gemini Live is available. Update the Gemini app and your system software for the best experience. Rollout is gradual, so the option may not appear immediately on every device.

## How to enable Guided Vision

You can turn it on through the Gemini app or Android accessibility settings.

### In the Gemini app

1. Open the Gemini app and tap your profile picture or account name.
2. Go to Settings.
3. Find and switch on “Use Guided Vision in Live.”
4. A prompt may appear offering to start a Live session right away. Accept it if you want to begin immediately.

Once enabled, start Gemini Live from the app and tap the camera share control. The session begins with the camera feed active.

### Using accessibility shortcuts

1. Open Android Settings.
2. Tap Accessibility.
3. Select Vision assistance, then Guided Vision.
4. Configure an Accessibility shortcut. Options include the floating accessibility button, a two-finger swipe gesture, or pressing both volume keys together.

This lets you launch Guided Vision quickly without opening the Gemini app first.

### With TalkBack

If you use Google TalkBack:

1. Perform a three-finger tap to open the TalkBack menu.
2. Select Guided Vision from the options.

The feature launches directly into a camera-sharing Live session.

## Using Guided Vision in a Live session

Start a Gemini Live conversation after enabling the setting. Share your camera when prompted or by tapping the camera icon.

Speak naturally. Ask questions such as “What does this label say?” or “Where is the black pepper?” Gemini describes the scene and answers follow-ups. If the camera angle is off, it gives spoken cues to reposition the phone.

You can interrupt and redirect at any time, the same way regular Gemini Live works. The conversation stays contextual as long as the camera remains shared.

For best results:

- Hold the phone steady once Gemini confirms good framing.
- Ask specific follow-ups after the initial description.
- Use it in good lighting when possible, though it handles dim conditions for text reading.
- End the camera share when finished to return to voice-only Live or close the session.

If the feature is missing, check for app and system updates, confirm Gemini Live is supported in your region, and try again later as the rollout continues.

## Tips and limitations

Combine Guided Vision with other Gemini Live tools if needed. Screen sharing remains available for digital content, while Guided Vision focuses on the physical camera view.

Privacy controls follow standard Gemini Apps Activity settings. Review your activity history preferences in the Gemini app if you want to manage stored conversations.

Because it relies on generative AI, descriptions can be incomplete or incorrect. Verify critical information such as medication labels or safety instructions with another method.

## Conclusion

Guided Vision adds practical, conversational visual help to Gemini Live. Enable it in the Gemini app settings or through Android accessibility shortcuts, share your camera, and ask for descriptions or object locations. The feature is available on compatible Android devices running version 9 or higher, with gradual rollout.

Update your apps, test the toggle, and try it on a simple task such as reading a package label. It keeps you in control while providing spoken guidance that earlier camera sharing did not emphasize.
