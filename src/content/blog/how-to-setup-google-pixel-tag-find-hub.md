Google Pixel Tag is now available, giving Android users a first-party way to track keys, wallets, bags, and luggage. Announced at Made by Google in August and on sale from 11 November 2026, it costs $29.99 for one or $99.99 for a four-pack. It works with any Android phone running Android 9 or later through the Find Hub app and the network of more than one billion Android devices.

This guide walks through official setup, finding items nearby or far away, ringing the tag or your phone, and sharing access. All steps come from Google’s Pixel Phone Help and product blog posts.

The November Pixel Drop also brought Gemini Intelligence features to the Pixel 10 series. See the [how-to on Rambler and Create My Widget](/blog/gemini-intelligence-pixel-10-rambler-create-widget-guide/) for those updates.

## What you need before starting

Pixel Tag requires:

- An Android phone running Android 9 or later.
- Bluetooth turned on.
- An internet connection.
- The phone unlocked on the home screen.
- The plastic battery tab removed from the tag.

Find Hub is the app that manages the tag. It is available on the Play Store and may already be present on recent Pixel devices. Location services should be enabled for full functionality.

The tag itself is IP67 water- and dust-resistant, measures about 5.4 mm thick, and uses a user-replaceable CR2032 coin-cell battery rated for over one year of typical use. It includes a speaker and a physical button.

## Step-by-step setup with Fast Pair

Google designed pairing to be quick via Fast Pair.

1. Turn on Bluetooth on your Android phone.
2. Confirm the phone has internet access and is unlocked on the home screen.
3. Place the Pixel Tag next to the phone.
4. Completely remove the plastic tab from the battery contact point on the tag. The tag may play a short sound when it powers on.
5. A pop-up appears on the phone. Tap Connect.
6. Read the Acceptable Use Policy and tap Continue.
7. Read the Terms and Conditions and tap I accept.
8. When prompted for Location permissions, select When using this app.
9. The tag is added to Find Hub.

After setup, open Find Hub, select the tag, and choose a category such as Keys, Backpack, or Other. The name defaults to something like “Your name’s keys.” You can edit it.

If the pop-up does not appear:

- Wait five minutes (or 24 hours after a second dismissal). Restarting the phone or toggling Bluetooth can clear the wait.
- Go to Settings → Connected devices. The tag may appear at the top.
- Open the Find Hub app, tap Add Device, and follow the on-screen steps.

Avoid placing the tag inside carbon-fiber or aluminum luggage or RFID-blocking wallets, as these can block the signal. For checked baggage, use exterior fabric pockets. The battery complies with airline rules for both carry-on and checked luggage.

## Locate items on the map and ring the tag

Open the Find Hub app to see registered items on a map. The last known location appears when the tag is out of Bluetooth range. The crowdsourced Find Hub network of Android devices helps update the location securely with end-to-end encrypted data.

When the tag is nearby (within Bluetooth range):

- Tap Play sound or Ring to make it chime. Volume can be adjusted in the tag’s settings.
- Use Find nearby for directional help.

Ultra-Wideband (UWB) with Bluetooth Channel Sounding provides visual distance and arrow guidance on compatible phones. Support includes recent Pixel Pro models and certain other Android devices. Check your phone’s settings for UWB and keep the tag charged for best results.

You can also enable Left Behind alerts so the phone notifies you if you leave the tagged item behind.

## Ring your phone from the tag

The physical button on the back of the Pixel Tag works in reverse. Press it twice while the tag is nearby to make your phone ring, even if the phone is on silent. Press once to stop the sound, or use the phone’s power button after unlocking.

This is useful if you misplace the phone but still have the tagged keys or bag. NFC on the tag (located near the battery door) lets you tap it to a compatible phone to open the device details page quickly.

## Share the tag with others

You can share a Pixel Tag with up to 10 people so family or colleagues can also locate shared items such as car keys or a backpack.

From the owner’s device:

1. Open Find Hub and select the tag.
2. Tap Share device.
3. Follow the prompts to send an invitation via any messaging app.

The recipient has 24 hours to accept on an Android device. They may need to install Find Hub. After acceptance, the owner confirms the share in the tag’s settings. A 4-digit PIN appears for added security.

Location detection after sharing can take several minutes for privacy reasons.

## Tips and maintenance

- Replace the CR2032 battery by twisting the battery door counterclockwise, swapping the cell, and closing it. No tools are required.
- Keep Bluetooth and location services enabled on phones that will locate the tag.
- For precision finding, use a phone that supports UWB and Channel Sounding.
- Rename tags clearly and assign categories so multiple tags are easy to distinguish.
- Review the Acceptable Use Policy. Tracking people without consent is prohibited and may be illegal.

If a tag is lost far away, mark it as lost in Find Hub so someone who finds it can contact you via the message or details you set.

## Conclusion

Setting up a Google Pixel Tag takes only a couple of minutes with Fast Pair and Find Hub. Once paired, you can see its location on a map, ring it when nearby, get precision guidance on supported phones, and reverse-ring your phone from the tag’s button. Sharing with up to 10 people covers household items, while the replaceable battery and IP67 rating support everyday use.

Start by pairing one tag to your keys or wallet, test the ring and Find nearby features at home, then decide if a four-pack makes sense for more items. Keep the system updated so new Find Hub improvements appear.
