Pixel Watch users gained a practical training tool with the November 2026 Pixel Drop. Step-by-step workouts now deliver live guidance for each exercise directly on your wrist.

This feature, first shown with Pixel Watch 5, rolls out to Pixel Watch 3 and newer models. You follow strength sessions, mixed routines, or Coach-built plans without pulling out your phone between sets. The watch and Google Health app stay in sync the whole time.

![Person checking smartwatch during gym workout](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1764426445444-5a528029fd62?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What you need before starting

Supported watches include Pixel Watch 3, 4, and 5 running Wear OS 7.1 or later. Update the Google Health app on both your phone and watch. Phone version 5.12 or newer and watch version 4.12 or newer are required according to support notes.

Google Health Coach workouts need a Google Health Premium subscription plus an internet connection for generation. Custom routines you build yourself from the exercise library do not require Premium. Pair your watch with an Android phone running the latest Google Health app.

Check for updates in the Play Store, then open the watch Settings > System > System update if the feature does not appear immediately. Rollouts continue over several weeks after the November 10 start.

## Build or request a workout

Open the Google Health app on your phone. You have two paths.

For a custom routine, choose the option to build a workout manually. Select exercises from the library of over 1,000 predefined moves. Set sets, reps, weights, and rest periods. Save the routine.

For AI help, go to Google Health Coach (Premium). Describe what you want, such as a 30-minute dumbbell session or a full-body strength plan that accounts for recovery data. The Coach generates a tailored routine. You can edit it before starting.

Once ready, tap Start. The workout launches on both your phone and paired Pixel Watch automatically.

## Follow the session on your watch

The watch screen shows the current exercise, target reps or duration, form cues, and a preview of the next move. Live metrics such as heart rate appear on the watch or phone.

Log completed reps and the weight used with a few taps on the watch face. You do not need your phone nearby. Edit the plan mid-workout if a weight feels wrong or you want fewer reps. Changes sync to the other device.

Automatic progression is on by default. Visual timers and audio cues move you to the next set or rest period when the goal finishes. Turn this off during the workout if you prefer manual control. Pause or end the session from either device; the other updates right away.

If Bluetooth drops, continue on one device. The watch keeps tracking. Stats appear in the Google Health app and on the watch after you finish.

## Practical tips for better sessions

Start with a short custom routine to learn the flow before using Coach plans. Keep the watch snug so heart-rate readings stay reliable during lifts.

Use the phone view if you want a larger list of remaining exercises. After the workout, review sets, weights, and heart-rate zones in the app to adjust future sessions.

Pair this with other Pixel Drop tools. For example, our guide on [Pixel Search](/blog/how-to-use-pixel-search-on-pixel-6a-phones/) helps you quickly find Health app settings or past workouts on your phone.

Confirm Google Health Premium status if Coach options do not appear. Results vary by device software version and region.

## Conclusion

Step-by-step workouts turn your Pixel Watch into a practical gym companion. Build routines yourself or let Google Health Coach create them, then follow clear guidance and log everything on your wrist. Update your apps, start a short session, and check how the sync works for your training style.
