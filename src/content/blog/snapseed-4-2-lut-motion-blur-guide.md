Google’s free Snapseed photo editor just received version 4.2. The update adds Motion Blur, depth-based Lens Blur with Bokeh Boost, full multi-wheel Color Grading on Android, and the ability to import presets and LUTs from other apps.

The rollout is gradual on Android and iOS. Check the Play Store or App Store for the update. Once it arrives, these tools give mobile photographers more control without leaving the free app.

This guide shows how to use the main new features with clear steps based on the official changelog shared by the Snapseed team.

## Import Presets and LUTs

Snapseed 4.2 lets you bring in presets and Look Up Tables you already use in apps such as Lightroom. Supported formats include .cube, .xmp, .3dl, .png, and .snapseed files.

Open the gallery menu in Snapseed and tap Manage Looks. Select the import option and choose your file. The Look appears in your library so you can apply it to any photo.

You can also share your own custom Looks. Choose whether to include the LUTs they use when you export. Community Looks tagged #SnapseedLook appear inside the app for browsing and testing.

## Apply Motion Blur

The new Motion Blur tool adds a sense of speed to still photos. You have three options: blur the entire scene, streak the background behind a sharp subject for a panning effect, or add motion directly to the subject.

Open a photo and select the Motion Blur tool from the tools list. Adjust the strength slider and choose the area to affect. Preview the result and refine until the direction and intensity match your goal.

This works well for sports, vehicles, or any subject that should feel dynamic. Start with a moderate strength so the subject remains recognizable.

## Use Depth-Based Lens Blur

Lens Blur already existed in Snapseed. Version 4.2 adds a Depth option that uses the scene’s depth information to soften the background more naturally, similar to a wide-aperture lens.

Open Lens Blur and switch to the Depth mode. Snapseed analyzes the photo and separates foreground from background. Increase the blur amount and turn on Bokeh Boost if you want highlights to bloom into softer circles.

The effect depends on the original photo having usable depth data or clear subject separation. Test on portraits or close subjects first for the strongest results.

## Color Grading on Android

Full multi-wheel Color Grading is rolling out to Android users alongside the LUT import support. The tool lets you adjust shadows, midtones, and highlights separately with color wheels.

Open the Color Grade tool on a photo. Select the luminance or hue wheel for each tonal range. Make small adjustments and check the overall balance before exporting.

Combine it with imported LUTs for a consistent look across your library. iOS users already had landscape orientation support, which now extends to iPhone and iPad for easier editing.

## Tips for Best Results

- Update Snapseed fully before trying the new tools. The gradual rollout means some devices receive features later than others.
- Import one or two LUTs at a time and test them on different photos to see how they render.
- For Motion Blur, shoot or choose images with clear subjects against simpler backgrounds so the effect stays readable.
- Depth-based blur works best when the subject is distinctly separated from the background.
- Save a stack before heavy edits so you can return to earlier versions without losing work.

If you develop Android apps that handle photos or media, consider how users export edited images. Our earlier guide on [Android CLI skills for any agent](/blog/android-cli-skills-any-agent/) covers ways to automate related tasks in development workflows.

## Conclusion

Snapseed 4.2 brings professional-style tools into a free mobile editor. Import the presets you already trust, add motion or depth blur for impact, and grade colors with the multi-wheel controls on Android.

Check for the update in your app store. Once it appears, open Manage Looks to start importing files and experiment with the new blur options on a few test photos.

The changes keep Snapseed competitive with paid alternatives while remaining free and available on both Android and iOS.
