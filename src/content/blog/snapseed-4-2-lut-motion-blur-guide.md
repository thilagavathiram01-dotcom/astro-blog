Google’s free Snapseed app is rolling out version 4.2 on Android and iOS. The update adds Motion Blur (now on Android), depth-based Lens Blur, LUT and preset imports, and full multi-wheel Color Grading for Android users.

This guide shows how to access the new tools once the update reaches your device. The rollout is gradual, so check the Play Store or App Store if the features are missing.

![Person holding a smartphone camera](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1516035069371-29a1b244cc32?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

## What’s new in Snapseed 4.2

The Snapseed team described the goal as giving more creative control so users can bring favorite editing styles into the app, share Looks, and try new tools. Key additions include:

- **Motion Blur**: Blur the whole scene, create a panning effect on the background, or apply motion to the subject.
- **Depth-based Lens Blur**: Uses scene depth for more natural background softening, plus a Bokeh Boost control.
- **Preset & LUT Import**: Support for .cube, .xmp, .3dl, .png, and .snapseed files. Share custom Looks with optional LUTs included.
- **Color Grading**: Full multi-wheel controls rolling out on Android.
- **Community Looks**: Browse and try Looks shared by other users (tagged #SnapseedLook).
- **Landscape mode**: Available on iPhone and iPad.

These features close some gaps with paid editors while keeping Snapseed free and ad-free.

## Update Snapseed first

1. Open the Google Play Store or Apple App Store.
2. Search for Snapseed.
3. Tap Update if version 4.2 or later is available.
4. Restart the app after the update finishes.

Some features may depend on a server-side switch, so they can appear a short time after the app update installs. If LUT import is missing, wait and check again later.

## How to import LUTs and presets

Snapseed now accepts files from Lightroom and other editors. Supported formats are .cube, .xmp, .3dl, .png, and .snapseed.

1. Open Snapseed and go to the gallery view.
2. Tap the menu (usually three dots or the gallery menu).
3. Select Manage Looks.
4. Choose Import.
5. Select the LUT or preset file from your device storage or cloud.
6. Confirm the import.

Once imported, the Look appears in your Looks list. You can apply it to any photo and adjust further with other tools. You can also share your own Looks and choose whether to include the LUTs they use.

Tip: Start with a simple .xmp or .cube file you already know works in another app. Test on a non-critical photo first.

## Using Motion Blur

Motion Blur was previously limited to iOS. Android users now have it too. It helps turn still photos into images that feel dynamic.

1. Open a photo in Snapseed.
2. Tap Tools.
3. Select Motion Blur (it may appear under Style or as a dedicated tool).
4. Choose the mode:
   - Whole scene blur.
   - Background only (panning effect).
   - Subject only.
5. Adjust strength, direction, and any available sliders.
6. Use masks or subregions if you need precise control.
7. Tap the checkmark to apply.

Experiment with sports, cars, or any subject that benefits from implied movement. Combine it with Selective or Brush tools for better subject isolation.

## Depth-based Lens Blur

Lens Blur already existed. The new Depth option uses the photo’s depth information for more natural results.

1. Open Tools and select Lens Blur.
2. Switch to the Depth mode.
3. Position the focus area.
4. Adjust blur strength.
5. Raise Bokeh Boost if you want highlights to bloom more.
6. Apply and refine with masks if needed.

On the first use, Snapseed may download additional data. After that, the tool works offline.

## Color Grading on Android

Full multi-wheel Color Grading is rolling out to Android alongside the LUT tools. It lets you adjust shadows, midtones, and highlights separately.

1. Open a photo.
2. Tap Tools.
3. Select Color Grade (or the equivalent color tool).
4. Use the color wheels for shadows, midtones, and highlights.
5. Adjust luminance or other sliders as available.
6. Combine with an imported LUT for a starting point, then fine-tune.

This gives more precise control than basic filters.

## Browse Community Looks

Snapseed now surfaces community-made Looks inside the app. Look for the Discover or Community section, often tied to the #SnapseedLook tag.

You can try a Look, inspect the edit recipe, and adapt it to your own photos. Sharing works in both directions, so your own Looks can reach other users.

## Tips for better results

- Work on copies of important photos so you can compare before and after.
- Import one LUT at a time until you confirm it applies cleanly.
- Use masks after Motion Blur or Lens Blur to protect the subject.
- Pair new tools with existing ones such as Curves, Selective, and Healing.
- Check for the update periodically if features are missing—rollouts take time.

## Conclusion

Snapseed 4.2 brings practical pro tools to a free app. LUT imports reduce the need to rebuild looks from scratch. Motion Blur and depth-aware Lens Blur add creative options for stills. Color Grading rounds out the color controls on Android.

Update the app, try the new tools on a few photos, and share Looks with the community if you create something useful. The gradual rollout means patience may be required, but the features are worth the wait once they appear.
